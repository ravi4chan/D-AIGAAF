# D-AIGAAF Reference Model

## 1. Purpose

The Defence AI Governance, Assurance & Operational Authorisation Framework (D-AIGAAF) provides a structured lifecycle approach for governing, securing, testing, assuring, authorising, employing and continuously monitoring AI-enabled capabilities in defence.

The framework connects technical AI capability with:

- mission requirements;
- operational context;
- risk;
- human authority;
- security;
- assurance evidence;
- legal and policy requirements; and
- operational authorisation.

---

## 2. Definition

> **D-AIGAAF is a defence-specific lifecycle framework for governing, securing, testing, assuring, authorising, employing and continuously monitoring AI-enabled capabilities according to mission, environment, autonomy, consequence and human authority.**

---

## 3. Central Question

The framework is built around one central question:

> **Can this AI capability be trusted to perform this mission, under these conditions, at this level of autonomy, with this level of human authority?**

This question deliberately combines five elements:

1. AI capability
2. Mission
3. Environment
4. Autonomy
5. Human authority

An AI system should therefore not be treated as universally "trusted" or "approved" independent of its intended use.

---

## 4. Six-Layer Reference Architecture

D-AIGAAF consists of six primary layers.

### Layer 1 — Strategy & Governance

Defines the strategic and institutional framework within which defence AI is developed and employed.

It addresses:

- strategy;
- governance;
- accountability;
- policy;
- acceptable risk;
- institutional priorities;
- organisational responsibilities; and
- oversight.

---

### Layer 2 — Mission & Risk

Establishes why AI is required and determines the risk associated with its intended use.

It addresses:

- mission need;
- operational use case;
- mission criticality;
- consequence;
- autonomy;
- operational environment;
- human control;
- hazards; and
- threats.

---

### Layer 3 — System & Trust

Addresses the technical and informational foundations on which AI assurance depends.

It includes:

- models;
- software;
- hardware;
- datasets;
- sensors;
- interfaces;
- communications;
- cybersecurity;
- dependencies;
- configuration;
- supply chain;
- permissions; and
- sovereignty.

---

### Layer 4 — Assurance

Determines whether sufficient evidence exists to establish trustworthiness for the intended use.

It includes:

- testing;
- evaluation;
- verification;
- validation;
- adversarial testing;
- cybersecurity assessment;
- environmental testing;
- human-AI assessment;
- operational trials; and
- mission-level evaluation.

---

### Layer 5 — Operational Authorisation

Determines whether an AI capability may be employed for a defined mission under defined conditions.

Operational authorisation binds:

**AI Capability × Mission × Environment × Autonomy × Human Authority**

It therefore does not represent unrestricted approval of an AI system.

---

### Layer 6 — Continuous Assurance

Maintains assurance after deployment.

It includes:

- operational monitoring;
- performance monitoring;
- behavioural monitoring;
- security monitoring;
- configuration monitoring;
- incident management;
- change assessment;
- revalidation; and
- reauthorisation.

> **Operational authorisation is not the end of assurance. It is the beginning of continuous assurance.**

---

## 5. Cross-Cutting Functions

Several functions operate across all six layers.

### Human Authority

Ensures that responsibility, authority, intervention and accountability remain explicit.

### AI Security

Addresses threats to the AI system and threats arising from AI behaviour.

### Information Assurance

Addresses accuracy, integrity, provenance, completeness, timeliness and uncertainty of information used by AI.

### Legal & Policy

Ensures that technical capability and assurance remain subject to applicable law, policy and delegated authority.

### Workforce

Ensures that personnel possess the knowledge and competence required to develop, assure, operate and authorise AI.

### Supply Chain & Sovereignty

Addresses dependencies on suppliers, models, data, infrastructure, external services, updates and other strategic dependencies.
