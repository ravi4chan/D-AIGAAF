# Retirement

## Summary

Retirement governs the controlled removal of an AI capability from operational service when it is no longer required, no longer suitable, no longer supportable, or has been superseded.

Retirement is an operational and governance decision. It does not mean that all technical or historical records disappear.

The core principle is:

**An AI capability should not simply stop being used; its operational authority, dependencies, data flows, configurations, records and residual risks must be deliberately closed or transferred.**

The core chain is:

**Retirement Trigger → Assessment → Decision → Operational Withdrawal → Dependency Closure → Record Preservation → Residual Risk Transfer → Decommissioning**

---

## 1. Purpose

Retirement provides a controlled process to:

- remove obsolete AI capabilities from operational use;
- prevent unintended continued use;
- preserve necessary evidence and records;
- manage dependencies;
- transfer residual risks;
- protect sensitive information;
- support transition to replacement capabilities;
- prepare for formal decommissioning.

---

## 2. Core Principle

Retirement is different from decommissioning.

### Retirement

Ends the capability's authorised operational use.

### Decommissioning

Physically or technically removes the capability, infrastructure, software, data dependencies or services as appropriate.

A capability may therefore be:

**Retired → Retained for Record / Training / Analysis → Decommissioned**

or may be retired and decommissioned through a controlled sequence.

---

## 3. Retirement Triggers

Retirement may be initiated because:

- mission need no longer exists;
- capability is superseded;
- performance is inadequate;
- risk is no longer acceptable;
- assurance cannot be maintained;
- security cannot be adequately sustained;
- dependencies are unavailable;
- supplier support ends;
- technology becomes obsolete;
- legal or policy conditions change;
- operating environment changes;
- sustainment cost or complexity becomes disproportionate;
- the capability cannot be reliably controlled.

---

## 4. Retirement Assessment

Before retirement, assess:

- mission dependency;
- current operational use;
- active users;
- authorised configurations;
- connected systems;
- data dependencies;
- security dependencies;
- safety dependencies;
- contractual dependencies;
- replacement capability;
- outstanding incidents;
- open assurance findings;
- residual risk.

---

## 5. Retirement Decision

The retirement decision should identify:

- capability;
- reason for retirement;
- effective date;
- final authorised configuration;
- affected users;
- affected systems;
- replacement or transition arrangements;
- residual risks;
- records to retain;
- decommissioning requirements;
- responsible authority.

---

## 6. Operational Authority

Once retirement becomes effective:

- operational authorisation should be withdrawn or allowed to expire as formally decided;
- operational employment should cease;
- unauthorised continued use should be prevented;
- exceptions should require explicit authority.

Retirement should be reflected in the Defence AI Capability Register and relevant authorisation records.

---

## 7. Transition Planning

Where a replacement exists, transition should address:

- mission continuity;
- user transition;
- data migration;
- interface changes;
- training;
- configuration changes;
- assurance status of the replacement;
- operational gaps.

A replacement capability should not inherit the retired capability's authorisation automatically.

---

## 8. Dependency Assessment

Identify dependencies that may remain after operational retirement:

- software;
- hardware;
- data stores;
- models;
- APIs;
- cloud or infrastructure services;
- communications interfaces;
- authentication;
- logging;
- monitoring;
- security services;
- supplier services.

---

## 9. Continued Non-Operational Use

A retired capability may be retained for legitimate non-operational purposes such as:

- historical analysis;
- assurance research;
- training;
- investigation;
- lessons learned;
- audit;
- legal requirements.

Such retention should be explicitly separated from operational authority.

---

## 10. Data Retention

Data should be retained, archived or destroyed according to applicable:

- legal requirements;
- policy;
- security requirements;
- records-management requirements;
- contractual obligations;
- mission needs.

Retention should preserve necessary evidence without creating unnecessary exposure.

---

## 11. Model and Configuration Records

Retain appropriate records of:

- model versions;
- system versions;
- configuration baselines;
- training data references;
- test results;
- assurance evidence;
- authorisations;
- incidents;
- changes;
- operational limitations.

These records may be necessary to reconstruct historical decisions and system behaviour.

---

## 12. Security Considerations

Retirement should address:

- credentials;
- access rights;
- certificates;
- secrets;
- interfaces;
- network access;
- administrative accounts;
- supplier access;
- remote-management mechanisms;
- external services.

Access should be removed or appropriately transferred.

---

## 13. Safety Considerations

Where the AI capability interacts with physical systems, retirement should establish that:

- operational functions have ceased;
- residual automated actions are prevented;
- safety controls remain effective during transition;
- dependent systems are left in a safe state.

---

## 14. Human Authority

Users should know:

- when the capability is retired;
- what replaces it, if applicable;
- what functions are no longer available;
- what temporary procedures apply;
- who owns transition decisions.

Retirement should not create ambiguity about operational responsibility.

---

## 15. Operational Records

Final operational records should be preserved where required.

Relevant records may include:

- final configuration;
- final authorisation;
- monitoring history;
- incidents;
- significant decisions;
- performance history;
- known limitations;
- retirement decision.

---

## 16. Incident and Investigation Hold

If the capability is associated with:

- a significant incident;
- investigation;
- legal proceeding;
- safety review;
- security investigation;
- assurance finding;

relevant records and evidence should be preserved before destruction or disposal.

---

## 17. Supplier and Contract Closure

For externally supplied capabilities, retirement should address:

- support termination;
- licences;
- subscriptions;
- hosted services;
- data return;
- data deletion;
- supplier access;
- intellectual property;
- retained dependencies;
- contractual record requirements.

---

## 18. Supply-Chain Closure

Identify and close or transfer critical external dependencies.

Particular attention should be given to components capable of:

- modifying the model;
- modifying software;
- accessing operational data;
- changing configuration;
- accessing infrastructure.

---

## 19. Interoperability and Interfaces

Retirement should identify systems that depend on the capability.

Interfaces should be:

- removed;
- disabled;
- redirected;
- replaced;
- formally retained for non-operational purposes.

Orphaned interfaces should not remain active without a defined owner and purpose.

---

## 20. Access Control

Retirement should trigger review of:

- operator accounts;
- administrator accounts;
- service accounts;
- API credentials;
- certificates;
- privileged access;
- supplier access.

Access no longer required should be revoked.

---

## 21. Configuration Closure

The final configuration should be identified and preserved as a historical baseline where required.

Configuration records should establish:

**What Was Approved → What Was Deployed → What Was Operated → What Was Retired**

---

## 22. Monitoring During Retirement

Monitoring may be required during transition to confirm:

- operational use has ceased;
- interfaces are closed;
- automated processes have stopped;
- no unexpected activity remains;
- replacement systems function as intended.

---

## 23. Retirement in Degraded Conditions

Retirement should account for:

- disconnected operation;
- loss of communications;
- unavailable support personnel;
- degraded infrastructure;
- emergency operational requirements.

Predefined fallback procedures should exist where mission continuity is important.

---

## 24. Emergency Retirement

Emergency retirement may be required where continued operation presents unacceptable risk.

Triggers may include:

- critical safety failure;
- uncontrolled behaviour;
- serious security compromise;
- loss of human control;
- unacceptable mission risk.

Emergency retirement should be documented and followed by formal review.

---

## 25. Residual Risk

Retirement does not necessarily eliminate all risk.

Residual risks may arise from:

- retained data;
- archived models;
- remaining infrastructure;
- legacy interfaces;
- unsupported dependencies;
- historical vulnerabilities;
- knowledge or records.

Each significant residual risk should have an identified owner.

---

## 26. Knowledge Transfer

Important knowledge should be captured before personnel, suppliers or systems are removed.

Capture may include:

- operating limitations;
- lessons learned;
- known failure modes;
- configuration information;
- assurance findings;
- incidents;
- transition issues.

---

## 27. Replacement Capability

Where retirement is caused by replacement, compare:

- mission coverage;
- risk;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- assurance;
- interoperability;
- operating environment;
- limitations.

The replacement must undergo its own lifecycle governance.

---

## 28. Retirement Review

A final review should confirm:

- operational authority ended;
- users were informed;
- dependencies were addressed;
- access was closed or transferred;
- records were preserved;
- residual risks were assigned;
- transition was completed;
- decommissioning requirements were identified.

---

## 29. Retirement Outcomes

A working status model is:

### Planned

Retirement decision made; transition preparation underway.

### Transitioning

Operational use is being withdrawn.

### Retired

Operational authority has ended.

### Retired — Retained

Capability is retained for non-operational purposes.

### Ready for Decommissioning

Operational and governance closure is complete.

### Closed

All applicable retirement actions are complete.

---

## 30. Retirement Record

A Retirement Record should include:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Retirement ID | Unique identifier |
| Capability ID | AI capability |
| Reason | Retirement rationale |
| Final Configuration | Last authorised baseline |
| Final Authorisation | Last operational authority |
| Effective Date | Retirement date |
| Users | Affected users |
| Dependencies | Systems/services affected |
| Transition | Replacement/fallback |
| Data | Retention/disposal decision |
| Security | Access/credential closure |
| Incidents | Outstanding matters |
| Residual Risk | Remaining risk |
| Records | Retention requirements |
| Decommissioning | Required actions |
| Authority | Responsible decision-maker |
| Status | Retirement status |

---

## 31. Retirement and Decommissioning

The relationship is:

**Retirement = End of Operational Authority**

**Decommissioning = Controlled Technical / Physical Closure**

Retirement should normally precede or explicitly govern decommissioning.

---

## 32. Retirement and Assurance

Assurance records should remain available where necessary to establish:

- what was tested;
- what was authorised;
- what limitations existed;
- what incidents occurred;
- what decisions were made.

Retirement does not erase the historical assurance record.

---

## 33. Retirement and Accountability

Records should allow historical reconstruction of:

**Capability → Configuration → Authorisation → Employment → Monitoring → Incident / Outcome → Retirement**

This supports audit, investigation, lessons learned and institutional accountability.

---

## 34. Common Failure Modes

### 34.1 Simply Stopping Use

Stopping normal use without formal retirement can leave authority, access and dependencies ambiguous.

### 34.2 Retiring Without Closing Access

Inactive capabilities may retain credentials or interfaces.

### 34.3 Destroying Evidence Too Early

Records may be required for audit, investigation or legal purposes.

### 34.4 Assuming Replacement Is Automatically Authorised

A replacement capability requires its own assurance and authority.

### 34.5 Ignoring Legacy Dependencies

Other systems may continue to rely on the retired capability.

### 34.6 Ignoring Residual Risk

Retirement does not eliminate risks associated with retained data, infrastructure or knowledge.

---

## 35. Core Rules

1. **Retirement must formally end operational use and authority.**
2. **Retirement and decommissioning are distinct lifecycle decisions.**
3. **Retired capabilities must not remain operational through undocumented exceptions.**
4. **Dependencies must be identified and closed, transferred or explicitly retained.**
5. **Security access should be revoked or transferred appropriately.**
6. **Safety must be preserved during transition and closure.**
7. **Historical configuration and assurance records should be retained where required.**
8. **Incident and investigation evidence must be preserved before disposal.**
9. **Replacement capabilities require their own assurance and authorisation.**
10. **Residual risks must have identified owners.**
11. **Non-operational retention must be explicitly separated from operational authority.**
12. **Emergency retirement should be available where continued operation creates unacceptable risk.**
13. **Retirement should be traceable from final operational state through closure.**
14. **Retirement findings should feed lessons learned and future capability development.**

---

## 36. Golden Thread

Retirement closes the lifecycle while preserving traceability:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Capability → Assurance → Authority → Employment → Monitoring → Change / Incident → Revalidation → Reauthorisation → Retirement → Decommissioning**
