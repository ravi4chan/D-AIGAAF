# D-AIGAAF — TEVV Independent Evaluation & Review

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the principles and requirements for independent evaluation and review of Defence AI capabilities within the Testing, Evaluation, Verification and Validation (TEVV) process.

The objective is to provide an appropriate level of independent scrutiny, challenge and confidence in evidence and assurance conclusions.

## 2. Core Principle

> **The greater the consequence, autonomy, complexity and uncertainty of a Defence AI capability, the greater the need for appropriately independent evaluation and review.**

Independence should be proportionate to risk and should not become a procedural substitute for technical or operational competence.

## 3. Scope

Independent evaluation and review may consider:

- TEVV plans.
- Test methods.
- Evaluation design.
- Test results.
- Findings.
- Evidence quality.
- Risk assessments.
- Human-AI evaluation.
- Security assessments.
- Autonomy assessments.
- Mission effectiveness.
- Assurance conclusions.
- Operational limitations.

## 4. Independence

Independence means that the reviewer has sufficient separation from the activity being assessed to provide credible challenge.

The appropriate degree of independence may vary according to:

- Consequence of failure.
- Mission criticality.
- Autonomy.
- System complexity.
- Novelty.
- Evidence uncertainty.
- Organisational risk.

Independence should be assessed rather than assumed solely from organisational structure.

## 5. Competence

Independent reviewers should collectively possess appropriate knowledge of:

- AI and machine learning.
- System engineering.
- TEVV methods.
- Cybersecurity.
- Safety.
- Human factors.
- Operational context.
- Risk management.
- Applicable legal and policy requirements.

The review team should have sufficient competence to challenge both technical and operational claims.

## 6. Independence from Development

Where practicable, significant evaluations should include personnel who were not responsible for developing the specific capability being assessed.

This reduces the risk of:

- Confirmation bias.
- Acceptance of unsupported assumptions.
- Under-reporting limitations.
- Over-reliance on development evidence.

Development teams should nevertheless remain available to explain system behaviour and resolve technical questions.

## 7. Independent Test Review

Before significant testing, an independent reviewer should consider whether:

- Objectives are clear.
- Requirements are traceable.
- Test scenarios are appropriate.
- Acceptance criteria are measurable.
- Operational conditions are sufficiently represented.
- Important failure modes are addressed.
- Adversarial conditions are considered where relevant.
- Human-control requirements are evaluated.

## 8. Independent Evidence Review

Reviewers should assess whether evidence is:

- Relevant.
- Sufficient.
- Credible.
- Traceable.
- Current.
- Representative.
- Appropriately independent.

Evidence should not be accepted solely because it originates from the system developer or supplier.

## 9. Challenge Function

Independent review should provide constructive challenge to:

- Requirements.
- Assumptions.
- Test coverage.
- Performance claims.
- Risk treatment.
- Evidence interpretation.
- Known limitations.
- Assurance conclusions.

The purpose of challenge is to improve decision quality, not to create unnecessary procedural barriers.

## 10. Review of Findings

Independent reviewers should examine whether findings are:

- Supported by evidence.
- Correctly characterised.
- Appropriately risk assessed.
- Assigned to responsible owners.
- Properly treated.
- Retested where required.
- Closed with sufficient evidence.

Disagreement between evaluators and system owners should be recorded rather than suppressed.

## 11. Review of Assurance Conclusions

Where assurance conclusions support consequential operational decisions, independent review should assess whether:

- The conclusion follows from the evidence.
- Important evidence gaps are visible.
- Limitations are clearly stated.
- Residual risk is understood.
- Conditions are enforceable.
- The conclusion is limited to the assessed operational envelope.

The reviewer should challenge conclusions that appear broader than the evidence supports.

## 12. Independence and High-Consequence Systems

For high-consequence or highly autonomous capabilities, independent review should be strengthened as appropriate.

This may include:

- Independent test teams.
- Independent technical review.
- Independent safety assessment.
- Independent security assessment.
- Independent operational evaluation.
- Senior assurance review.

The required arrangement should be determined by risk rather than by a universal organisational template.

## 13. Supplier-Provided Evidence

Supplier or developer evidence can be valuable and should not be automatically rejected.

However, the level of independent scrutiny should reflect:

- Supplier assurance maturity.
- Evidence quality.
- System criticality.
- Access to underlying evidence.
- Ability to reproduce results.
- Supply-chain dependency.
- Consequence of failure.

Critical conclusions should not depend exclusively on unverified supplier claims.

## 14. Conflict of Interest

Potential conflicts of interest should be identified and managed.

Reviewers should disclose relationships or responsibilities that could reasonably affect, or appear to affect, impartiality.

Where a conflict cannot be adequately managed, an alternative reviewer should be considered.

## 15. Review Records

Independent reviews should produce controlled records containing, where applicable:

- Scope.
- Review objectives.
- Reviewers.
- Competence.
- Evidence examined.
- Findings.
- Challenges raised.
- Responses.
- Outstanding issues.
- Review conclusion.
- Date.
- Applicable system baseline.

## 16. Disagreement and Escalation

Where significant disagreement remains, the issue should be:

1. Documented.
2. Supported by the relevant evidence.
3. Risk assessed.
4. Escalated to the appropriate authority.
5. Resolved, accepted or carried forward as a condition.

The existence of unresolved professional disagreement should be visible to the decision maker.

## 17. Relationship to Operational Authorisation

Independent review informs, but does not replace, operational authorisation.

The relationship is:

**TEVV Evidence → Independent Challenge → Assurance Conclusion → Authorisation Decision**

The authorising authority remains responsible for determining whether employment is acceptable.

## 18. Review Frequency

Independent review should occur at points proportionate to risk, including where appropriate:

- Before initial authorisation.
- Following major system changes.
- Following significant incidents.
- When operational conditions change.
- When new evidence challenges previous conclusions.
- During periodic assurance reviews.

## 19. Revalidation Triggers

Independent review should be reconsidered when material changes affect:

- Model behaviour.
- Data.
- Architecture.
- Autonomy.
- Mission.
- Environment.
- Human authority.
- Security posture.
- Critical dependencies.

New threats or previously unknown failure modes may also trigger review.

## 20. Core Rule

> **Independent evaluation should provide credible challenge without becoming detached from operational reality.**

The objective is an assurance process in which consequential Defence AI decisions can be supported by evidence that has been technically credible, operationally relevant and appropriately independently scrutinised.
