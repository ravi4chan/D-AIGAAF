# D-AIGAAF — TEVV Evidence Management & Records

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how TEVV evidence and records should be identified, controlled, preserved and maintained throughout the Defence AI lifecycle.

The objective is to ensure that evidence supporting safety, security, performance, human control and operational authorisation remains traceable, credible and available for review.

## 2. Core Principle

> **If evidence cannot be reliably traced to the capability, configuration, conditions and decision it supports, its assurance value is materially reduced.**

TEVV evidence should therefore be treated as a controlled lifecycle asset.

## 3. Scope

Evidence management should cover:

- Test plans.
- Test procedures.
- Evaluation records.
- Verification results.
- Validation results.
- Trial records.
- Test data.
- Logs.
- Findings.
- Evidence gaps.
- Assurance conclusions.
- Independent reviews.
- Readiness assessments.
- Authorisation inputs.

## 4. Evidence Identification

Each significant evidence item should be identifiable and associated, where applicable, with:

- Capability identifier.
- Use case.
- Mission.
- System baseline.
- Model version.
- Software version.
- Hardware configuration.
- Data version or source.
- Test environment.
- Test date.
- Responsible organisation.
- Evidence owner.

## 5. Evidence Traceability

Evidence should support traceability across:

**Requirement → Risk → Test → Result → Finding → Assurance → Authorisation**

Where practicable, evidence should also be traceable to the exact configuration and conditions under which it was generated.

## 6. Evidence Integrity

Evidence should be protected against:

- Unauthorised modification.
- Accidental deletion.
- Misattribution.
- Loss of context.
- Uncontrolled version changes.

Appropriate integrity and access controls should be applied according to the sensitivity and importance of the evidence.

## 7. Evidence Classification

Evidence should be handled according to applicable information-security, legal and organisational requirements.

D-AIGAAF does not prescribe a universal classification scheme.

The framework should instead require that the applicable handling requirements are identified and followed.

## 8. Test Data

Test data should be managed so that its:

- Source.
- Provenance.
- Version.
- Preparation.
- Transformation.
- Intended use.
- Limitations.

can be established where necessary to interpret the results.

Sensitive or operationally restricted data should be handled under applicable controls.

## 9. Test Logs and System Records

Where technically and operationally appropriate, records should capture:

- System state.
- Configuration.
- Inputs.
- Outputs.
- Alerts.
- Errors.
- Human interventions.
- Autonomy transitions.
- Fail-safe events.
- Relevant environmental conditions.

Logging should be proportionate to the system's consequence and operational requirements.

## 10. Findings Management

TEVV findings should be recorded in a controlled register.

Each material finding should include:

- Description.
- Evidence.
- Requirement or risk affected.
- Severity or significance.
- Responsible owner.
- Treatment.
- Status.
- Retest requirement.
- Closure evidence.

Findings should remain traceable throughout their lifecycle.

## 11. Evidence Gaps

Evidence gaps should be explicitly recorded rather than silently excluded from the assurance package.

The record should identify:

- Missing evidence.
- Reason for the gap.
- Potential consequence.
- Risk.
- Compensating controls.
- Additional evidence required.
- Responsible owner.
- Resolution status.

## 12. Version and Baseline Control

Evidence should identify the system baseline to which it applies.

This is particularly important where changes occur to:

- Models.
- Data.
- Software.
- Hardware.
- Sensors.
- Interfaces.
- Dependencies.
- Autonomy.

Evidence from an earlier baseline should not automatically be treated as valid for a later configuration.

## 13. Evidence Reuse

Existing evidence may be reused where its applicability can be demonstrated.

Reuse should consider:

- Configuration equivalence.
- Mission equivalence.
- Environmental equivalence.
- Risk equivalence.
- Autonomy equivalence.
- Currency.
- Known changes.

Where applicability cannot be demonstrated, additional evidence should be generated.

## 14. Independent Evidence

Evidence generated by developers, suppliers, operators or independent assessors should be identified by source.

The source should not by itself determine whether evidence is accepted.

Evidence should instead be evaluated for:

- Relevance.
- Credibility.
- Sufficiency.
- Independence.
- Reproducibility.
- Limitations.

## 15. Evidence Package

For significant authorisation decisions, evidence should be assembled into a controlled assurance package.

The package may contain:

1. Requirements.
2. Risk assessment.
3. TEVV plan.
4. Test and evaluation results.
5. Operational trial results.
6. Human-AI evaluation.
7. Security evaluation.
8. Autonomy evaluation.
9. Reliability and robustness evidence.
10. Mission effectiveness evidence.
11. Findings.
12. Evidence gaps.
13. Independent review.
14. Readiness assessment.
15. Limitations and conditions.

## 16. Evidence Sufficiency

The evidence package should support a reasoned answer to:

- Does the system meet its relevant requirements?
- Are important risks understood?
- Are critical controls effective?
- Is human authority preserved?
- Is the system sufficiently robust?
- Are security risks adequately addressed?
- Is the proposed autonomy demonstrated?
- Is mission effectiveness supported?
- Are remaining limitations understood?

## 17. Record Retention

TEVV records should be retained for a period appropriate to:

- Mission consequence.
- Legal requirements.
- Regulatory requirements.
- Organisational policy.
- Operational lifetime.
- Incident investigation needs.
- Audit requirements.
- Revalidation and reauthorisation.

Retention periods should be defined by the responsible authority.

## 18. Evidence Preservation After Incident

Following a significant incident, relevant records should be preserved to support:

- Investigation.
- Root-cause analysis.
- Accountability.
- Risk reassessment.
- Corrective action.
- Revalidation.
- Reauthorisation decisions.

Relevant evidence should not be altered or discarded before appropriate review.

## 19. Auditability

Evidence management should allow authorised reviewers to reconstruct, to a reasonable extent:

- What was tested.
- Which system was tested.
- Under what conditions.
- What happened.
- What was learned.
- What findings resulted.
- What assurance conclusion was reached.
- Which operational decision followed.

## 20. Relationship to D-AIGAAF Core Records

TEVV evidence should feed the broader D-AIGAAF record architecture, including:

- **Defence AI Capability Register (DAICR)** — what capability exists.
- **Defence AI Assurance Record (DAAR)** — what evidence demonstrates.
- **Operational Authorisation Record** — why and under what conditions employment is authorised.
- **Operational Record** — what occurred during employment.

These records should remain logically connected without unnecessarily duplicating underlying evidence.

## 21. Continuous Assurance

Evidence management continues after authorisation.

Operational monitoring, incidents, changes and new evaluations should generate additional evidence that can:

- Confirm existing assurance.
- Identify degradation.
- Reveal new risks.
- Trigger revalidation.
- Support reauthorisation.

## 22. Core Rule

> **TEVV evidence should remain traceable, current, protected and interpretable for as long as the assurance decision depends upon it.**

The evidence record is therefore not merely an administrative archive; it is part of the Defence AI assurance system.
