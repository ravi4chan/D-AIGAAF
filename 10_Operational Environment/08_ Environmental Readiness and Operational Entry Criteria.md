# D-AIGAAF — Environmental Readiness and Operational Entry Criteria

## 1. Purpose

This document defines criteria for determining whether the operational environment is sufficiently understood and controlled for a Defence AI capability to enter an authorised operational state.

The objective is to prevent deployment based solely on technical readiness when environmental assumptions, dependencies or limitations remain inadequately understood.

## 2. Core Principle

> **Operational entry requires sufficient evidence that the capability can operate within its authorised environment with acceptable risk and effective human authority.**

## 3. Operational Entry

Operational entry is the transition from assurance and preparation into an authorised operational state.

It should occur only after the applicable:

- Environmental conditions.
- Risks.
- Controls.
- Dependencies.
- Human arrangements.
- Autonomy boundaries.
- Evidence requirements.

have been addressed to the level required by the authorising authority.

## 4. Entry Criteria

Before operational entry, the organisation should establish that:

1. The intended operational environment is defined.
2. Material environmental variables are characterised.
3. Relevant environmental boundaries are established.
4. Required TEVV has been completed.
5. Significant environmental limitations are understood.
6. Degraded and disconnected behaviour is addressed where relevant.
7. Adversarial environmental conditions are considered.
8. Human control remains effective.
9. Environmental dependencies are identified.
10. Required monitoring and response mechanisms are available.

## 5. Evidence of Readiness

Readiness should be supported by evidence appropriate to the capability.

Evidence may include:

- Environmental characterisation.
- Technical testing.
- Operational trials.
- Human factors assessment.
- Security evaluation.
- Reliability assessment.
- Autonomy evaluation.
- Mission effectiveness evaluation.
- Independent review.
- Findings closure or accepted risk decisions.

## 6. Entry Conditions

Operational entry criteria should distinguish between:

### Mandatory conditions

Conditions that must be satisfied before entry because failure to satisfy them would create unacceptable or uncontrolled risk.

### Conditional conditions

Conditions under which entry may be permitted subject to:

- Restrictions.
- Additional supervision.
- Reduced autonomy.
- Additional monitoring.
- Defined review periods.

### Deferred conditions

Conditions that require further evidence before the capability can operate in the affected environment.

## 7. Environmental Boundaries

The operational entry decision should identify the environmental envelope within which the capability is authorised.

This may include:

- Permitted conditions.
- Restricted conditions.
- Prohibited conditions.
- Boundary-crossing indicators.
- Required responses.

The authorisation should not imply capability outside the demonstrated envelope.

## 8. Degraded and Disconnected Readiness

Where degraded or disconnected operation is foreseeable, readiness should establish:

- Defined system behaviour.
- Appropriate fallback.
- Human authority arrangements.
- Autonomy restrictions.
- Data-freshness controls.
- Recovery and resynchronisation.
- Logging and records.

## 9. Adversarial Readiness

Where relevant, readiness should establish that foreseeable adversarial environmental conditions have been sufficiently assessed.

This should include consideration of:

- Input integrity.
- Sensor resilience.
- Information manipulation.
- Communication disruption.
- System integrity.
- Human decision-making under uncertainty.

## 10. Human Readiness

Operational entry should confirm that personnel are ready to exercise their assigned responsibilities.

This may require:

- Role assignment.
- Competence.
- Training.
- Interface familiarisation.
- Intervention training.
- Understanding of uncertainty.
- Knowledge of autonomy boundaries.
- Awareness of fail-safe procedures.

## 11. Autonomy Readiness

Where autonomy is authorised, readiness should establish that:

- The authorised autonomy level has been evaluated.
- Environmental conditions supporting autonomy are defined.
- Human control remains appropriate.
- Transition mechanisms are understood.
- Failure and degradation behaviour has been assessed.

Autonomy should not exceed the level supported by the evidence and authorisation decision.

## 12. Dependency Readiness

Material dependencies should be available and appropriately controlled before operational entry.

Readiness should consider:

- Communications.
- Data.
- Sensors.
- Navigation or positioning.
- Computing.
- Power.
- Supporting systems.
- External services.

Critical dependency failures should have predefined responses.

## 13. Environmental Monitoring Readiness

Where monitoring is required, the organisation should confirm that:

- Relevant indicators are identified.
- Monitoring is available.
- Thresholds or conditions are understood.
- Responsible personnel are identified.
- Escalation paths exist.
- Records are maintained.

## 14. Findings

Open findings should be reviewed before operational entry.

Each material finding should have one of the following dispositions:

- Closed.
- Accepted as residual risk by the authorised authority.
- Controlled by an explicit operational restriction.
- Deferred with documented justification.

Unresolved findings should not be concealed by declaring the capability ready.

## 15. Environmental Risk Acceptance

Where environmental limitations remain, the relevant authority should understand:

- The limitation.
- Associated risk.
- Evidence supporting the assessment.
- Mitigating controls.
- Residual uncertainty.
- Consequences of failure.

Risk acceptance should remain attributable to the appropriate authority.

## 16. Readiness Review

A formal readiness review should be conducted where appropriate to mission consequence.

The review should bring together relevant:

- Operational stakeholders.
- Technical authorities.
- Assurance personnel.
- Security stakeholders.
- Human factors representatives.
- AI specialists.
- Authorisation authorities.

## 17. Readiness Decision

A readiness decision may result in:

- **Ready for authorised entry**
- **Ready with conditions**
- **Not ready**
- **Additional evidence required**

The terminology may be adapted to organisational policy.

## 18. Operational Entry Record

The readiness record should document, as appropriate:

- Capability.
- Mission/use case.
- Environmental envelope.
- Evidence considered.
- Dependencies.
- Open findings.
- Restrictions.
- Human-control arrangements.
- Autonomy level.
- Monitoring requirements.
- Fail-safe requirements.
- Readiness conclusion.
- Responsible authority.
- Review date.

## 19. Transition to Authorisation

Environmental readiness is an input to operational authorisation.

The readiness assessment should allow the authorising authority to determine whether:

**The capability + intended mission + operating environment + controls + evidence**

provide a sufficient basis for authorised employment.

## 20. Post-Entry Confirmation

Operational entry should not be treated as proof that all assumptions are correct.

Early operational use should be monitored for:

- Unexpected environmental effects.
- Performance degradation.
- Boundary crossings.
- Human-control issues.
- New dependencies.
- Previously unknown failure modes.

Material findings should enter continuous assurance.

## 21. Revalidation Triggers

Environmental readiness should be reassessed when:

- New environmental conditions are introduced.
- Material dependencies change.
- Significant incidents occur.
- Performance differs materially from expectations.
- New environmental risks are identified.
- The system baseline changes materially.

## 22. Governance Questions

Before operational entry, decision-makers should be able to answer:

1. What environment is the capability authorised for?
2. What evidence demonstrates readiness?
3. What environmental conditions remain uncertain?
4. What restrictions apply?
5. What happens under degradation or disconnection?
6. What happens under adversarial conditions?
7. Can humans maintain meaningful control?
8. Is the authorised autonomy supported by evidence?
9. Are monitoring and fail-safe mechanisms ready?
10. Who is accountable for the entry decision?

## 23. Core Rule

> **Operational readiness means more than a technically functioning system; it means a capability, its people and its operating environment are sufficiently understood and controlled for the authorised mission.**
