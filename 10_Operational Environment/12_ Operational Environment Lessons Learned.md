# D-AIGAAF — Operational Environment Lessons Learned

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how lessons from operational experience involving the environment of a Defence AI capability should be captured, assessed and incorporated into governance, assurance and future system development.

The objective is to ensure that operational experience improves the reliability, safety, security, human control and authorisation of Defence AI capabilities.

## 2. Core Principle

> **Operational experience is assurance evidence when it is systematically captured, assessed and connected to decisions.**

Lessons learned should not remain informal observations. Material lessons should enter the appropriate governance and assurance processes.

## 3. Sources of Lessons

Lessons may originate from:

- Routine operational employment.
- Environmental monitoring.
- Incidents.
- Near misses.
- Boundary crossings.
- Degraded or disconnected operations.
- Adversarial conditions.
- Operator feedback.
- Command reviews.
- Technical investigations.
- TEVV.
- Independent evaluations.
- Maintenance activities.
- System updates.
- Revalidation.
- Reauthorisation.

## 4. Types of Lessons

Lessons may concern:

### Environmental assumptions

Conditions assumed during development or assurance that proved incomplete or inaccurate.

### System behaviour

Unexpected or poorly understood behaviour under particular environmental conditions.

### Human interaction

Unexpected effects on workload, situational awareness, interpretation or intervention.

### Autonomy

Conditions under which authorised autonomy performed differently from expectations.

### Dependencies

Unexpected reliance on communications, data, sensors, navigation, computing or external services.

### Security

Environmental conditions that increased vulnerability to manipulation or disruption.

### Mission effectiveness

Environmental factors that affected achievement of the intended mission outcome.

## 5. Lesson Identification

A potential lesson should be recorded when an observation indicates that existing:

- Assumptions.
- Controls.
- Procedures.
- Training.
- Environmental boundaries.
- Monitoring.
- TEVV.
- Risk assessments.

may require improvement.

## 6. Lesson Assessment

Each significant lesson should be assessed for:

- What was observed?
- Under what conditions?
- What was expected?
- What actually occurred?
- Why did the difference matter?
- Was the event foreseeable?
- Was it previously tested?
- Does existing assurance remain valid?
- Is the lesson applicable to other capabilities?

## 7. Materiality

Lessons should be prioritised according to potential impact on:

- Human life.
- Property.
- Mission effectiveness.
- Human control.
- Autonomy.
- Security.
- Reliability.
- Operational authorisation.

High-consequence lessons should receive formal governance attention.

## 8. Generalisation

A lesson should be assessed to determine whether it is:

- Capability-specific.
- Mission-specific.
- Environment-specific.
- Systemic.
- Applicable across multiple AI capabilities.

Systemic lessons should be considered for incorporation into framework-level requirements, standards or policy.

## 9. Assurance Feedback

Lessons should feed back into the assurance lifecycle.

Potential actions include:

- New TEVV scenarios.
- Additional environmental trials.
- Revised test conditions.
- Updated risk assessments.
- Revised environmental boundaries.
- Improved monitoring.
- Additional human factors evaluation.
- Revised autonomy constraints.
- New fail-safe requirements.

## 10. Operational Boundary Updates

Lessons may demonstrate that an existing environmental boundary is:

- Too broad.
- Too narrow.
- Poorly defined.
- Difficult to detect.
- Insufficiently controlled.

Changes to operational boundaries should follow the applicable change and authorisation processes.

## 11. Human Factors Lessons

Operational experience should identify lessons concerning:

- Automation bias.
- Over-reliance on recommendations.
- Excessive workload.
- Reduced situational awareness.
- Unclear system explanations.
- Difficulty intervening.
- Difficulty overriding.
- Misunderstanding of uncertainty.
- Ambiguous responsibility.

Where relevant, these lessons should update training, interface design and operating procedures.

## 12. Autonomy Lessons

Operational experience should be assessed for whether it indicates that:

- An autonomy level is inappropriate.
- Transition conditions are unclear.
- Human intervention time is inadequate.
- Environmental conditions reduce effective control.
- The system behaves differently near operational boundaries.

Lessons affecting authorised autonomy should be considered for revalidation or reauthorisation.

## 13. Lessons from Degraded and Disconnected Operations

Particular attention should be given to experience involving:

- Loss of communications.
- Reduced data availability.
- Data staleness.
- Sensor degradation.
- Navigation limitations.
- Computing constraints.
- Recovery and resynchronisation.

These lessons should inform future degraded-mode design and TEVV.

## 14. Lessons from Adversarial Conditions

Operational experience should be used to identify:

- New manipulation patterns.
- Unexpected system vulnerabilities.
- Environmental indicators of attack.
- Human responses to uncertainty.
- Weaknesses in monitoring or escalation.

Relevant lessons should enter AI security and adversarial assurance processes.

## 15. Operational AI Advisor

Where an Operational AI Advisor is part of the governance model, the advisor may help translate operational observations into AI assurance implications.

The advisor may connect:

**Operational observation → AI implication → Risk → Assurance gap → Recommended action**

The advisor remains advisory and does not replace the designated authority.

## 16. Corrective and Preventive Actions

Lessons may result in:

- Immediate corrective action.
- Procedure changes.
- Training changes.
- Additional monitoring.
- Technical changes.
- Additional TEVV.
- Risk treatment.
- Autonomy restrictions.
- Environmental restrictions.
- Revalidation.
- Reauthorisation.
- Procurement or design requirements for future systems.

## 17. Knowledge Management

Lessons should be stored in a structured and searchable knowledge base where practicable.

Records should preserve:

- Source.
- Context.
- Evidence.
- Finding.
- Impact.
- Action.
- Owner.
- Status.
- Applicability.
- Closure decision.

Sensitive operational information should be handled according to applicable security and information-management requirements.

## 18. Cross-Capability Learning

Where a lesson has relevance beyond a single capability, it should be evaluated for dissemination across:

- Programmes.
- Mission areas.
- Services or organisations.
- Acquisition activities.
- AI developers.
- Assurance teams.
- Training organisations.

This supports institutional rather than isolated learning.

## 19. Relationship to Continuous Assurance

Lessons learned form an important input to continuous assurance.

The assurance cycle should therefore include:

**Observe → Record → Assess → Learn → Act → Verify → Update**

## 20. Verification of Actions

Lessons should not be considered resolved merely because an action has been assigned.

Where appropriate, the organisation should verify that the action:

- Addressed the identified issue.
- Reduced the relevant risk.
- Did not create a new unacceptable risk.
- Was incorporated into the appropriate baseline.
- Has supporting evidence.

## 21. Revalidation and Reauthorisation

A lesson should trigger consideration of revalidation when it challenges an existing assurance claim.

Reauthorisation should be considered where the lesson changes:

- Operational risk.
- Environmental boundaries.
- Human authority.
- Autonomy.
- Mission conditions.
- Material system assumptions.

## 22. Governance Questions

Decision-makers should be able to answer:

1. What did operational experience teach us?
2. Was the lesson previously foreseeable?
3. Does it invalidate an existing assumption?
4. Does it affect the environmental envelope?
5. Does it affect human control or autonomy?
6. Does existing evidence remain sufficient?
7. Is additional TEVV required?
8. Is the lesson applicable to other capabilities?
9. What corrective or preventive action is required?
10. Has the effectiveness of that action been verified?

## 23. Core Rule

> **A Defence AI governance system should learn from operational experience faster than the operational environment changes.**

Operational learning should continuously strengthen the relationship between **mission, environment, risk, AI capability, human authority, evidence and operational authorisation**.
