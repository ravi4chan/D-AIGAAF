# D-AIGAAF — Operational Environment Governance Review

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the governance review used to determine whether the operational environment controls, assumptions, evidence and monitoring arrangements for a Defence AI capability remain adequate over time.

The review provides a structured mechanism for senior operational, technical and assurance authorities to confirm that environmental assumptions supporting operational authorisation remain valid.

## 2. Core Principle

> **Operational environment governance must remain active for the life of the capability.**

An environment that was adequately understood at authorisation may change over time. Governance review therefore provides a recurring checkpoint between operational experience and continued authorisation.

## 3. Review Objectives

The review should determine whether:

- The authorised environmental envelope remains appropriate.
- Environmental assumptions remain valid.
- Monitoring remains effective.
- Environmental risks remain acceptable.
- Human control remains effective.
- Autonomy remains appropriate.
- Dependencies remain understood.
- Existing assurance evidence remains applicable.
- Lessons and incidents have been incorporated.
- Changes requiring revalidation or reauthorisation have been identified.

## 4. Review Inputs

Relevant inputs may include:

- Operational Environment Assessment.
- Environmental readiness assessment.
- Monitoring records.
- Environmental incidents.
- Near misses.
- Operational lessons learned.
- TEVV results.
- Performance information.
- Risk assessments.
- Autonomy assessments.
- Human factors findings.
- Security assessments.
- Dependency changes.
- Configuration records.
- Previous governance decisions.

## 5. Review Frequency

Review frequency should be proportionate to:

- Mission consequence.
- Autonomy.
- Environmental volatility.
- Operational tempo.
- Dependency criticality.
- Known uncertainty.
- Incident history.

Additional reviews should occur when significant environmental changes or incidents arise.

## 6. Review Scope

The review should consider the complete relationship:

**Mission → AI Capability → Environment → Risk → Human Authority → Autonomy → Evidence → Authorisation**

A review should avoid assessing environmental conditions in isolation from the mission and system for which they matter.

## 7. Environmental Envelope Review

The review should examine whether:

- Current operations remain within authorised boundaries.
- Boundaries remain measurable or detectable.
- Conditions previously considered nominal remain representative.
- New conditions have emerged.
- Restricted or prohibited conditions remain appropriate.

## 8. Environmental Assumption Review

Each material assumption should be assessed as:

- Validated.
- Supported by current evidence.
- Partially supported.
- No longer valid.
- Unknown.

Invalid or materially uncertain assumptions should enter the appropriate assurance process.

## 9. Monitoring Review

Monitoring arrangements should be reviewed for:

- Coverage.
- Reliability.
- Timeliness.
- Thresholds.
- Escalation.
- Human interpretation.
- Monitoring failure.
- Record keeping.

The review should determine whether monitoring remains capable of detecting conditions that could invalidate operational assumptions.

## 10. Human Authority Review

The review should assess whether personnel can still:

- Understand system outputs.
- Recognise uncertainty.
- Maintain situational awareness.
- Intervene when required.
- Override where authorised.
- Respond within the required timeframe.
- Understand current autonomy boundaries.

Human authority should be assessed as an operational capability, not merely as a policy statement.

## 11. Autonomy Review

Where autonomy is authorised, the review should examine:

- Current autonomy level.
- Environmental conditions supporting autonomy.
- Transition behaviour.
- Human intervention capability.
- Boundary detection.
- Fail-safe behaviour.
- Evidence supporting continued autonomy.

A change in environmental risk may require a lower autonomy level even without a technical system change.

## 12. Dependency Review

Material dependencies should be reassessed, including:

- Communications.
- Data.
- Sensors.
- Navigation or positioning.
- Computing.
- Power.
- External services.
- Supporting systems.

Changes in dependency availability or integrity should be assessed for assurance impact.

## 13. Incident and Lessons Review

The review should examine whether recent incidents, near misses and lessons indicate:

- New failure modes.
- Inadequate environmental assumptions.
- Weak monitoring.
- Inadequate controls.
- Human-AI interaction problems.
- Incorrect autonomy boundaries.
- Previously unrecognised dependencies.

Repeated issues should be treated as potential systemic risks.

## 14. Evidence Currency

The review should consider whether existing TEVV and assurance evidence remains representative of current operations.

Evidence may become less applicable because of:

- Environmental change.
- Mission change.
- System change.
- Data change.
- Dependency change.
- New threat conditions.
- New operational experience.

## 15. Review Outcomes

The governance review may conclude:

### Continue

Existing environmental controls and evidence remain adequate.

### Continue with conditions

Operation may continue subject to additional restrictions, monitoring or supervision.

### Additional assurance required

Further evidence is required before continued or expanded use.

### Revalidation required

Existing assurance claims require formal reassessment.

### Reauthorisation required

The operational authorisation should be reconsidered.

### Suspend

Continued operation is not justified until specified concerns are addressed.

## 16. Governance Actions

Actions should be assigned an:

- Owner.
- Priority.
- Required evidence.
- Target date.
- Decision authority.
- Verification method.

Material actions should remain visible until resolved or formally accepted.

## 17. Independent Review

For higher-consequence capabilities, independent review should be considered.

Independence may provide an additional challenge to:

- Environmental assumptions.
- Evidence interpretation.
- Risk acceptance.
- Autonomy decisions.
- Human-control claims.

## 18. Operational AI Advisor

Where an Operational AI Advisor exists, the advisor may support the review by integrating operational observations with technical and assurance information.

The advisor may identify:

- Emerging environmental risks.
- Evidence gaps.
- Changes in system behaviour.
- Human-control concerns.
- Implications for autonomy.

The advisor does not hold command or authorisation authority unless separately designated by applicable governance arrangements.

## 19. Review Record

The review record should capture, as appropriate:

- Capability.
- Mission/use case.
- Current environmental envelope.
- Material environmental changes.
- Assumptions.
- Monitoring results.
- Incidents and lessons.
- Evidence status.
- Risk position.
- Human-control assessment.
- Autonomy position.
- Dependencies.
- Findings.
- Decisions.
- Conditions.
- Required actions.
- Responsible authority.
- Next review point.

## 20. Escalation

Issues should be escalated when they may:

- Increase risk materially.
- Affect human life or property.
- Reduce effective human control.
- Exceed authorised autonomy.
- Move operation outside the environmental envelope.
- Invalidate material assurance evidence.
- Require suspension or reauthorisation.

## 21. Relationship to Continuous Assurance

The governance review is one part of continuous assurance.

The overall cycle is:

**Monitor → Detect → Assess → Review → Decide → Act → Verify → Reassess**

Continuous monitoring should not wait for a scheduled governance review where immediate action is required.

## 22. Governance Questions

Decision-makers should be able to answer:

1. Has the operating environment changed?
2. Are our environmental assumptions still valid?
3. Is the authorised envelope still appropriate?
4. Is monitoring adequate?
5. Are current TEVV results still representative?
6. Are humans still able to exercise meaningful control?
7. Is the authorised autonomy still justified?
8. Have incidents or lessons changed the risk position?
9. Are material dependencies still reliable?
10. Does the existing operational authorisation remain justified?

## 23. Core Rule

> **Continued operational authorisation depends not only on the AI system remaining unchanged, but on the continuing validity of the environment, assumptions, evidence, controls and human authority on which that authorisation was based.**
