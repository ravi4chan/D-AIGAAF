# 12 Authorisation Delegation and Escalation

## 1. Purpose

This document defines governance for delegation and escalation of operational authorisation responsibilities and decisions within D-AIGAAF.

The objective is to ensure that delegated authority remains explicit, proportionate, bounded and traceable, particularly where decisions may affect human life, mission outcomes, autonomy, safety, security or operational control.

---

## 2. Core Principle

**Operational authority may be delegated only when the scope, limits, accountability and conditions of that delegation are explicitly defined.**

Delegation shall not obscure who ultimately holds responsibility for an operational authorisation decision.

---

## 3. Delegation

Delegation is the formal transfer of specified decision authority from one authorised authority to another.

Delegation should identify:

- Delegating authority.
- Delegated authority.
- Specific decision right.
- Scope.
- Conditions.
- Duration.
- Limitations.
- Escalation requirements.
- Reporting requirements.
- Withdrawal conditions.

---

## 4. Authority Cannot Be Implied

Authority should not be inferred from:

- Rank alone.
- Technical expertise alone.
- Previous practice.
- System access.
- User permissions.
- Urgency.
- Informal instructions.
- AI recommendations.

Where authority is unclear, the matter should be escalated to the appropriate authority.

---

## 5. Delegation Boundaries

Delegation should specify whether the delegate may:

- Approve authorisation.
- Impose conditions.
- Modify conditions.
- Restrict employment.
- Suspend authority.
- Restore authority.
- Approve exceptions.
- Approve emergency action.
- Recommend reauthorisation.

The delegate should not exercise decision rights outside the defined delegation.

---

## 6. Non-Delegable Authority

Organisations may designate certain decisions as non-delegable.

These may include decisions involving:

- Highest-consequence autonomy.
- Fundamental legal constraints.
- Strategic-level authority.
- Material expansion of operational scope.
- Major residual risk acceptance.
- Permanent revocation.
- Other decisions designated by applicable policy.

D-AIGAAF does not prescribe a universal list; organisations should define it according to their governance structure.

---

## 7. Competence of Delegated Authority

A delegated authority should possess sufficient competence to understand:

- Mission context.
- AI capability.
- Risk.
- Autonomy.
- Human control.
- Operational environment.
- Assurance evidence.
- Conditions and restrictions.
- Consequences of the decision.

Delegation should not be used to transfer difficult decisions to individuals without appropriate competence.

---

## 8. Separation of Duties

Where proportionate to risk, the following functions should remain appropriately separated:

- Capability ownership.
- Development.
- Technical authority.
- Assurance/TEVV.
- Security.
- Operational command.
- Risk ownership.
- Operational AI advisory.
- Final authorisation.

A person or organisation should not be the sole source of evidence, assurance and final authority for a high-consequence capability.

---

## 9. Escalation

Escalation is the process of transferring a decision or issue to a higher or more appropriate authority because:

- Authority is exceeded.
- Risk exceeds delegated tolerance.
- Conditions are unclear.
- Evidence is insufficient.
- Consequence is unusually high.
- Autonomy changes.
- Operational circumstances materially change.
- A conflict exists.
- An incident occurs.
- Emergency conditions arise.

---

## 10. Escalation Triggers

Escalation should be considered when:

- The capability approaches an operational boundary.
- A mandatory condition cannot be satisfied.
- Human control is degraded.
- AI behaviour is unexpected.
- Confidence or uncertainty becomes material.
- Security is compromised or suspected.
- A critical dependency fails.
- The system operates outside its authorised environment.
- Mission objectives change materially.
- Higher autonomy is requested.
- Residual risk increases.
- Existing evidence is no longer sufficient.

---

## 11. Escalation Path

A generic escalation path may be:

**Operator → System Manager → Operational AI Advisor → Operational/Command Authority → Authorising Authority**

Technical or security matters may additionally escalate through:

**Technical Authority / Security Authority / Assurance Authority → Appropriate Decision Authority**

The exact organisational structure may differ.

---

## 12. Operational AI Advisor

Where an Operational AI Advisor (OAIA) role exists, the OAIA may facilitate escalation by explaining:

- AI capability.
- Technical limitations.
- Model uncertainty.
- Operational implications.
- Autonomy implications.
- Risk implications.
- Available technical options.

The OAIA should not replace command authority or assume decision rights unless formally designated.

---

## 13. Emergency Escalation

During an emergency:

- Immediate protective action should not be delayed where delay could increase harm.
- Pre-authorised emergency procedures should be used where available.
- The responsible authority should be informed as soon as practicable.
- Actions should be recorded.
- The decision should be reviewed after the event.

Emergency escalation should preserve accountability even when normal communications are unavailable.

---

## 14. Escalation Under Degraded Communications

Where communications are degraded or unavailable:

- Local authority arrangements should apply.
- Predefined contingency procedures should be followed.
- Delegated authority should remain within its defined limits.
- AI should not acquire additional authority because human communication is unavailable.
- Reconnection should trigger appropriate reporting and review.

---

## 15. Delegation for Restricted Operations

Delegation may permit lower-level authorities to manage restricted or low-consequence operational decisions.

The delegation should specify:

- Permitted scope.
- Maximum autonomy.
- Maximum consequence.
- Environmental boundaries.
- Conditions.
- Required monitoring.
- Escalation threshold.

This can enable operational responsiveness without creating uncontrolled authority.

---

## 16. Delegation of Emergency Protective Action

Specific personnel may be pre-authorised to:

- Reduce autonomy.
- Stop a function.
- Transfer control.
- Move to a safe state.
- Suspend employment.

Such delegation should be explicitly documented and tested where practicable.

---

## 17. Delegation and AI Recommendations

AI recommendations do not constitute delegated authority.

A delegated human authority may consider an AI recommendation, but remains responsible for determining whether to:

- Accept it.
- Reject it.
- Seek additional information.
- Escalate it.
- Request human review.

The AI shall not determine who has authority to act.

---

## 18. Disagreement and Escalation

Where:

- AI recommends one course of action.
- Human authority believes another course is appropriate.

the human decision should prevail where the human retains the applicable authority.

For consequential decisions, the rationale for rejecting or bypassing an AI recommendation should be recorded where practicable.

Where the human decision maker is uncertain, escalation should be available.

---

## 19. Conflicting Authorities

Where two authorities appear to have conflicting decision rights:

1. Identify the applicable authorisation.
2. Identify the defined decision right.
3. Identify any delegation.
4. Apply the higher or specifically designated authority.
5. Escalate unresolved conflict.
6. Record the resolution.

Operational employment should not depend on informal assumptions about precedence.

---

## 20. Withdrawal of Delegation

Delegation may be withdrawn when:

- Risk changes.
- Conditions are breached.
- Competence is insufficient.
- An incident occurs.
- The authority structure changes.
- The delegated authority is unavailable.
- The original delegation is no longer appropriate.

Withdrawal should be recorded and communicated to affected personnel.

---

## 21. Temporary Delegation

Temporary delegation may be used for:

- Absence of the normal authority.
- Specific missions.
- Exercises.
- Emergencies.
- Transitional arrangements.

Temporary delegation should have clear termination conditions.

---

## 22. Delegation Register

A Delegation Register should capture:

- Delegation identifier.
- Delegating authority.
- Delegated authority.
- Decision rights.
- Scope.
- Conditions.
- Autonomy limits.
- Mission/environment limits.
- Start date.
- End date.
- Escalation requirements.
- Reporting requirements.
- Withdrawal conditions.
- Approval.
- Status.

---

## 23. Escalation Record

Material escalations should record:

- Issue.
- Capability.
- Mission.
- Environment.
- Configuration.
- Autonomy.
- Human authority.
- Risk.
- Evidence available.
- Decision required.
- Authority consulted.
- Decision.
- Rationale.
- Conditions.
- Follow-up actions.

---

## 24. Accountability

Delegation does not automatically eliminate accountability of the delegating authority.

The organisation should distinguish between:

- Authority to make the decision.
- Responsibility for executing the decision.
- Accountability for governance.
- Technical responsibility.
- Assurance responsibility.
- Risk ownership.

These relationships should be explicit.

---

## 25. Review of Delegated Authority

Delegations should be reviewed:

- Periodically.
- When organisational structures change.
- When mission or risk changes.
- When autonomy changes.
- After significant incidents.
- When competence requirements change.
- When the underlying authorisation changes.

A delegation should remain aligned with the current Authorisation Record.

---

## 26. Delegation and Authorisation Scope

Delegation cannot expand the underlying operational authorisation.

A delegate may exercise only the authority that:

1. Exists within the underlying authorisation.
2. Has been explicitly delegated.
3. Remains within applicable conditions and restrictions.

Any expansion requires the appropriate governance process.

---

## 27. Golden Thread

Delegation and escalation preserve the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Reauthorisation**

The framework should make it possible to trace a consequential decision from the operational requirement through the evidence and authority to the human decision maker who exercised the relevant authority.

---

## 28. Core Rule

**No consequential AI-enabled action should depend on assumed or implied authority. Decision rights must be explicitly assigned, delegation must be bounded, escalation must be available, and the responsible human authority must remain identifiable.**
