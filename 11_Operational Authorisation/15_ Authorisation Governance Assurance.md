# 15 Authorisation Governance Assurance

## 1. Purpose

This document defines assurance requirements for the governance system that grants, conditions, monitors, reviews, restricts, suspends and revokes operational authorisation for Defence AI capabilities.

The objective is to ensure that operational authorisation is itself governed, evidenced and subject to assurance rather than being treated as a one-time administrative approval.

---

## 2. Core Principle

**The process by which operational authority is granted shall itself be demonstrably controlled, competent, independent where required, evidence-based and auditable.**

A well-assured AI capability is not sufficient if the governance process authorising its employment is weak.

---

## 3. Scope

Authorisation governance assurance should examine:

- Decision rights.
- Delegation.
- Competence.
- Independence.
- Evidence sufficiency.
- Risk acceptance.
- Conditions and restrictions.
- Authorisation records.
- Review and renewal.
- Change control.
- Suspension and revocation.
- Compliance.
- Dispute resolution.
- Emergency procedures.
- Monitoring.
- Auditability.

---

## 4. Governance Assurance Objectives

Governance assurance should establish that:

1. Appropriate authority exists.
2. Decision rights are clearly defined.
3. Authorities are competent.
4. Conflicts of interest are controlled.
5. Evidence is sufficient for the decision.
6. Residual risk is explicitly understood and accepted by the appropriate authority.
7. Conditions are enforceable.
8. Authorisation scope is clear.
9. Changes can invalidate or modify authority.
10. Suspension and revocation mechanisms work.
11. Decisions are traceable.
12. Governance remains effective during operational change.

---

## 5. Governance Assurance Questions

A governance assurance review should ask:

- Who is authorised to make this decision?
- How was that authority established?
- Can the authority be delegated?
- Is the delegate competent?
- Is the decision supported by sufficient evidence?
- Who owns the residual risk?
- Are assurance findings resolved?
- Are conditions explicit?
- Can compliance be demonstrated?
- What happens if conditions fail?
- What changes require reauthorisation?
- Can authority be suspended quickly?
- Is the decision independently reviewable?
- Can the decision be reconstructed later?

---

## 6. Assurance Independence

Independence should be proportionate to consequence and risk.

For higher-consequence capabilities, assurance should avoid situations where the same person or organisational function:

- Develops the capability.
- Produces all supporting evidence.
- Determines whether the evidence is sufficient.
- Accepts residual risk.
- Grants final operational authority.

Independence does not require organisational separation in every case, but conflicts of interest should be identified and controlled.

---

## 7. Competence Assurance

Personnel exercising authorisation responsibilities should have appropriate competence in:

- Operational context.
- AI capabilities and limitations.
- Risk.
- Autonomy.
- Human control.
- Assurance and TEVV.
- Security.
- Data.
- Applicable law and policy.
- Authorisation governance.

Competence requirements should be proportionate to the consequence of the decisions being made.

---

## 8. Decision Quality

Authorisation decisions should be based on explicit reasoning rather than:

- Reputation.
- Rank.
- Vendor assurances alone.
- Previous successful use.
- Technical demonstrations alone.
- AI recommendations alone.
- Operational pressure without risk assessment.

The decision should explain why the evidence supports the authorised scope and conditions.

---

## 9. Evidence Sufficiency

Governance assurance should examine whether evidence is:

- Relevant.
- Current.
- Traceable.
- Representative.
- Sufficient.
- Independently assessed where required.
- Applicable to the intended mission and environment.

Evidence should support the specific authorisation being granted rather than merely demonstrate generic system performance.

---

## 10. Risk Acceptance Assurance

Where residual risk remains, assurance should verify:

- Risk has been identified.
- Consequence has been assessed.
- Controls have been considered.
- Residual risk has been documented.
- The appropriate risk owner has been identified.
- Acceptance authority is valid.
- Conditions associated with acceptance are explicit.

Risk acceptance should never be implied merely because an authorisation was granted.

---

## 11. Authorisation Condition Assurance

Assurance should verify that conditions are:

- Clear.
- Measurable where practicable.
- Communicated.
- Technically or operationally enforceable.
- Monitored.
- Assigned to responsible parties.
- Linked to the authorisation decision.

A condition that cannot be understood or monitored may not provide effective control.

---

## 12. Scope Assurance

The reviewer should verify that authorisation scope clearly identifies:

- Capability.
- Configuration.
- Mission.
- Environment.
- Autonomy.
- Human authority.
- Users.
- Functions.
- Dependencies.
- Geographic or operational limits.
- Validity period.

Scope ambiguity should be treated as a governance weakness.

---

## 13. Delegation Assurance

Delegation arrangements should be reviewed to establish that:

- Delegations are explicit.
- Decision rights are bounded.
- Delegates are competent.
- Delegation does not exceed the delegator's authority.
- Delegation is recorded.
- Expiry or withdrawal is defined.
- Escalation remains available.

Delegation should distribute authority without creating ambiguous accountability.

---

## 14. Review and Renewal Assurance

The organisation should verify that:

- Authorisations are reviewed at appropriate intervals.
- Material changes trigger appropriate review.
- Evidence remains current.
- Environmental assumptions remain valid.
- Human-control assumptions remain valid.
- Security posture remains acceptable.
- Risk remains within tolerance.
- Renewal is not treated as automatic.

---

## 15. Change-Control Assurance

Governance assurance should test whether changes are correctly classified.

Particular attention should be given to changes affecting:

- Model behaviour.
- Model weights or hidden layers.
- Training or fine-tuning.
- Software.
- Hardware.
- Data.
- Interfaces.
- Security controls.
- Autonomy.
- Human supervision.
- Mission.
- Environment.
- Dependencies.

The key question is:

**Does the change preserve the assurance basis on which operational authority was granted?**

---

## 16. Suspension and Revocation Assurance

The organisation should periodically verify that it can:

- Restrict employment.
- Reduce autonomy.
- Transfer control.
- Suspend authority.
- Revoke authority.
- Communicate status.
- Preserve evidence.
- Prevent unauthorised restoration.

These mechanisms should be tested where practicable.

---

## 17. Emergency Governance Assurance

Emergency procedures should demonstrate that:

- Protective action can occur quickly.
- Human authority remains identifiable.
- Pre-authorised actions are bounded.
- Loss of communications does not create uncontrolled AI authority.
- Escalation remains available.
- Actions are recorded.
- Post-event review occurs.

Emergency procedures should be exercised periodically where appropriate.

---

## 18. Compliance Assurance

Governance assurance should determine whether the organisation can detect:

- Expired authorisations.
- Scope violations.
- Unauthorised configurations.
- Unapproved autonomy changes.
- Failed conditions.
- Missing supervision.
- Monitoring failures.
- Unauthorised employment.

Detection mechanisms should be proportionate to operational consequence.

---

## 19. Dispute and Challenge Assurance

The governance system should allow appropriate personnel to challenge:

- Insufficient evidence.
- Unsafe behaviour.
- Unclear authority.
- Excessive autonomy.
- Inadequate human control.
- Security concerns.
- Risk acceptance.

Assurance should examine whether challenges are genuinely considered and whether escalation mechanisms work.

---

## 20. Auditability

An independent reviewer should be able to reconstruct:

**Mission → Risk → Requirements → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Employment → Monitoring → Change / Incident → Review**

The ability to reconstruct this chain is a key indicator of governance maturity.

---

## 21. Governance Assurance Evidence

Typical evidence may include:

- Governance policies.
- Decision-rights matrices.
- Delegation records.
- Competence records.
- Authorisation Records.
- Assurance Records.
- TEVV reports.
- Risk assessments.
- Meeting or decision records.
- Condition registers.
- Change records.
- Compliance records.
- Incident records.
- Suspension/revocation records.
- Review records.
- Audit findings.
- Training and exercise records.

---

## 22. Governance Assurance Findings

Findings may be classified as:

| Finding | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **G1 — Observation** | Improvement opportunity |
| **G2 — Minor Weakness** | Limited governance deficiency |
| **G3 — Significant Weakness** | Deficiency capable of affecting authorisation validity |
| **G4 — Critical Weakness** | Governance failure capable of creating unacceptable risk |

G3 and G4 findings should normally require formal corrective action and escalation.

---

## 23. Governance Assurance Outcomes

Possible outcomes include:

1. **Assured**
2. **Assured with Improvements Required**
3. **Conditionally Assured**
4. **Additional Evidence Required**
5. **Corrective Action Required**
6. **Reassessment Required**
7. **Authorisation Governance Not Adequate**

The outcome should be recorded with rationale and follow-up actions.

---

## 24. Continuous Governance Assurance

Governance assurance should not be limited to periodic audits.

Relevant indicators may include:

- Repeated authorisation deviations.
- Frequent emergency interventions.
- Recurrent disputes.
- Repeated condition failures.
- Unplanned autonomy changes.
- Expired evidence.
- Delayed reauthorisation.
- Repeated suspension.
- Unresolved audit findings.
- Significant incidents.

Trends may indicate that the authorisation model itself requires change.

---

## 25. Governance Metrics

Organisations may track metrics such as:

- Percentage of capabilities with current authorisation.
- Percentage with complete authorisation records.
- Percentage with current assurance evidence.
- Number of overdue reviews.
- Number of material changes processed correctly.
- Number of authorisation violations.
- Time to suspend a capability.
- Time to complete material reviews.
- Number of unresolved governance findings.
- Percentage of relevant personnel meeting competence requirements.

Metrics should support governance decisions rather than become targets that encourage superficial compliance.

---

## 26. Governance Assurance Review Cycle

A generic cycle is:

**Plan → Assess → Test → Evidence → Findings → Correct → Verify → Report → Improve**

Where appropriate, assurance should feed directly into:

- Authorisation review.
- Revalidation.
- Reauthorisation.
- Policy revision.
- Training.
- Technical controls.
- Operational procedures.

---

## 27. Relationship with Continuous Assurance

Operational authorisation governance is one component of D-AIGAAF Continuous Assurance.

The relationship is:

**Operational Monitoring → Governance Signal → Assurance Assessment → Authority Decision → Corrective Action → Revalidation / Reauthorisation**

This prevents authorisation from becoming disconnected from real-world system behaviour.

---

## 28. Golden Thread

Governance assurance preserves the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Reauthorisation**

The governance process itself should be traceable through this chain.

---

## 29. Core Rule

**Operational authorisation is only as credible as the governance process that grants and maintains it. D-AIGAAF shall therefore require authorisation governance to be competent, evidence-based, proportionate, traceable, reviewable and capable of detecting and correcting failures in the exercise of operational authority.**
