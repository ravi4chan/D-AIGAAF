# 16 Authorisation Audit and Evidence

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the audit and evidence requirements for operational authorisation within D-AIGAAF.

The objective is to ensure that decisions granting, conditioning, reviewing, restricting, suspending or revoking operational authority can be supported by reliable evidence and independently reconstructed when required.

---

## 2. Core Principle

**Every material operational authorisation decision should be supported by sufficient, traceable and appropriately protected evidence that demonstrates what was decided, why it was decided, who had authority to decide it, and what conditions applied.**

---

## 3. Scope

Authorisation audit and evidence should cover:

- Authorisation decisions.
- Decision rights.
- Delegations.
- Conditions and restrictions.
- Risk acceptance.
- Assurance evidence.
- TEVV evidence.
- Configuration.
- Mission and environment.
- Autonomy.
- Human authority.
- Security.
- Changes.
- Compliance.
- Incidents.
- Reviews.
- Suspension and revocation.
- Reauthorisation.

---

## 4. Evidence Objectives

Evidence should enable an authorised reviewer to establish:

1. What capability was considered.
2. What mission and use case were authorised.
3. What environment was considered.
4. What autonomy was authorised.
5. Who held human authority.
6. What evidence supported the decision.
7. What risks remained.
8. What conditions applied.
9. What configuration was authorised.
10. Whether the capability remained within scope.
11. What changes or incidents occurred.
12. What subsequent decisions were made.

---

## 5. Authorisation Evidence Chain

A minimum evidence chain should support:

**Mission → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Employment → Monitoring → Change / Incident → Review / Reauthorisation**

This chain should align with the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread.

---

## 6. Evidence Categories

Evidence may include:

### 6.1 Governance Evidence

- Policies.
- Decision-rights matrices.
- Delegations.
- Committee decisions.
- Authority designations.

### 6.2 Capability Evidence

- System description.
- Model information.
- Configuration baseline.
- Dependencies.
- Intended functionality.
- Known limitations.

### 6.3 Risk Evidence

- Risk assessments.
- Hazard analysis.
- Residual-risk assessments.
- Risk acceptance records.

### 6.4 Assurance Evidence

- TEVV reports.
- Validation results.
- Security assessments.
- Human-control assessments.
- Operational-environment assessments.

### 6.5 Operational Evidence

- Mission records.
- Environment observations.
- Monitoring records.
- Human intervention records.
- Performance observations.

### 6.6 Change and Incident Evidence

- Change requests.
- Impact assessments.
- Incident reports.
- Corrective actions.
- Revalidation records.
- Reauthorisation decisions.

---

## 7. Evidence Quality

Evidence should be assessed for:

- Authenticity.
- Integrity.
- Accuracy.
- Relevance.
- Completeness.
- Currency.
- Traceability.
- Provenance.
- Applicability.
- Independence where required.

Evidence should not be treated as sufficient merely because it exists.

---

## 8. Evidence Sufficiency

The amount and quality of evidence should be proportionate to:

- Consequence.
- Mission criticality.
- Autonomy.
- Operational environment.
- Human-control requirements.
- Security risk.
- Uncertainty.
- Novelty.
- Change magnitude.

Higher-consequence and higher-autonomy capabilities generally require stronger evidence.

---

## 9. Evidence Provenance

Where practicable, evidence should identify:

- Source.
- Creator.
- Date.
- Version.
- Configuration.
- Method.
- Environment.
- Scope.
- Assumptions.
- Review status.

This enables later assessment of whether evidence remains applicable.

---

## 10. Evidence Currency

Evidence may become invalid or less relevant because of:

- Model updates.
- Software changes.
- Hardware changes.
- Data changes.
- Environmental changes.
- New threats.
- Changed mission.
- Changed autonomy.
- Changed human supervision.
- New incidents.
- Changed dependencies.

Evidence currency should therefore be assessed during authorisation review and change assessment.

---

## 11. Configuration Traceability

Evidence should be linked, where practicable, to the configuration under which it was produced.

Configuration information may include:

- Model/version identifier.
- Software version.
- Hardware configuration.
- Relevant parameters.
- Data version.
- Interfaces.
- Security controls.
- Applicable system settings.

A test result should not automatically be assumed to apply to a materially different configuration.

---

## 12. Decision Evidence

A material authorisation decision should record:

- Decision.
- Decision maker.
- Authority.
- Date.
- Capability.
- Mission.
- Environment.
- Autonomy.
- Human authority.
- Evidence considered.
- Residual risk.
- Conditions.
- Rationale.
- Review requirements.

The record should be sufficiently clear to reconstruct the decision later.

---

## 13. Audit Trail

The audit trail should allow reconstruction of material events, including:

- Creation of authorisation.
- Amendments.
- Delegations.
- Conditions.
- Reviews.
- Changes.
- Incidents.
- Restrictions.
- Suspensions.
- Restorations.
- Revocations.
- Reauthorisations.

Material records should not be silently altered or deleted.

---

## 14. Evidence Integrity

Evidence repositories should provide appropriate controls for:

- Access.
- Authentication.
- Integrity.
- Versioning.
- Retention.
- Backup.
- Recovery.
- Audit logging.
- Controlled amendment.

The protection level should reflect the sensitivity and consequence of the evidence.

---

## 15. Independent Audit

Independent audit should be considered where proportionate to:

- Consequence.
- Autonomy.
- Organisational risk.
- Regulatory requirements.
- Strategic importance.
- Material governance concerns.

Auditors should have appropriate competence and sufficient independence.

---

## 16. Audit Scope

An audit may examine:

- Whether authority was valid.
- Whether conditions were followed.
- Whether evidence supported the decision.
- Whether risk acceptance was valid.
- Whether changes were properly governed.
- Whether monitoring occurred.
- Whether incidents were addressed.
- Whether suspension mechanisms worked.
- Whether reauthorisation occurred when required.

---

## 17. Audit Findings

Findings may be classified as:

| Finding | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A1 — Observation** | Improvement opportunity |
| **A2 — Minor Finding** | Limited control weakness |
| **A3 — Significant Finding** | Material governance deficiency |
| **A4 — Critical Finding** | Deficiency capable of undermining operational authority or creating unacceptable risk |

A3 and A4 findings should normally trigger formal corrective action and escalation.

---

## 18. Evidence Gaps

An evidence gap exists where required evidence:

- Does not exist.
- Cannot be located.
- Is incomplete.
- Is outdated.
- Does not apply to the current configuration.
- Does not cover the relevant environment.
- Does not adequately demonstrate the required control.

Evidence gaps should be assessed for their impact on assurance and authorisation.

---

## 19. Evidence Contradictions

Where evidence conflicts, the organisation should not simply select the favourable result.

The conflict should be:

1. Identified.
2. Recorded.
3. Investigated.
4. Assessed for significance.
5. Resolved where possible.
6. Escalated where necessary.

Material unresolved contradictions may require restriction, additional assurance, revalidation or reauthorisation.

---

## 20. Audit of Human Authority

Audits should verify:

- The responsible human authority was identifiable.
- Decision rights were valid.
- Delegation was documented.
- Required supervision existed.
- Intervention capability was available where required.
- Consequential decisions were appropriately recorded.

Where an AI recommendation influenced a consequential decision, the record should distinguish the AI contribution from the human decision authority.

---

## 21. Audit of Autonomy

Audit should verify that:

- Actual autonomy remained within authorised limits.
- Autonomy transitions were controlled.
- Human supervision matched the authorised level.
- Autonomous functions were appropriately bounded.
- Higher autonomy was not introduced through configuration or software changes without governance.

---

## 22. Audit of Conditions and Restrictions

The audit should establish whether:

- Conditions were clearly defined.
- Personnel understood them.
- Controls enforced them where practicable.
- Monitoring detected violations.
- Deviations were reported.
- Corrective action occurred.

A condition that repeatedly cannot be satisfied may indicate that the authorisation needs revision.

---

## 23. Audit of Changes

Audit should examine whether material changes were:

- Identified.
- Classified.
- Assessed.
- Tested.
- Approved.
- Recorded.
- Monitored after implementation.

Particular attention should be given to changes that may alter model behaviour, hidden/model layers, autonomy, human control or operational scope.

---

## 24. Audit of Incidents

Following a significant incident, audit or review should establish:

- What happened.
- What was authorised.
- What configuration was active.
- What controls operated.
- What human authority existed.
- What evidence was available.
- Whether authorisation remained valid.
- Whether corrective action was completed.

---

## 25. Evidence Retention

Evidence should be retained for a period sufficient to support:

- Operational accountability.
- Assurance review.
- Incident investigation.
- Legal or regulatory obligations.
- Lessons learned.
- Reauthorisation.
- Historical reconstruction.

Retention requirements should be determined by applicable law, policy and organisational requirements.

---

## 26. Evidence Disposal

When evidence reaches the end of its authorised retention period:

- Disposal should be controlled.
- Applicable legal holds should be respected.
- Disposal should be recorded where required.
- Critical historical evidence should not be destroyed merely because an authorisation has ended.

---

## 27. Evidence Access

Access should follow appropriate principles of:

- Need.
- Authority.
- Role.
- Sensitivity.
- Security.
- Accountability.

Auditability does not require unrestricted access to all evidence.

---

## 28. Authorisation Evidence Register

A register may capture:

- Evidence identifier.
- Authorisation identifier.
- Evidence category.
- Description.
- Source.
- Version.
- Date.
- Configuration.
- Environment.
- Owner.
- Assurance status.
- Applicability.
- Expiry/review date.
- Related findings.
- Storage location.
- Integrity status.

---

## 29. Audit and Evidence Status

Evidence may be classified as:

- **E1 — Verified**
- **E2 — Conditionally Verified**
- **E3 — Partially Verified**
- **E4 — Unverified**
- **E5 — Contradicted**
- **E6 — Superseded**

The status should be updated when new information affects evidence validity.

---

## 30. Corrective Action

Audit findings should have:

- Responsible owner.
- Required action.
- Priority.
- Due date.
- Verification method.
- Closure authority.
- Evidence of completion.

A finding should not be considered closed solely because an action was reported as complete.

---

## 31. Continuous Audit

Where proportionate, audit activities may be supported by continuous monitoring of:

- Authorisation expiry.
- Scope changes.
- Configuration changes.
- Autonomy changes.
- Compliance deviations.
- Security events.
- Incidents.
- Evidence currency.
- Unresolved findings.

Automated monitoring may identify potential issues, but consequential governance decisions remain subject to appropriate human authority.

---

## 32. Audit Triggers

An additional audit or focused review may be initiated by:

- Major incident.
- Security compromise.
- Repeated non-compliance.
- Material model change.
- Significant autonomy change.
- Change in mission.
- Change in operating environment.
- New threat.
- New legal or policy requirement.
- Serious dispute.
- Evidence contradiction.
- Repeated failure of conditions.

---

## 33. Audit Independence and Conflicts

Auditors should disclose relevant conflicts of interest.

Where independence is required, the person responsible for producing evidence should not be the sole person responsible for determining whether that evidence establishes adequate assurance.

---

## 34. Governance Reporting

Material audit findings should be reported to the appropriate governance authority.

Reporting should identify:

- Finding.
- Consequence.
- Affected capability.
- Authorisation.
- Risk.
- Evidence.
- Immediate action.
- Corrective action.
- Required decision.

---

## 35. Audit of Authorisation Governance

The organisation should periodically assess whether the authorisation framework itself remains effective.

This should consider:

- Decision quality.
- Delegation.
- Escalation.
- Evidence quality.
- Review timeliness.
- Suspension capability.
- Compliance trends.
- Recurring disputes.
- Incidents.
- Lessons learned.

The objective is to assure not only individual authorisations but the governance system that produces them.

---

## 36. Golden Thread

Audit and evidence should preserve the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Reauthorisation**

An auditor should be able to move both forward and backward through this chain.

---

## 37. Core Rule

**If an operational authorisation decision cannot be supported by reliable evidence and reconstructed through an auditable chain of authority, risk, assurance, conditions and operational activity, the credibility of that authorisation is weakened. D-AIGAAF therefore treats auditability and evidence traceability as integral components of operational authority.**
