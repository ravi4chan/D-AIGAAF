# 17 Authorisation Closure and Withdrawal

## 1. Purpose

This document defines governance for the planned or unplanned closure and withdrawal of operational authorisation for Defence AI capabilities.

The objective is to ensure that operational authority ends in a controlled, explicit and traceable manner and that withdrawal does not leave unresolved safety, security, accountability or assurance gaps.

---

## 2. Core Principle

**Operational authority shall end through an explicit governance decision or a defined expiry condition. Closure or withdrawal of authorisation shall not be confused with technical decommissioning, and neither shall occur without appropriate control of residual risk and records.**

---

## 3. Scope

This document applies to:

- Planned closure.
- Authorisation expiry.
- Withdrawal of operational need.
- Withdrawal of authority.
- Suspension leading to closure.
- Revocation.
- Retirement.
- Emergency withdrawal.
- Capability replacement.
- Loss of required assurance.
- Permanent cessation of authorised employment.

---

## 4. Closure and Withdrawal

For D-AIGAAF:

**Closure** means ending a defined authorisation or operational approval because its authorised purpose or validity has ended.

**Withdrawal** means removing operational authority before or at the end of its expected period because continued employment is no longer justified or permitted.

Withdrawal may therefore be planned or triggered by an adverse event.

---

## 5. Closure Does Not Equal Retirement

Ending operational authorisation does not necessarily mean the AI capability is technically retired.

A capability may:

- Lose authority for one mission but remain authorised elsewhere.
- Be suspended pending review.
- Be withdrawn from one environment.
- Remain available for testing.
- Be replaced operationally while retained for historical purposes.
- Proceed later through a new authorisation process.

Technical retirement and decommissioning are governed by the lifecycle framework.

---

## 6. Planned Closure

Planned closure may occur when:

- Mission need ends.
- Authorisation period expires.
- Capability is replaced.
- Operational requirements change.
- A programme ends.
- A new capability supersedes the authorised capability.
- The capability no longer provides sufficient operational value.
- Policy or legal requirements change.

Planned closure should be scheduled and communicated where practicable.

---

## 7. Withdrawal Triggers

Operational authority may require withdrawal because of:

- Serious incident.
- Loss of meaningful human control.
- Unacceptable performance.
- Security compromise.
- Critical dependency failure.
- Material unauthorised change.
- Failed revalidation.
- Failed reauthorisation.
- Invalid operating assumptions.
- Significant environmental change.
- Loss of required assurance evidence.
- Repeated non-compliance.
- Legal or policy restriction.
- Unacceptable residual risk.

---

## 8. Immediate Withdrawal

Where continued operation could create unacceptable risk, authorised personnel should be able to withdraw or restrict authority immediately in accordance with established decision rights.

Protective action may include:

- Stopping affected functions.
- Reducing autonomy.
- Transferring control.
- Restricting mission scope.
- Moving to a safe state.
- Suspending employment.

Post-event governance should determine the longer-term status.

---

## 9. Withdrawal Authority

The authority responsible for withdrawal should be defined in advance.

Decision rights should identify who may:

- Restrict.
- Suspend.
- Withdraw.
- Revoke.
- Restore.
- Close.

Emergency protective authorities may be broader than normal authority, but should remain bounded and documented.

---

## 10. Closure Decision

A closure decision should consider:

- Current authorisation.
- Mission status.
- Operational need.
- Residual risk.
- Incidents.
- Compliance.
- Assurance status.
- Security status.
- Outstanding findings.
- Dependencies.
- Replacement capability.
- Legal and policy requirements.
- Records and evidence.

---

## 11. Closure States

A generic status model is:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Active** | Operational authority remains valid |
| **Restricted** | Authority remains but scope is reduced |
| **Suspended** | Operational authority temporarily withdrawn |
| **Withdrawn** | Operational authority removed |
| **Expired** | Defined validity period has ended |
| **Closed** | Authorisation formally concluded |
| **Revoked** | Authority formally withdrawn due to governance or risk grounds |
| **Retired** | Capability has entered lifecycle retirement |

These states should be clearly distinguished in organisational records.

---

## 12. Expiry

An authorisation should not automatically be treated as valid after its defined expiry.

Before expiry, the organisation should determine whether to:

- Renew.
- Renew with revised conditions.
- Revalidate.
- Reauthorise.
- Restrict.
- Close.

Where renewal has not occurred, employment should cease or follow an explicitly authorised transitional arrangement.

---

## 13. Transitional Closure

Where a capability is being replaced, transitional arrangements may be required.

These should define:

- Final authorised missions.
- End date.
- Remaining users.
- Remaining environments.
- Data handling.
- Support responsibilities.
- Monitoring.
- Incident reporting.
- Handover.
- Final records.

Transition should not silently extend operational authority.

---

## 14. Withdrawal During an Incident

Where withdrawal follows an incident:

1. Protect people and assets.
2. Restrict or suspend the capability.
3. Preserve evidence.
4. Identify the affected configuration.
5. Establish the operational circumstances.
6. Assess whether other capabilities are affected.
7. Investigate.
8. Determine whether revalidation or reauthorisation is required.
9. Record the final decision.

Incident management and authorisation withdrawal should remain linked.

---

## 15. Security-Driven Withdrawal

A security concern may require immediate withdrawal where:

- System integrity is uncertain.
- Unauthorised access is suspected.
- Critical dependencies are compromised.
- Data integrity is in doubt.
- Security controls have failed.
- Malicious manipulation is suspected.

Restoration should require an appropriate security and assurance determination.

---

## 16. Withdrawal for Loss of Human Control

Where meaningful human control can no longer be demonstrated:

- Consequential autonomous functions should be restricted or stopped as appropriate.
- Human authority should be re-established.
- The condition should be recorded.
- The cause should be assessed.
- Revalidation should be considered before restoration.
- Higher autonomy should not resume automatically.

---

## 17. Withdrawal for Environmental Change

If the operational environment moves outside the authorised envelope:

- The capability should follow defined boundary procedures.
- Operational scope may need to be reduced.
- Authority may be suspended.
- Environmental assessment should be performed.
- Revalidation or reauthorisation may be required.

Environmental change should not silently expand the meaning of the original authorisation.

---

## 18. Withdrawal for Configuration Change

Where an unauthorised or insufficiently assessed configuration is detected:

- The affected configuration should be identified.
- Operational authority for that configuration should be assessed.
- Use should be restricted or suspended where necessary.
- The approved baseline should be restored where practicable.
- Change governance should determine further assurance requirements.

---

## 19. Withdrawal and Data Integrity

Where critical data integrity is uncertain:

- Affected functions should be assessed.
- Use may be restricted.
- Data sources and provenance should be reviewed.
- Relevant safeguards should be applied.
- Revalidation should occur where the assurance basis is affected.

---

## 20. Withdrawal and Dependencies

If a critical dependency becomes unavailable or unreliable, the organisation should determine whether:

- The capability can continue safely.
- A degraded mode is authorised.
- Human control can be maintained.
- A substitute dependency is acceptable.
- Operational authority should be restricted or suspended.

Loss of a dependency should not automatically result in uncontrolled fallback behaviour.

---

## 21. Restoration After Withdrawal

Restoration should require a defined decision.

Depending on the cause, restoration may require:

- Corrective action.
- Security remediation.
- Additional testing.
- TEVV.
- Risk reassessment.
- Environmental reassessment.
- Human-control assessment.
- Revalidation.
- Reauthorisation.

Restoration should not be assumed merely because the immediate problem appears resolved.

---

## 22. Closure Criteria

An authorisation may be closed when:

- Operational need has ended.
- Authority has been withdrawn or expired.
- Outstanding operational actions are addressed.
- Incidents are appropriately recorded.
- Required evidence is preserved.
- Residual risks are transferred or closed.
- Dependencies are addressed.
- Closure authority approves the decision.

---

## 23. Final Operational Review

Before planned closure, the organisation should consider:

- Mission outcomes.
- Performance.
- Incidents.
- Near misses.
- Human factors.
- Security events.
- Environmental experience.
- Compliance.
- Lessons learned.
- Assurance findings.
- Remaining risks.
- Future reuse.

The review should feed lessons into the broader D-AIGAAF lifecycle.

---

## 24. Records at Closure

The final record should include:

- Authorisation identifier.
- Capability.
- Final configuration.
- Final mission scope.
- Final environment.
- Final autonomy.
- Human authority.
- Closure/withdrawal reason.
- Effective date.
- Decision authority.
- Outstanding issues.
- Residual risk.
- Related incidents.
- Final assurance status.
- Lessons learned.
- Record-retention requirements.

---

## 25. Operational Record Closure

Operational records should be reconciled with the final authorisation status.

The organisation should establish:

- What was authorised.
- What was actually employed.
- Whether any deviations occurred.
- Whether incidents remain open.
- Whether corrective actions remain outstanding.

Closure of the authorisation should not erase the historical operational record.

---

## 26. Knowledge Preservation

Closure should preserve knowledge required for:

- Future assurance.
- Incident investigation.
- Lessons learned.
- Reauthorisation of successor capabilities.
- Training.
- Audit.
- Legal accountability.
- Historical reconstruction.

Retirement of a capability should not result in uncontrolled loss of governance knowledge.

---

## 27. Reuse of a Closed Capability

A previously closed capability should not automatically regain operational authority.

If it is later proposed for employment:

- Current configuration should be established.
- Current mission should be assessed.
- Current environment should be assessed.
- Current risk and autonomy should be assessed.
- Current assurance evidence should be reviewed.
- Appropriate revalidation and reauthorisation should occur.

Past authorisation is evidence of historical governance, not automatic current authority.

---

## 28. Closure and Decommissioning

Where closure leads to technical retirement:

**Operational Closure → Retirement Decision → Decommissioning → Record Preservation**

Technical decommissioning should follow `26 Retirement & Decommissioning` and applicable security, data and supply-chain requirements.

---

## 29. Governance Review

Patterns of repeated withdrawal or early closure should be reviewed for systemic causes, including:

- Poor requirements.
- Inadequate TEVV.
- Unrealistic environmental assumptions.
- Excessive autonomy.
- Weak human control.
- Supplier dependencies.
- Security weaknesses.
- Poor change management.
- Inadequate operational monitoring.

Withdrawal data should contribute to continuous assurance.

---

## 30. Golden Thread

Closure and withdrawal preserve the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Reauthorisation**

The end of operational authority should remain traceable to the conditions, evidence and governance decisions that led to closure.

---

## 31. Core Rule

**Operational authority must have a controlled end state. Whether an authorisation expires, is withdrawn, suspended, revoked or closed, the organisation shall preserve human accountability, control residual risk, protect evidence, document the decision and prevent unintended continuation of authority.**
