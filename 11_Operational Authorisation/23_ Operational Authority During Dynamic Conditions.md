# 23 Operational Authority During Dynamic Conditions

## 1. Purpose

Operational conditions can change rapidly after a Defence AI capability has been authorised, declared ready, deployed and placed into employment.

This document establishes governance for maintaining operational authority when mission conditions, environmental conditions, human availability, system behaviour, autonomy, data, security or dependencies change during employment.

The objective is to ensure that operational authority remains controlled even when conditions evolve faster than formal governance processes can normally operate.

---

## 2. Core Principle

A change in operational conditions shall not automatically expand the authority of a Defence AI capability.

When conditions change, the responsible authority shall determine whether the capability can:

- continue within existing authority;
- continue with additional restrictions;
- reduce autonomy;
- transition to human control;
- enter a contingency or safe state;
- suspend employment; or
- require revalidation or reauthorisation.

The fundamental rule is:

**Changing circumstances do not create implied AI authority.**

---

## 3. Dynamic Conditions

Dynamic conditions may include changes to:

- mission objectives;
- operational priorities;
- environment;
- communications;
- sensors;
- navigation;
- data availability;
- adversarial activity;
- system performance;
- human availability;
- human workload;
- autonomy;
- security posture;
- critical dependencies;
- legal or policy constraints; or
- risk and consequence.

The organisation should distinguish between expected operational variation and conditions that materially alter the assurance or authorisation basis.

---

## 4. Dynamic Authority Object

Operational authority remains defined by:

**AI Capability × Mission × Environment × Autonomy × Human Authority**

A material change to any of these dimensions should trigger an appropriate governance response.

Examples:

| Change | Potential Governance Effect |
|---|---|
| Mission objective changes | Reassessment of authorised use |
| Environment deteriorates | Restricted or degraded operation |
| Human supervision decreases | Reduced autonomy or suspension |
| AI behaviour changes | Investigation and assurance review |
| Critical dependency fails | Contingency or safe state |
| Security posture changes | Restriction or suspension |
| Autonomy changes | Reassessment and potentially reauthorisation |

---

## 5. Conditions and Thresholds

Where practicable, operational plans should define thresholds that indicate when existing authority may no longer be appropriate.

Thresholds may relate to:

- environmental conditions;
- system performance;
- confidence or uncertainty;
- communications;
- sensor reliability;
- navigation;
- human workload;
- autonomy transitions;
- security indicators;
- dependency availability; and
- mission conditions.

Thresholds should be linked to predefined responses.

---

## 6. Dynamic Response Model

A working D-AIGAAF response model is:

**Continue → Monitor Closely → Restrict → Reduce Autonomy → Human Control → Contingency → Safe State → Suspend**

The appropriate response should depend on:

- consequence;
- urgency;
- confidence in available information;
- reversibility;
- human control;
- mission criticality;
- environmental conditions; and
- authorised procedures.

The response should be proportionate rather than automatically maximal.

---

## 7. Continue

Continuation may be appropriate where:

- conditions remain within the authorised envelope;
- performance remains acceptable;
- human authority remains available;
- autonomy remains controlled;
- required safeguards remain functional; and
- no material change has occurred.

Continuation should remain subject to normal monitoring.

---

## 8. Restrict

Restriction may be appropriate where the capability remains useful but the original operating conditions have become less favourable.

Restrictions may include:

- reduced mission scope;
- reduced autonomy;
- restricted functions;
- additional human confirmation;
- restricted environment;
- enhanced monitoring;
- shorter operating periods; or
- additional reporting.

Restrictions should be explicit and recorded.

---

## 9. Reduce Autonomy

A reduction in autonomy may be appropriate where:

- uncertainty increases;
- human supervision remains available but conditions deteriorate;
- environmental assumptions become less reliable;
- system performance degrades;
- data quality declines;
- security concerns emerge; or
- the capability approaches an authorised boundary.

Reduction in autonomy should move the system toward a previously assessed and authorised state wherever practicable.

---

## 10. Human Control

Where automated or autonomous operation can no longer be confidently maintained, the capability should transition to the highest level of human control that is feasible and authorised.

This may require:

- human confirmation;
- manual operation;
- increased supervision;
- restriction of AI functions;
- temporary cessation of autonomous actions; or
- safe-state transition.

Human control should be meaningful rather than nominal. A human who lacks sufficient information, time, competence or practical ability to intervene should not be treated as providing effective oversight.

---

## 11. Contingency Conditions

A contingency state may be entered where:

- normal operating assumptions no longer hold;
- communications are degraded;
- critical information is unavailable;
- environmental conditions change;
- dependencies fail;
- security conditions deteriorate; or
- mission conditions require a predefined alternative operating mode.

Contingency modes should be defined in advance where practicable and should have clear entry, operating and exit criteria.

---

## 12. Safe State

A safe state should be available where continued operation cannot be justified.

Safe-state behaviour should be:

- defined;
- tested;
- appropriate to the capability;
- proportionate to the risk;
- accessible to authorised personnel; and
- understood by responsible operators and commanders.

The safe state should not be assumed to be identical for every Defence AI capability.

---

## 13. Suspension

Employment should be suspended where:

- meaningful human control is lost;
- material authorisation conditions fail;
- system behaviour becomes materially unpredictable;
- security integrity is compromised;
- critical safety controls fail;
- the operating environment exceeds authorised boundaries;
- a serious incident occurs;
- required evidence is invalidated; or
- continued operation cannot be justified within the existing authority.

Suspension should be treated as a protective governance mechanism.

---

## 14. Rapid Operational Decisions

Dynamic conditions may require decisions faster than the normal governance cycle permits.

In such circumstances:

- predefined authority should be used where available;
- emergency procedures should be followed;
- protective action should prioritise preservation of human control;
- the minimum necessary operational authority should be used;
- decisions should be recorded as soon as practicable; and
- post-event review should determine whether additional assurance or reauthorisation is required.

Urgency should accelerate governance, not eliminate it.

---

## 15. Loss of Communications

Where communication with the responsible human authority is lost, the AI capability should follow its pre-authorised behaviour.

Depending on the capability and authorised operating mode, this may include:

- continued restricted operation;
- reduced autonomy;
- local human control;
- temporary holding state;
- safe state;
- suspension of autonomous functions.

Loss of communications shall not automatically grant the AI greater decision authority.

---

## 16. Loss of Human Supervision

If required human supervision becomes unavailable:

1. Determine whether the authorised autonomy level remains valid.
2. Apply the predefined autonomy transition.
3. Reduce or suspend autonomous functions where required.
4. Enter the approved contingency or safe state where necessary.
5. Restore appropriate human authority as soon as practicable.
6. Record the event and assess its impact on assurance.

A system should not continue at an autonomy level that depends on supervision that is no longer available.

---

## 17. Environmental Boundary Crossing

Where the operational environment approaches or exceeds the authorised envelope:

- identify the boundary condition;
- assess its significance;
- apply predefined restrictions;
- reduce autonomy where appropriate;
- increase human oversight;
- transition to contingency or safe state where required; and
- determine whether revalidation or reauthorisation is necessary.

Operating outside a demonstrated envelope should not become normal practice through repeated informal use.

---

## 18. Unexpected AI Behaviour

Unexpected behaviour should be assessed according to:

- consequence;
- repeatability;
- severity;
- predictability;
- impact on human control;
- impact on mission;
- security implications;
- affected functions; and
- potential wider impact.

Where behaviour creates unacceptable uncertainty or risk, operational authority should be restricted or suspended as appropriate.

---

## 19. Operational Disagreement

Where command, technical, assurance, security or AI advisory personnel disagree about continued employment:

- the disagreement should be recorded;
- existing authority should not be expanded merely because consensus is absent;
- the issue should be escalated according to defined decision rights;
- immediate protective action remains available;
- the appropriate authority should resolve the operational question; and
- the final decision and rationale should be traceable.

Disagreement should be treated as a governance signal.

---

## 20. Dynamic Changes to Mission

A change in mission objective may materially alter:

- risk;
- consequence;
- acceptable uncertainty;
- autonomy requirements;
- human-control requirements;
- data requirements;
- environmental assumptions; and
- assurance needs.

A capability authorised for one mission should not automatically be treated as authorised for a materially different mission.

Mission expansion should follow the applicable use-case, change-control and authorisation process.

---

## 21. Dynamic Changes to Autonomy

A change in autonomy should be treated as a potentially material governance change.

Moving from:

**Information → Recommendation → Human-Authorised Action → Supervised Autonomous Action → Independent Consequential Autonomy**

may materially alter:

- consequence;
- human-control requirements;
- assurance burden;
- operational risk;
- traceability requirements; and
- authorisation requirements.

Technical availability of a higher autonomy mode shall not constitute authority to activate it.

---

## 22. Dynamic Security Conditions

A changing security environment may require:

- increased monitoring;
- restricted functions;
- reduced autonomy;
- isolation of affected components;
- enhanced human review;
- suspension; or
- incident response.

Security decisions should be coordinated with the AI Security governance model and reflected in operational authorisation where material.

---

## 23. Dynamic Data Conditions

Where data becomes:

- incomplete;
- delayed;
- inconsistent;
- corrupted;
- unreliable;
- manipulated; or
- materially different from the conditions under which the capability was assured,

the responsible authority should determine whether the capability remains suitable.

The AI should not compensate for missing or unreliable information by silently increasing confidence or authority.

---

## 24. Dynamic Dependency Conditions

Failure or degradation of a critical dependency should trigger assessment of:

- affected capability;
- mission impact;
- human-control impact;
- autonomy impact;
- fail-safe behaviour;
- continuity arrangements; and
- authorisation validity.

Where a dependency is essential to safe operation, its loss may require immediate restriction or suspension.

---

## 25. Dynamic Authority Register

For higher-consequence capabilities, organisations should maintain a current record of:

- active authorisation;
- active readiness status;
- current mission;
- current environment;
- current autonomy;
- current human authority;
- current restrictions;
- active contingency state;
- significant changes;
- current incidents; and
- outstanding governance actions.

This provides a common operational view of the authority currently in force.

---

## 26. Post-Event Review

Material dynamic-condition events should be reviewed after the situation stabilises.

The review should determine:

- what changed;
- when it changed;
- what the AI did;
- what humans observed;
- what decision was made;
- what authority applied;
- what controls were used;
- whether the response was appropriate;
- whether assumptions remained valid;
- whether the authorisation basis remains valid; and
- what changes to assurance or governance are required.

---

## 27. Relationship to Continuous Assurance

Dynamic operational conditions are a primary source of continuous-assurance evidence.

The feedback cycle is:

**Condition Change → Detection → Response → Evidence → Assessment → Risk → Assurance → Authorisation Decision**

This allows D-AIGAAF to adapt governance based on observed operational reality.

---

## 28. Relationship to Revalidation and Reauthorisation

Dynamic conditions should trigger revalidation or reauthorisation where they materially affect:

- mission;
- risk;
- autonomy;
- human control;
- environment;
- data;
- security;
- dependencies;
- configuration; or
- assurance evidence.

Not every operational change requires formal reauthorisation. The required response should be determined by the materiality of the change.

---

## 29. Golden Thread

Dynamic operational authority remains connected to the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Readiness → Deployment → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Reauthorisation**

This ensures that rapidly changing operational conditions remain connected to the underlying governance basis.

---

## 30. Governance Questions

When operational conditions change, responsible authorities should ask:

1. What has changed?
2. Is the change within expected operating variation?
3. Does it affect mission?
4. Does it affect risk or consequence?
5. Does it affect the environment?
6. Does it affect data or information?
7. Does it affect security?
8. Does it affect critical dependencies?
9. Does it affect human supervision?
10. Does it affect autonomy?
11. Does it affect meaningful human control?
12. Does the existing authorisation remain valid?
13. What restrictions should apply?
14. Should autonomy be reduced?
15. Should human control be increased?
16. Should contingency or safe state be entered?
17. Is suspension required?
18. Is additional assurance required?
19. Is revalidation or reauthorisation required?
20. Has the decision and rationale been recorded?

---

## 31. Core Rule

> **When operational conditions change, a Defence AI capability shall remain within defined human authority and authorised boundaries. Dynamic circumstances may require restriction, reduced autonomy, human control, contingency operation or suspension, but shall not create implied or uncontrolled AI authority.**
