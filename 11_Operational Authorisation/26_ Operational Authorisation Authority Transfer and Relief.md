# 26 Operational Authorisation Authority Transfer and Relief

## 1. Purpose

Defence AI capabilities may remain operational while responsibility for their employment changes between commanders, shifts, organisations, teams or authorised personnel.

This document defines governance for the controlled transfer of operational authority and responsibility without creating ambiguity about who is authorised to make consequential decisions.

The objective is to ensure that:

- authority remains identifiable;
- incoming personnel understand the current authorisation;
- restrictions and conditions are transferred explicitly;
- autonomy and human-control arrangements remain intact;
- configuration and readiness are understood;
- outstanding risks and incidents are visible;
- delegation does not create unintended authority; and
- transfer of responsibility is recorded and auditable.

---

## 2. Core Principle

**Operational authority may be transferred only through defined decision rights and a controlled handover.**

A change of personnel, command, shift or organisational responsibility shall not by itself expand, reduce or otherwise alter the authority granted to an AI capability.

---

## 3. Authority Transfer Object

A transfer should establish continuity of:

**Capability × Mission × Environment × Autonomy × Human Authority × Configuration × Conditions**

The incoming authority should be able to determine exactly what remains authorised and what requires a new decision.

---

## 4. Types of Transfer

Authority transfer may occur through:

- planned command succession;
- shift or duty handover;
- temporary relief;
- permanent reassignment;
- delegation;
- organisational transfer;
- emergency succession;
- transfer following an incident; or
- transition between operational phases.

The governance requirements should be proportionate to the consequence and scope of the authority being transferred.

---

## 5. Transfer of Authority vs Transfer of Accountability

Transfer of operational responsibility should not create uncertainty about accountability.

The organisation should distinguish between:

- transfer of operational control;
- delegation of a specific decision right;
- transfer of system management;
- transfer of technical responsibility;
- transfer of assurance responsibility; and
- transfer of authorising authority.

Each should have a defined scope.

A person receiving operational responsibility should not automatically receive every authority associated with the capability.

---

## 6. Incoming Authority

Before assuming operational responsibility, the incoming authority should understand, as applicable:

- capability identity;
- authorised mission;
- use case;
- operating environment;
- current authorisation status;
- current readiness;
- deployment status;
- employment status;
- authorised autonomy;
- current autonomy;
- human decision rights;
- restrictions;
- mandatory conditions;
- configuration baseline;
- assurance status;
- unresolved findings;
- incidents;
- security status;
- critical dependencies;
- contingency arrangements; and
- suspension or termination triggers.

---

## 7. Outgoing Authority

The outgoing authority should provide a controlled handover of material information.

The handover should identify:

- decisions already taken;
- current operational status;
- current AI contribution;
- material AI recommendations;
- accepted or rejected consequential recommendations where relevant;
- current restrictions;
- interventions and overrides;
- incidents;
- outstanding risks;
- pending changes;
- pending approvals;
- environmental conditions;
- autonomy transitions; and
- unresolved issues.

The handover should not conceal uncertainty or unresolved concerns.

---

## 8. Handover Verification

The incoming authority should verify the material elements of the handover.

Verification may include:

- authorisation record;
- configuration;
- readiness;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- operating boundaries;
- conditions;
- restrictions;
- security status;
- dependency status;
- evidence currency;
- incident status; and
- contingency arrangements.

The receiving authority should not be expected to rely solely on verbal assurances for consequential matters.

---

## 9. Handover Record

A controlled handover record should identify:

- capability;
- mission/use case;
- outgoing authority;
- incoming authority;
- effective date/time;
- authorisation status;
- readiness status;
- autonomy;
- configuration;
- conditions;
- restrictions;
- incidents;
- outstanding actions;
- verification performed;
- unresolved issues;
- acceptance by incoming authority; and
- required follow-up.

---

## 10. Shift Handover

For recurring operational shifts, the organisation should establish a standard minimum handover.

It should address:

- current mission;
- current AI status;
- current autonomy;
- current environment;
- active restrictions;
- recent incidents;
- abnormal behaviour;
- pending interventions;
- changes;
- dependencies;
- monitoring concerns; and
- immediate escalation conditions.

The level of detail should be proportionate to the consequence of AI-supported activity.

---

## 11. Emergency Transfer

Where the normal authority becomes unavailable, predefined emergency succession arrangements may be used.

Emergency transfer should preserve:

- human authority;
- authorised boundaries;
- autonomy restrictions;
- fail-safe controls;
- operational records; and
- escalation requirements.

Emergency succession should not automatically authorise broader mission scope or higher autonomy.

---

## 12. Delegated Authority

Delegated authority should identify:

- delegator;
- delegate;
- exact decision rights;
- scope;
- conditions;
- duration;
- limitations;
- escalation requirements; and
- withdrawal mechanism.

The delegate should not exercise authority beyond the defined delegation.

Delegation should not transfer accountability for decisions beyond the scope legally or organisationally assigned to the delegate.

---

## 13. Transfer During Degraded Operations

Where communications or supporting infrastructure are degraded, authority transfer may require approved fallback procedures.

The organisation should determine how the incoming authority can verify:

- current authorisation;
- configuration;
- autonomy;
- restrictions;
- human-control capability;
- environmental status; and
- critical dependencies.

Where material status cannot be established, employment should be restricted or suspended as defined by the applicable contingency arrangements.

---

## 14. Transfer During Loss of Communications

Loss of communications should not create an assumption that the person or system with local technical access automatically becomes the operational authority.

Fallback authority should be defined in advance.

Where the designated authority cannot be reached, predefined escalation and emergency protective procedures should apply.

---

## 15. Transfer Following an Incident

Authority transfer following an incident should include specific consideration of:

- incident status;
- immediate protective actions;
- affected configuration;
- loss of human control;
- security concerns;
- environmental conditions;
- data integrity;
- autonomy restrictions;
- evidence preservation; and
- suspension or revalidation requirements.

The receiving authority should understand whether the capability remains authorised, restricted or suspended.

---

## 16. Transfer of Autonomy

A transfer of human authority should not silently change the authorised autonomy level.

The incoming authority should verify:

- authorised autonomy;
- permitted autonomy transitions;
- current autonomy;
- supervision requirements;
- intervention capability;
- override capability; and
- conditions for reducing or suspending autonomy.

Any proposed increase in autonomy should follow the applicable authorisation process.

---

## 17. Transfer of Configuration Responsibility

Where system or technical responsibility changes, the receiving authority should verify the configuration baseline.

This should include, where applicable:

- model/system version;
- software version;
- configuration;
- relevant data dependencies;
- security configuration;
- approved interfaces;
- approved operating parameters; and
- outstanding changes.

A change in responsible personnel should not permit unapproved configuration changes.

---

## 18. Transfer of Risk Ownership

Risk ownership should remain explicit during authority transfer.

The handover should identify:

- material risks;
- residual risks;
- accepted risks;
- risk treatment;
- risk acceptance authority;
- risk trends;
- unresolved risk decisions; and
- conditions associated with risk acceptance.

An incoming authority should not be assumed to have accepted an unresolved risk merely by taking over operational responsibility.

---

## 19. Transfer of Assurance Responsibility

Where assurance responsibilities transfer, the receiving authority should understand:

- current assurance status;
- evidence available;
- evidence gaps;
- unresolved findings;
- upcoming TEVV;
- revalidation requirements;
- reauthorisation requirements; and
- evidence that may have been invalidated by recent changes or incidents.

Assurance responsibility should not be separated from the ability to access relevant evidence.

---

## 20. Transfer of Security Responsibility

Where security responsibility changes, the handover should identify:

- current security posture;
- open security issues;
- relevant incidents;
- known dependencies;
- monitoring status;
- unresolved vulnerabilities or integrity concerns; and
- escalation procedures.

Where material security uncertainty exists, the receiving authority should have access to the responsible Security Authority or defined escalation mechanism.

---

## 21. Transfer of Environmental Responsibility

Where responsibility changes during ongoing employment, the receiving authority should understand the current operational environment.

This may include:

- physical conditions;
- sensor conditions;
- communication conditions;
- electromagnetic conditions;
- navigation conditions;
- adversarial conditions;
- human operational conditions; and
- whether the current environment remains within the authorised envelope.

---

## 22. Transfer of Monitoring Responsibility

Monitoring responsibilities should be explicit.

The receiving authority should know:

- what is being monitored;
- which indicators matter;
- current status;
- thresholds;
- recent alerts;
- unresolved anomalies;
- escalation criteria; and
- who must be contacted when thresholds are crossed.

Monitoring should not stop because personnel responsibility changes.

---

## 23. Transfer and Outstanding Actions

The handover should identify outstanding:

- approvals;
- investigations;
- corrective actions;
- assurance activities;
- security actions;
- configuration changes;
- risk decisions;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation;
- incident reviews; and
- governance reviews.

Each material action should have an owner and due date where appropriate.

---

## 24. Incomplete Handover

Where a complete handover cannot be achieved, the organisation should determine whether operational employment can safely continue.

Possible responses include:

**Continue → Increase Supervision → Restrict → Reduce Autonomy → Human Control → Safe State → Suspend**

The response should be proportionate to the missing information and resulting uncertainty.

---

## 25. Handover Acceptance

The receiving authority should formally or operationally acknowledge acceptance of responsibility where required.

Acceptance should mean that the receiving authority has:

- identified the applicable authority;
- understood the boundaries;
- reviewed material conditions;
- identified current risks;
- confirmed required human-control arrangements; and
- identified outstanding issues.

Acceptance should not be interpreted as acceptance of every unresolved risk.

---

## 26. Authority Transfer During Coalition or Organisational Boundaries

Where operational responsibility moves between organisations, additional governance should address:

- applicable legal and policy authorities;
- differing authorisation regimes;
- information-sharing restrictions;
- security responsibilities;
- data responsibilities;
- configuration control;
- human decision rights;
- autonomy limitations;
- evidence compatibility; and
- incident reporting.

Transfer should occur only where the receiving organisation has appropriate authority and competence.

---

## 27. Revocation of Previous Authority

When authority is transferred, the previous authority should be:

- terminated;
- superseded;
- retained for defined purposes; or
- explicitly delegated.

There should be no ambiguity about whether two people simultaneously hold conflicting operational authority.

---

## 28. Authority Conflicts

If two authorities issue conflicting directions concerning consequential AI employment, the organisation should use predefined escalation and command arrangements.

Pending resolution:

- existing authorised boundaries remain applicable;
- the AI system should not resolve the dispute itself;
- higher-risk or ambiguous actions should be restricted where appropriate; and
- emergency protective actions remain available.

---

## 29. Records and Auditability

Authority transfers should be traceable.

The record should support reconstruction of:

**Previous Authority → Transfer Decision → Handover Evidence → New Authority → Operational State**

This should connect to the Operational Authorisation Record and Operational Record.

---

## 30. Training and Competence

Personnel assuming responsibility should possess the competence required for their assigned authority.

Where the role requires AI-specific knowledge, the receiving authority should understand:

- capability limitations;
- uncertainty;
- autonomy;
- human-control mechanisms;
- operational boundaries;
- relevant failure modes;
- escalation procedures; and
- applicable authorisation conditions.

Competence requirements should be proportionate to consequence and autonomy.

---

## 31. Periodic Review

Authority-transfer arrangements should be periodically reviewed for:

- clarity;
- timeliness;
- role definition;
- delegation effectiveness;
- human-control continuity;
- record quality;
- emergency usability;
- degraded-operation effectiveness; and
- lessons learned.

Exercises should test whether personnel can transfer responsibility without creating gaps in authority.

---

## 32. Relationship to Continuous Assurance

Authority transfer provides another control point in the continuous assurance loop:

**Operational Status → Handover → Verification → Human Authority → Employment → Monitoring → Review**

Material uncertainty identified during handover should feed into risk and assurance processes.

---

## 33. Golden Thread

Authority transfer remains connected to the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Readiness → Deployment → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Transfer/Review → Reauthorisation**

---

## 34. Governance Questions

Responsible authorities should be able to determine:

1. Who currently holds operational authority?
2. Who previously held that authority?
3. What exactly was transferred?
4. What authority was retained by the outgoing person?
5. What authority was delegated to the incoming person?
6. What mission is currently authorised?
7. What environment is authorised?
8. What autonomy is authorised?
9. What autonomy is currently active?
10. What configuration is authorised?
11. What restrictions and conditions apply?
12. What risks remain unresolved?
13. What incidents are outstanding?
14. What assurance evidence remains current?
15. Can meaningful human control be maintained?
16. What happens if the normal authority becomes unavailable?
17. What happens if the handover is incomplete?
18. Are conflicting authorities possible?
19. Has the transfer been recorded?
20. Who can suspend or terminate employment if required?

---

## 35. Core Rule

> **Transfer of operational responsibility for a Defence AI capability shall be explicit, bounded and traceable. A change of commander, operator, shift, organisation or responsible role shall not create implied authority, expand autonomy or alter authorised mission boundaries. Before assuming consequential responsibility, the receiving authority shall have sufficient understanding of the current authorisation, readiness, configuration, environment, autonomy, human-control arrangements, conditions, restrictions, risks and incidents to exercise that authority safely and accountably.**
