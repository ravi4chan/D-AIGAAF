# 27 Operational Authorisation Mission Completion and Closeout

## 1. Purpose

Operational authorisation should not end simply because an AI capability is no longer actively executing a task.

A mission, operational phase or authorised employment period may conclude normally, be terminated early, or be interrupted by an incident, environmental change, loss of control or suspension.

This document defines governance for controlled mission completion and closeout.

The objective is to ensure that:

- authorised employment ends deliberately;
- AI activity does not continue beyond mission authority;
- temporary conditions and restrictions are closed or transferred;
- consequential decisions and actions are recorded;
- incidents and anomalies are captured;
- evidence is preserved;
- residual risks are identified;
- post-mission assurance is completed; and
- lessons are returned to the wider D-AIGAAF lifecycle.

---

## 2. Core Principle

**Operational authority should have a defined and controlled end point.**

Completion of a mission should result in a deliberate transition from operational employment to an appropriate post-employment state.

Mission completion should not create uncertainty about whether the AI capability remains authorised for further activity.

---

## 3. Closeout Object

Mission closeout should consider:

**Mission → AI Capability → Configuration → Autonomy → Human Authority → Actions → Outcomes → Incidents → Evidence → Residual Risk → Future Authority**

The closeout process should establish what happened, what authority ended, what authority remains and what must happen before future employment.

---

## 4. Mission Completion States

A working closeout model may include:

| State | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Active** | Authorised operational employment is ongoing. |
| **Mission Complete** | The authorised mission objective has been completed. |
| **Employment Terminated** | AI employment has been deliberately stopped. |
| **Early Termination** | Employment ended before planned completion. |
| **Contingency Closeout** | Employment ended following contingency operation. |
| **Suspended Closeout** | Employment ended while an authorisation or incident issue remains unresolved. |
| **Closed** | Required closeout activities have been completed. |

The exact states may be adapted to the use case.

---

## 5. Normal Mission Completion

Normal completion should verify:

- mission objective or authorised phase has ended;
- AI employment has ceased or transitioned as authorised;
- autonomous activity has ended;
- human authority remains established during transition;
- relevant systems have entered the approved post-mission state;
- temporary restrictions or conditions are identified;
- operational records are preserved; and
- post-mission actions are assigned.

---

## 6. Mission Boundary Completion

The organisation should define what constitutes completion.

Completion may be based on:

- achievement of mission objectives;
- completion of a defined operational phase;
- expiry of a time-limited authority;
- reaching a geographic or operational boundary;
- completion of an authorised task;
- transfer of responsibility; or
- another predefined condition.

The AI system should not independently redefine mission completion unless such behaviour has been explicitly authorised and appropriately controlled.

---

## 7. Termination of AI Employment

At mission completion, the organisation should determine the required transition.

Possible states include:

**Continue Under New Authority → Standby → Restricted → Safe State → Terminate Employment**

The appropriate state should depend on whether another authorised mission or operational purpose exists.

Completion of one mission does not automatically authorise another mission.

---

## 8. Early Termination

Early termination may result from:

- mission objective change;
- environmental change;
- unacceptable risk;
- loss of human control;
- performance degradation;
- security concern;
- data-integrity concern;
- dependency failure;
- unexpected AI behaviour;
- autonomy concern;
- incident;
- command decision; or
- authorisation suspension or revocation.

The reason for early termination should be recorded.

---

## 9. Contingency Closeout

Where employment occurs under contingency conditions, closeout should specifically verify:

- contingency state entered;
- authority used;
- autonomy level;
- human interventions;
- fail-safe activity;
- degraded/disconnected operation;
- deviations from normal conditions;
- incidents;
- recovery actions; and
- whether additional assurance is required.

---

## 10. Autonomy Termination

Where the capability has operated at an authorised autonomy level, closeout should verify:

- current autonomy;
- final autonomy transition;
- human supervision;
- intervention or override events;
- autonomous consequential actions, where authorised;
- safe termination of autonomous activity; and
- confirmation that no unauthorised autonomous activity remains active.

---

## 11. Human Authority at Closeout

Human authority should remain explicit until operational employment has ended.

The responsible authority should determine:

- when AI activity ends;
- whether the capability may transition to standby;
- whether another authority assumes responsibility;
- whether the capability can be reused;
- whether restrictions apply; and
- whether further authorisation is required.

---

## 12. Post-Mission Configuration

The post-mission configuration should be controlled.

Where applicable, the organisation should verify:

- system state;
- model/system version;
- operational configuration;
- temporary settings;
- mission-specific parameters;
- data handling state;
- security state;
- stored outputs;
- pending updates; and
- rollback requirements.

Temporary operational changes should not become the default configuration without appropriate governance.

---

## 13. Data and Information Closeout

Post-mission handling should address:

- operational data;
- AI inputs;
- AI outputs;
- decisions and recommendations;
- mission records;
- sensor or information products where applicable;
- provenance;
- retention;
- access;
- security;
- privacy;
- integrity; and
- disposal requirements.

Data required for assurance, audit or investigation should be preserved according to applicable policy.

---

## 14. Decision and Action Records

Consequential decisions and actions should be reviewed for completeness.

The closeout record should support reconstruction of:

**Operational Context → AI Contribution → Human Assessment/Authority → Decision → Action → Outcome**

This should include material interventions, overrides, rejected recommendations and authorised autonomous actions where applicable.

---

## 15. Incident Identification

Closeout should provide an opportunity to identify incidents or anomalies that may not have been recognised during active employment.

Examples include:

- unexpected AI behaviour;
- incorrect or misleading outputs;
- excessive uncertainty;
- human-control issues;
- autonomy anomalies;
- security events;
- data-integrity problems;
- environmental boundary events;
- dependency failures;
- performance degradation; or
- near misses.

Unresolved concerns should be escalated rather than closed administratively.

---

## 16. Performance Review

Where appropriate, mission closeout should compare actual performance with authorised expectations.

The review may consider:

- mission effectiveness;
- technical performance;
- reliability;
- robustness;
- uncertainty;
- human-AI interaction;
- autonomy behaviour;
- environmental performance;
- security;
- data quality; and
- operational outcomes.

The purpose is not simply to determine whether the mission succeeded, but whether the AI capability behaved within its authorised assurance basis.

---

## 17. Residual Risk

Closeout should identify risks that remain after employment ends.

These may include:

- unresolved technical issues;
- unclosed security concerns;
- data risks;
- configuration concerns;
- supplier/dependency issues;
- lessons indicating assurance gaps;
- environmental limitations;
- human factors findings; or
- risks affecting future missions.

Residual risks should have an owner and treatment where appropriate.

---

## 18. Evidence Preservation

Closeout should preserve evidence necessary for:

- operational accountability;
- audit;
- incident investigation;
- TEVV;
- continuous assurance;
- future authorisation;
- lessons learned; and
- legal or policy requirements.

Evidence should remain attributable, protected and traceable.

---

## 19. Closeout Review

A closeout review should determine:

1. Was the mission completed as authorised?
2. Did employment remain within the authorised scope?
3. Did autonomy remain within approved limits?
4. Was human authority maintained?
5. Did the environment remain within the authorised envelope?
6. Were there material incidents?
7. Did unexpected AI behaviour occur?
8. Did performance meet authorised expectations?
9. Were there material configuration changes?
10. Were security and data-integrity requirements maintained?
11. Were all consequential actions traceable?
12. What residual risks remain?
13. What evidence was generated?
14. What lessons should enter continuous assurance?
15. Does the capability remain suitable for future employment?

---

## 20. Closeout Outcomes

Possible outcomes include:

- **C1 Closed — No Material Findings**
- **C2 Closed with Lessons**
- **C3 Closed with Corrective Actions**
- **C4 Additional Assurance Required**
- **C5 Revalidation Required**
- **C6 Reauthorisation Required**
- **C7 Restricted for Future Employment**
- **C8 Suspended Pending Investigation**

The outcome should be based on evidence rather than mission success alone.

---

## 21. Future Employment

Mission completion should establish whether the capability may proceed to another authorised use.

Possible pathways include:

**Closeout → Ready for Future Authorised Mission**

or:

**Closeout → Additional Assurance → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Future Employment**

A successful mission does not automatically validate future missions, environments or autonomy levels.

---

## 22. Mission-to-Mission Transition

Where a capability moves directly from one authorised mission to another, the organisation should verify:

- new mission authority;
- applicable use case;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- configuration;
- conditions;
- readiness;
- evidence currency; and
- dependencies.

The completion of Mission A should not be treated as implicit authority for Mission B.

---

## 23. Closeout Following Suspension or Revocation

Where employment ends because authority was suspended or revoked, closeout should preserve:

- reason;
- authority;
- effective time;
- affected scope;
- actions taken;
- system state;
- evidence;
- incident information;
- investigation status; and
- restoration or retirement requirements.

Such a closeout should not be treated as a normal successful mission completion.

---

## 24. Closeout Following Authority Transfer

Where responsibility transfers rather than employment ends, the closeout should distinguish between:

**Mission Closeout**

and:

**Authority Transfer / Continued Employment**

The records should establish whether the original authority ended, was transferred, or was superseded.

---

## 25. Closeout Record

A Mission Closeout Record should include, as applicable:

- mission identifier;
- capability;
- use case;
- authorisation identifier;
- authority;
- start and end time;
- environment;
- configuration;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- mission outcome;
- consequential actions;
- incidents;
- interventions;
- performance findings;
- security findings;
- data status;
- residual risk;
- evidence;
- lessons;
- corrective actions;
- future-use decision;
- closeout authority; and
- date/time of closure.

---

## 26. Closeout Ownership

The organisation should define responsibility for:

- mission closeout;
- operational records;
- incident review;
- performance review;
- evidence preservation;
- residual risk;
- corrective actions;
- assurance follow-up; and
- future authorisation decisions.

Responsibility should remain identifiable after active employment ends.

---

## 27. Continuous Assurance Feedback

Mission closeout is an important assurance feedback point.

The process should feed relevant findings into:

- risk management;
- TEVV;
- operational environment assessment;
- human-AI interaction;
- autonomy assurance;
- security assurance;
- data governance;
- supply-chain assurance;
- training;
- system improvement;
- operational procedures; and
- future authorisation.

The cycle is:

**Employment → Outcome → Review → Evidence → Learning → Control Improvement**

---

## 28. Closeout and Change

If closeout identifies material changes required to the capability, those changes should enter the appropriate lifecycle and change-control processes.

They should not be introduced informally under the assumption that the mission has already ended.

---

## 29. Governance Records

Closeout records should link to:

- Operational Authorisation Record;
- Operational Readiness Record;
- Deployment Record;
- Operational Employment Record;
- Operational Decision and Action Traceability Record;
- Incident Record;
- Assurance Record;
- Change Register; and
- Lessons Learned records.

This maintains traceability across the lifecycle.

---

## 30. Golden Thread

Mission closeout remains connected to the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Readiness → Deployment → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Closeout → Revalidation/Reauthorisation**

---

## 31. Governance Questions

Responsible authorities should be able to determine:

1. Has the authorised mission actually ended?
2. When did AI employment end?
3. Who ended the employment?
4. What was the final autonomy state?
5. Was human authority maintained through termination?
6. Did the capability remain within authorised boundaries?
7. What consequential actions occurred?
8. Were all material actions traceable?
9. Did any incidents or anomalies occur?
10. Did AI behaviour differ materially from expectations?
11. Did performance meet authorised expectations?
12. What configuration was used?
13. What evidence was generated?
14. What residual risks remain?
15. What corrective actions are required?
16. Does additional assurance need to be performed?
17. Is revalidation required?
18. Is reauthorisation required?
19. Is the capability suitable for another mission?
20. Has the mission been formally closed?

---

## 32. Core Rule

> **A Defence AI mission shall have a controlled closeout process that establishes when authorised employment has ended, preserves human accountability and operational records, identifies incidents and residual risk, captures assurance evidence and determines whether additional assurance, revalidation, reauthorisation, restriction or suspension is required before future employment. Completion of one mission shall not create implied authority for another mission.**
