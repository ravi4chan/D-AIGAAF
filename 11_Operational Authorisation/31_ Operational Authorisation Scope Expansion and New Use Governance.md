# 31 Operational Authorisation Scope Expansion and New Use Governance

## 1. Purpose

A Defence AI capability may initially be authorised for a limited mission, environment, autonomy level or user population and later be considered for broader employment.

Expansion may involve a new mission, additional functions, wider geography, new users, different environmental conditions, higher autonomy, new data sources or integration with other systems.

Such expansion can materially change the assurance basis.

This document defines governance for controlled expansion of an existing operational authorisation.

The objective is to ensure that:

- existing authority is not treated as blanket authority;
- proposed expansion is explicitly identified;
- additional risks are assessed;
- new assurance evidence is generated where required;
- human authority remains appropriate;
- boundaries are clearly defined;
- scope expansion does not occur through informal operational practice; and
- the resulting authority is formally recorded.

---

## 2. Core Principle

**Operational authority is not transferable from a narrower use to a broader use merely because the underlying AI capability is unchanged.**

Expansion of scope shall be assessed against the changed mission, environment, autonomy, human authority, configuration, dependencies and risk.

---

## 3. Scope Expansion Object

The assessment should compare:

**Existing Authorisation → Proposed Scope → Delta → Risk → Assurance → Decision**

The scope delta should identify what is changing and why the existing assurance basis may or may not remain sufficient.

---

## 4. Types of Scope Expansion

Expansion may involve:

- new mission;
- new use case;
- additional operational function;
- new geographic area;
- new environment;
- wider environmental envelope;
- additional users;
- additional organisational units;
- higher autonomy;
- longer operating duration;
- new data sources;
- new interfaces;
- additional platforms;
- new external dependencies;
- increased consequence; or
- broader decision authority.

Multiple changes should be assessed together where they interact.

---

## 5. New Mission

A new mission should normally be treated as a new authorisation question where it changes:

- mission objective;
- decision context;
- consequence;
- operational environment;
- required autonomy;
- human authority;
- data;
- system behaviour; or
- acceptable risk.

A successful previous mission does not establish automatic authority for a materially different mission.

---

## 6. New Use Case

A use case expansion should identify:

- purpose;
- users;
- inputs;
- outputs;
- decisions influenced;
- actions enabled;
- operational context;
- consequences;
- human authority;
- autonomy;
- limitations; and
- prohibited uses.

The use case should be assessed against the D-AIGAAF mission and risk framework.

---

## 7. Geographic Expansion

A wider geographic area may introduce changes in:

- terrain;
- weather;
- illumination;
- electromagnetic conditions;
- communications;
- navigation;
- infrastructure;
- population or human context;
- adversarial conditions; and
- legal or policy requirements.

Geographic expansion should therefore be assessed as an environmental and operational change where relevant.

---

## 8. Environmental Expansion

Expansion beyond a demonstrated operating envelope should require appropriate evidence.

The organisation should assess:

- environmental assumptions;
- sensor performance;
- communications;
- data availability;
- navigation;
- computing;
- human factors;
- adversarial conditions;
- degraded operation; and
- fail-safe behaviour.

Untested environments should not be treated as demonstrated merely because the system performed adequately elsewhere.

---

## 9. Autonomy Expansion

An increase in autonomy may materially increase consequence and reduce the frequency of direct human intervention.

The organisation should assess:

- current autonomy;
- proposed autonomy;
- decision authority;
- action authority;
- supervision;
- intervention;
- override;
- uncertainty;
- failure behaviour;
- loss-of-control scenarios; and
- additional TEVV requirements.

Higher autonomy should require proportionately stronger assurance and authority.

---

## 10. Human Authority Expansion

Where more users or commanders are permitted to employ the capability, the organisation should assess:

- competence;
- AI literacy;
- decision rights;
- supervision;
- workload;
- situational awareness;
- intervention capability;
- accountability; and
- training.

Wider access should not dilute accountability.

---

## 11. Functional Expansion

Adding a new function may change risk even if the underlying model remains unchanged.

Examples include expansion from:

**Observation → Analysis → Recommendation → Action Support**

or from one operational function to another.

Each additional function should be assessed for its effect on consequence, autonomy, human control, security and mission risk.

---

## 12. Data Expansion

New data sources may change:

- data quality;
- provenance;
- representativeness;
- distribution;
- uncertainty;
- bias;
- integrity;
- security; and
- model behaviour.

The organisation should determine whether additional data changes require data assurance, TEVV, revalidation or reauthorisation.

---

## 13. Interface Expansion

Adding a new interface or connected system may introduce:

- new dependencies;
- new trust boundaries;
- new information flows;
- new failure modes;
- new security risks;
- autonomy composition; or
- new operational effects.

Cross-system governance should be applied where material.

---

## 14. Configuration Expansion

Where scope expansion requires a new configuration, the organisation should identify:

- approved configuration;
- changed components;
- changed parameters;
- changed model/system version;
- assurance evidence;
- deployment status;
- rollback capability; and
- applicable authorisation.

The expanded configuration should not be treated as authorised until the applicable governance decision is complete.

---

## 15. Risk Assessment

Scope expansion should reassess:

- consequence;
- mission criticality;
- autonomy;
- environment;
- human control;
- security;
- data;
- dependencies;
- operational effectiveness;
- failure modes;
- uncertainty; and
- residual risk.

The assessment should identify whether risk remains within previously accepted boundaries.

---

## 16. Assurance Gap Analysis

The organisation should compare existing evidence with evidence required for the expanded scope.

The gap analysis should consider:

- technical performance;
- reliability;
- robustness;
- adversarial resilience;
- operational environment;
- human-AI interaction;
- security;
- autonomy and control;
- mission effectiveness; and
- other applicable assurance domains.

The absence of a known failure should not be treated as evidence of demonstrated performance in the expanded scope.

---

## 17. TEVV for Expansion

Additional TEVV should be proportionate to the scope delta.

It may include:

- targeted testing;
- environmental trials;
- adversarial testing;
- human-AI evaluation;
- autonomy testing;
- security evaluation;
- mission effectiveness testing;
- interface testing;
- degraded-operation testing; and
- operational trials.

The objective is to demonstrate that the expanded capability remains sufficiently understood and controlled.

---

## 18. Human-Control Assessment

Expansion should verify whether existing human-control arrangements remain adequate.

Questions include:

- Can the responsible human understand the new use?
- Can they intervene?
- Can they override?
- Can they recognise uncertainty?
- Is workload acceptable?
- Is situational awareness maintained?
- Is the decision authority still appropriate?

---

## 19. Operational AI Advisor

Where scope expansion introduces substantial operational or AI complexity, the Operational AI Advisor may support the assessment by connecting:

**Mission Context ↔ AI Capability ↔ Risk ↔ Assurance ↔ Human Authority**

The OAIA remains advisory unless separately assigned formal authority.

---

## 20. Expansion Decision Outcomes

Possible outcomes include:

- **E1 — No Expansion Required**
- **E2 — Expansion Approved Within Existing Authority**
- **E3 — Conditional Expansion**
- **E4 — Additional Assurance Required**
- **E5 — Revalidation Required**
- **E6 — Reauthorisation Required**
- **E7 — Expansion Not Approved**
- **E8 — Existing Authority Restricted or Suspended**

The outcome should be recorded with its rationale.

---

## 21. Conditional Expansion

Conditional expansion may impose:

- restricted environments;
- reduced autonomy;
- additional supervision;
- limited users;
- limited functions;
- increased monitoring;
- additional testing;
- shorter validity;
- additional reporting; or
- defined exit criteria.

Conditions should form part of the authorisation record.

---

## 22. Temporary Expansion

Temporary scope expansion may be considered where there is a defined operational need.

It should specify:

- purpose;
- scope;
- authority;
- duration;
- conditions;
- safeguards;
- evidence;
- monitoring;
- termination criteria; and
- post-use review.

Temporary expansion should not become permanent through repeated informal use.

---

## 23. Emergency Expansion

Emergency conditions may require accelerated decision-making.

However:

**Emergency Need ≠ Unrestricted AI Authority**

Emergency expansion should use defined emergency authority and protective procedures.

Where delay creates unacceptable risk, pre-authorised emergency actions may be used within established boundaries, with subsequent review and documentation.

---

## 24. Scope Expansion During Employment

A request to expand scope during active employment should be treated carefully.

The organisation should determine whether the request represents:

- routine operation within existing authority;
- a permitted transition;
- a temporary restriction change;
- a material scope expansion; or
- a new authorisation requirement.

The capability should not begin expanded activity merely because operational circumstances make it desirable.

---

## 25. Scope Expansion and Mission Success

Previous mission success may provide useful evidence, but it does not automatically demonstrate:

- different mission performance;
- different environmental performance;
- higher autonomy;
- different human-control arrangements;
- new data performance;
- new security properties; or
- new system interactions.

Evidence should be relevant to the proposed scope.

---

## 26. Scope Expansion and Dependencies

Expansion may introduce new dependencies.

These should be assessed for:

- criticality;
- availability;
- security;
- integrity;
- continuity;
- supplier dependence;
- failure modes; and
- fallback.

A new critical dependency may change the operational authorisation basis.

---

## 27. Scope Expansion and Legal/Policy Requirements

Expansion should consider applicable:

- law;
- policy;
- rules;
- organisational authorities;
- information-handling requirements;
- procurement conditions;
- coalition arrangements; and
- other governance obligations.

A technically feasible expansion may still be unauthorised.

---

## 28. Scope Expansion Record

A controlled Scope Expansion Record should include:

- existing authorisation;
- proposed scope;
- scope delta;
- mission;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- configuration;
- dependencies;
- risk assessment;
- assurance gap analysis;
- TEVV evidence;
- conditions;
- decision;
- authority;
- validity; and
- review requirements.

---

## 29. Authorisation Amendment or Reauthorisation

The governance decision should determine whether expansion requires:

**No Change → Authorisation Amendment → Revalidation → Reauthorisation**

The determination should be evidence-based and proportionate to the change.

---

## 30. Monitoring Expanded Scope

Approved expansion should receive appropriate monitoring.

Monitoring should identify:

- performance;
- unexpected behaviour;
- uncertainty;
- human-control issues;
- environmental issues;
- security;
- data;
- dependencies;
- incidents; and
- mission effectiveness.

Material deterioration should trigger the applicable governance response.

---

## 31. Withdrawal of Expanded Scope

Where evidence becomes insufficient or risk increases, the organisation may:

- remove the expanded scope;
- reduce autonomy;
- restrict environments;
- restrict users;
- increase supervision;
- suspend the expanded function; or
- suspend the underlying capability.

Withdrawal should be recorded.

---

## 32. Governance Review

Scope-expansion decisions should be periodically reviewed to determine:

- whether the expanded use remains justified;
- whether assumptions remain valid;
- whether evidence remains current;
- whether conditions remain effective;
- whether incidents have occurred;
- whether new risks have emerged; and
- whether continued authorisation remains appropriate.

---

## 33. Relationship to Continuous Assurance

Scope expansion follows the control loop:

**Proposed Expansion → Scope Delta → Risk → Assurance Gap → TEVV → Decision → Authorisation → Monitoring → Review**

This ensures that expansion becomes part of the continuous governance lifecycle.

---

## 34. Golden Thread

Scope expansion remains connected to the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Readiness → Deployment → Employment → Monitoring → Change → Scope Assessment → Revalidation/Reauthorisation**

---

## 35. Governance Questions

Responsible authorities should be able to determine:

1. What existing authority applies?
2. What exactly is being expanded?
3. Is the mission changing?
4. Is the environment changing?
5. Is the autonomy changing?
6. Is human authority changing?
7. Is the configuration changing?
8. Are new data sources involved?
9. Are new systems or dependencies involved?
10. Does consequence increase?
11. What assurance evidence already exists?
12. What assurance gaps remain?
13. What additional TEVV is required?
14. Does meaningful human control remain adequate?
15. What conditions or restrictions are required?
16. Is amendment sufficient?
17. Is revalidation required?
18. Is reauthorisation required?
19. Who approved the expansion?
20. When does the expanded authority expire or require review?

---

## 36. Core Rule

> **Expansion of Defence AI operational scope shall be treated as a governance decision, not as an automatic extension of existing authority. Any material change to mission, use case, environment, autonomy, human authority, configuration, data, dependencies or consequence shall be assessed against the existing assurance basis, with proportionate additional TEVV, revalidation or reauthorisation where required. No capability shall acquire broader operational authority merely because it has demonstrated acceptable performance within a narrower authorised scope.**
