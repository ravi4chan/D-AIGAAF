# 32 Operational Authorisation Risk Acceptance and Accountability

## 1. Purpose

Operational authorisation for Defence AI may require acceptance of residual risk after controls, testing and assurance have been completed.

Risk acceptance is therefore a governance decision, not a technical declaration that risk has been eliminated.

This document defines how residual risk associated with operational employment is assessed, accepted, rejected, escalated and reviewed.

The objective is to ensure that:

- residual risk is explicitly identified;
- the risk owner is identifiable;
- acceptance authority is appropriate to consequence;
- decision-makers understand relevant uncertainty and limitations;
- risk acceptance is linked to operational authorisation;
- conditions and controls accompany accepted risk;
- unacceptable risk is not normalised through repeated acceptance;
- changes in risk trigger review; and
- every material acceptance decision is traceable.

---

## 2. Core Principle

**No Defence AI capability is risk-free. Operational authority requires an explicit determination that residual risk is understood, controlled to the extent reasonably practicable, and acceptable to an appropriately authorised human authority for the defined mission and conditions.**

---

## 3. Residual Risk

Residual risk is the risk remaining after applicable controls, safeguards, assurance activities and mitigations have been applied.

The risk assessment should distinguish:

- inherent risk;
- treated risk;
- residual risk;
- emerging risk; and
- unknown or insufficiently characterised risk.

Unknown risk should not automatically be treated as low risk.

---

## 4. Risk Acceptance Object

Risk acceptance should be considered in the context of:

**AI Capability × Mission × Environment × Autonomy × Human Authority × Configuration**

Risk acceptance should therefore remain linked to the specific operational circumstances for which authority is requested.

---

## 5. Risk Owner

Every material operational risk should have an identifiable risk owner.

The risk owner should have:

- appropriate authority;
- sufficient competence;
- understanding of the mission;
- understanding of AI limitations;
- understanding of residual risk;
- access to relevant evidence; and
- responsibility for ensuring that accepted risk remains within approved boundaries.

Technical ownership and risk ownership should not be assumed to be the same role.

---

## 6. Risk Acceptance Authority

Risk acceptance authority should be proportionate to:

- consequence;
- mission criticality;
- autonomy;
- human-control requirements;
- environmental complexity;
- uncertainty;
- operational scope;
- security implications; and
- potential impact on human life, property or mission outcomes.

A person should not accept risk beyond the authority delegated to them.

---

## 7. Risk Acceptance Criteria

Risk acceptance should consider whether:

- the intended mission is clearly defined;
- material risks are identified;
- controls have been implemented;
- evidence is sufficient;
- limitations are understood;
- uncertainty is appropriately communicated;
- human control is meaningful;
- fail-safe arrangements are credible;
- operational boundaries are defined;
- residual risk is proportionate to mission need; and
- appropriate authority is available.

---

## 8. Risk Acceptance Categories

A working D-AIGAAF model may use:

- **RA1 — Acceptable**
- **RA2 — Acceptable with Conditions**
- **RA3 — Additional Assurance Required**
- **RA4 — Escalated Acceptance Required**
- **RA5 — Not Acceptable**

These categories should be mapped to applicable organisational risk policies before formal adoption.

---

## 9. Consequence and Risk

Risk acceptance should account for consequence, not merely probability.

Particular attention should be given where AI-supported decisions may affect:

- human life;
- physical safety;
- critical infrastructure;
- essential services;
- operational mission outcomes;
- sensitive information;
- security;
- property; or
- other consequential interests.

Low observed frequency of failure should not by itself justify acceptance of high-consequence failure modes.

---

## 10. Autonomy and Risk Acceptance

Increasing autonomy may increase:

- speed of action;
- scale of effect;
- difficulty of intervention;
- consequence of error;
- dependence on system behaviour;
- difficulty of human understanding; and
- potential loss of control.

Risk acceptance should therefore explicitly consider the authorised autonomy level.

A capability should not receive higher autonomy solely because it performs adequately at a lower autonomy level.

---

## 11. Human-Control Risk

Risk acceptance should consider whether human control remains meaningful.

Relevant questions include:

- Can the human understand the AI contribution?
- Can they identify important uncertainty?
- Can they intervene?
- Can they override?
- Is sufficient time available?
- Is workload acceptable?
- Is situational awareness maintained?
- Is the authority clearly identified?
- Can the human reject or bypass the AI recommendation?

Loss of meaningful human control may require immediate restriction, reduced autonomy, safe state or suspension.

---

## 12. Evidence Supporting Acceptance

Risk acceptance should be based on relevant evidence, including:

- TEVV results;
- operational environment assessments;
- security assessments;
- human-AI evaluation;
- autonomy and control evaluation;
- mission effectiveness evidence;
- incident history;
- performance monitoring;
- configuration records;
- data assurance;
- supplier/dependency assurance; and
- lessons learned.

Evidence should be current enough to support the decision.

---

## 13. Evidence Limitations

Decision-makers should be informed where evidence is:

- incomplete;
- indirect;
- outdated;
- contradictory;
- environment-specific;
- based on limited trials;
- based on assumptions; or
- unavailable.

A lack of evidence should be visible in the risk acceptance decision.

---

## 14. Uncertainty

AI systems should communicate material uncertainty and limitations in a manner appropriate to the operational context.

Risk acceptance should consider:

- model uncertainty;
- data uncertainty;
- environmental uncertainty;
- sensor uncertainty;
- prediction uncertainty;
- distribution shift;
- ambiguity;
- confidence calibration; and
- unknown or poorly characterised conditions.

High apparent confidence should not be treated as evidence of correctness.

---

## 15. Compensating Controls

Where residual risk is higher than normally preferred but operational need justifies continued consideration, compensating controls may include:

- reduced autonomy;
- additional human supervision;
- restricted mission scope;
- restricted environment;
- additional monitoring;
- limited users;
- shorter validity;
- additional testing;
- redundant information sources;
- enhanced intervention capability; or
- predefined suspension criteria.

Compensating controls should be measurable and enforceable where practicable.

---

## 16. Conditional Risk Acceptance

Conditional acceptance should specify:

- accepted risk;
- conditions;
- authority;
- duration;
- scope;
- controls;
- monitoring;
- review date;
- trigger conditions; and
- withdrawal criteria.

A condition that cannot be satisfied should invalidate the basis for continued acceptance where it is material.

---

## 17. Escalated Risk Acceptance

Escalation should occur where:

- consequence exceeds delegated authority;
- autonomy increases;
- uncertainty is material;
- assurance evidence is incomplete;
- human control is disputed;
- security implications are significant;
- mission scope expands;
- risk is cross-organisational; or
- the risk falls outside normal acceptance criteria.

Escalation should preserve existing operational restrictions while the decision is pending.

---

## 18. Risk Acceptance and Authorisation

Risk acceptance does not itself create operational authority.

The relationship is:

**Risk Assessment → Risk Treatment → Residual Risk → Risk Acceptance → Assurance Decision → Operational Authorisation**

An accepted risk should be reflected in the Operational Authorisation Record where material.

---

## 19. Risk Acceptance and Exceptions

An exception or derogation does not automatically justify risk acceptance.

Where an exception affects risk, the organisation should assess:

- why the exception is necessary;
- additional risk created;
- compensating controls;
- authority;
- duration;
- monitoring; and
- whether reauthorisation is required.

---

## 20. Risk Acceptance During Dynamic Conditions

Risk may change during employment because of:

- environmental change;
- mission change;
- system degradation;
- new information;
- adversarial activity;
- security events;
- data integrity issues;
- human availability;
- dependency failure;
- unexpected AI behaviour; or
- increased consequence.

Operational employment should respond according to predefined thresholds.

Possible responses include:

**Continue → Restrict → Reduce Autonomy → Human Control → Contingency → Safe State → Suspend**

---

## 21. Emerging Risk

New evidence may reveal risks that were not known when authorisation was granted.

Emerging risk should trigger proportionate action such as:

- increased monitoring;
- additional assessment;
- temporary restrictions;
- targeted testing;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation; or
- suspension.

Previous acceptance should not prevent reconsideration when the risk basis changes.

---

## 22. Unknown Risk

Where risk cannot be adequately characterised, the organisation should distinguish:

**Known Acceptable Risk**

from:

**Unknown or Insufficiently Characterised Risk**

Unknown risk should receive proportionate controls and assurance rather than being treated as negligible by default.

---

## 23. Risk Acceptance for Autonomous Actions

Where authorised autonomy permits AI to initiate consequential actions, risk acceptance should explicitly address:

- action authority;
- permitted action types;
- boundaries;
- supervision;
- intervention;
- timing;
- failure modes;
- uncertainty;
- safe-state behaviour;
- monitoring;
- traceability; and
- suspension criteria.

The human authority responsible for accepting the risk should remain identifiable.

---

## 24. Risk Acceptance and Fail-Safe

Risk acceptance should verify that fail-safe arrangements are proportionate to consequence.

The organisation should understand:

- what triggers protective action;
- what the system does;
- who can intervene;
- what authority exists during failure;
- how safe state is achieved;
- how recovery occurs; and
- whether failure of the fail-safe itself is addressed.

---

## 25. Risk Acceptance and Security

Cybersecurity or AI security risk may directly affect operational risk.

Acceptance should consider:

- compromise;
- adversarial manipulation;
- data poisoning;
- model manipulation;
- integrity failure;
- unauthorised changes;
- supply-chain compromise;
- loss of monitoring; and
- security dependency failure.

Material security uncertainty should not be hidden within generic operational risk.

---

## 26. Risk Acceptance and Dependencies

Critical external dependencies may create residual operational risk.

Assessment should consider:

- dependency criticality;
- failure probability;
- consequence;
- fallback;
- continuity;
- supplier assurance;
- change control;
- external updates; and
- restoration.

Dependency risk should remain visible to the authority accepting operational risk.

---

## 27. Risk Acceptance Record

A controlled Risk Acceptance Record should include:

- risk identifier;
- capability;
- mission;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- configuration;
- risk description;
- consequence;
- likelihood where applicable;
- inherent risk;
- controls;
- residual risk;
- evidence;
- uncertainty;
- compensating controls;
- risk owner;
- accepting authority;
- conditions;
- validity;
- review date;
- withdrawal triggers; and
- decision rationale.

---

## 28. Accountability

The person accepting residual risk should be identifiable.

The record should establish:

**Risk → Evidence → Assessment → Authority → Acceptance Decision → Conditions → Operational Use**

Acceptance should not be attributed generically to an organisation where a defined individual or role is required.

---

## 29. No Silent Risk Acceptance

Risk should not become accepted merely because:

- the system is already deployed;
- the capability has been used previously;
- no incident has yet occurred;
- users continue to operate it;
- an exception has been repeatedly extended;
- a supplier states that the system is safe; or
- an AI system reports acceptable confidence.

Continued operation is not equivalent to explicit risk acceptance.

---

## 30. Disagreement on Risk Acceptance

Where technical, operational, assurance or command authorities disagree:

- the disagreement should be recorded;
- the relevant evidence should be identified;
- the appropriate authority should resolve or escalate the issue;
- existing operational restrictions should remain enforceable; and
- immediate protective action should remain available.

Disagreement should not create implied permission to continue higher-risk employment.

---

## 31. Review and Reacceptance

Risk acceptance should be reviewed when:

- authorisation is renewed;
- material changes occur;
- new evidence emerges;
- incidents occur;
- environmental assumptions change;
- autonomy changes;
- human-control arrangements change;
- security conditions change;
- dependencies change; or
- residual risk materially changes.

Reacceptance should not be automatic.

---

## 32. Withdrawal of Risk Acceptance

Risk acceptance may be withdrawn where:

- controls fail;
- assumptions become invalid;
- evidence is contradicted;
- consequence increases;
- risk exceeds authority;
- meaningful human control is lost;
- security is compromised;
- monitoring becomes inadequate; or
- new evidence makes the previous decision untenable.

Withdrawal should trigger proportionate operational action.

---

## 33. Risk Acceptance and Continuous Assurance

The governance cycle is:

**Monitor → Detect Change → Reassess Risk → Review Evidence → Accept/Reject → Adjust Authority → Monitor**

This ensures that risk acceptance remains connected to actual operational conditions.

---

## 34. Golden Thread

Risk acceptance remains connected to the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Reassessment → Reauthorisation**

---

## 35. Governance Questions

Responsible authorities should be able to determine:

1. What residual risk remains?
2. Who owns the risk?
3. Who has authority to accept it?
4. What evidence supports the assessment?
5. What evidence is missing or uncertain?
6. What assumptions underpin the decision?
7. What controls reduce the risk?
8. What compensating controls are required?
9. Does autonomy increase the consequence?
10. Does meaningful human control remain?
11. What uncertainty is material?
12. Are security and dependency risks included?
13. What conditions apply?
14. When does acceptance expire?
15. What events trigger reassessment?
16. What happens if the risk increases?
17. Can the acceptance be withdrawn?
18. Is the decision linked to operational authorisation?
19. Can the decision be reconstructed?
20. Has the accepting authority been clearly identified?

---

## 36. Core Rule

> **Residual risk associated with Defence AI operational employment shall be explicitly assessed, attributable to an identified risk owner and accepted only by an appropriately authorised human authority. Risk acceptance shall be based on relevant evidence, transparent uncertainty, defined controls and known operational boundaries. Acceptance shall not create authority beyond the approved mission, environment, autonomy, human-control arrangements, configuration or conditions, and shall be reconsidered whenever material evidence, risk or operating conditions change.**
