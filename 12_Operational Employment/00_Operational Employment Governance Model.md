# 00 — Operational Employment Governance Model

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the governance model for the operational employment of Defence AI capabilities after operational authorisation has been granted.

Operational employment is the stage at which an AI capability is actually used to support, influence or perform authorised functions in an operational context.

The purpose of this module is to ensure that operational use remains within the basis on which authority was granted and that human authority, operational control, situational awareness, autonomy, boundaries, monitoring and traceability remain effective during employment.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **A Defence AI capability shall be employed only within its authorised mission, environment, autonomy, human authority, configuration, conditions and operational boundaries, with continuous human oversight and monitoring proportionate to consequence and autonomy.**

Operational employment does not create new authority.

---

## 3. Employment versus Authorisation

D-AIGAAF distinguishes:

**Operational Authorisation** — permission to employ the capability under defined conditions.

**Operational Readiness** — confirmation that the capability is currently suitable for the intended employment.

**Operational Deployment** — controlled transition of the capability into its operational setting.

**Operational Employment** — actual use of the capability.

A capability may be:

- authorised but not ready;
- ready but not deployed;
- deployed but not currently employed;
- employed under restrictions.

---

## 4. Employment Object

Operational employment should be governed as:

**AI Capability × Mission × Environment × Autonomy × Human Authority × Configuration × Conditions**

This ensures that employment remains connected to the specific authorisation basis.

---

## 5. Employment States

D-AIGAAF uses the following working employment states:

- **E0 — Not Employed**
- **E1 — Planned Employment**
- **E2 — Active Employment**
- **E3 — Restricted Employment**
- **E4 — Degraded Employment**
- **E5 — Contingency Employment**
- **E6 — Safe State**
- **E7 — Suspended**
- **E8 — Terminated**

Transitions between material states should be governed and recorded where required.

---

## 6. Employment Planning

Before employment, the responsible authority should establish:

- mission;
- intended AI function;
- authorised configuration;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- applicable conditions;
- operational boundaries;
- monitoring;
- contingency arrangements;
- intervention mechanisms;
- required competence.

Employment planning should not expand the existing authorisation without appropriate governance.

---

## 7. Employment Execution

During employment, personnel should ensure that:

- the capability remains within authorised scope;
- the approved configuration is used;
- required human authority remains available;
- autonomy remains within authorised limits;
- operational boundaries remain understood;
- monitoring remains active;
- material AI uncertainty remains visible;
- intervention remains available where required;
- consequential decisions and actions remain traceable.

---

## 8. Human Authority

Operational employment shall preserve clearly identifiable human authority.

The responsible human authority should understand:

- what the AI is doing;
- what the AI is permitted to do;
- what the AI is not permitted to do;
- relevant limitations;
- material uncertainty;
- current autonomy;
- current environmental conditions;
- available intervention options.

Human presence alone should not be treated as meaningful control.

---

## 9. AI as Advisor versus Actor

D-AIGAAF distinguishes:

### Advisory AI

AI provides:

- information;
- analysis;
- prediction;
- recommendation;
- decision support.

The human makes the consequential decision.

### Authorised Autonomous Function

The AI may perform defined actions within an explicitly authorised autonomy level and boundary.

The authority for such autonomy must already exist through operational authorisation.

A system should not transition from advisory behaviour to consequential autonomous action without appropriate authority.

---

## 10. Operational Situational Awareness

AI may contribute to situational awareness through:

- information aggregation;
- detection;
- classification;
- analysis;
- prediction;
- anomaly identification;
- decision support.

However, AI-generated situational awareness should be treated as an input to operational judgement rather than an unquestionable representation of reality.

Material uncertainty, conflicting information and missing information should be visible.

---

## 11. Uncertainty

During employment, the system should communicate material uncertainty appropriately.

Operational personnel should be able to distinguish:

- observed information;
- inferred information;
- predicted information;
- recommendation;
- uncertainty;
- missing information.

Where uncertainty materially affects a consequential decision, the issue should be escalated or subject to the applicable human review requirement.

---

## 12. Operational Autonomy

Autonomy should remain within the authorised level.

Working D-AIGAAF autonomy levels are:

- **A0 — No Meaningful AI Decision**
- **A1 — Information / Observation**
- **A2 — Analysis / Recommendation**
- **A3 — Human-Authorised Action**
- **A4 — Supervised Autonomous Action**
- **A5 — Independent Consequential Autonomy**

These are working governance constructs and should be mapped to applicable national, defence, legal, doctrinal and international terminology.

---

## 13. Dynamic Conditions

Operational conditions may change during employment.

Changes may involve:

- mission;
- environment;
- communications;
- sensors;
- navigation;
- information;
- AI behaviour;
- human availability;
- autonomy;
- security;
- dependencies;
- consequence.

The response should be proportionate:

**Continue → Monitor → Restrict → Reduce Autonomy → Human Control → Contingency → Safe State → Suspend**

Changing conditions should not create implied AI authority.

---

## 14. Operational Boundaries

Employment should remain within defined:

- mission boundaries;
- functional boundaries;
- environmental boundaries;
- geographic boundaries;
- temporal boundaries;
- autonomy boundaries;
- human-authority boundaries;
- configuration boundaries;
- data boundaries;
- dependency boundaries.

Technical capability beyond these boundaries does not constitute permission to use it.

---

## 15. Human-AI Interaction

The operational interface should support:

- understanding;
- appropriate trust;
- uncertainty awareness;
- intervention;
- rejection;
- escalation;
- workload management;
- situational awareness.

The organisation should monitor for:

- automation bias;
- over-reliance;
- inappropriate deference;
- alert overload;
- loss of situational awareness;
- misunderstanding of AI outputs.

---

## 16. Operational Intervention

Personnel should have defined mechanisms to:

- pause;
- restrict;
- reduce autonomy;
- override where authorised;
- transition to human control;
- enter a safe state;
- suspend employment.

Intervention mechanisms should be proportionate to the consequence and autonomy of the capability.

---

## 17. Fail-Safe

Where applicable, the capability should have a defined fail-safe or safe-state mechanism.

The organisation should establish:

- activation conditions;
- authorised users;
- expected behaviour;
- limitations;
- recovery;
- post-event review.

Fail-safe mechanisms should be tested and maintained.

---

## 18. Degraded and Disconnected Employment

The capability may encounter:

- communications loss;
- sensor degradation;
- navigation uncertainty;
- data loss;
- computing degradation;
- dependency failure.

Employment should follow predefined degraded or contingency arrangements.

Disconnection should not automatically result in increased AI authority.

---

## 19. Adversarial Employment Conditions

Operational employment may involve deliberate attempts to:

- deceive sensors;
- manipulate inputs;
- corrupt data;
- disrupt communications;
- compromise systems;
- induce unsafe behaviour.

The organisation should maintain appropriate monitoring and protective responses.

---

## 20. Monitoring

Operational monitoring should cover, as applicable:

- technical performance;
- AI behaviour;
- mission effectiveness;
- uncertainty;
- autonomy;
- human control;
- environment;
- data integrity;
- security;
- dependencies;
- boundaries;
- incidents.

Monitoring should support timely human decisions.

---

## 21. Operational Performance

The organisation should distinguish:

**Technical Functioning** — whether the system is operating.

**AI Performance** — whether the AI is producing expected outputs.

**Mission Effectiveness** — whether the capability contributes effectively to its authorised purpose.

A technically functioning system may still be operationally unsuitable.

---

## 22. Operational Decision-Making

For consequential AI-supported decisions, the organisation should preserve:

**Context → AI Contribution → Human Assessment → Authority → Decision → Action → Outcome**

Where autonomous action is authorised, the record should identify the applicable autonomous process and authorisation basis.

---

## 23. Operational Records

Operational employment should generate appropriate records covering:

- employment period;
- mission;
- system/configuration;
- autonomy;
- environment;
- material AI contributions;
- consequential decisions;
- interventions;
- overrides;
- incidents;
- significant boundary events;
- outcomes.

Record requirements should be proportionate to consequence and applicable information-handling requirements.

---

## 24. Incident Management

Operational incidents should be handled through the applicable D-AIGAAF incident governance.

Potential triggers include:

- unexpected behaviour;
- loss of human control;
- boundary violation;
- security compromise;
- fail-safe failure;
- significant performance degradation;
- unauthorised autonomy;
- material environmental change.

Immediate protective action should remain available.

---

## 25. Change During Employment

Material changes should not be introduced merely because the system is already deployed.

Changes affecting:

- model;
- software;
- configuration;
- data;
- autonomy;
- environment;
- human control;
- dependencies;

should undergo the applicable change assessment.

---

## 26. Operational Employment Authority

The operational authority should retain the ability to:

- continue employment;
- impose restrictions;
- reduce autonomy;
- require human control;
- terminate employment;
- initiate suspension;
- escalate for revalidation or reauthorisation.

No AI system should determine its own operational authority.

---

## 27. Operational AI Advisor

The OAIA may support employment by providing advice on:

- AI behaviour;
- limitations;
- uncertainty;
- autonomy;
- environmental suitability;
- emerging risks;
- system performance;
- assurance implications.

The OAIA does not automatically replace command or operational authority.

---

## 28. Multiple AI Systems

Where multiple AI capabilities interact during employment, personnel should consider:

- combined outputs;
- recommendation chains;
- conflicting recommendations;
- shared data;
- shared dependencies;
- cascading failures;
- combined autonomy;
- emergent behaviour.

Individually authorised systems should not acquire additional authority merely through interaction.

---

## 29. Employment Continuity

Continuity arrangements should address:

- personnel changes;
- communication disruption;
- dependency failure;
- degraded infrastructure;
- environmental change;
- security events.

Continuity should preserve human authority and defined operational boundaries.

---

## 30. Employment Termination

Employment may end because:

- mission is complete;
- authorised period expires;
- conditions are no longer satisfied;
- capability becomes unsuitable;
- incident occurs;
- suspension is required;
- authority is withdrawn;
- configuration changes;
- environment leaves the authorised envelope.

Termination should be recorded where required.

---

## 31. Post-Employment Review

Where appropriate, post-employment review should assess:

- mission effectiveness;
- AI performance;
- human-AI interaction;
- uncertainty;
- autonomy;
- incidents;
- boundary events;
- environmental observations;
- security;
- lessons;
- residual risk.

Material findings should feed into continuous assurance and future authorisation decisions.

---

## 32. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. What is the capability currently being used for?
2. Is that use within its authorisation?
3. Is the current configuration authorised?
4. Is the current environment within the demonstrated envelope?
5. What autonomy is currently active?
6. Who holds operational authority?
7. Can the human intervene?
8. What does the AI know, infer or recommend?
9. What uncertainty exists?
10. Is situational awareness sufficient?
11. What happens if communications fail?
12. What happens if the AI behaves unexpectedly?
13. Can the capability be restricted or stopped?
14. Are material decisions and actions traceable?
15. Has anything changed that could require revalidation or reauthorisation?

---

## 33. Golden Thread

Operational employment remains connected to:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation**

This ensures that actual operational use remains connected to the evidence and authority supporting it.

---

## 34. Core Rule

> **Operational employment of Defence AI shall remain continuously within the mission, environment, autonomy, human authority, configuration, conditions and operational boundaries established through D-AIGAAF authorisation. During employment, human authority, situational awareness, uncertainty communication, autonomy control, intervention, monitoring, security and traceability shall remain effective and proportionate to consequence. Changes in operational conditions shall not create implied AI authority. Where the authorisation basis is materially weakened by unexpected behaviour, loss of human control, environmental change, security compromise, boundary violation, dependency failure or other significant conditions, the capability shall move through appropriate protective measures, including restriction, reduced autonomy, human control, safe state or suspension, followed by revalidation or reauthorisation where required.**
