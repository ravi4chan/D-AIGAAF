# 01 — Employment Planning and Preparation

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the planning and preparation requirements that should be completed before a Defence AI capability enters operational employment.

The objective is to ensure that an authorised capability is prepared for the specific mission, environment, configuration, autonomy level and human-control conditions in which it will actually be used.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **Operational employment shall begin only after the capability, mission, environment, configuration, autonomy, human authority, conditions, personnel, dependencies and safeguards have been confirmed as suitable for the planned employment.**

Planning does not create operational authority.

---

## 3. Employment Planning Object

Planning should consider:

**Capability × Mission × Environment × Autonomy × Human Authority × Configuration × Conditions**

The planning process should connect the intended employment to the existing Operational Authorisation Record.

---

## 4. Planning Inputs

Employment planning should consider:

- operational objective;
- authorised use case;
- authorisation scope;
- current readiness;
- configuration;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- environment;
- data and information;
- dependencies;
- security;
- monitoring;
- contingency arrangements;
- known limitations;
- recent incidents;
- lessons learned.

---

## 5. Mission Confirmation

Before employment, confirm:

- the planned mission is authorised;
- the intended use matches the approved use case;
- the AI function is appropriate to the mission;
- mission success criteria remain valid;
- mission conditions have not materially changed.

A new mission should not be treated as automatically authorised merely because the same AI capability has been used previously.

---

## 6. Employment Scope

The planned employment should define:

- purpose;
- permitted functions;
- operational area;
- duration;
- users;
- autonomy;
- configuration;
- relevant dependencies;
- conditions;
- restrictions;
- prohibited uses.

Scope should remain within the authorisation.

---

## 7. Current Authorisation Check

Before employment, verify:

- authorisation is current;
- authorisation has not expired;
- no suspension applies;
- no material restriction prevents the planned use;
- current configuration matches the authorised configuration;
- required conditions can be satisfied.

If these cannot be demonstrated, employment should not proceed until the appropriate authority determines the required action.

---

## 8. Readiness Confirmation

Planning should confirm operational readiness across:

- mission;
- system;
- assurance;
- environment;
- human competence;
- autonomy;
- security;
- data;
- dependencies;
- fail-safe;
- monitoring.

**Authorised ≠ Ready**

---

## 9. Configuration Verification

Before employment, confirm:

- approved model/system version;
- approved software;
- approved hardware;
- approved configuration;
- approved interfaces;
- approved security controls;
- approved data configuration;
- relevant dependencies.

Configuration discrepancies should be assessed before consequential employment.

---

## 10. Model and AI Behaviour Baseline

Where appropriate, establish the expected behaviour baseline for the deployed capability.

This may include:

- expected functions;
- known limitations;
- known failure modes;
- expected performance;
- uncertainty characteristics;
- autonomy behaviour;
- safety mechanisms.

The baseline provides a reference for detecting unexpected behaviour during employment.

---

## 11. Environment Confirmation

Confirm that current conditions remain within the authorised and demonstrated operating envelope.

Consider:

- terrain;
- weather;
- illumination;
- sensors;
- communications;
- navigation;
- electromagnetic conditions;
- information availability;
- computing;
- human conditions;
- adversarial conditions.

Material environmental differences should trigger the applicable assessment process.

---

## 12. Data and Information Readiness

Confirm that required data and information are:

- available;
- sufficiently reliable;
- relevant;
- timely;
- appropriately governed;
- consistent with the authorised use;
- within applicable handling requirements.

Material data-quality or integrity concerns should be escalated.

---

## 13. Human Authority Confirmation

Identify the personnel who will:

- exercise operational authority;
- supervise the capability;
- operate the system;
- make consequential decisions;
- provide technical support;
- provide AI advice where required;
- accept or manage risk;
- respond to incidents.

Authority should be explicit before employment begins.

---

## 14. Human Competence

Confirm that relevant personnel have:

- required training;
- current competence;
- knowledge of AI limitations;
- understanding of uncertainty;
- understanding of autonomy;
- knowledge of operational boundaries;
- intervention competence;
- emergency competence.

A competence gap may require additional supervision or restriction.

---

## 15. Operational AI Advisor

Where required, the OAIA should be available to support planning and employment.

The OAIA may confirm:

- AI capabilities;
- limitations;
- assurance status;
- autonomy;
- uncertainty;
- environmental suitability;
- known issues;
- recent lessons;
- implications of planned use.

The OAIA advises; designated human authorities retain authority.

---

## 16. Autonomy Confirmation

Confirm:

- authorised autonomy level;
- current autonomy level;
- permitted transitions;
- supervision requirements;
- intervention requirements;
- prohibited autonomy states.

The working D-AIGAAF model is:

- **A0 — No Meaningful AI Decision**
- **A1 — Information / Observation**
- **A2 — Analysis / Recommendation**
- **A3 — Human-Authorised Action**
- **A4 — Supervised Autonomous Action**
- **A5 — Independent Consequential Autonomy**

These are working constructs and should be mapped to applicable terminology.

---

## 17. Human-Control Confirmation

Before employment, confirm that required human control is practically available.

Consider:

- operator availability;
- communication;
- workload;
- situational awareness;
- intervention;
- override;
- escalation;
- authority to stop or restrict.

Human presence should not be treated as sufficient if the person cannot realistically exercise control.

---

## 18. Operational Boundaries Brief

Personnel should understand:

- what the AI may do;
- what it may not do;
- where it may operate;
- when it may operate;
- what autonomy is permitted;
- what actions require human authority;
- what conditions trigger restriction;
- what conditions trigger suspension.

The briefing should be consistent with the authorisation record.

---

## 19. Uncertainty Preparation

Personnel should understand known uncertainty and limitations.

Planning should identify:

- known weak conditions;
- known data limitations;
- expected uncertainty;
- confidence limitations;
- situations requiring additional human review;
- conditions requiring escalation.

The objective is appropriate reliance rather than blind trust.

---

## 20. Situational Awareness Preparation

Where AI contributes to situational awareness, personnel should understand:

- information sources;
- known information gaps;
- AI-derived information;
- human-observed information;
- conflicting information;
- uncertainty;
- time sensitivity.

AI-generated situational awareness should remain an input to operational judgement.

---

## 21. Dependency Readiness

Confirm critical dependencies such as:

- communications;
- data;
- sensors;
- navigation;
- compute;
- power;
- software services;
- external systems;
- identity/security services.

For each critical dependency, identify the expected response to failure.

---

## 22. Security Readiness

Confirm:

- system integrity;
- configuration integrity;
- data integrity;
- access controls;
- security status;
- known vulnerabilities;
- security monitoring;
- incident procedures.

Material security concerns should be resolved or escalated before employment.

---

## 23. Monitoring Plan

Define what will be monitored during employment:

- technical performance;
- AI behaviour;
- mission effectiveness;
- uncertainty;
- autonomy;
- human control;
- environment;
- security;
- data;
- dependencies;
- boundaries.

Define relevant thresholds and response authorities.

---

## 24. Contingency Planning

Before employment, establish responses to:

- communications loss;
- sensor degradation;
- navigation uncertainty;
- data loss;
- computing degradation;
- dependency failure;
- unexpected AI behaviour;
- loss of human control;
- security incident;
- environmental change.

Possible responses include:

**Continue → Restrict → Reduce Autonomy → Human Control → Safe State → Suspend**

---

## 25. Fail-Safe Readiness

Where applicable, confirm:

- fail-safe availability;
- intervention mechanism;
- safe-state mechanism;
- authorised users;
- expected behaviour;
- recovery arrangements.

Critical protective mechanisms should not be assumed operational without appropriate verification.

---

## 26. Employment Brief

Before employment, relevant personnel should receive a concise operational brief covering:

- mission;
- authorised AI function;
- configuration;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- boundaries;
- conditions;
- limitations;
- uncertainty;
- monitoring;
- contingency;
- intervention;
- escalation.

---

## 27. Final Go/No-Go Decision

Before employment, the responsible authority should determine whether:

**GO**

All mandatory conditions are satisfied.

**GO WITH CONDITIONS**

Employment may proceed within explicitly defined additional restrictions.

**NO-GO**

A mandatory condition, assurance basis, readiness requirement or authority requirement is not satisfied.

The decision should be recorded where required.

---

## 28. No-Go Conditions

Employment should normally not proceed where:

- authorisation is absent or expired;
- mandatory conditions cannot be met;
- configuration is materially uncertain;
- required human authority is unavailable;
- required competence is absent;
- critical fail-safe cannot be relied upon;
- environment is outside the authorised envelope;
- material security compromise exists;
- critical dependencies have failed;
- required monitoring is unavailable;
- material evidence contradicts the employment basis.

The appropriate authority should determine whether restriction, remediation, revalidation, reauthorisation or suspension is required.

---

## 29. Last-Minute Changes

Changes immediately before employment should be assessed for materiality.

Changes to:

- mission;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- configuration;
- data;
- human authority;
- dependencies;
- security;

should not be treated as administrative merely because employment is imminent.

---

## 30. Handover

Where employment responsibility transfers, the receiving personnel should be briefed on:

- current authorisation;
- mission;
- configuration;
- autonomy;
- restrictions;
- conditions;
- risks;
- environment;
- incidents;
- monitoring status;
- contingency arrangements.

The transfer should be recorded where required.

---

## 31. Employment Preparation Record

A controlled Employment Preparation Record may capture:

- capability;
- mission;
- date/time;
- authorisation;
- configuration;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- personnel;
- readiness;
- conditions;
- monitoring;
- dependencies;
- contingency;
- go/no-go decision;
- decision-maker;
- relevant observations.

---

## 32. Link to Operational Employment

Once preparation is complete, the capability transitions into actual employment.

The governance sequence is:

**Plan → Verify → Brief → Decide → Employ → Monitor**

Failure of a required preparation condition should prevent or constrain the transition to employment.

---

## 33. Governance Questions

Before employment, the organisation should be able to answer:

1. Is the intended use authorised?
2. Is the current configuration authorised?
3. Is the capability currently ready?
4. Is the environment within its authorised envelope?
5. What autonomy is active?
6. Who holds operational authority?
7. Are required personnel competent?
8. What are the operational boundaries?
9. What uncertainty is known?
10. What dependencies are critical?
11. What happens if communications fail?
12. What happens if the AI behaves unexpectedly?
13. Can the capability be restricted or stopped?
14. Is monitoring available?
15. Who makes the final go/no-go decision?

---

## 34. Golden Thread

Employment preparation remains connected to:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Readiness → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation**

---

## 35. Core Rule

> **Before operational employment, a Defence AI capability shall undergo a structured preparation and verification process confirming that the intended mission, configuration, environment, autonomy, human authority, competence, boundaries, conditions, dependencies, security, monitoring and contingency arrangements remain consistent with its authorisation and current readiness. Material uncertainty or failure of a mandatory condition shall prevent or constrain employment until the appropriate authority determines the required action. Operational urgency shall not convert an unverified or unauthorised condition into operational permission.**
