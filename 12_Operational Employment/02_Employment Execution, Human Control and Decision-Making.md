# 02 — Employment Execution, Human Control and Decision-Making

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Defence AI capabilities should be governed while they are actively employed.

It focuses on the interaction between AI outputs, human judgement, operational authority and consequential decisions during employment.

The objective is to ensure that authorised AI remains within its approved role and that humans retain the level of control required by the applicable authorisation.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **During operational employment, Defence AI shall remain within its authorised function and autonomy while human authorities retain effective control over consequential decisions and actions to the extent required by the applicable authorisation.**

Operational employment should not transform advisory capability into unauthorised authority.

---

## 3. Employment Execution

During active employment, responsible personnel should continuously confirm that:

- the intended mission remains valid;
- the AI function remains appropriate;
- the authorised configuration remains in use;
- the current environment remains within the authorised envelope;
- autonomy remains within authorised limits;
- human authority remains available;
- operational boundaries remain understood;
- monitoring remains effective.

---

## 4. AI Contribution

AI may contribute through:

- observation;
- information processing;
- detection;
- classification;
- analysis;
- prediction;
- recommendation;
- decision support;
- authorised autonomous action.

The operational role of AI should remain consistent with the authorisation.

---

## 5. Human Decision-Making

Where human decision-making is required, the human should:

- understand the relevant AI contribution;
- consider material uncertainty;
- consider other available information;
- assess operational context;
- determine whether the recommendation is appropriate;
- accept, reject, modify or disregard the recommendation within their authority;
- escalate where necessary.

AI output should not be treated as a command.

---

## 6. Human Authority

The responsible human authority should be identifiable for consequential decisions.

The organisation should be able to determine:

- who had authority;
- what authority they held;
- what information they received;
- what AI contributed;
- what decision was made;
- what action followed.

This supports accountability without requiring every routine interaction to be logged at the same level of detail.

---

## 7. Meaningful Human Control

Meaningful human control requires more than placing a person in the decision loop.

The organisation should consider whether the human has:

- sufficient situational awareness;
- sufficient understanding;
- sufficient competence;
- sufficient time where practicable;
- authority to act;
- ability to intervene;
- ability to override where authorised;
- ability to stop or restrict the capability.

If these conditions materially fail, the employment state should be reassessed.

---

## 8. Human Oversight

Oversight should be proportionate to:

- consequence;
- autonomy;
- uncertainty;
- mission criticality;
- environmental complexity;
- system reliability;
- operational tempo.

Higher-consequence or higher-autonomy employment generally requires stronger oversight.

---

## 9. Human Assessment of AI Recommendations

A human decision-maker should not accept an AI recommendation solely because:

- it is presented confidently;
- it appears precise;
- the system has performed well previously;
- the recommendation is consistent with expectations;
- the recommendation reduces workload.

Relevant evidence and context should remain available for judgement.

---

## 10. Rejection and Bypass

Where AI provides a recommendation, the authorised human may reject or bypass it where appropriate.

The organisation should recognise that:

**Accepting AI output** and **rejecting AI output** are both potentially consequential decisions.

For material decisions, the basis for rejection or bypass should be recorded where required by the applicable governance process.

---

## 11. Human-AI Disagreement

Disagreement between human judgement and AI output should not automatically be treated as an AI failure or human error.

The organisation should consider:

- quality of available information;
- uncertainty;
- competing evidence;
- system limitations;
- human situational awareness;
- environmental conditions.

Material unresolved disagreement should trigger escalation where required.

---

## 12. AI Output Categories

Operational personnel should distinguish:

### Observation

Information detected or received by the system.

### Analysis

AI-generated interpretation or processing.

### Prediction

An estimate of a possible future state.

### Recommendation

A proposed course of action or decision.

### Authorised Action

An action that the system or human is explicitly permitted to perform.

These categories should not be conflated.

---

## 13. Uncertainty

AI systems should communicate material uncertainty appropriately.

Personnel should understand:

- what is known;
- what is inferred;
- what is uncertain;
- what information is missing;
- what assumptions influence the output.

Uncertainty should influence the level of human review and escalation.

---

## 14. Conflicting Information

Where AI output conflicts with:

- human observation;
- other sensors;
- other information sources;
- another AI system;
- established operational information;

the conflict should be recognised rather than silently resolved by the AI.

The appropriate human authority should determine the response according to applicable procedures.

---

## 15. Situational Awareness

AI may improve situational awareness by processing large volumes of information.

However, AI-derived situational awareness should be treated as one component of the operational picture.

Personnel should remain alert to:

- missing data;
- stale information;
- false positives;
- false negatives;
- model limitations;
- sensor limitations;
- contradictory sources;
- environmental changes.

---

## 16. Automation Bias

Operational employment should account for the risk that personnel may:

- over-trust AI;
- defer to AI recommendations;
- ignore contradictory evidence;
- assume AI is more accurate than humans;
- continue relying on AI after conditions change.

Training, interface design and operational procedures should reduce these risks.

---

## 17. Human Workload

The organisation should monitor whether AI:

- reduces workload;
- increases workload;
- creates excessive alerts;
- requires constant verification;
- increases cognitive burden;
- causes loss of situational awareness.

Excessive workload may materially weaken meaningful human control.

---

## 18. Time-Critical Decisions

Time pressure may affect the practicality of human review.

The authorisation should therefore define, where relevant:

- decisions requiring human confirmation;
- decisions permitted under pre-authorised autonomy;
- intervention thresholds;
- emergency procedures;
- escalation requirements.

Time pressure should not silently create additional AI authority.

---

## 19. Consequential Decisions

For decisions capable of materially affecting:

- human life;
- physical safety;
- critical infrastructure;
- significant property;
- major mission outcomes;

the applicable authorisation should define the required level of human authority and control.

---

## 20. Autonomous Actions

Where autonomous action is authorised, the system should operate only within:

- approved mission;
- approved environment;
- approved autonomy;
- approved configuration;
- approved boundaries;
- approved conditions.

Autonomous action does not eliminate the organisation's responsibility to maintain governance.

---

## 21. Autonomy State Awareness

Personnel should know the current autonomy state.

D-AIGAAF working levels are:

- **A0 — No Meaningful AI Decision**
- **A1 — Information / Observation**
- **A2 — Analysis / Recommendation**
- **A3 — Human-Authorised Action**
- **A4 — Supervised Autonomous Action**
- **A5 — Independent Consequential Autonomy**

These are working governance constructs and should be mapped to applicable terminology.

---

## 22. Autonomy Transitions During Employment

Any authorised transition between autonomy states should have:

- defined trigger;
- defined authority;
- defined conditions;
- defined limits;
- defined monitoring;
- defined response if transition fails.

Unexpected movement to a higher autonomy state should be treated as a material event.

---

## 23. Intervention

Where intervention is required, personnel should be able to:

- pause;
- restrict;
- reduce autonomy;
- assume human control;
- override where authorised;
- place the system in a safe state;
- terminate employment.

The required intervention capability should be proportionate to consequence and autonomy.

---

## 24. Operational Boundaries

During employment, personnel should continuously respect:

- mission boundaries;
- geographic boundaries;
- environmental boundaries;
- temporal boundaries;
- autonomy boundaries;
- human-authority boundaries;
- configuration boundaries;
- data boundaries;
- dependency boundaries.

Technical ability beyond these limits does not create permission.

---

## 25. Dynamic Conditions

When conditions change, personnel should reassess whether employment remains appropriate.

Relevant changes include:

- mission;
- environment;
- communications;
- sensors;
- navigation;
- data;
- AI behaviour;
- human availability;
- autonomy;
- security;
- dependencies.

The appropriate response may range from continued employment to suspension.

---

## 26. Degraded Operations

During degraded operation, personnel should confirm:

- what remains functional;
- what information is unavailable;
- what autonomy remains authorised;
- what human control remains;
- what dependencies have failed;
- whether current employment remains within the authorisation.

Degradation should not automatically increase autonomy.

---

## 27. Communication Loss

Where communications are unavailable:

- predefined continuity arrangements should apply;
- human authority should remain bounded;
- authorised autonomy may continue only within established conditions;
- unplanned mission expansion should not occur;
- restoration should require controlled verification.

---

## 28. Operational Security

During employment, personnel should remain alert to:

- unexpected system behaviour;
- suspicious inputs;
- data integrity concerns;
- configuration anomalies;
- unauthorised access;
- adversarial manipulation;
- dependency compromise.

Material security concerns should be escalated according to applicable procedures.

---

## 29. System and Human Override

Override mechanisms should have:

- defined authority;
- defined activation conditions;
- defined consequences;
- appropriate safeguards;
- records where required.

The existence of an override should not be treated as evidence of effective human control unless personnel can realistically use it.

---

## 30. Operational Monitoring

Employment should be monitored for:

- performance;
- AI behaviour;
- uncertainty;
- autonomy;
- human control;
- environmental conditions;
- security;
- data integrity;
- dependencies;
- boundaries.

Monitoring should support timely decisions.

---

## 31. Operational Decision Traceability

For material consequential decisions, the organisation should preserve, where feasible:

**Context → AI Contribution → Human Assessment → Authority → Decision → Action → Outcome**

The level of detail should be proportionate to consequence and operational feasibility.

---

## 32. AI-Assisted Decision versus AI-Determined Decision

The organisation should distinguish:

**AI-Assisted Decision**

AI contributes information, analysis or recommendation and the authorised human determines the decision.

**AI-Determined/Autonomous Action**

The system determines or executes an action within a separately authorised autonomy level.

The distinction should be explicit in governance and records.

---

## 33. Decision Accountability

The presence of AI does not by itself transfer accountability from the authorised human authority to the AI system.

Where autonomous action is authorised, accountability should remain linked to:

- the authorisation;
- the responsible authority;
- the defined autonomy;
- the operating conditions;
- the system configuration.

---

## 34. Escalation

Escalation should occur when:

- uncertainty becomes material;
- human control degrades;
- autonomy behaves unexpectedly;
- operational boundaries become unclear;
- risk increases;
- environment leaves the authorised envelope;
- security is compromised;
- critical dependencies fail;
- AI and human judgement materially conflict.

---

## 35. Protective Response

Where employment conditions become unsafe or unauthorised, the response may be:

**Continue → Monitor → Restrict → Reduce Autonomy → Human Control → Safe State → Suspend**

The response should be proportionate to consequence and urgency.

---

## 36. Operational AI Advisor

The OAIA may support employment decisions by interpreting:

- AI outputs;
- uncertainty;
- system limitations;
- autonomy;
- environmental suitability;
- assurance implications;
- emerging behaviour.

The OAIA should not become an implicit source of operational authority.

---

## 37. Multiple AI Systems

Where multiple AI systems contribute to a decision, personnel should consider:

- conflicting outputs;
- recommendation chaining;
- common data;
- shared dependencies;
- cascading errors;
- combined autonomy;
- emergent behaviour.

The combined interaction should remain within the applicable governance basis.

---

## 38. Employment Records

Material operational records should capture, where required:

- mission;
- system;
- configuration;
- autonomy;
- environment;
- significant AI contribution;
- human decision;
- consequential action;
- intervention;
- incident;
- boundary event;
- outcome.

Records should support accountability and later assurance.

---

## 39. Operational Pause

A temporary pause may be appropriate where:

- uncertainty is unresolved;
- conditions are changing;
- human control is temporarily unavailable;
- monitoring is unavailable;
- a system anomaly requires assessment;
- authority is unclear.

A pause may be preferable to continuing uncertain employment.

---

## 40. Termination of Employment

Employment should terminate when:

- mission is complete;
- authorised period ends;
- conditions cease to be satisfied;
- capability becomes unsuitable;
- suspension is required;
- authority is withdrawn;
- configuration changes materially;
- environment leaves the authorised envelope.

Termination should prevent unintended continuation.

---

## 41. Post-Employment Feedback

Operational employment should generate feedback on:

- mission effectiveness;
- AI performance;
- human-AI interaction;
- uncertainty;
- autonomy;
- boundaries;
- incidents;
- environment;
- security;
- dependencies;
- lessons.

Material findings should feed continuous assurance.

---

## 42. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. Is the AI performing only its authorised function?
2. Who currently holds operational authority?
3. What autonomy is active?
4. Is human control effective?
5. What uncertainty exists?
6. Is situational awareness sufficient?
7. Are personnel appropriately interpreting AI outputs?
8. Are operational boundaries being respected?
9. Has the environment changed?
10. Have any dependencies failed?
11. Can personnel intervene?
12. What happens if communications are lost?
13. What happens if AI behaviour becomes unexpected?
14. Are consequential decisions traceable?
15. Does anything require restriction, escalation, revalidation or suspension?

---

## 43. Golden Thread

Operational execution remains connected to:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Human Decision → Action → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation**

---

## 44. Core Rule

> **During operational employment, Defence AI shall remain within its authorised mission, function, environment, autonomy, configuration, conditions and operational boundaries. Human authorities shall retain effective control over consequential decisions to the extent required by the applicable authorisation, with sufficient understanding of AI contributions, uncertainty, limitations and operational context to exercise that authority meaningfully. AI recommendations shall not be treated as commands, and technical capability shall not be interpreted as operational permission. Where human control degrades, uncertainty becomes material, boundaries become unclear, autonomy behaves unexpectedly, security or dependencies are compromised, or operating conditions materially change, employment shall be subject to proportionate restriction, reduced autonomy, human control, safe-state transition or suspension, with appropriate escalation, recording and revalidation or reauthorisation where required.**
