# 08 — Operational Decision, Action and Activity Records

## 1. Purpose

This document defines governance requirements for recording material decisions, AI outputs, human actions, autonomous actions, interventions and other operational activities associated with Defence AI employment.

The objective is to ensure that material AI-supported activity can be understood, reconstructed, reviewed and evidenced after the event.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **Material Defence AI decisions, recommendations, actions, interventions and autonomy changes shall be recorded with sufficient context to establish what occurred, what the AI contributed, what humans decided, what authority applied, and what action resulted. Records shall support accountability, assurance, incident investigation, learning and reauthorisation.**

---

## 3. Purpose of Records

Operational records should enable the organisation to determine:

- what happened;
- when it happened;
- which capability was involved;
- which configuration was active;
- what information was available;
- what the AI produced;
- what uncertainty existed;
- who exercised human authority;
- what decision was made;
- what action followed;
- what autonomy was active;
- what constraints applied;
- what outcome occurred.

---

## 4. Recording Scope

The level of recording should be proportionate to:

- consequence;
- autonomy;
- mission criticality;
- decision significance;
- intervention requirements;
- legal and policy obligations;
- security requirements.

Not every low-consequence system event requires the same level of evidence as a consequential AI-supported action.

---

## 5. Operational Record Object

A material operational record should establish the relationship:

**Mission × AI Capability × Configuration × Environment × Autonomy × Human Authority × Input/Output × Decision × Action × Outcome**

This provides an operational extension of the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread.

---

## 6. Record Categories

Operational records may include:

1. employment initiation;
2. system status;
3. AI outputs;
4. recommendations;
5. human decisions;
6. autonomous actions;
7. interventions;
8. overrides;
9. autonomy transitions;
10. boundary events;
11. alerts and anomalies;
12. incidents;
13. mission outcomes;
14. employment closeout.

---

## 7. Employment Start Record

Where appropriate, the start of employment should record:

- capability;
- mission;
- authorisation;
- environment;
- configuration;
- autonomy;
- responsible authority;
- operating conditions;
- applicable restrictions;
- readiness status.

---

## 8. AI Output Records

For material outputs, records should capture where practicable:

- output type;
- timestamp;
- relevant input or input reference;
- model/system identity;
- configuration;
- confidence or uncertainty;
- provenance;
- applicable warnings;
- human interpretation where material.

Records should not imply that an AI output was a fact merely because it was generated by the system.

---

## 9. Recommendation Records

Where AI provides a recommendation that materially contributes to a decision, the record should establish:

- recommendation;
- relevant context;
- uncertainty or limitations;
- human assessment;
- whether the recommendation was accepted, modified or rejected;
- resulting decision.

---

## 10. Human Decision Records

For consequential AI-supported decisions, the record should identify where practicable:

- authorised decision-maker;
- decision;
- decision time;
- relevant AI contribution;
- material information considered;
- material uncertainty;
- applicable authority;
- applicable constraints;
- rationale or justification where required.

The record should not transfer accountability from the authorised human decision-maker to the AI system.

---

## 11. Autonomous Action Records

Where authorised autonomous actions occur, records should capture:

- action;
- time;
- active autonomy state;
- applicable authorisation;
- relevant conditions;
- triggering input or event;
- system response;
- outcome;
- intervention, if any.

The purpose is to establish whether the action remained within authorised boundaries.

---

## 12. Autonomy Transition Records

Material transitions should be recorded, including:

- previous autonomy state;
- new autonomy state;
- transition time;
- trigger;
- authority;
- system behaviour;
- whether the transition was expected;
- resulting restrictions.

Unexpected autonomy transitions should receive appropriate review.

---

## 13. Human Intervention Records

Material intervention records should establish:

- trigger;
- detected condition;
- intervention authority;
- action taken;
- autonomy before and after;
- system response;
- outcome;
- subsequent decision.

These records should connect with the requirements in Serial 06.

---

## 14. Override Records

Where an authorised override occurs, record where practicable:

- overriding authority;
- reason;
- function affected;
- action taken;
- time;
- system response;
- resulting operational state;
- subsequent restoration or suspension decision.

Override records should support review of whether the override was authorised and appropriate.

---

## 15. Safe-State Records

Entry into or exit from a safe state should be recorded where material.

Records may include:

- trigger;
- system state;
- autonomy;
- authority;
- safe-state action;
- duration;
- assessment;
- exit conditions;
- restoration decision.

---

## 16. Boundary Event Records

Material boundary events should record:

- boundary concerned;
- observed condition;
- expected boundary;
- actual condition;
- uncertainty;
- action taken;
- authority;
- outcome.

This may include mission, geographic, temporal, functional, autonomy, environmental or human-authority boundaries.

---

## 17. Alert and Anomaly Records

Material alerts and anomalies should be recorded with:

- timestamp;
- indicator;
- observed condition;
- threshold;
- assessment;
- response;
- outcome.

Repeated anomalies should be assessed for systemic significance.

---

## 18. Decision Traceability

Where an AI contribution materially affects a consequential decision, the record should establish a traceable chain:

**Input/Information → AI Output → Human Assessment → Decision → Authority → Action → Outcome**

Where an autonomous action is authorised:

**Input/Event → AI Processing → Authorised Autonomous Action → Outcome**

---

## 19. Activity Timeline

For consequential systems, the organisation should maintain sufficient temporal information to reconstruct relevant sequences.

The timeline should distinguish, where applicable:

- system events;
- AI outputs;
- human decisions;
- human actions;
- autonomous actions;
- interventions;
- environmental changes;
- security events.

---

## 20. Configuration Traceability

Operational records should identify the configuration relevant to material events.

This may include:

- model version;
- software version;
- rules;
- parameters;
- data source;
- hardware;
- interfaces;
- connected dependencies.

Configuration traceability is essential for meaningful reconstruction.

---

## 21. Environment Traceability

Where environmental conditions materially affect interpretation, records should reference:

- environmental state;
- communications status;
- sensor status;
- navigation status;
- information availability;
- relevant adversarial conditions.

---

## 22. Human Authority Traceability

Records should establish the applicable human authority at the time of a material decision or action.

Where authority changes during employment, the record should identify:

- previous authority;
- new authority;
- time of transfer;
- reason;
- acknowledgement where applicable.

---

## 23. Uncertainty and Information Gaps

Material uncertainty should be recorded where it influenced:

- AI output;
- human assessment;
- decision;
- intervention;
- operational restriction.

Information that was unavailable but material to the decision should be identified where practicable.

---

## 24. Conflicting Information

Where materially conflicting information existed, records should establish:

- sources;
- conflict;
- assessment;
- resolution or treatment;
- resulting decision.

This supports later evaluation of whether the decision process was reasonable given the information available at the time.

---

## 25. Record Integrity

Operational records should be protected against:

- unauthorised modification;
- deletion;
- corruption;
- loss;
- inappropriate access.

Integrity mechanisms should be proportionate to consequence and information sensitivity.

---

## 26. Record Time

Material events should have reliable timestamps sufficient for reconstruction.

Where multiple systems are involved, time alignment should be considered so that events can be correctly ordered.

---

## 27. Distributed and Offline Operations

Where connectivity is limited or unavailable, recording mechanisms should support local capture where practicable.

Upon reconnection, records should be synchronised or transferred using controlled mechanisms.

Loss of connectivity should not automatically eliminate the requirement to record material events.

---

## 28. Record Availability

Records should remain accessible to authorised personnel for:

- operational review;
- incident investigation;
- assurance;
- audit;
- legal or policy review;
- lessons learned;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation.

Access should remain subject to applicable security and information-handling requirements.

---

## 29. Data Minimisation

Recording should be sufficient for accountability and assurance without unnecessarily collecting information that has no legitimate operational, safety, security or governance purpose.

Retention should follow applicable requirements.

---

## 30. Privacy and Sensitive Information

Where records contain personal, operationally sensitive or otherwise protected information, handling should comply with applicable legal, policy and security requirements.

This framework does not override information-classification or protection requirements.

---

## 31. Evidence Preservation

Where a material incident, anomaly or investigation occurs, relevant records should be preserved to prevent loss or alteration.

Preservation should include relevant:

- system logs;
- AI outputs;
- decisions;
- configuration;
- environment information;
- interventions;
- security events.

---

## 32. Record Completeness

The organisation should identify critical records that must exist for consequential activity.

Missing material records should be treated as an assurance concern where they prevent reconstruction or accountability.

---

## 33. Record Quality

Records should be:

- accurate;
- attributable;
- timely;
- complete enough for their purpose;
- traceable;
- protected from unauthorised alteration.

---

## 34. Decision Attribution

Records should distinguish between:

- AI-generated information;
- AI recommendation;
- human judgement;
- human decision;
- authorised autonomous action;
- technical system behaviour.

This distinction is essential to prevent ambiguity regarding responsibility.

---

## 35. Accountability

Operational records should support identification of the relevant authority for actions involving material risk.

The existence of an AI contribution does not by itself transfer accountability to the AI system.

---

## 36. Multi-AI Records

Where multiple AI systems contribute to an operational decision, records should establish, where material:

- systems involved;
- individual outputs;
- dependencies;
- conflicts;
- human assessment;
- final decision;
- resulting action.

---

## 37. Record Review

Material records should be reviewed following:

- incidents;
- unexpected autonomy;
- significant intervention;
- material performance degradation;
- boundary violations;
- security events;
- major mission outcomes.

Review should determine whether governance or assurance changes are required.

---

## 38. Records and Continuous Assurance

Operational records provide evidence for continuous assurance.

They may reveal:

- performance trends;
- recurring anomalies;
- human-control issues;
- autonomy problems;
- environmental limitations;
- dependency weaknesses;
- emerging risks.

Relevant findings should feed into Module 13.

---

## 39. Records and Revalidation

Where operational evidence indicates that assumptions or assurance claims may no longer hold, records should support:

- impact assessment;
- additional testing;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation;
- restriction or suspension.

---

## 40. Records and Lessons Learned

Operational records should support structured lessons learned.

The learning cycle is:

**Record → Analyse → Identify Lesson → Assess Generalisability → Update Controls/Assurance → Verify**

---

## 41. Record Retention

Retention periods should be defined according to:

- legal requirements;
- policy;
- mission consequence;
- audit requirements;
- incident requirements;
- assurance needs;
- historical value.

Records required for unresolved investigations or assurance decisions should not be destroyed merely because a routine retention period has expired.

---

## 42. Record Disposal

Disposal should be:

- authorised;
- controlled;
- documented;
- consistent with applicable retention requirements.

Disposal should not compromise required evidence.

---

## 43. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. Which operational AI activities must be recorded?
2. What level of detail is required?
3. Can consequential decisions be reconstructed?
4. Can AI outputs be distinguished from human decisions?
5. Is the applicable human authority identifiable?
6. Can the active configuration be established?
7. Can autonomy transitions be reconstructed?
8. Are interventions and overrides recorded?
9. Can records be maintained during disconnected operations?
10. How is record integrity protected?
11. How are material incidents preserved?
12. How long are records retained?
13. Who may access them?
14. How are records used for assurance and reauthorisation?

---

## 44. Core Rule

> **Operational records shall preserve sufficient evidence to reconstruct material Defence AI activity, including relevant inputs, outputs, uncertainty, configuration, environment, autonomy, human authority, decisions, actions, interventions and outcomes. Records shall distinguish AI contribution from human judgement and decision authority, remain appropriately protected and traceable, and support accountability, incident investigation, assurance, learning, revalidation and reauthorisation.**

---

## 45. Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Autonomy → Employment → Output → Human Assessment → Decision → Action → Record → Monitoring → Incident/Change → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation**
