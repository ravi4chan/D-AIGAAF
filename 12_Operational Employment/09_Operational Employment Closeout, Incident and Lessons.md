# 09 — Operational Employment Closeout, Incident and Lessons

## 1. Purpose

This document defines governance requirements for concluding Defence AI operational employment and ensuring that material outcomes, incidents, decisions, evidence and lessons are captured and incorporated into the assurance and governance lifecycle.

Closeout is not merely the cessation of system use. It is the controlled transition from active employment to a defined post-employment state.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **Defence AI operational employment shall conclude through a controlled closeout process that confirms the employment state, preserves material evidence, records outcomes and incidents, identifies lessons, assesses continuing assurance implications and determines whether the capability may be restored, restricted, revalidated, reauthorised, retired or decommissioned.**

---

## 3. Closeout Scope

Closeout should consider:

- mission completion;
- planned termination;
- operational suspension;
- emergency termination;
- system withdrawal;
- incident-driven termination;
- change-driven termination;
- authorisation expiry;
- environmental exit;
- capability retirement.

---

## 4. Closeout States

An organisation may use the following states:

### C0 — Active Employment

The capability remains operationally employed.

### C1 — Employment Concluded

The planned operational activity has ended.

### C2 — Restricted Post-Employment

The capability remains available only under defined restrictions.

### C3 — Suspended

Employment is prohibited pending assessment.

### C4 — Withdrawn

The capability has been removed from operational use.

### C5 — Retired

The capability is no longer authorised for operational employment.

### C6 — Decommissioned

The capability has completed its controlled lifecycle exit.

---

## 5. Planned Closeout

Planned closeout should occur when:

- the mission objective is complete;
- the authorised employment period ends;
- the capability is no longer required;
- the operational context changes;
- the capability reaches an established exit condition.

The responsible authority should confirm the transition.

---

## 6. Immediate Termination

Operational employment may require immediate termination following:

- unacceptable risk;
- loss of human control;
- critical system failure;
- serious security event;
- material boundary violation;
- unsafe autonomous behaviour;
- failed intervention;
- critical dependency failure.

Immediate termination should prioritise protection of people, assets and the operational system.

---

## 7. Mission Closeout

At mission completion, the organisation should establish:

- whether mission objectives were achieved;
- how AI contributed;
- material decisions;
- material failures;
- significant deviations;
- unexpected outcomes;
- human-control observations;
- operational limitations.

Mission success should not be assessed solely from AI system performance.

---

## 8. System State at Closeout

The final operational state should be established where practicable, including:

- system condition;
- configuration;
- autonomy;
- environment;
- connectivity;
- dependencies;
- security status;
- outstanding alerts;
- pending actions.

---

## 9. Safe Termination

Termination should leave the capability in a controlled state appropriate to its design.

Where required, this may involve:

- stopping consequential functions;
- reducing autonomy;
- transferring control;
- entering safe state;
- controlled shutdown;
- isolation.

Termination procedures should be tested before operational use where practicable.

---

## 10. Evidence Preservation

Material evidence should be preserved following closeout.

This may include:

- operational logs;
- AI outputs;
- decisions;
- actions;
- configuration;
- autonomy transitions;
- interventions;
- alerts;
- environmental information;
- security events;
- mission outcomes.

Evidence should remain protected against unauthorised modification or loss.

---

## 11. Operational Record Completion

Required records should be reviewed for completeness.

Material missing information should be identified and, where possible, reconstructed or documented as unavailable.

The organisation should avoid presenting reconstructed information as original records.

---

## 12. Incident Identification

Closeout should determine whether any event during employment meets applicable incident criteria.

Potential indicators include:

- unsafe AI behaviour;
- unexpected autonomy;
- loss of human control;
- boundary violation;
- material security event;
- significant performance degradation;
- consequential error;
- intervention failure;
- unexpected mission effect.

---

## 13. Incident Handover

Where an incident exists, responsibility should be transferred to the appropriate incident-management process.

Closeout should not prematurely close an unresolved incident.

Relevant evidence should remain available for investigation.

---

## 14. Post-Employment Review

A post-employment review should assess:

- mission performance;
- AI performance;
- uncertainty;
- human interaction;
- autonomy;
- environmental suitability;
- security;
- dependencies;
- interventions;
- incidents;
- unexpected effects;
- lessons.

The depth of review should be proportionate to consequence.

---

## 15. Performance Assessment

Performance should be compared with:

- authorised performance expectations;
- TEVV evidence;
- operational baselines;
- mission success criteria;
- applicable thresholds.

Differences should be assessed rather than automatically treated as failure.

---

## 16. Human Factors Review

Where AI materially influenced human decisions, review should consider:

- workload;
- automation bias;
- reliance on recommendations;
- disagreement;
- situational awareness;
- intervention effectiveness;
- training adequacy;
- interface limitations.

Human factors findings should feed into future assurance and training.

---

## 17. Autonomy Review

The review should assess:

- autonomy actually exercised;
- authorised autonomy;
- autonomy transitions;
- unexpected behaviour;
- human intervention;
- boundary adherence.

Material deviations should trigger appropriate governance action.

---

## 18. Environmental Review

Operational evidence should be assessed against the environment originally characterised.

Consider:

- conditions encountered;
- conditions outside the assessed envelope;
- environmental changes;
- sensor limitations;
- communications;
- navigation;
- electromagnetic conditions;
- adversarial factors.

Findings should feed Module 10 and relevant assurance processes.

---

## 19. Security Review

Where appropriate, closeout should determine whether:

- security controls functioned as expected;
- suspicious activity occurred;
- data or model integrity was affected;
- dependencies behaved as expected;
- new threats were identified.

Security findings should be transferred to the relevant security governance process.

---

## 20. Lessons Learned

Lessons should be derived from both successful and unsuccessful employment.

The objective is not simply to record what happened but to determine:

- why it happened;
- whether it is repeatable;
- whether it reveals a systemic issue;
- what should change;
- how the change will be verified.

---

## 21. Lessons Classification

Lessons may concern:

- mission design;
- requirements;
- data;
- model/system behaviour;
- human authority;
- autonomy;
- environment;
- security;
- dependencies;
- TEVV;
- monitoring;
- intervention;
- authorisation;
- training;
- acquisition.

---

## 22. Lesson Generalisation

A lesson should be assessed for applicability beyond the immediate employment.

The organisation should determine whether it affects:

- the same capability;
- similar use cases;
- related systems;
- the operational environment;
- organisational policy;
- the broader D-AIGAAF control framework.

---

## 23. Corrective and Preventive Actions

Lessons that identify deficiencies should produce appropriate actions.

Actions may include:

- configuration change;
- software or model change;
- control improvement;
- additional testing;
- training;
- monitoring enhancement;
- operational restriction;
- policy change;
- supplier action;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation.

---

## 24. Assurance Impact

Closeout findings should be assessed for their effect on existing assurance claims.

Questions include:

- Does the evidence remain valid?
- Did an assumption fail?
- Did operational conditions differ materially?
- Did performance differ materially?
- Was human control effective?
- Did autonomy behave as expected?
- Did dependencies introduce new risk?

---

## 25. Revalidation Trigger

Closeout may trigger revalidation where evidence indicates that:

- assumptions changed;
- performance degraded;
- operational conditions exceeded the assessed envelope;
- material limitations were discovered;
- controls were ineffective;
- new failure modes emerged.

---

## 26. Reauthorisation Trigger

Reauthorisation may be required where closeout identifies a material change to:

- mission;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- configuration;
- risk;
- operational boundaries;
- conditions of use.

---

## 27. Suspension

Where assurance is insufficient, the capability should remain suspended until the responsible authority determines that adequate evidence and controls exist.

Suspension should not be treated as an administrative inconvenience; it is an operational risk-control measure.

---

## 28. Restoration

If the capability is to return to employment, restoration should confirm:

- system integrity;
- configuration;
- environment;
- human authority;
- autonomy;
- security;
- dependencies;
- authorisation;
- readiness.

Where required, restoration should follow revalidation or reauthorisation.

---

## 29. Knowledge Capture

Relevant closeout information should be incorporated into organisational knowledge repositories.

This may include:

- lessons;
- evidence;
- incident findings;
- performance data;
- environmental observations;
- training requirements;
- control changes.

---

## 30. Feedback to the Lifecycle

Closeout should feed information back into earlier lifecycle stages.

Relevant updates may affect:

- requirements;
- development;
- acquisition;
- data;
- TEVV;
- operational environment;
- authorisation;
- employment controls.

This creates a closed governance loop rather than treating employment as the end of assurance.

---

## 31. Feedback to Continuous Assurance

Operational experience should update continuous assurance through:

**Observe → Record → Assess → Learn → Act → Verify → Update**

The purpose is to ensure that operational experience changes assurance where warranted.

---

## 32. Closeout Evidence Package

For material employment, a closeout package may include:

- employment record;
- mission outcome;
- performance summary;
- material decisions;
- AI outputs;
- intervention records;
- incident records;
- environmental observations;
- security findings;
- lessons;
- corrective actions;
- assurance impact assessment;
- restoration or reauthorisation recommendation.

---

## 33. Closeout Authority

The organisation should define who may formally conclude employment.

This authority should be distinct from technical shutdown capability where appropriate.

The authority should also determine whether unresolved matters prevent closure.

---

## 34. Handover

Where responsibility transfers after employment, the handover should establish:

- current system state;
- outstanding risks;
- unresolved incidents;
- restrictions;
- evidence status;
- pending actions;
- responsible authority.

---

## 35. Termination during Degraded Conditions

Where employment ends during degraded or disconnected conditions, the organisation should apply predefined termination and evidence-preservation arrangements.

Reconnection should not automatically restore operational authority.

---

## 36. Termination during Adversarial Conditions

Where employment ends following suspected adversarial activity, closeout should preserve relevant evidence and coordinate with security and incident processes.

Restoration should require appropriate integrity assessment.

---

## 37. Multi-AI Closeout

Where multiple AI systems were employed together, closeout should consider:

- system interactions;
- cascading effects;
- shared dependencies;
- combined performance;
- conflicting outputs;
- collective autonomy behaviour.

Individual system closeout may be insufficient to establish system-of-systems assurance.

---

## 38. Operational Feedback to Training

Material findings should inform:

- operator training;
- commander education;
- technical training;
- intervention exercises;
- autonomy understanding;
- uncertainty interpretation;
- incident response.

Training changes should be verified where material.

---

## 39. Closeout Review Outcomes

Possible outcomes include:

- Continue authorised employment;
- Continue with additional conditions;
- Restrict employment;
- Additional monitoring;
- Corrective action;
- Revalidation;
- Reauthorisation;
- Suspend;
- Withdraw;
- Retire;
- Decommission.

---

## 40. Records

The closeout process should preserve sufficient records to establish:

- who concluded employment;
- when it ended;
- why it ended;
- final system state;
- material events;
- incidents;
- outcomes;
- lessons;
- subsequent actions.

---

## 41. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. What constitutes operational closeout?
2. Who can conclude employment?
3. How is emergency termination handled?
4. Is the final system state recorded?
5. Are material decisions and actions preserved?
6. Are incidents identified before closure?
7. How are unresolved incidents transferred?
8. How is mission effectiveness assessed?
9. How are human factors assessed?
10. How are autonomy and boundary events reviewed?
11. How are lessons captured?
12. How are lessons generalised?
13. When does closeout trigger revalidation?
14. When does it trigger reauthorisation?
15. What must be verified before restoration?
16. How does operational experience feed the wider lifecycle?

---

## 42. Core Rule

> **Operational employment shall not be considered fully closed until the applicable authority has confirmed the employment state, material evidence has been preserved, outcomes and incidents have been assessed, and relevant lessons and assurance implications have been identified. Material findings shall feed into corrective action, continuous assurance, TEVV, revalidation, reauthorisation, training and lifecycle governance as appropriate.**

---

## 43. Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Autonomy → Employment → Monitoring → Decision/Action → Closeout → Incident/Lessons → Corrective Action → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Lifecycle Learning**
