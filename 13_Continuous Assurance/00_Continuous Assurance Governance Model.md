# 00 — Continuous Assurance Governance Model

## 1. Purpose

This module defines the governance framework for maintaining confidence that a Defence AI capability remains safe, secure, reliable, effective, controllable and appropriately authorised throughout its operational life.

Continuous assurance recognises that assurance is not a one-time activity completed before deployment. Operational conditions, data, threats, configurations, models, dependencies, human interaction and mission requirements can change over time.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **A Defence AI capability shall remain subject to continuous assurance throughout its lifecycle. Continued confidence shall be based on current evidence, operational experience, monitoring, testing, incidents, changes, human-control effectiveness and environmental conditions. Where assurance assumptions no longer remain valid, the organisation shall take proportionate action, including additional controls, monitoring, revalidation, reauthorisation, restriction or suspension.**

---

## 3. Assurance Objective

Continuous assurance should establish whether:

- the original assurance claims remain valid;
- operational evidence remains consistent with expected behaviour;
- risks remain within accepted limits;
- controls remain effective;
- human authority remains meaningful;
- autonomy remains appropriate;
- the operating environment remains suitable;
- security remains adequate;
- dependencies remain acceptable;
- mission effectiveness remains demonstrated.

---

## 4. Assurance Object

The continuous assurance object is:

**AI Capability × Mission × Environment × Autonomy × Human Authority × Configuration × Conditions**

Assurance should therefore consider not only the AI model or technical system but the complete operational context in which it is authorised and employed.

---

## 5. Assurance Lifecycle

Continuous assurance follows the cycle:

**Establish → Monitor → Collect Evidence → Assess → Challenge → Act → Verify → Update Assurance**

This cycle operates throughout operational employment and across relevant lifecycle changes.

---

## 6. Initial Assurance Baseline

Before operational employment, the organisation should establish an assurance baseline containing:

- approved requirements;
- identified risks;
- applicable controls;
- TEVV evidence;
- environmental assumptions;
- human-control assumptions;
- autonomy assumptions;
- security evidence;
- dependency assumptions;
- authorisation conditions;
- operational boundaries.

The baseline provides the reference against which subsequent evidence is assessed.

---

## 7. Assurance Claims

Assurance should be expressed through explicit claims where practicable.

Examples include:

- the capability performs its intended function;
- the capability remains within defined performance limits;
- AI outputs contain appropriately communicated uncertainty;
- human control remains effective;
- autonomy remains within authorised boundaries;
- security controls remain effective;
- the capability remains suitable for its operating environment.

Each material claim should have supporting evidence.

---

## 8. Evidence-Based Assurance

Assurance should be based on evidence rather than assumption.

Evidence may include:

- TEVV results;
- operational monitoring;
- incident records;
- intervention records;
- performance data;
- environmental observations;
- security assessments;
- configuration records;
- audit findings;
- human factors observations;
- lessons learned.

Evidence should be traceable to the assurance claim it supports.

---

## 9. Evidence Currency

Evidence has a period of relevance.

The organisation should consider whether evidence remains current following:

- time;
- operational experience;
- environmental change;
- threat change;
- configuration change;
- model update;
- data change;
- dependency change;
- mission change.

Old evidence should not automatically be treated as invalid, but its continuing relevance should be assessed.

---

## 10. Assurance Confidence

A useful assurance assessment may distinguish:

### Demonstrated

Evidence directly supports the assurance claim under relevant conditions.

### Partially Demonstrated

Evidence supports the claim but important conditions remain limited or uncertain.

### Indirectly Supported

Evidence provides supporting indications but does not directly demonstrate the claim.

### Untested

Insufficient evidence exists.

### Contradicted

Available evidence indicates that the claim may not remain valid.

---

## 11. Assurance Health

The organisation may maintain an overall assurance status:

### Green — Assured

Current evidence supports continued confidence.

### Amber — Conditional

Evidence identifies limitations requiring increased controls, monitoring or review.

### Red — Insufficient

Confidence is materially inadequate for unrestricted continued employment.

The status should not replace detailed evidence or professional judgement.

---

## 12. Assurance Domains

Continuous assurance should consider, as applicable:

1. mission;
2. risk;
3. AI performance;
4. data;
5. model/system behaviour;
6. security;
7. supply chain;
8. human authority;
9. autonomy;
10. operational environment;
11. dependencies;
12. TEVV evidence;
13. operational employment;
14. incidents;
15. configuration;
16. legal and policy compliance.

---

## 13. Monitoring Inputs

Continuous assurance should consume information from operational monitoring, including:

- performance indicators;
- uncertainty;
- anomalies;
- environmental conditions;
- autonomy;
- human-control indicators;
- security indicators;
- dependency status;
- mission effectiveness.

Monitoring provides evidence; it does not by itself determine assurance status.

---

## 14. Operational Experience

Actual employment provides important assurance evidence.

Operational experience may reveal:

- previously unidentified failure modes;
- environmental limitations;
- unexpected human behaviour;
- performance degradation;
- autonomy anomalies;
- dependency weaknesses;
- new security threats.

Such evidence should be systematically assessed.

---

## 15. Incident-Driven Assurance

Material incidents should trigger an assurance review.

The review should determine:

- what assurance claim was affected;
- whether existing controls worked;
- whether assumptions were incorrect;
- whether evidence remains valid;
- whether the incident is isolated or systemic;
- what corrective action is required.

---

## 16. Change-Driven Assurance

Changes should be assessed for their potential effect on assurance.

Relevant changes include:

- model;
- software;
- hardware;
- data;
- configuration;
- interfaces;
- dependencies;
- mission;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- threat conditions.

Material changes may require revalidation or reauthorisation.

---

## 17. Threat-Driven Assurance

Emerging threats may invalidate previous assurance.

The organisation should consider:

- new adversarial techniques;
- new AI-specific attacks;
- supply-chain threats;
- data manipulation;
- model compromise;
- infrastructure compromise;
- changes in the operational threat environment.

Assurance should adapt where the threat landscape changes materially.

---

## 18. Environment-Driven Assurance

Changes to the operational environment may affect:

- sensor performance;
- communications;
- navigation;
- data quality;
- model performance;
- human control;
- autonomy;
- mission effectiveness.

Material environmental change should trigger the applicable assessment process.

---

## 19. Human-Control Assurance

Continuous assurance should establish whether meaningful human control remains effective.

Consider:

- authority;
- competence;
- workload;
- situational awareness;
- intervention;
- override;
- automation bias;
- decision time;
- human-AI disagreement.

Human-control assurance is not satisfied merely because an interface technically provides an override function.

---

## 20. Autonomy Assurance

Continuous assurance should establish whether:

- authorised autonomy remains appropriate;
- actual autonomy matches authorised autonomy;
- transitions occur as expected;
- autonomy boundaries remain effective;
- human control remains sufficient;
- autonomous behaviour remains within demonstrated conditions.

Unexpected autonomy behaviour should receive proportionate review.

---

## 21. Security Assurance

Security assurance should consider continuing effectiveness of:

- access controls;
- integrity controls;
- data protection;
- model protection;
- software security;
- interface security;
- dependency security;
- monitoring;
- incident response.

Security assurance should be integrated with AI security governance.

---

## 22. Supply-Chain Assurance

Continuing assurance should consider changes in:

- suppliers;
- components;
- models;
- software dependencies;
- data sources;
- hosting arrangements;
- ownership;
- support arrangements;
- strategic dependencies.

Material supplier or dependency changes should be assessed before continued unrestricted employment where required.

---

## 23. Assurance Indicators

Indicators may include:

- performance drift;
- anomaly frequency;
- incident frequency;
- intervention frequency;
- boundary events;
- autonomy deviations;
- security events;
- environmental excursions;
- human-control concerns;
- unresolved findings;
- evidence ageing;
- dependency failures.

Indicators should be interpreted in context.

---

## 24. Assurance Thresholds

Predefined thresholds should identify when additional action is required.

Possible outcomes include:

**Continue → Continue with Conditions → Enhanced Monitoring → Restrict → Revalidate → Reauthorise → Suspend**

Thresholds should reflect consequence and uncertainty.

---

## 25. Assurance Triggers

An assurance review may be triggered by:

- material performance degradation;
- unexpected behaviour;
- significant incident;
- security compromise;
- environmental change;
- material configuration change;
- model update;
- data change;
- autonomy change;
- dependency change;
- mission change;
- human-control concern;
- evidence expiry or loss.

---

## 26. Assurance Review

An assurance review should assess:

- current evidence;
- current risks;
- current conditions;
- operational experience;
- changes;
- incidents;
- control effectiveness;
- remaining uncertainty.

The review should produce a documented outcome.

---

## 27. Independent Challenge

For consequential capabilities, assurance should include appropriate independent challenge.

Independent review may examine:

- evidence sufficiency;
- assumptions;
- methodology;
- risk treatment;
- human control;
- autonomy;
- security;
- operational suitability.

Independence should be proportionate to consequence.

---

## 28. Assurance Decision

Possible assurance decisions include:

1. Continue;
2. Continue with conditions;
3. Increase monitoring;
4. Add controls;
5. Restrict functionality;
6. Reduce autonomy;
7. Conduct additional testing;
8. Revalidate;
9. Reauthorise;
10. Suspend.

The decision should be attributable to the appropriate authority.

---

## 29. Relationship with Operational Authorisation

Continuous assurance does not replace operational authorisation.

The relationship is:

**Assurance → Evidence of Confidence**

**Authorisation → Permission to Employ**

A capability may be technically assured but not authorised for a particular mission or condition.

---

## 30. Relationship with TEVV

TEVV establishes evidence through structured testing, evaluation, verification and validation.

Continuous assurance incorporates TEVV evidence together with operational evidence.

Operational experience may identify conditions requiring additional TEVV.

---

## 31. Relationship with Operational Monitoring

Operational monitoring observes the capability and operating context.

Continuous assurance assesses what the accumulated evidence means for continuing confidence.

Thus:

**Monitoring → Evidence → Assurance Assessment → Governance Action**

---

## 32. Relationship with Revalidation

Revalidation is required when assurance evidence indicates that previously demonstrated claims may no longer hold.

Continuous assurance therefore provides one mechanism for identifying revalidation triggers.

---

## 33. Assurance Debt

An organisation should identify unresolved assurance gaps that have not yet invalidated employment but require action.

Examples include:

- incomplete testing;
- ageing evidence;
- unresolved anomalies;
- uncertain environmental applicability;
- pending corrective action.

Assurance debt should have owners and target resolution conditions.

---

## 34. Residual Uncertainty

Continuous assurance should explicitly track important uncertainty.

Uncertainty may concern:

- model behaviour;
- data;
- environment;
- human interaction;
- threats;
- dependencies;
- mission outcomes.

Uncertainty should not be hidden by aggregate assurance ratings.

---

## 35. Assurance of AI Limitations

Known limitations should remain visible to relevant users and authorities.

Where operational experience identifies new limitations, they should be incorporated into:

- documentation;
- training;
- monitoring;
- controls;
- TEVV;
- authorisation conditions.

---

## 36. Assurance Records

The organisation should maintain an Assurance Record containing, where appropriate:

- assurance claims;
- evidence;
- evidence status;
- assumptions;
- findings;
- incidents;
- changes;
- review dates;
- responsible authorities;
- decisions;
- conditions;
- outstanding actions.

---

## 37. Assurance Traceability

Each material assurance claim should be traceable to:

**Requirement → Risk → Control → Test/Evaluation → Evidence → Assurance Claim → Authority**

Operational findings should be traceable back into the same chain.

---

For material capabilities, an assurance view may include:

---

## 39. Assurance Review Frequency

Review frequency should be proportionate to:

- consequence;
- autonomy;
- operational tempo;
- rate of change;
- threat level;
- environmental variability;
- system complexity;
- evidence stability.

High-consequence systems may require more frequent review.

---

## 40. Continuous Assurance during Disconnected Operations

Where communications are limited, local assurance evidence should continue to be captured where practicable.

Reconnection should enable controlled consolidation and assessment of accumulated evidence.

Loss of connectivity should not automatically imply continued unrestricted assurance.

---

## 41. Assurance during Multi-AI Operations

Where multiple AI systems interact, assurance should consider:

- individual assurance;
- interaction effects;
- shared dependencies;
- combined autonomy;
- emergent behaviour;
- system-of-systems performance.

Assurance of individual components does not automatically establish assurance of their combined operational behaviour.

---

## 42. Assurance Escalation

Material assurance concerns should be escalated according to predefined authority.

Escalation may involve:

- operational authority;
- technical authority;
- security authority;
- safety authority;
- OAIA;
- governance committee;
- independent review body.

---

## 43. Assurance Closure

An assurance finding should be considered closed only when:

- the issue has been assessed;
- required corrective action is complete;
- evidence demonstrates the intended result;
- responsible authority accepts the outcome.

Closure should not be based solely on completion of an administrative action.

---

## 44. Assurance and Learning

Lessons from incidents, monitoring, testing and operational employment should update assurance.

The learning cycle is:

**Observe → Record → Assess → Learn → Act → Verify → Update Assurance**

---

## 45. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. What assurance claims must remain valid?
2. What evidence supports each claim?
3. Is the evidence current?
4. What operational indicators can invalidate confidence?
5. What events trigger an assurance review?
6. How are incidents incorporated?
7. How are changes incorporated?
8. How is human-control assurance maintained?
9. How is autonomy assurance maintained?
10. How are emerging threats incorporated?
11. How are environmental changes incorporated?
12. Who independently challenges assurance?
13. What thresholds trigger restriction, revalidation or suspension?
14. Who owns unresolved assurance gaps?
15. How does assurance feed reauthorisation?

---

## 46. Core Rule

> **Continuous assurance shall maintain evidence-based confidence that a Defence AI capability remains suitable for its authorised purpose and conditions. Monitoring, operational experience, incidents, changes, threats, human-control observations, environmental evidence and TEVV results shall be integrated into assurance assessment. Where confidence is materially reduced, the organisation shall take proportionate action rather than allowing technical availability or existing authorisation to imply continued suitability.**

---

## 47. Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Autonomy → Employment → Monitoring → Incident/Change → Continuous Assurance → Action → Verification → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Learning**
