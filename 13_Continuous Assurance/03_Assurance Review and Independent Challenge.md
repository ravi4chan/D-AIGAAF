# 03 — Assurance Review and Independent Challenge

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the governance requirements for conducting structured assurance reviews and obtaining appropriate independent challenge of Defence AI assurance conclusions.

The purpose is to ensure that continuing confidence is periodically and event-driven assessed by competent personnel who can question evidence, assumptions, risks, controls and conclusions without inappropriate pressure to preserve existing authorisation.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **Material Defence AI assurance conclusions shall be subject to structured review and, where proportionate to consequence, independent challenge. Review shall examine evidence, assumptions, risks, controls, operational experience and uncertainty, and shall be capable of producing outcomes that restrict, suspend, revalidate or reauthorise employment where confidence is no longer sufficient.**

---

## 3. Assurance Review Objective

An assurance review should determine:

- whether assurance claims remain supported;
- whether evidence remains current and relevant;
- whether risks remain acceptable;
- whether controls remain effective;
- whether operating conditions remain applicable;
- whether human control remains meaningful;
- whether autonomy remains appropriate;
- whether incidents or changes affect confidence;
- whether further assurance is required.

---

## 4. Review Triggers

An assurance review may be initiated by:

- scheduled review;
- material performance change;
- significant incident;
- security event;
- unexpected AI behaviour;
- autonomy anomaly;
- human-control concern;
- environmental change;
- material configuration change;
- model or data change;
- dependency change;
- mission change;
- emerging threat;
- evidence gap;
- revalidation requirement;
- reauthorisation requirement.

---

## 5. Review Frequency

Review frequency should be proportionate to:

- consequence;
- autonomy;
- rate of change;
- threat;
- environmental variability;
- operational tempo;
- evidence stability.

High-consequence capabilities may require more frequent or more independent review.

---

## 6. Review Scope

A review may be:

### Focused

Limited to a specific assurance concern.

### Domain Review

Focused on one assurance domain such as security, autonomy or human control.

### Capability Review

Covers the complete assurance position of a capability.

### Mission Review

Assesses assurance in relation to a particular mission or use case.

### System-of-Systems Review

Assesses interactions among multiple AI-enabled capabilities.

---

## 7. Review Inputs

Relevant inputs may include:

- assurance claims;
- risk assessments;
- authorisation;
- TEVV evidence;
- operational monitoring;
- performance data;
- incidents;
- interventions;
- configuration records;
- environmental observations;
- security findings;
- human-factors evidence;
- lessons learned;
- open corrective actions.

---

## 8. Review Questions

A review should consider:

1. What was previously claimed?
2. What evidence supported the claim?
3. What has changed?
4. What evidence has emerged?
5. Are assumptions still valid?
6. Are controls still effective?
7. Has risk changed?
8. Has human control changed?
9. Has autonomy changed?
10. Does the operating environment remain suitable?
11. Is continued authorisation justified?

---

## 9. Assurance Review Process

A structured review may follow:

**Scope → Collect Evidence → Assess → Challenge → Identify Findings → Determine Impact → Decide → Record → Follow Up**

The process should be proportionate to the materiality of the review.

---

## 10. Evidence Review

Reviewers should assess evidence for:

- relevance;
- sufficiency;
- currency;
- coverage;
- quality;
- independence;
- consistency;
- uncertainty.

Contradictory or negative evidence should be included.

---

## 11. Assumption Review

Material assumptions supporting assurance should be explicitly identified and reassessed.

Examples include assumptions about:

- operating environment;
- data;
- threat;
- human competence;
- communications;
- dependencies;
- autonomy;
- system behaviour.

An invalidated assumption may require revalidation even where performance has not yet visibly degraded.

---

## 12. Risk Review

The review should consider whether:

- identified risks remain valid;
- risk levels have changed;
- new risks have emerged;
- controls remain effective;
- residual risk remains acceptable;
- risk acceptance remains appropriate.

Risk changes should be linked to the applicable authority.

---

## 13. Control Effectiveness

Controls should be assessed for:

- existence;
- implementation;
- effectiveness;
- continued applicability;
- operational usability.

A documented control that is not effective in practice should not be treated as an assurance success.

---

## 14. Human-Control Review

Where applicable, the review should assess:

- human decision rights;
- competence;
- workload;
- situational awareness;
- intervention;
- override;
- automation bias;
- human-AI disagreement;
- ability to stop or restrict the capability.

---

## 15. Autonomy Review

The review should determine whether:

- authorised autonomy remains appropriate;
- actual autonomy matches authorisation;
- autonomy transitions behave as expected;
- autonomy boundaries remain effective;
- human intervention remains adequate.

---

## 16. Environmental Review

Review should consider whether:

- the operational environment remains within the assessed envelope;
- new conditions have emerged;
- environmental assumptions remain valid;
- degraded or disconnected conditions remain supported;
- adversarial conditions have changed.

---

## 17. Security Review

Security review should consider:

- emerging threats;
- security incidents;
- control effectiveness;
- model integrity;
- data integrity;
- dependency security;
- interface security;
- supply-chain concerns.

---

## 18. Operational Evidence Review

Operational experience should be compared against pre-employment evidence.

Differences should be investigated rather than automatically treated as either acceptable or unacceptable.

Particular attention should be given to:

- unexpected failure modes;
- recurring anomalies;
- intervention frequency;
- performance drift;
- human-control issues;
- environmental limitations.

---

## 19. Independent Challenge

Independent challenge provides a deliberate mechanism for questioning assurance conclusions.

It should be proportionate to:

- consequence;
- autonomy;
- organisational risk;
- uncertainty;
- complexity.

---

## 20. Independence

Independence may be established through:

- separation from the development team;
- separation from operational ownership;
- independent technical review;
- independent safety review;
- independent security review;
- governance-level challenge.

The appropriate model depends on organisational structure.

---

## 21. Conflict of Interest

Reviewers should disclose material conflicts of interest.

Where independence is required, individuals with significant conflicts should not be the sole basis for the assurance conclusion.

---

## 22. Independent Challenge Scope

Independent challenge may examine:

- assurance claims;
- evidence;
- methodology;
- assumptions;
- risk treatment;
- controls;
- human authority;
- autonomy;
- environmental suitability;
- security;
- operational evidence;
- decision quality.

---

## 23. Challenge Questions

Independent reviewers should be able to ask:

- What evidence supports this claim?
- What evidence contradicts it?
- What assumptions are being made?
- What has changed?
- What remains unknown?
- What happens if the assumption fails?
- Is the evidence representative?
- Can the result be reproduced?
- Is the human-control mechanism effective?
- Is continued authorisation justified?

---

## 24. Red-Team Challenge

Where appropriate, independent challenge may include structured adversarial examination.

This may test:

- assumptions;
- failure modes;
- boundaries;
- human control;
- security;
- autonomy;
- environmental limitations.

Such activity should remain controlled and governed.

---

## 25. Review Findings

Findings may be classified according to materiality.

### Observation

A condition worth recording but not currently requiring corrective action.

### Minor Finding

A limited deficiency that does not materially invalidate assurance.

### Significant Finding

A deficiency that requires corrective action or increased controls.

### Critical Finding

A condition that may materially invalidate assurance or continued operational employment.

---

## 26. Finding Assessment

Each material finding should consider:

- cause;
- consequence;
- scope;
- recurrence;
- affected assurance claim;
- affected risk;
- control effectiveness;
- required action.

---

## 27. Assurance Impact

Each significant finding should be assessed for its effect on:

- assurance confidence;
- authorisation;
- operational boundaries;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- mission suitability.

---

## 28. Review Outcomes

Possible outcomes include:

- assurance maintained;
- assurance maintained with conditions;
- enhanced monitoring;
- additional controls;
- restricted employment;
- reduced autonomy;
- additional testing;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation;
- suspension.

---

## 29. Corrective Actions

Material findings should result in defined actions with:

- owner;
- action;
- priority;
- target date;
- acceptance criteria;
- verification method.

---

## 30. Corrective Action Verification

Closure should require evidence that the corrective action has achieved its intended result.

Administrative completion alone should not be sufficient.

---

## 31. Escalation

Critical findings should be escalated immediately to the appropriate authority.

Escalation may include:

- operational authority;
- technical authority;
- safety authority;
- security authority;
- OAIA;
- governance committee.

---

## 32. Review Decision Rights

The organisation should define who may:

- initiate a review;
- appoint reviewers;
- accept findings;
- require corrective action;
- impose restrictions;
- require revalidation;
- recommend suspension;
- approve restoration.

---

## 33. Review Records

A review record should contain, where appropriate:

- scope;
- date;
- reviewers;
- assurance claims;
- evidence reviewed;
- assumptions;
- findings;
- challenges;
- conclusions;
- actions;
- authority decision.

---

## 34. Evidence of Independent Challenge

Where independent challenge is required, the organisation should retain evidence that:

- the challenge occurred;
- reviewers had appropriate competence;
- conflicts were considered;
- material challenges were addressed;
- unresolved disagreements were escalated.

---

## 35. Disagreement

If reviewers disagree with the assurance conclusion, the disagreement should be documented.

The organisation should not suppress material professional disagreement merely to achieve a single consensus position.

Where necessary, the issue should be escalated to the appropriate governance authority.

---

## 36. Review Transparency

Assurance reviews should clearly distinguish:

- evidence;
- interpretation;
- judgement;
- assumption;
- recommendation;
- final authority decision.

This reduces ambiguity about how the conclusion was reached.

---

## 37. Review and Authorisation

An assurance review may inform an authorisation decision but should not automatically constitute authorisation.

The distinction remains:

**Review → Assurance Conclusion → Authority Decision**

---

## 38. Review and Revalidation

Where assurance claims are materially challenged, the review should determine whether revalidation is required.

Revalidation should be proportionate to the affected claim and change.

---

## 39. Review and Reauthorisation

Where a review identifies a material change to the authorised operating basis, reauthorisation may be required.

The review should identify the relevant changed:

- mission;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- configuration;
- risk;
- conditions.

---

## 40. Review Closure

A review should be closed only when:

- findings have been dispositioned;
- required actions are assigned;
- assurance impact is determined;
- authority decisions are recorded;
- unresolved issues are transferred appropriately.

---

## 41. Periodic Governance Review

At organisational level, assurance reviews should be analysed for recurring patterns.

These may reveal:

- systemic control weaknesses;
- repeated evidence gaps;
- common human-control problems;
- recurring supplier issues;
- recurring environmental limitations;
- inadequate monitoring.

---

## 42. Lessons from Reviews

Review findings should feed into:

- requirements;
- controls;
- TEVV;
- monitoring;
- training;
- authorisation;
- policy;
- procurement;
- future system design.

---

## 43. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. What triggers an assurance review?
2. How often are reviews conducted?
3. Who can initiate a review?
4. Who can conduct the review?
5. When is independent challenge mandatory?
6. How is independence established?
7. How are conflicts of interest managed?
8. What evidence is reviewed?
9. How are disagreements handled?
10. How are findings classified?
11. Who accepts findings?
12. Who verifies corrective actions?
13. What findings require revalidation?
14. What findings require reauthorisation or suspension?
15. How are recurring findings identified?

---

## 44. Core Rule

> **Assurance reviews shall periodically and when triggered examine whether current evidence, risks, controls, assumptions, human control, autonomy, environmental suitability, security and operational experience continue to support assurance claims. For consequential capabilities, independent challenge shall provide a credible mechanism for questioning those conclusions. Material findings shall be attributable, acted upon, verified and capable of triggering restriction, revalidation, reauthorisation or suspension.**

---

## 45. Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance Claim → Monitoring → Review → Independent Challenge → Finding → Decision → Corrective Action → Verification → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Learning**
