# 04 — Assurance Findings and Corrective Action

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how D-AIGAAF identifies, evaluates, records, prioritises, resolves and verifies findings that affect the continuing assurance of Defence AI capabilities.

The objective is to ensure that weaknesses identified through monitoring, testing, incidents, audits, reviews or operational experience result in proportionate and verified action.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **Material assurance findings shall be identified, assessed, assigned, tracked and resolved through a controlled corrective-action process. Corrective action shall address the underlying cause and shall be verified for effectiveness. Administrative closure alone shall not restore assurance confidence.**

---

## 3. Sources of Findings

Findings may originate from:

- TEVV;
- operational monitoring;
- assurance reviews;
- independent challenge;
- audits;
- incidents;
- security assessments;
- human-factors assessments;
- environmental assessments;
- supplier assessments;
- configuration reviews;
- lessons learned;
- user feedback.

---

## 4. Finding Object

A material finding should establish:

**Observation → Condition → Requirement/Claim → Impact → Cause → Action → Verification → Closure**

This structure supports traceability from the observed problem to the resulting governance response.

---

## 5. Finding Definition

A finding exists where evidence identifies a condition that:

- fails to meet a requirement;
- weakens a control;
- reduces assurance confidence;
- contradicts an assurance claim;
- creates a new or increased risk;
- identifies an unresolved limitation;
- reveals a material process deficiency.

---

## 6. Finding Classification

Findings may be classified as:

### Observation

A condition worth recording but not currently requiring corrective action.

### Minor Finding

A limited deficiency with no immediate material effect on assurance.

### Significant Finding

A deficiency requiring defined corrective action or additional controls.

### Critical Finding

A condition that may materially invalidate assurance or create unacceptable operational risk.

Classification should consider consequence, likelihood, scope and assurance impact.

---

## 7. Finding Severity

Severity should consider:

- potential consequence;
- actual consequence;
- affected population or scope;
- reversibility;
- recurrence;
- autonomy;
- human-control implications;
- mission criticality.

Severity should not be based solely on technical impact.

---

## 8. Assurance Impact

Each material finding should determine whether it affects:

- assurance confidence;
- assurance claims;
- risk;
- controls;
- operational boundaries;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- authorisation.

---

## 9. Immediate Protective Action

Where a finding creates immediate unacceptable risk, protective action should not wait for completion of the corrective-action process.

Possible actions include:

- increased monitoring;
- functional restriction;
- reduced autonomy;
- human control;
- safe state;
- suspension.

---

## 10. Root Cause

Where proportionate, the organisation should identify the underlying cause of the finding.

Possible causes include:

- requirements deficiency;
- design deficiency;
- data issue;
- model limitation;
- software defect;
- integration failure;
- configuration error;
- human-factor issue;
- training deficiency;
- environmental assumption;
- security weakness;
- supplier issue;
- governance failure.

---

## 11. Contributing Factors

In addition to root cause, reviews should identify relevant contributing factors.

These may include:

- workload;
- communication limitations;
- degraded conditions;
- unclear authority;
- inadequate monitoring;
- insufficient evidence;
- unexpected interactions.

---

## 12. Corrective Action

Corrective action should address the identified deficiency.

Examples include:

- correcting a defect;
- improving a control;
- changing configuration;
- improving data;
- updating documentation;
- increasing monitoring;
- reducing autonomy;
- improving training;
- conducting additional TEVV;
- changing operational restrictions.

---

## 13. Preventive Action

Where a finding indicates a broader systemic risk, preventive action should address conditions that could cause similar findings elsewhere.

Preventive action may apply to:

- related capabilities;
- common suppliers;
- shared controls;
- organisational procedures;
- training;
- governance.

---

## 14. Action Ownership

Each material action should have:

- accountable owner;
- responsible implementer;
- required outcome;
- priority;
- target completion;
- verification method.

Responsibility should not remain ambiguous.

---

## 15. Action Priority

Priority should reflect:

- consequence;
- urgency;
- exposure;
- likelihood;
- affected assurance claim;
- ability to compensate through controls.

Critical findings should receive immediate governance attention.

---

## 16. Compensating Controls

Where permanent corrective action cannot be implemented immediately, temporary controls may be used.

Examples include:

- restricted mission;
- restricted environment;
- reduced autonomy;
- additional human review;
- increased monitoring;
- limited functionality.

Compensating controls should have defined owners and review dates.

---

## 17. Corrective Action Acceptance Criteria

Every material action should define what constitutes successful completion.

Acceptance criteria may include:

- technical correction demonstrated;
- control effectiveness demonstrated;
- additional testing passed;
- human usability demonstrated;
- security verification completed;
- environmental suitability confirmed.

---

## 18. Verification

Corrective action should be independently or appropriately verified according to consequence.

Verification should establish that:

- the action was implemented;
- the underlying issue was addressed;
- the intended result was achieved;
- unintended effects were not introduced.

---

## 19. Re-Test

Where a finding concerns technical or AI behaviour, corrective action may require additional:

- testing;
- evaluation;
- verification;
- validation;
- security testing;
- human-factors evaluation.

The required level should reflect the materiality of the change.

---

## 20. Revalidation

Corrective action should trigger revalidation where it changes or challenges a previously demonstrated assurance claim.

Revalidation should establish whether the original claim remains valid.

---

## 21. Reauthorisation

Reauthorisation may be required where corrective action materially changes:

- capability;
- mission;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- configuration;
- conditions;
- risk.

Corrective action does not itself create authority to resume employment.

---

## 22. Suspension

Where assurance cannot be restored through immediate controls, the capability should remain restricted or suspended until sufficient evidence exists.

---

## 23. Finding Status

A finding may progress through:

**Open → Assessed → Action Assigned → Action Implemented → Verification Pending → Verified → Closed**

Additional states may include:

- Accepted with Conditions;
- Deferred;
- Superseded;
- Rejected with Justification.

---

## 24. Finding Closure

A material finding should not be closed merely because an action was completed.

Closure should require:

- evidence of implementation;
- verification;
- assurance-impact assessment;
- responsible authority acceptance.

---

## 25. Overdue Findings

Overdue material findings should be visible to responsible authorities.

Repeated delays may indicate:

- inadequate resourcing;
- governance weakness;
- underestimated risk;
- ineffective ownership.

---

## 26. Recurring Findings

Repeated findings should be assessed for systemic causes.

Recurring findings may require:

- broader corrective action;
- policy change;
- architecture change;
- supplier action;
- additional assurance;
- governance escalation.

---

## 27. Cross-Capability Findings

Where a finding may affect multiple AI capabilities, the organisation should determine whether a common corrective action is required.

A local fix should not conceal a systemic issue.

---

## 28. Supplier Findings

Where a finding originates from a supplier or external dependency, corrective action should establish:

- supplier responsibility;
- required evidence;
- verification;
- timelines;
- interim controls;
- escalation.

Supplier claims should not be accepted without appropriate evidence.

---

## 29. Human-Control Findings

Findings involving human control may concern:

- unclear authority;
- excessive workload;
- ineffective intervention;
- poor situational awareness;
- automation bias;
- inadequate training;
- inability to override.

Such findings may require operational restriction even where technical performance remains acceptable.

---

## 30. Autonomy Findings

Autonomy findings may concern:

- unexpected transitions;
- boundary violations;
- excessive autonomy;
- intervention failure;
- unsafe autonomous behaviour.

Depending on consequence, these may require immediate autonomy reduction or suspension.

---

## 31. Security Findings

Security findings may involve:

- compromised integrity;
- unauthorised access;
- data manipulation;
- model compromise;
- dependency compromise;
- failed security controls.

Security findings should be coordinated with applicable incident-response processes.

---

## 32. Environmental Findings

Environmental findings may identify:

- unsupported conditions;
- environmental assumptions that failed;
- performance degradation;
- inadequate monitoring;
- inadequate operating boundaries.

These should feed into operational-environment governance and assurance.

---

## 33. Finding Escalation

Escalation should be based on:

- severity;
- consequence;
- urgency;
- assurance impact;
- recurrence;
- inability to control risk.

Escalation routes should be predefined.

---

## 34. Finding Disagreement

Where responsible personnel disagree with a finding:

- the disagreement should be recorded;
- supporting evidence should be considered;
- unresolved material disagreement should be escalated;
- closure should not depend on suppressing legitimate challenge.

---

## 35. Finding Records

A material finding record should contain:

- finding ID;
- date;
- source;
- capability;
- mission;
- configuration;
- observation;
- requirement or claim affected;
- severity;
- impact;
- root cause;
- action;
- owner;
- due date;
- verification;
- status;
- closure decision.

---

## 36. Corrective Action Register

The organisation should maintain a register of material corrective actions.

The register should support:

- prioritisation;
- ownership;
- tracking;
- overdue monitoring;
- escalation;
- assurance reporting.

---

---

## 38. Finding Trends

Trend analysis should identify:

- increasing findings;
- recurring categories;
- common causes;
- overdue actions;
- control failures;
- supplier patterns.

Trends may indicate declining assurance even where individual findings appear manageable.

---

## 39. Corrective Action Effectiveness

The organisation should assess whether corrective action:

- reduced the original risk;
- restored the affected control;
- restored assurance confidence;
- introduced new risks;
- resolved the root cause.

---

## 40. Lessons Learned

Material findings should contribute to organisational learning.

Lessons may affect:

- requirements;
- architecture;
- controls;
- TEVV;
- training;
- monitoring;
- authorisation;
- procurement;
- policy.

---

## 41. Relationship with Continuous Assurance

Findings provide a direct feedback mechanism:

**Observation → Finding → Corrective Action → Verification → Assurance Update**

The corrective-action process therefore forms part of the continuous assurance cycle.

---

## 42. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. What constitutes an assurance finding?
2. How are findings classified?
3. Who determines severity?
4. Who owns corrective action?
5. What immediate action is required for critical findings?
6. How are temporary controls governed?
7. What constitutes successful corrective action?
8. Who verifies completion?
9. When is revalidation required?
10. When is reauthorisation required?
11. How are recurring findings identified?
12. How are systemic findings handled?
13. How are overdue actions escalated?
14. How are findings retained as assurance evidence?

---

## 43. Core Rule

> **Material assurance findings shall result in proportionate, owned and verifiable corrective action. Immediate protective measures shall be applied where necessary, and unresolved findings shall remain visible to the responsible authority. Corrective action shall not be considered complete until its effectiveness has been demonstrated and the resulting assurance impact has been assessed. Material findings shall be capable of triggering restriction, suspension, revalidation or reauthorisation.**

---

## 44. Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance Claim → Monitoring → Finding → Root Cause → Corrective/Preventive Action → Verification → Assurance Update → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Learning**
