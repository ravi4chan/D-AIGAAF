# 08 — Assurance Records, Reporting and Dashboard

## 1. Purpose

This document defines governance requirements for maintaining assurance records and communicating the continuing assurance position of Defence AI capabilities to appropriate decision-makers.

The objective is to provide an authoritative, traceable and proportionate view of assurance status without reducing complex evidence, uncertainty or material findings to a single unexplained score.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **Assurance records and reporting shall provide sufficient, accurate, traceable and appropriately current evidence to support governance decisions. Assurance dashboards shall communicate material conditions and trends without concealing critical findings, uncertainty, unresolved assurance gaps or limitations behind aggregate status indicators.**

---

## 3. Assurance Record

An assurance record should establish:

- what is being assured;
- under what conditions;
- what evidence supports the assurance;
- what assumptions apply;
- what limitations exist;
- what findings remain open;
- what authority applies;
- when the assurance was last reviewed;
- what events may invalidate it.

---

## 4. Assurance Record Structure

A material assurance record may contain:

1. capability identity;
2. mission;
3. environment;
4. configuration;
5. autonomy;
6. human authority;
7. assurance claims;
8. risks;
9. controls;
10. evidence;
11. indicators;
12. findings;
13. incidents;
14. changes;
15. assurance conclusion;
16. conditions;
17. review date;
18. responsible authority.

---

## 5. Record Traceability

Material assurance information should be traceable through:

**Requirement → Risk → Control → Test/Evaluation → Evidence → Assurance Claim → Indicator → Review → Decision**

Operational evidence may extend the chain through:

**Employment → Monitoring → Observation → Evidence → Assurance**

---

## 6. Evidence Register

The organisation should maintain an evidence register identifying:

- evidence ID;
- assurance claim;
- source;
- date;
- conditions;
- configuration;
- method;
- result;
- limitations;
- reviewer;
- status.

The register should identify evidence gaps and ageing where relevant.

---

## 7. Assurance Claim Register

Material assurance claims should be maintained in a structured register.

Each claim may include:

- claim ID;
- statement;
- affected capability;
- conditions;
- supporting evidence;
- confidence;
- limitations;
- owner;
- review date;
- status.

---

## 8. Finding Register

The assurance process should maintain a register of material findings.

The register should support:

- severity;
- ownership;
- corrective action;
- due dates;
- verification;
- assurance impact;
- escalation.

---

## 9. Assurance Decision Record

Material assurance decisions should identify:

- decision;
- authority;
- date;
- evidence considered;
- material uncertainty;
- findings;
- conditions;
- rationale where required;
- resulting action.

---

## 10. Assurance History

Historical assurance decisions should remain available where required to understand:

- how confidence changed;
- why restrictions were imposed;
- why authorisation changed;
- how findings were resolved;
- how evidence evolved.

---

## 11. Reporting Objective

Assurance reporting should support decisions about:

- continued employment;
- restrictions;
- additional controls;
- increased monitoring;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation;
- suspension;
- retirement.

Reports should focus on decision-relevant information.

---

## 12. Reporting Levels

Reporting may be structured at:

### Operational Level

Immediate assurance conditions affecting current employment.

### Capability Level

Overall assurance of a specific AI capability.

### Organisational Level

Portfolio-level assurance across multiple capabilities.

### Strategic Level

Major systemic risks, trends and governance concerns.

---

## 13. Operational Assurance Report

An operational report may include:

- current mission;
- active configuration;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human-control status;
- performance;
- uncertainty;
- security;
- dependencies;
- incidents;
- restrictions;
- intervention events.

---

## 14. Capability Assurance Report

A capability-level report may include:

- assurance claims;
- evidence status;
- risk;
- performance;
- human control;
- autonomy;
- environment;
- security;
- dependencies;
- open findings;
- assurance debt;
- recent changes;
- current conclusion.

---

## 15. Portfolio Assurance Report

An organisational portfolio report may identify:

- capabilities by assurance status;
- critical findings;
- recurring weaknesses;
- ageing evidence;
- systemic risks;
- suspended capabilities;
- major dependency concerns;
- revalidation workload.

---

## 16. Strategic Reporting

Strategic reporting should identify:

- emerging risks;
- systemic assurance trends;
- common control weaknesses;
- technology trends;
- threat trends;
- workforce issues;
- supplier concerns;
- policy gaps.

Strategic reporting should not expose information beyond the authority and classification appropriate to the audience.

---

## 17. Dashboard Purpose

An assurance dashboard should provide a concise view of the current assurance position while allowing authorised users to access underlying evidence.

The dashboard is an interface to assurance information, not a substitute for it.

---

## 18. Dashboard Domains

A dashboard may include:

| Domain | Example Status |
|---|---|
| Mission | Green / Amber / Red |
| Risk | Green / Amber / Red |
| Performance | Green / Amber / Red |
| Data | Green / Amber / Red |
| Human Control | Green / Amber / Red |
| Autonomy | Green / Amber / Red |
| Environment | Green / Amber / Red |
| Security | Green / Amber / Red |
| Dependencies | Green / Amber / Red |
| TEVV Evidence | Green / Amber / Red |
| Operational Evidence | Green / Amber / Red |
| Incidents | Green / Amber / Red |
| Findings | Green / Amber / Red |

---

## 19. Dashboard Interpretation

Status indicators should have documented definitions.

For example:

### Green

Current evidence supports continued confidence under defined conditions.

### Amber

Material limitation or deviation exists and requires management.

### Red

Assurance is materially insufficient or a critical condition requires immediate action.

---

## 20. Critical Gating Conditions

Some conditions should not be masked by an overall positive status.

Examples include:

- loss of meaningful human control;
- invalid authorisation;
- critical security compromise;
- critical boundary violation;
- unacceptable residual risk;
- failure of a mandatory safety control.

Such conditions may require independent gating status.

---

## 21. Uncertainty Reporting

Reports should explicitly identify material uncertainty.

Uncertainty may concern:

- evidence;
- performance;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human control;
- security;
- dependencies;
- mission effectiveness.

Uncertainty should not disappear into a single assurance score.

---

## 22. Trend Reporting

Reports should identify material trends in:

- performance;
- anomalies;
- interventions;
- incidents;
- findings;
- assurance debt;
- evidence ageing;
- security events;
- dependency failures.

Trend information may provide earlier warning than individual threshold breaches.

---

## 23. Comparative Reporting

Where appropriate, assurance reporting may compare:

- current versus baseline;
- current versus previous review;
- expected versus observed performance;
- authorised versus actual autonomy;
- assessed versus observed environment.

Comparisons should preserve context.

---

## 24. Reporting Thresholds

Material events should trigger reporting according to predefined criteria.

Examples include:

- critical finding;
- significant incident;
- material performance degradation;
- unexpected autonomy;
- security compromise;
- environmental excursion;
- human-control concern;
- material configuration change.

---

## 25. Escalation Reporting

Material assurance concerns should be escalated to the appropriate authority.

Reports should state:

- what happened;
- why it matters;
- confidence in the assessment;
- current controls;
- immediate action;
- recommended decision.

---

## 26. Reporting Frequency

Frequency should reflect:

- consequence;
- autonomy;
- operational tempo;
- volatility;
- threat;
- assurance status.

Higher-risk or higher-consequence capabilities may require more frequent reporting.

---

## 27. Event-Driven Reporting

A report should be generated or updated following material:

- incidents;
- findings;
- changes;
- autonomy anomalies;
- security events;
- environmental changes;
- assurance reviews.

---

## 28. Reporting Accuracy

Assurance reports should distinguish:

- observed facts;
- evidence;
- analysis;
- judgement;
- assumptions;
- recommendation;
- authority decision.

This reduces the risk of presenting interpretation as established fact.

---

## 29. Reporting Limitations

Reports should identify material limitations such as:

- incomplete evidence;
- unavailable data;
- untested conditions;
- conflicting evidence;
- uncertain attribution;
- incomplete operational records.

---

## 30. Report Approval

Material assurance reports should be reviewed and approved according to organisational authority.

Approval should not imply certainty beyond the evidence.

---

## 31. Record Integrity

Assurance records should be protected against:

- unauthorised modification;
- deletion;
- corruption;
- inappropriate access.

Material changes to assurance records should be traceable.

---

## 32. Version Control

Assurance reports should have controlled:

- version;
- date;
- author;
- reviewer;
- approver;
- status.

Superseded reports should remain identifiable where retention requires them.

---

## 33. Access Control

Access to assurance information should follow:

- role;
- authority;
- need;
- information sensitivity;
- applicable security requirements.

---

## 34. Evidence Linkage

Where technically possible, reports should link directly to underlying:

- evidence;
- findings;
- incidents;
- monitoring data;
- change records;
- revalidation records.

This supports efficient challenge and audit.

---

## 35. Dashboard Data Quality

Dashboard information should itself be subject to controls for:

- completeness;
- accuracy;
- timeliness;
- integrity;
- provenance.

Incorrect dashboard status can create false assurance.

---

## 36. Dashboard Ownership

Each dashboard domain should have an accountable owner for:

- data;
- status;
- interpretation;
- update;
- escalation.

---

## 37. Dashboard Limitations

Dashboards should not:

- replace professional judgement;
- conceal uncertainty;
- hide critical findings;
- imply certainty where evidence is weak;
- automatically determine authorisation.

---

## 38. Assurance Reporting and Authorisation

Assurance reporting informs operational-authorisation decisions.

The relationship remains:

**Evidence → Assurance Assessment → Report → Authority Decision**

A favourable dashboard status does not itself create operational authority.

---

## 39. Assurance Reporting and Revalidation

Reports should identify conditions that may require revalidation, including:

- evidence degradation;
- performance drift;
- environmental change;
- autonomy change;
- human-control concerns;
- security events;
- material configuration changes.

---

## 40. Assurance Reporting and Reauthorisation

Where the authorised operating basis changes, reports should identify the need for reauthorisation.

Relevant changes include:

- mission;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- configuration;
- risk;
- conditions.

---

## 41. Assurance Reporting and Audit

Assurance records should provide sufficient evidence for authorised audit and review.

Auditors should be able to trace material conclusions to underlying evidence.

---

## 42. Reporting and Lessons Learned

Reporting should support organisational learning by identifying:

- recurring findings;
- common causes;
- successful controls;
- emerging risks;
- unresolved weaknesses.

---

## 43. Records Retention

Retention should be defined according to:

- legal requirements;
- policy;
- mission consequence;
- assurance needs;
- audit;
- incident investigation.

---

## 44. Record Disposal

Disposal should be:

- authorised;
- controlled;
- documented;
- consistent with applicable retention requirements.

Records subject to investigation or unresolved assurance decisions should be preserved.

---

## 45. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. What assurance records must be maintained?
2. Who owns assurance records?
3. How are assurance claims recorded?
4. How is evidence linked to claims?
5. How are findings and corrective actions tracked?
6. How are assurance decisions recorded?
7. What should appear on an assurance dashboard?
8. Which conditions require independent gating?
9. How is uncertainty reported?
10. How are trends reported?
11. Who receives assurance reports?
12. What triggers event-driven reporting?
13. How is dashboard data validated?
14. How are reports version-controlled?
15. How do reports support revalidation and reauthorisation?

---

## 46. Core Rule

> **Assurance records shall provide an authoritative and traceable basis for understanding the continuing assurance position of a Defence AI capability. Reporting and dashboards shall communicate evidence, material findings, uncertainty, trends and decision-relevant conditions without masking critical weaknesses through aggregation. Material assurance conclusions shall remain traceable to their underlying evidence and attributable to the responsible authority.**

---

## 47. Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance Claim → Indicators → Review → Findings → Assurance Decision → Report → Authority → Conditions → Monitoring → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Learning**
