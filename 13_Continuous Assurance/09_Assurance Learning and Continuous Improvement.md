# 09 — Assurance Learning and Continuous Improvement

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how D-AIGAAF converts assurance evidence, operational experience, incidents, findings, changes and lessons into sustained improvement of Defence AI governance and assurance.

The objective is to ensure that assurance is not a static certification activity but a continuing learning process that improves capability, controls, evidence, human performance and governance over time.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **Assurance shall operate as a learning system. Evidence, operational experience, incidents, findings, changes, failures, successes and emerging risks shall be systematically assessed and translated into proportionate improvements to requirements, controls, testing, monitoring, training, authorisation and governance. Lessons shall be verified for effectiveness rather than merely recorded.**

---

## 3. Continuous Improvement Cycle

The assurance learning cycle is:

**Observe → Capture → Assess → Learn → Prioritise → Act → Verify → Institutionalise → Monitor**

The cycle should operate at operational, capability, organisational and strategic levels.

---

## 4. Sources of Learning

Learning may arise from:

- operational employment;
- assurance monitoring;
- TEVV;
- incidents;
- near misses;
- assurance findings;
- corrective actions;
- security events;
- environmental changes;
- autonomy events;
- human-control events;
- performance degradation;
- data issues;
- supplier events;
- configuration changes;
- independent reviews;
- exercises;
- user feedback;
- emerging threats;
- external research;
- regulatory or policy developments.

---

## 5. Learning Object

A lesson should identify:

- what was observed;
- what was expected;
- what differed;
- why it mattered;
- contributing factors;
- affected assumptions;
- affected controls;
- affected assurance claims;
- recommended action;
- responsible owner;
- verification method.

---

## 6. Operational Lessons

Operational lessons should capture experience from actual employment, including:

- successful use;
- unexpected behaviour;
- failure;
- degraded operation;
- intervention;
- override;
- autonomy transition;
- human-AI disagreement;
- uncertainty;
- environmental effects;
- security events.

The purpose is to improve future assurance rather than assign blame without analysis.

---

## 7. Near Misses

Near misses should be treated as valuable assurance evidence.

A near miss may reveal:

- latent control weakness;
- inadequate boundary;
- misleading output;
- insufficient human oversight;
- environmental limitation;
- monitoring gap;
- training deficiency.

---

## 8. Incident Learning

Incident investigations should determine whether lessons affect:

- risk;
- requirements;
- controls;
- evidence;
- operating boundaries;
- human authority;
- autonomy;
- security;
- monitoring;
- authorisation.

Material lessons should trigger proportionate assurance action.

---

## 9. Findings as Learning

Recurring assurance findings should be analysed for systemic causes.

Examples include:

- repeated evidence gaps;
- recurring human-control weaknesses;
- repeated configuration errors;
- repeated supplier issues;
- repeated monitoring failures.

Systemic issues should not be treated as isolated administrative findings.

---

## 10. Corrective and Preventive Action

Learning may result in:

### Corrective Action

Addresses an identified problem.

### Preventive Action

Reduces the likelihood of recurrence or similar failure.

### Systemic Improvement

Changes a broader process, control, standard or governance mechanism.

---

## 11. Learning Prioritisation

Lessons should be prioritised according to:

- consequence;
- likelihood;
- recurrence;
- scope;
- uncertainty;
- affected capabilities;
- assurance impact;
- urgency.

High-consequence lessons should receive appropriate priority even where occurrence is rare.

---

## 12. Cross-Capability Learning

Lessons from one Defence AI capability should be assessed for applicability to other capabilities.

Potentially transferable lessons include:

- common control weaknesses;
- model failure patterns;
- human factors;
- security vulnerabilities;
- environmental limitations;
- autonomy risks;
- supplier dependencies.

---

## 13. Cross-Domain Learning

Learning should also inform related governance domains, including:

- risk;
- AI lifecycle;
- data;
- security;
- supply chain;
- human authority;
- TEVV;
- operational environment;
- operational authorisation;
- operational employment.

---

## 14. Learning and Assurance Claims

Lessons should be assessed for their effect on existing assurance claims.

Possible outcomes include:

- no material effect;
- increased confidence;
- reduced confidence;
- evidence gap;
- claim limitation;
- claim invalidation.

---

## 15. Learning and Risk

Lessons should be assessed for effects on:

- known risks;
- residual risk;
- risk controls;
- assumptions;
- risk acceptance.

New or materially changed risks should enter the appropriate risk-governance process.

---

## 16. Learning and Human Control

Lessons should examine whether human control remains effective.

Relevant factors include:

- workload;
- training;
- situational awareness;
- automation bias;
- interface design;
- intervention;
- override;
- decision time;
- human-AI disagreement.

---

## 17. Learning and Autonomy

Lessons should assess whether observed autonomy behaviour remains consistent with:

- authorised autonomy;
- operational boundaries;
- transition rules;
- human authority;
- expected failure behaviour.

Unexpected autonomy behaviour should receive heightened scrutiny.

---

## 18. Learning and Environment

Operational experience should be used to refine understanding of:

- operating envelope;
- environmental assumptions;
- degraded conditions;
- disconnected operation;
- adversarial conditions;
- sensor limitations;
- communication conditions.

Observed environmental behaviour should inform future TEVV and monitoring.

---

## 19. Learning and Security

Security lessons should inform:

- threat models;
- security controls;
- adversarial testing;
- monitoring;
- incident response;
- supplier assurance;
- configuration management.

Material security lessons may require immediate protective action.

---

## 20. Learning and Dependencies

Lessons should identify failures or weaknesses involving:

- communications;
- infrastructure;
- data sources;
- external services;
- suppliers;
- hardware;
- software;
- human expertise.

Dependency lessons should inform resilience and continuity planning.

---

## 21. Learning from Positive Evidence

Continuous improvement should not focus only on failures.

Successful controls and effective practices should also be captured where they:

- reduce risk;
- improve human control;
- improve resilience;
- improve evidence quality;
- improve operational effectiveness.

Positive lessons should be validated before being generalised.

---

## 22. Evidence-Based Learning

A lesson should distinguish between:

- observed fact;
- interpretation;
- hypothesis;
- conclusion;
- recommendation.

Lessons should not become governance requirements solely because of unsupported assumptions.

---

## 23. Independent Challenge

Material lessons should be subject to independent challenge where appropriate.

Challenge should test:

- evidence;
- causation;
- applicability;
- significance;
- recommended action.

---

## 24. Lesson Verification

A lesson should not be considered fully implemented until the resulting action has been assessed for effectiveness.

Verification may include:

- testing;
- monitoring;
- inspection;
- operational evaluation;
- review;
- independent assessment.

---

## 25. Learning Effectiveness

The organisation should assess whether learning has actually improved:

- risk;
- performance;
- human control;
- autonomy assurance;
- resilience;
- security;
- evidence quality;
- governance.

---

## 26. Recurring Lessons

Repeated lessons should trigger consideration of:

- systemic corrective action;
- additional controls;
- process redesign;
- training changes;
- technology changes;
- procurement changes;
- policy changes.

Recurring lessons should be escalated where local corrective action is insufficient.

---

## 27. Learning from Change

Change-related outcomes should be compared with the original change-impact assessment.

This helps identify:

- underestimated impacts;
- unexpected interactions;
- ineffective assumptions;
- inadequate testing;
- missing indicators.

---

## 28. Learning from Revalidation

Revalidation should produce lessons about:

- evidence sufficiency;
- testing methods;
- assumptions;
- failure modes;
- operational boundaries;
- assurance gaps.

These lessons should improve future revalidation activities.

---

## 29. Learning from Reauthorisation

Reauthorisation decisions should be reviewed for:

- quality of evidence;
- clarity of conditions;
- effectiveness of restrictions;
- decision timeliness;
- governance effectiveness.

---

## 30. Learning and Training

Material lessons should inform:

- operator training;
- commander training;
- technical training;
- AI literacy;
- intervention exercises;
- emergency procedures;
- assurance training.

Training updates should be verified where the lesson affects consequential decisions.

---

## 31. Learning and TEVV

Lessons should feed back into TEVV through:

- new test cases;
- new scenarios;
- regression tests;
- adversarial tests;
- human-factors tests;
- environmental tests;
- autonomy tests.

Operational experience should progressively strengthen the test corpus.

---

## 32. Learning and Monitoring

Lessons should be considered when defining or updating:

- indicators;
- thresholds;
- alerts;
- monitoring frequency;
- escalation triggers.

Monitoring should evolve as understanding of failure modes improves.

---

## 33. Learning and Authorisation Conditions

Where a lesson reveals a control weakness, authorisation conditions may require:

- restriction;
- additional supervision;
- reduced autonomy;
- enhanced monitoring;
- additional testing;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation.

---

## 34. Knowledge Management

The organisation should maintain a structured knowledge base containing, where appropriate:

- lessons;
- findings;
- incidents;
- assurance decisions;
- test results;
- recurring patterns;
- control effectiveness;
- improvement actions.

Access should follow applicable authority and information-handling requirements.

---

## 35. Lesson Classification

Lessons may be classified as:

- local;
- capability-specific;
- cross-capability;
- organisational;
- strategic.

Classification should determine the level of governance response.

---

## 36. Learning Register

A Learning Register should include:

- lesson ID;
- source;
- date;
- capability;
- mission;
- observation;
- significance;
- affected domains;
- recommended action;
- owner;
- priority;
- status;
- verification;
- applicability.

---

## 37. Learning Status

A practical status model is:

**Captured → Assessed → Accepted → Action Assigned → Implemented → Verified → Institutionalised → Closed**

Lessons requiring no action should retain a documented rationale.

---

## 38. Institutionalisation

A lesson is institutionalised when the resulting improvement has been incorporated into the relevant:

- requirement;
- process;
- control;
- standard;
- training;
- monitoring;
- TEVV method;
- authorisation condition;
- policy.

---

## 39. Assurance Feedback Loop

Learning should feed back into the assurance cycle:

**Operational Experience → Evidence → Assessment → Assurance Finding/Lesson → Improvement → Verification → Updated Assurance Baseline**

---

## 40. Continuous Improvement Metrics

Useful indicators may include:

- recurring finding rate;
- overdue corrective actions;
- lesson implementation rate;
- lesson verification rate;
- time to implement material lessons;
- systemic issue rate;
- repeat incident rate;
- control effectiveness;
- number of assurance claims improved;
- number of test cases generated from operational lessons.

Metrics should support learning rather than encourage superficial closure.

---

## 41. Avoiding Metric Gaming

Learning metrics should not reward:

- closing lessons without effectiveness verification;
- reducing reported incidents;
- suppressing negative findings;
- reclassifying material issues as minor;
- generating excessive low-value lessons.

Quality and impact should matter more than volume.

---

## 42. Governance Review

Periodic governance review should examine:

- major lessons;
- recurring patterns;
- systemic weaknesses;
- effectiveness of corrective actions;
- emerging risks;
- changes in technology;
- changes in operational conditions.

---

## 43. Escalation

Lessons should be escalated when they indicate:

- immediate safety concern;
- loss of human control;
- unexpected autonomy;
- critical security weakness;
- invalid assurance claim;
- systemic governance failure;
- material risk increase.

---

## 44. Learning and Assurance Confidence

Continuous learning should influence assurance confidence.

A mature assurance system should be capable of showing:

- what was previously believed;
- what new evidence was obtained;
- what changed;
- why confidence changed;
- what action followed.

---

## 45. Learning Records

Material learning records should remain traceable to:

- source evidence;
- incident or finding;
- affected assurance claim;
- action;
- verification;
- resulting governance change.

---

## 46. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. How are lessons captured?
2. What sources generate assurance learning?
3. How are near misses treated?
4. How are lessons assessed for significance?
5. How are systemic lessons identified?
6. How are lessons transferred across capabilities?
7. How do lessons affect assurance claims?
8. How do lessons affect risk?
9. How do lessons affect human-control and autonomy assurance?
10. How are lessons incorporated into TEVV?
11. How are monitoring indicators updated?
12. How are training requirements updated?
13. How are lessons verified for effectiveness?
14. How are recurring lessons escalated?
15. How is organisational learning demonstrated?

---

## 47. Core Rule

> **A Defence AI assurance system shall learn from both failure and success. Lessons shall be evidence-based, assessed for applicability and significance, translated into proportionate action, and verified for effectiveness. Material lessons shall feed back into risk, requirements, controls, TEVV, monitoring, human authority, operational authorisation and future assurance decisions.**

---

## 48. Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Incident/Change → Learning → Improvement → Verification → Updated Assurance → Revalidation/Reauthorisation**
