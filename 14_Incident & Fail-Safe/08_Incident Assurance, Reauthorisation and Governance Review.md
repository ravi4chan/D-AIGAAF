# 08 — Incident Assurance, Reauthorisation and Governance Review

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how material Defence AI incidents are translated into assurance decisions and governance actions, including decisions concerning continued employment, restriction, revalidation, reauthorisation, suspension and broader governance review.

The objective is to ensure that incident closure is not treated as separate from assurance or operational authority.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **A material Defence AI incident shall trigger an explicit assessment of its effect on assurance, risk, controls and operational authority. Where the incident changes the basis on which confidence or authorisation was established, the organisation shall take proportionate action, including additional controls, enhanced monitoring, revalidation, reauthorisation, restriction or suspension.**

---

## 3. Assurance Review Object

The post-incident assurance review should consider:

**AI Capability × Mission × Environment × Autonomy × Human Authority × Configuration × Conditions × Dependencies**

The review should determine whether the previous assurance basis remains valid.

---

## 4. Assurance Impact Assessment

The organisation should assess the incident's effect on:

- assurance claims;
- evidence;
- assumptions;
- risks;
- controls;
- performance;
- human control;
- autonomy;
- environment;
- security;
- dependencies;
- mission effectiveness.

---

## 5. Assurance Claim Review

For each affected claim, determine whether it remains:

- valid;
- conditionally valid;
- partially supported;
- unsupported;
- contradicted;
- requiring new evidence.

---

## 6. Evidence Reassessment

Incident evidence should be assessed for:

- relevance;
- quality;
- integrity;
- currency;
- representativeness;
- applicability.

Previously accepted evidence may require reassessment where the incident reveals that an assumption was incorrect.

---

## 7. Assurance Confidence

The incident may result in:

- increased confidence;
- unchanged confidence;
- reduced confidence;
- insufficient confidence.

Confidence should be based on evidence rather than administrative status.

---

## 8. Residual Risk

The post-incident assessment should determine whether:

- residual risk remains acceptable;
- controls remain effective;
- new risks exist;
- risk acceptance remains valid.

---

## 9. Control Effectiveness

The organisation should determine whether affected controls:

- operated as intended;
- operated partially;
- failed;
- were bypassed;
- were ineffective under actual conditions.

A control that exists on paper but fails operationally should not be treated as effective.

---

## 10. Human-Control Assurance

Where the incident involves human interaction, the review should examine:

- decision authority;
- situational awareness;
- workload;
- intervention;
- override;
- training;
- automation bias;
- human-AI disagreement.

---

## 11. Autonomy Assurance

Where autonomy is involved, assess:

- authorised autonomy;
- observed autonomy;
- transition behaviour;
- autonomous actions;
- boundary compliance;
- intervention;
- safe-state behaviour.

---

## 12. Environment Assurance

Assess whether the incident reveals limitations in:

- environmental assumptions;
- operating envelope;
- degraded conditions;
- disconnected conditions;
- adversarial conditions;
- sensor conditions;
- communication conditions.

---

## 13. Security Assurance

Where security is relevant, assess:

- integrity;
- compromise;
- attack surface;
- configuration;
- data;
- interfaces;
- dependencies.

Security findings affecting operational behaviour should be incorporated into the overall assurance decision.

---

## 14. Mission Assurance

Assess whether the incident affects:

- mission effectiveness;
- decision quality;
- timing;
- reliability;
- operational suitability;
- mission success criteria.

---

## 15. Incident-to-Assurance Decision

A structured process should be used:

**Incident → Investigation → Findings → Assurance Impact → Risk Reassessment → Decision**

The decision should be documented.

---

## 16. Possible Assurance Outcomes

Possible outcomes include:

### No Material Assurance Impact

Existing assurance remains supported.

### Conditional Assurance

Assurance remains sufficient subject to defined conditions.

### Reduced Assurance

Additional controls or monitoring are required.

### Insufficient Assurance

Further evidence or revalidation is required before unrestricted operation.

### Invalidated Assurance

The previous assurance basis is no longer sufficient.

---

## 17. Operational Outcomes

Based on the assurance assessment, possible operational outcomes include:

- continue;
- continue with restrictions;
- reduced autonomy;
- enhanced human supervision;
- enhanced monitoring;
- restricted mission;
- restricted environment;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation;
- suspension.

---

## 18. Revalidation Trigger

Revalidation should be considered where the incident indicates that existing evidence may no longer support relevant claims.

Triggers may include:

- unexpected model behaviour;
- failed safety control;
- unexpected autonomy;
- human-control failure;
- material performance degradation;
- environmental limitation;
- security compromise;
- material configuration issue.

---

## 19. Reauthorisation Trigger

Reauthorisation should be considered where the incident changes the authorised basis, including:

- mission;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- operational boundaries;
- conditions;
- risk acceptance.

---

## 20. Relationship with Module 11

Module 11 defines the operational-authorisation governance process.

This module determines **when an incident creates sufficient assurance concern to require that authorisation process to be reconsidered**.

The distinction should remain explicit:

**Incident Assurance Assessment → Determines Assurance/Authority Impact**

**Operational Authorisation Process → Determines Operational Authority**

---

## 21. Suspension

Suspension should be considered where:

- assurance is materially insufficient;
- human control is lost;
- critical controls fail;
- risk is unacceptable;
- authorisation conditions cannot be satisfied;
- security integrity is uncertain.

---

## 22. Restrictions

Where complete suspension is unnecessary, restrictions may include:

- reduced autonomy;
- narrower mission;
- narrower environment;
- additional human supervision;
- limited functionality;
- enhanced monitoring.

Restrictions should be explicit and traceable.

---

## 23. Independent Challenge

Material post-incident assurance conclusions should receive independent challenge where proportionate to:

- consequence;
- autonomy;
- uncertainty;
- security;
- systemic significance.

---

## 24. Governance Review

A governance review should determine whether the incident indicates:

- local capability weakness;
- cross-capability weakness;
- organisational weakness;
- systemic governance weakness.

---

## 25. Local versus Systemic Impact

An incident may be local where the cause is isolated and controls are specific.

It may be systemic where:

- the same weakness exists elsewhere;
- a common control is ineffective;
- common dependencies are affected;
- governance processes are inadequate.

---

## 26. Portfolio Impact

Where a material lesson may affect multiple capabilities, the organisation should assess whether to:

- review related capabilities;
- issue a common control;
- increase monitoring;
- conduct targeted testing;
- initiate portfolio-wide assurance review.

---

## 27. Emergency Governance Action

Critical incidents may require immediate governance action before investigation is complete.

Possible actions include:

- temporary restriction;
- autonomy reduction;
- suspension;
- additional supervision;
- emergency review.

Final governance decisions should be updated as evidence develops.

---

## 28. Evidence Sufficiency

The authority should determine whether sufficient evidence exists to support:

- continued operation;
- restrictions;
- restoration;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation.

Where evidence is insufficient, the limitation should remain explicit.

---

## 29. Decision Quality

Post-incident decisions should distinguish:

- evidence;
- risk assessment;
- assumptions;
- uncertainty;
- judgement;
- authority decision.

This supports accountable governance.

---

## 30. Decision Records

Material decisions should record:

- incident;
- evidence;
- findings;
- assurance impact;
- risk;
- conditions;
- restrictions;
- decision;
- authority;
- date;
- review requirement.

---

## 31. Review Period

Where continued operation is permitted after an incident, the authority may specify:

- enhanced monitoring period;
- review date;
- performance thresholds;
- escalation criteria;
- restoration conditions.

---

## 32. Continued Monitoring

Post-incident monitoring should verify that:

- corrective actions remain effective;
- the incident does not recur;
- performance remains acceptable;
- human control remains effective;
- autonomy remains within bounds;
- new risks do not emerge.

---

## 33. Recurrence

A repeated incident should trigger reassessment of:

- corrective-action effectiveness;
- assurance confidence;
- residual risk;
- systemic significance;
- authorisation.

Repeated recurrence may justify escalation even where individual events were initially assessed as moderate.

---

## 34. Assurance Debt

Incident findings may increase assurance debt where:

- evidence is missing;
- testing is incomplete;
- assumptions remain unresolved;
- corrective actions are overdue.

Assurance debt should remain visible to decision-makers.

---

## 35. Governance Escalation

Escalation should occur where incidents indicate:

- critical safety concern;
- loss of human control;
- uncontrolled autonomy;
- systemic security weakness;
- repeated control failure;
- material invalidation of assurance.

---

## 36. Closure Criteria

Post-incident assurance review should not be closed until:

- assurance impact is assessed;
- required actions are assigned;
- authority implications are determined;
- restrictions are recorded;
- revalidation or reauthorisation requirements are identified.

---

## 37. Restoration Linkage

Restoration should be linked to the assurance outcome.

A capability should return to operation only at the level supported by:

- evidence;
- controls;
- risk;
- human control;
- authorisation.

---

## 38. Lessons to Governance

Material incidents should inform:

- policy;
- standards;
- requirements;
- controls;
- TEVV;
- monitoring;
- training;
- procurement;
- authorisation;
- fail-safe design.

---

## 39. Governance Review Frequency

Governance review should occur:

- after material incidents;
- after recurring incidents;
- following major corrective action;
- during periodic assurance review;
- when systemic trends emerge.

---

## 40. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. How is incident impact on assurance assessed?
2. Which assurance claims are reviewed?
3. How is evidence reassessed?
4. How is residual risk reconsidered?
5. How are human-control and autonomy impacts assessed?
6. How are environmental and security impacts assessed?
7. When does an incident require revalidation?
8. When does it require reauthorisation?
9. Who can restrict or suspend employment?
10. How is independent challenge applied?
11. How are systemic incidents identified?
12. How are portfolio-wide effects assessed?
13. How are post-incident decisions recorded?
14. What conditions are required for restoration?
15. How is post-incident monitoring conducted?
16. How are lessons translated into governance improvements?

---

## 41. Core Rule

> **Incident management shall not end when the immediate technical or operational problem is contained. Every material incident shall be assessed for its effect on continuing assurance and operational authority. Where confidence, risk, evidence, controls or authorised conditions have materially changed, the organisation shall impose proportionate restrictions and initiate revalidation, reauthorisation or suspension as necessary.**

---

## 42. Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Incident → Investigation → Findings → Assurance Impact → Risk Reassessment → Governance Decision → Revalidation/Reauthorisation/Suspension → Restoration → Learning**
