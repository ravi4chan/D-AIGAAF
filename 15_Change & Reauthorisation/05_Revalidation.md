# 05 — Revalidation

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the governance process for revalidation of a Defence AI capability following a change or other event that may affect the validity of existing assurance.

Revalidation determines whether previously established evidence and assurance remain applicable to the changed or current capability.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **Revalidation shall determine, using evidence, whether the existing assurance basis remains valid for the current capability, configuration, mission, environment, autonomy and human-authority conditions.**

Revalidation does not itself grant operational authority.

---

## 3. Scope

Revalidation may be required following:

- model changes;
- software changes;
- hardware changes;
- data changes;
- configuration changes;
- dependency changes;
- environmental changes;
- mission changes;
- autonomy changes;
- human-authority changes;
- security events;
- incidents;
- new evidence;
- material changes in assumptions.

---

## 4. Revalidation Object

The capability should be reassessed against:

**AI Capability × Mission × Environment × Autonomy × Human Authority × Configuration × Conditions × Dependencies**

---

## 5. Revalidation Trigger

Revalidation should be considered where there is reasonable concern that existing assurance may no longer support the current capability.

Triggers may include:

- material change;
- significant uncertainty;
- failed testing;
- contradictory evidence;
- incident findings;
- degraded performance;
- changed operating environment;
- changed autonomy;
- changed human-control arrangements;
- security compromise.

---

## 6. Revalidation versus Routine Monitoring

Routine monitoring determines whether assurance remains stable during normal operation.

Revalidation is a structured determination that existing assurance remains applicable after a relevant change or trigger.

---

## 7. Revalidation versus Reauthorisation

The distinction is:

**Revalidation:** Is the assurance basis still supported?

**Reauthorisation:** Is operational authority still justified?

A revalidation outcome may inform a subsequent reauthorisation decision.

---

## 8. Revalidation Initiation

The initiating authority should define:

- trigger;
- scope;
- affected claims;
- configuration;
- required evidence;
- responsible assessor;
- required independence.

---

## 9. Scope Determination

Revalidation scope should be proportionate to:

- change significance;
- consequence;
- autonomy;
- affected claims;
- uncertainty;
- operational exposure.

A targeted revalidation may be sufficient where impact is clearly bounded.

---

## 10. Assurance Claims

Affected assurance claims should be explicitly identified.

Claims may concern:

- performance;
- reliability;
- robustness;
- safety;
- security;
- human control;
- autonomy;
- environmental suitability;
- mission effectiveness.

---

## 11. Evidence Review

Existing evidence should be reviewed for:

- relevance;
- applicability;
- configuration;
- currency;
- representativeness;
- coverage;
- integrity.

---

## 12. Evidence Gap Assessment

The assessment should identify:

- missing evidence;
- outdated evidence;
- insufficient coverage;
- unresolved contradictions;
- untested conditions.

Evidence gaps should remain visible until addressed.

---

## 13. Additional Evidence

Additional evidence may be obtained through:

- testing;
- evaluation;
- trials;
- operational data;
- expert review;
- independent assessment;
- monitoring.

---

## 14. Risk Reassessment

Revalidation should consider whether the change or trigger affects:

- identified risks;
- controls;
- residual risk;
- assumptions;
- risk acceptance.

---

## 15. Control Reassessment

Determine whether controls remain:

- applicable;
- effective;
- observable;
- enforceable;
- sufficient.

---

## 16. Human-Control Revalidation

Where human interaction is affected, reassess:

- decision rights;
- situational awareness;
- workload;
- intervention;
- override;
- training;
- automation bias.

---

## 17. Autonomy Revalidation

Where autonomy is affected, reassess:

- autonomy level;
- transitions;
- boundaries;
- action authority;
- intervention;
- safe-state behaviour;
- observability.

---

## 18. Environmental Revalidation

Determine whether existing evidence remains representative of:

- authorised environments;
- degraded conditions;
- disconnected operation;
- adversarial conditions;
- sensor conditions;
- communication conditions.

---

## 19. Security Revalidation

Where security is affected, reassess:

- threat assumptions;
- controls;
- attack surface;
- integrity;
- dependencies;
- monitoring.

---

## 20. Mission Revalidation

Determine whether the capability continues to satisfy:

- mission purpose;
- mission constraints;
- success criteria;
- operational suitability;
- decision-quality requirements.

---

## 21. Configuration Verification

The configuration undergoing revalidation should be uniquely identified and verified.

The evidence should correspond to that configuration or be justified as applicable.

---

## 22. Evidence Sufficiency

The revalidation authority should determine whether sufficient evidence exists to support each material assurance claim.

Possible evidence conclusions include:

- sufficient;
- sufficient with conditions;
- partially sufficient;
- insufficient;
- contradicted.

---

## 23. Assurance Confidence

Revalidation should establish a current confidence level:

- High;
- Moderate;
- Low;
- Insufficient.

Confidence should reflect evidence quality and relevance, not administrative completion.

---

## 24. Assurance Debt

Revalidation may identify assurance debt arising from:

- incomplete testing;
- unresolved assumptions;
- evidence gaps;
- deferred verification;
- incomplete corrective actions.

---

## 25. Independent Challenge

Independent review should be proportionate to:

- consequence;
- autonomy;
- uncertainty;
- security;
- change significance;
- systemic impact.

The reviewer should be able to challenge assumptions and conclusions.

---

## 26. Revalidation Findings

Findings should distinguish:

- confirmed evidence;
- observations;
- limitations;
- evidence gaps;
- contradictions;
- material concerns.

---

## 27. Revalidation Outcomes

Possible outcomes include:

### Validated

Existing assurance remains supported.

### Validated with Conditions

Assurance remains supported subject to specified restrictions or controls.

### Partially Validated

Some claims remain supported while others require further evidence.

### Not Validated

Existing assurance is not sufficient for continued unrestricted reliance.

---

## 28. Operational Consequences

Where assurance is insufficient, possible actions include:

- restriction;
- reduced autonomy;
- additional supervision;
- additional testing;
- enhanced monitoring;
- suspension.

---

## 29. Reauthorisation Referral

A revalidation outcome should be referred to the operational-authorisation process where the authorised basis has changed or continued authority requires formal reconsideration.

---

## 30. Revalidation Does Not Equal Approval

A positive revalidation conclusion establishes an assurance position.

It does not independently authorise:

- deployment;
- operational employment;
- increased autonomy;
- expanded mission scope.

---

## 31. Conditional Revalidation

Conditions may include:

- restricted mission;
- restricted environment;
- reduced autonomy;
- additional supervision;
- enhanced monitoring;
- defined review period.

Conditions should be explicit and traceable.

---

## 32. Failed Revalidation

Where revalidation fails, the capability should not continue unrestricted operation solely because a previous authorisation exists.

The organisation should determine whether to:

- correct;
- retest;
- restrict;
- suspend;
- retire.

---

## 33. Post-Revalidation Monitoring

Where continued operation is permitted, monitoring should verify that:

- assumptions remain valid;
- performance remains acceptable;
- controls remain effective;
- human control remains effective;
- autonomy remains within bounds.

---

## 34. Revalidation Records

Records should include:

- trigger;
- scope;
- configuration;
- assurance claims;
- evidence;
- findings;
- risk;
- confidence;
- conditions;
- conclusion;
- reviewer;
- date.

---

## 35. Traceability

The revalidation process should preserve:

**Change/Trigger → Impact → Evidence → Assessment → Assurance Conclusion → Authority Decision**

---

---

## 40. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. Why was revalidation triggered?
2. What configuration is being revalidated?
3. Which assurance claims are affected?
4. Which evidence remains valid?
5. What evidence gaps exist?
6. What additional evidence is required?
7. Has risk changed?
8. Are controls still effective?
9. Has human control changed?
10. Has autonomy changed?
11. Has environmental suitability changed?
12. Has security changed?
13. Is mission effectiveness still supported?
14. What confidence level is justified?
15. What assurance debt remains?
16. Is independent challenge required?
17. Does reauthorisation need to occur?
18. What restrictions or conditions apply?

---

## 41. Core Rule

> **Revalidation shall establish whether the assurance basis supporting a Defence AI capability remains valid for its current state. A previous assurance conclusion shall not be treated as permanently transferable across material changes in capability, context or conditions.**

---

## 42. Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Baseline → Change/Trigger → Impact Assessment → Evidence Reassessment → Revalidation → Assurance Conclusion → Reauthorisation if Required → Employment → Monitoring → Learning**
