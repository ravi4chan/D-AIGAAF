# 06 — Reauthorisation

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the change-driven reauthorisation process for a Defence AI capability when a change, revalidation outcome or other event may have altered the basis on which operational authority was granted.

This document complements Module 11, which establishes the broader operational-authorisation framework.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **Where a change materially affects the basis of operational authority, continued use shall require an explicit determination that the capability remains justified within its current mission, environment, autonomy, human-authority, risk and assurance conditions.**

---

## 3. Scope

Reauthorisation may be required following changes to:

- AI capability;
- mission;
- risk;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- operating boundaries;
- configuration;
- security;
- dependencies;
- authorisation conditions.

It may also follow incidents or assurance findings where operational authority is affected.

---

## 4. Reauthorisation Object

The reauthorisation decision should consider:

**AI Capability × Mission × Environment × Autonomy × Human Authority × Configuration × Conditions × Dependencies**

---

## 5. Reauthorisation Trigger

Reauthorisation should be considered where the authorised basis has materially changed.

Triggers may include:

- material capability change;
- material risk change;
- changed mission;
- changed operating environment;
- increased autonomy;
- changed human authority;
- changed operational boundaries;
- material security change;
- material dependency change;
- invalidated assurance;
- significant incident findings.

---

## 6. Revalidation Input

Where revalidation is required, its conclusions should form a key input to reauthorisation.

The authorising authority should consider:

- assurance claims;
- evidence;
- findings;
- residual risk;
- limitations;
- conditions.

---

## 7. Reauthorisation versus Revalidation

The distinction remains:

**Revalidation:** Is the assurance basis supported?

**Reauthorisation:** Is operational authority justified?

A capability may be technically and operationally well assured but still require a new authority decision when its authorised scope changes.

---

## 8. Authorisation Baseline

The current authorisation baseline should identify:

- capability;
- configuration;
- mission;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- boundaries;
- conditions;
- validity period.

---

## 9. Baseline Comparison

The proposed state should be compared with the previous authorised state.

Differences should be identified across:

- capability;
- mission;
- risk;
- assurance;
- human control;
- autonomy;
- environment;
- security;
- dependencies.

---

## 10. Authority Scope

The reauthorisation decision should define precisely what is authorised.

Scope may include:

- specific mission;
- defined environment;
- defined configuration;
- defined autonomy;
- defined human authority;
- specified conditions.

---

## 11. Risk Acceptance

The authorising authority should determine whether residual risk remains acceptable under the changed conditions.

Existing risk acceptance should not be presumed to transfer automatically.

---

## 12. Assurance Sufficiency

The authority should determine whether sufficient assurance exists for the proposed scope.

Consider:

- evidence quality;
- coverage;
- currency;
- uncertainty;
- testing;
- operational experience;
- independent challenge.

---

## 13. Human Authority

Reauthorisation should establish that appropriate human authority remains:

- defined;
- competent;
- available;
- practically capable of intervention;
- accountable.

---

## 14. Autonomy

Where autonomy changes, the authority should explicitly determine:

- permitted autonomy level;
- transition conditions;
- boundaries;
- intervention requirements;
- safe-state requirements.

An increase in consequential autonomy should require explicit authority.

---

## 15. Environmental Conditions

The reauthorisation decision should identify applicable:

- operating envelope;
- degraded conditions;
- disconnected conditions;
- adversarial conditions;
- sensor conditions;
- communication conditions.

---

## 16. Security Conditions

Where relevant, authority should specify security-related conditions concerning:

- integrity;
- access;
- interfaces;
- monitoring;
- dependencies;
- threat assumptions.

---

## 17. Operational Boundaries

Reauthorisation should explicitly define:

- permitted use;
- prohibited use;
- mission boundaries;
- environmental boundaries;
- autonomy boundaries;
- human-authority boundaries.

---

## 18. Conditions of Authorisation

Conditions may include:

- additional supervision;
- reduced autonomy;
- restricted mission;
- restricted environment;
- enhanced monitoring;
- additional testing;
- defined review intervals.

---

## 19. Conditional Reauthorisation

A capability may be reauthorised conditionally where evidence supports continued operation only within defined constraints.

Conditions should be:

- explicit;
- measurable where practical;
- enforceable;
- traceable.

---

## 20. Temporary Reauthorisation

Temporary authority may be appropriate where:

- additional evidence is pending;
- operational need is time-sensitive;
- a controlled trial is required.

Temporary authority should have:

- defined scope;
- duration;
- conditions;
- review point;
- termination criteria.

---

## 21. Emergency Reauthorisation

Emergency authority may be necessary to address urgent operational or safety requirements.

Emergency decisions should remain:

- bounded;
- documented;
- accountable;
- subject to subsequent review.

---

## 22. Restriction

Where full reauthorisation is not justified, authority may be reduced through:

- lower autonomy;
- narrower mission;
- narrower environment;
- additional supervision;
- limited functionality.

---

## 23. Suspension

Reauthorisation should not be granted where:

- assurance is materially insufficient;
- risk is unacceptable;
- human control is unavailable;
- critical controls fail;
- authorised boundaries cannot be maintained.

---

## 24. Independent Challenge

Material reauthorisation decisions should receive proportionate independent challenge.

Challenge should consider:

- evidence;
- risk;
- assumptions;
- conditions;
- uncertainty;
- decision rationale.

---

## 25. Decision Authority

The authority responsible for reauthorisation should be explicitly identified.

Decision authority should reflect:

- consequence;
- autonomy;
- scope;
- risk;
- organisational level.

---

## 26. Decision Criteria

The reauthorisation decision should consider whether:

1. assurance is sufficient;
2. residual risk is acceptable;
3. human authority is effective;
4. autonomy is bounded;
5. environmental conditions are understood;
6. security conditions are satisfied;
7. operational boundaries are enforceable;
8. required controls are implemented.

---

## 27. Decision Outcomes

Possible outcomes include:

- reauthorised;
- reauthorised with conditions;
- restricted;
- additional evidence required;
- revalidation required;
- suspended;
- not reauthorised.

---

## 28. Conditions and Restrictions

All conditions and restrictions should be recorded as part of the operational authority.

They should not exist solely as informal instructions.

---

## 29. Effective Date

The reauthorisation decision should specify when the new authority becomes effective.

The previous authority should remain governed according to its defined status until the transition is formally established.

---

## 30. Validity

Where appropriate, reauthorisation should specify:

- validity period;
- review date;
- renewal conditions;
- trigger events for reassessment.

---

## 31. Transition to New Authority

The transition should verify:

- approved configuration;
- applicable conditions;
- personnel readiness;
- monitoring;
- records;
- communication of authority.

---

## 32. Post-Reauthorisation Monitoring

Following reauthorisation, monitoring should verify:

- performance;
- risk indicators;
- human control;
- autonomy;
- environmental conditions;
- security;
- dependencies;
- compliance with conditions.

---

## 33. Reauthorisation Failure

If post-reauthorisation evidence shows that conditions are not being met, the authority may:

- restrict;
- reduce autonomy;
- require additional assurance;
- suspend;
- revoke or otherwise terminate authority as provided by the governing framework.

---

## 38. Decision Record

The reauthorisation record should include:

- trigger;
- previous authority;
- proposed authority;
- configuration;
- evidence;
- assurance conclusion;
- risk;
- conditions;
- restrictions;
- decision;
- authority;
- effective date;
- review date.

---

## 39. Traceability

The decision should be traceable through:

**Change → Impact → TEVV → Evidence → Revalidation → Risk Assessment → Reauthorisation Decision → Conditions → Employment → Monitoring**

---

## 40. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. Why was reauthorisation required?
2. What changed from the previous authorised state?
3. What evidence supports the new authority?
4. Was revalidation completed where required?
5. Has residual risk changed?
6. Are human-control arrangements adequate?
7. Has autonomy changed?
8. Are environmental conditions appropriate?
9. Are security conditions satisfied?
10. Are dependencies adequately controlled?
11. What operational boundaries apply?
12. What conditions apply?
13. Who made the decision?
14. What is the authority's validity?
15. What monitoring is required?
16. What triggers further reassessment?
17. What happens if conditions are violated?

---

## 41. Core Rule

> **Reauthorisation is an explicit governance decision that operational authority remains justified after a material change. It shall be based on the current evidence, risk, assurance, configuration and authorised conditions—not on the existence of a previous approval.**

---

## 42. Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Baseline → Change → Impact Assessment → TEVV → Revalidation → Risk Acceptance → Reauthorisation → Controlled Employment → Monitoring → Incident/Learning → Further Revalidation/Reauthorisation**
