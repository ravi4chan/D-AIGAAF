# 09 — Change Records, Governance Review & Learning

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the records, governance review and organisational learning requirements for changes to Defence AI capabilities.

The objective is to ensure that changes remain traceable from proposal through implementation, assurance, authorisation, operational use and subsequent learning.

---

## 2. Core Principle

> **Every material change shall leave a sufficiently complete evidence trail to reconstruct what changed, why it changed, who decided, what evidence supported the decision, what was implemented, what happened afterwards and what was learned.**

---

## 3. Scope

This document applies to records and governance review associated with:

- planned changes;
- significant changes;
- material changes;
- emergency changes;
- temporary changes;
- rejected changes;
- rolled-back changes;
- change-related incidents;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation.

---

## 4. Change Record

Each controlled change should have a unique record containing, as applicable:

- change identifier;
- capability;
- baseline;
- proposed configuration;
- reason;
- owner;
- classification;
- impact assessment;
- evidence;
- decision;
- implementation status;
- verification status.

---

## 5. Traceability

The change record should connect:

**Change → Classification → Impact → Risk → Evidence → Decision → Implementation → Verification → Revalidation → Reauthorisation**

---

## 6. Configuration Record

The record should identify the relevant:

- model;
- software;
- hardware;
- data;
- configuration;
- interfaces;
- dependencies;
- operating parameters.

---

## 7. Decision Record

Material change decisions should document:

- decision;
- rationale;
- evidence;
- assumptions;
- uncertainty;
- risk;
- conditions;
- authority;
- date.

---

## 8. Evidence Record

Evidence supporting the change decision should be identifiable and traceable.

This may include:

- TEVV results;
- assurance assessments;
- risk assessments;
- operational evidence;
- independent reviews;
- monitoring results.

---

## 9. Condition Record

Where approval or reauthorisation is conditional, each condition should be:

- explicit;
- assigned;
- measurable where practical;
- monitored;
- reviewable.

---

## 10. Exception Record

Exceptions should record:

- requirement or baseline affected;
- reason;
- authority;
- scope;
- duration;
- compensating controls;
- review requirement.

---

## 11. Emergency Change Record

Emergency changes should additionally record:

- emergency trigger;
- urgency;
- accelerated decision basis;
- evidence limitations;
- immediate controls;
- retrospective actions.

---

## 12. Temporary Change Record

Temporary changes should record:

- intended duration;
- start condition;
- expiry;
- termination criteria;
- responsible authority;
- monitoring;
- transition or rollback plan.

---

## 13. Rollback Record

Where rollback occurs, record:

- trigger;
- decision authority;
- configuration before rollback;
- rollback configuration;
- implementation;
- verification;
- resulting assurance status;
- follow-up actions.

---

## 14. Revalidation Record

Where revalidation is required, the record should identify:

- trigger;
- affected claims;
- evidence reviewed;
- additional evidence;
- findings;
- assurance conclusion;
- remaining limitations.

---

## 15. Reauthorisation Record

Where reauthorisation is required, the record should link to:

- previous authority;
- changed basis;
- assurance conclusion;
- risk position;
- new conditions;
- restrictions;
- authority decision.

Detailed authorisation governance remains in Module 11.

---

## 16. Monitoring Record

Post-change monitoring should record:

- indicators;
- thresholds;
- observations;
- anomalies;
- decisions;
- escalation;
- corrective action.

---

## 17. Change-Related Incident Linkage

Where a change contributes to an incident, the change record should link to the relevant incident record.

This should support:

**Change ↔ Incident ↔ Investigation ↔ Assurance ↔ Corrective Action**

---

## 18. Record Integrity

Change records should be protected against:

- unauthorised modification;
- deletion;
- loss;
- version confusion;
- incomplete updates.

---

## 19. Record Currency

Records should reflect the current status of:

- configuration;
- change;
- assurance;
- authorisation;
- restrictions;
- outstanding actions.

---

## 20. Governance Review

Governance review should determine whether change processes remain effective.

Review may consider:

- change volume;
- change significance;
- failed changes;
- rollback frequency;
- incidents;
- recurring findings;
- assurance debt;
- overdue actions.

---

## 21. Triggered Review

Additional governance review should occur where:

- material change occurs;
- repeated failures occur;
- systemic weaknesses emerge;
- assurance is repeatedly degraded;
- change-related incidents occur.

---

## 22. Independent Review

Material change governance should be subject to proportionate independent challenge.

The review should examine:

- classification;
- risk;
- evidence;
- assurance;
- decision quality;
- implementation;
- monitoring.

---

## 23. Cumulative Change Review

Governance review should consider whether multiple changes have collectively altered the capability beyond the assumptions of the original authorisation.

---

## 24. Configuration Drift Review

Repeated discrepancies between authorised and deployed configurations may indicate:

- weak change control;
- inadequate monitoring;
- poor configuration management;
- governance failure.

Such patterns should receive systemic attention.

---

## 25. Change Performance

Useful governance indicators may include:

- changes completed on time;
- failed changes;
- emergency changes;
- temporary changes;
- rollbacks;
- overdue revalidation;
- overdue reauthorisation;
- recurring change-related incidents.

Metrics should support control rather than incentivise superficial closure.

---

## 26. Change Risk Trends

Governance should review trends in:

- risk increases;
- recurring failure modes;
- assurance degradation;
- control failures;
- human-control concerns;
- autonomy concerns;
- security findings.

---

## 27. Assurance Trends

The organisation should monitor whether repeated changes are producing:

- assurance debt;
- evidence fragmentation;
- reduced confidence;
- increased testing burden;
- recurring uncertainty.

---

## 28. Lessons Learned

Change experience should inform:

- future change planning;
- requirements;
- risk assessment;
- configuration management;
- TEVV;
- monitoring;
- training;
- procurement;
- authorisation.

---

## 29. Failed Change Learning

Failed or rolled-back changes should be analysed to determine:

- why the change failed;
- whether the impact assessment was adequate;
- whether testing was sufficient;
- whether implementation controls worked;
- whether similar changes are affected.

---

## 30. Successful Change Learning

Successful changes should also be examined for practices that improved:

- assurance;
- reliability;
- implementation control;
- rollback;
- monitoring;
- human oversight.

---

## 31. Systemic Learning

Where repeated patterns emerge, learning should be elevated from individual capability management to organisational governance.

Possible responses include:

- common controls;
- revised policy;
- additional testing;
- supplier requirements;
- portfolio review;
- revised approval criteria.

---

## 32. Supplier and Dependency Learning

Change experience involving suppliers or dependencies should inform:

- supplier assurance;
- contractual requirements;
- dependency controls;
- continuity planning;
- change-notification requirements.

---

## 33. Workforce Learning

Change lessons may require updates to:

- technical training;
- operator training;
- governance training;
- change-management competence;
- emergency procedures.

---

## 34. Knowledge Management

Validated change lessons should be incorporated into the organisation's knowledge base.

Knowledge should be:

- searchable;
- traceable;
- categorised;
- current;
- accessible to authorised personnel.

---

## 35. Action Tracking

Governance actions should have:

- accountable owner;
- priority;
- target date;
- status;
- verification method.

---

## 36. Action Verification

An action should not be considered complete merely because a task was performed.

Verification should establish that the intended control or improvement is actually effective.

---

## 37. Revalidation from Learning

Where lessons reveal that existing assurance may no longer be valid, revalidation should be initiated as appropriate.

---

## 38. Reauthorisation from Learning

Where lessons alter the authorised basis, reauthorisation should be considered.

---

## 39. Continuous Improvement Cycle

The overall improvement cycle should be:

**Change → Observe → Assess → Learn → Improve → Verify → Update Assurance → Update Governance**

---

## 40. Record Retention

Change records should be retained for a period sufficient to support:

- audit;
- incident investigation;
- accountability;
- historical assurance;
- lessons learned;
- lifecycle review.

Retention requirements should align with applicable legal, policy and organisational requirements.

---

## 41. Governance Questions

The organisation should be able to answer:

1. What changed?
2. What was the previous baseline?
3. Who proposed and approved the change?
4. What evidence supported the decision?
5. What configuration was implemented?
6. Was revalidation required?
7. Was reauthorisation required?
8. What conditions applied?
9. Was rollback required?
10. What monitoring was performed?
11. Did an incident occur?
12. What lessons were identified?
13. Were corrective actions completed?
14. Were systemic implications assessed?
15. Were related capabilities reviewed?
16. How was the final state recorded?
17. How was organisational learning captured?

---

## 42. Core Rule

> **Change records are not administrative history alone. They are assurance and accountability evidence that connects technical change to risk, operational authority, employment, outcomes and organisational learning.**

---

## 43. Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Baseline → Change → Impact Assessment → Decision → Implementation → Verification → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Employment → Monitoring → Records → Learning → Continuous Improvement**
