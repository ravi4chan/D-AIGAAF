# 06 — Nonconformity, Corrective Action and Follow-Up

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how D-AIGAAF audit findings and nonconformities are converted into corrective action, tracked, verified, and formally closed.

The objective is not simply to resolve individual observations, but to ensure that material governance weaknesses are addressed at their underlying cause and that corrective actions are effective.

---

## 2. Corrective Action Principle

Corrective action should address the condition that caused or permitted the finding.

The basic lifecycle is:

**Finding → Containment → Cause → Action → Implementation → Verification → Effectiveness → Closure**

Completion of an action is not equivalent to effective remediation.

---

## 3. Nonconformity

A nonconformity is a demonstrated failure to satisfy an applicable requirement, approved condition, or defined governance expectation.

Nonconformities should be distinguished from:

- observations;
- improvement opportunities;
- evidence gaps;
- incidents;
- risks;
- control weaknesses where no requirement failure is established.

---

## 4. Finding Registration

Material findings should be formally registered.

A finding record should normally identify:

- finding ID;
- audit;
- date;
- criterion;
- condition;
- evidence;
- severity;
- responsible owner;
- required action;
- due date;
- verification method;
- status.

---

## 5. Immediate Containment

Where a finding presents an immediate material risk, temporary containment may be required before permanent corrective action.

Containment may include:

- restricting use;
- reducing autonomy;
- increasing human oversight;
- disabling a feature;
- suspending deployment;
- isolating a dependency;
- increasing monitoring.

Containment should be proportionate to risk.

---

## 6. Corrective Action

Corrective action is action taken to eliminate the cause or contributing conditions of an identified nonconformity and prevent recurrence.

It should address the underlying governance problem rather than only the visible symptom.

---

## 7. Root Cause

Where practical, the responsible owner should determine why the nonconformity occurred.

Potential root causes include:

- inadequate control design;
- unclear responsibility;
- insufficient competence;
- process weakness;
- technical failure;
- configuration error;
- inadequate monitoring;
- change-management failure;
- supplier dependency;
- organisational incentives.

Root-cause conclusions should be supported by evidence.

---

## 8. Contributing Factors

More than one factor may contribute to a finding.

These may include:

- workload;
- operational pressure;
- environmental conditions;
- human factors;
- dependency failure;
- inadequate information;
- unclear authority;
- insufficient training.

Contributing factors should not automatically be treated as root causes.

---

## 9. Corrective Action Plan

A corrective action plan should define:

- finding addressed;
- required outcome;
- action;
- owner;
- resources;
- target date;
- dependencies;
- interim controls;
- verification method;
- closure authority.

Actions should be specific enough to determine whether they have actually been completed.

---

## 10. Action Ownership

Each material corrective action should have an accountable owner.

The owner is responsible for:

- developing the response;
- implementing the action;
- providing evidence;
- managing dependencies;
- reporting status;
- demonstrating effectiveness.

The auditor should not become the owner of remediation.

---

## 11. Action Priority

Priority should reflect:

- finding severity;
- consequence;
- risk;
- autonomy;
- human-control implications;
- security;
- operational exposure;
- authorisation implications;
- recurrence;
- systemic impact.

Critical findings require prompt escalation and action.

---

## 12. Corrective Action Hierarchy

Where appropriate, responses should follow a hierarchy:

**Eliminate Cause → Correct Control → Strengthen Control → Add Compensating Control → Increase Monitoring → Restrict Use**

Temporary compensating controls should not become a permanent substitute without appropriate approval.

---

## 13. Corrective Action Evidence

Evidence of corrective action may include:

- revised procedures;
- updated configurations;
- completed testing;
- training records;
- changed governance arrangements;
- updated authorisation;
- new monitoring;
- supplier remediation;
- independent verification.

Evidence should demonstrate the actual corrective state.

---

## 14. Verification

Verification determines whether the stated corrective action was implemented.

Verification may involve:

- document review;
- inspection;
- configuration review;
- observation;
- sampling;
- technical testing;
- independent evidence;
- interviews.

Verification should be proportionate to finding significance.

---

## 15. Effectiveness Verification

For material findings, closure should normally require assessment of whether the corrective action is effective.

This may require:

- observing the control over time;
- testing recurrence;
- reviewing monitoring;
- examining new incidents;
- checking representative cases;
- confirming risk reduction.

---

## 16. Finding Closure

A finding may be closed when:

1. required action is completed;
2. implementation is demonstrated;
3. effectiveness is verified where required;
4. residual risk is acceptable or appropriately governed;
5. required approvals are obtained.

Closure should be recorded.

---

## 17. Partial Closure

Where only part of a finding has been addressed, the finding should remain open or be explicitly split into controlled actions.

Partial completion should not be represented as full closure.

---

## 18. Overdue Actions

Overdue corrective actions should be monitored.

Escalation should consider:

- severity;
- duration;
- risk;
- operational exposure;
- interim controls;
- repeated delay;
- management response.

An overdue critical action may require restriction or suspension of affected activity.

---

## 19. Extensions

Extensions may be granted where justified.

An extension should identify:

- reason;
- residual risk;
- interim controls;
- revised deadline;
- approving authority.

Repeated extensions should trigger governance review.

---

## 20. Ineffective Corrective Action

If corrective action does not address the underlying problem, the finding should not be considered effectively closed.

Possible responses include:

- revised corrective action;
- additional controls;
- expanded scope;
- independent review;
- risk reassessment;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation review.

---

## 21. Recurring Findings

Repeated findings should be analysed for systemic weaknesses.

Recurring findings may indicate:

- inadequate root-cause analysis;
- weak ownership;
- ineffective governance;
- insufficient resources;
- poor control design;
- inadequate monitoring.

Recurring findings should receive governance-level attention.

---

## 22. Systemic Corrective Action

Where a finding reflects a systemic issue, corrective action may need to address:

- multiple AI capabilities;
- common processes;
- shared infrastructure;
- suppliers;
- organisational policy;
- training;
- architecture;
- governance committees.

Correction should not be limited to the audited instance where broader exposure is demonstrated.

---

## 23. Risk Acceptance Instead of Correction

In some circumstances, a responsible authority may formally accept residual risk rather than implement the preferred corrective action.

Such acceptance should:

- be within delegated authority;
- use current evidence;
- identify residual risk;
- identify conditions;
- specify duration where appropriate;
- not override mandatory requirements.

Audit should record the decision but should not itself accept the risk.

---

## 24. Corrective Action and Authorisation

Where a finding affects an operational authorisation condition, corrective action may require:

- additional controls;
- restricted employment;
- reduced autonomy;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation;
- suspension.

Completion of corrective action does not automatically restore operational authority.

---

## 25. Corrective Action and Human Control

Where a finding affects meaningful human control, corrective action should consider:

- authority;
- competence;
- information;
- workload;
- intervention;
- override;
- situational awareness;
- traceability.

Human-control deficiencies should receive appropriate escalation based on consequence.

---

## 26. Corrective Action and Autonomy

Where findings concern autonomy, corrective action may require:

- revised autonomy boundaries;
- transition controls;
- improved monitoring;
- additional human oversight;
- safe-state mechanisms;
- testing;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation.

Unauthorised autonomy should not be treated as an ordinary administrative finding.

---

## 27. Corrective Action and Security

Security-related findings may require:

- containment;
- access changes;
- configuration correction;
- patching;
- dependency changes;
- additional monitoring;
- adversarial testing;
- incident response.

Corrective action should be coordinated with Module 06 and Module 14 where applicable.

---

## 28. Corrective Action and Change

A corrective action that modifies an AI capability or governance-relevant configuration should itself be subject to applicable change controls.

This may require:

- change classification;
- impact assessment;
- testing;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation.

Remediation should not create an uncontrolled new state.

---

## 29. Corrective Action and Incidents

Where a finding arises from an incident, corrective action should incorporate relevant investigation results.

The organisation should distinguish:

- immediate incident response;
- recovery;
- corrective action;
- preventive improvement;
- assurance restoration.

Module 14 governs incident management.

---

## 30. Preventive Improvement

Lessons from findings may justify preventive action beyond the original nonconformity.

Preventive improvement may include:

- broader control changes;
- updated standards;
- training;
- architecture changes;
- monitoring improvements;
- policy updates;
- supplier requirements.

---

## 31. Corrective Action Monitoring

The audit or governance function should maintain appropriate visibility of open actions.

Monitoring may include:

- status;
- age;
- severity;
- owner;
- due date;
- overdue status;
- risk;
- interim controls;
- verification status.

---

## 32. Corrective Action Metrics

Useful metrics may include:

- open findings;
- overdue findings;
- average time to closure;
- recurrence rate;
- ineffective closure rate;
- critical findings;
- systemic findings;
- actions requiring revalidation;
- actions affecting authorisation.

Metrics should not encourage premature closure.

---

## 33. Closure Quality

Closure quality should be assessed based on outcome rather than administrative completion.

A strong closure demonstrates:

**Action Completed + Cause Addressed + Control Effective + Risk Governed**

---

## 34. Independent Closure Review

Independent verification should be considered for:

- critical findings;
- high-consequence AI;
- significant human-control weaknesses;
- unauthorised autonomy;
- material security issues;
- systemic findings;
- disputed closures.

---

## 35. Evidence of Closure

Closure evidence should be sufficient to demonstrate:

- what changed;
- when it changed;
- who approved it;
- what testing occurred;
- what evidence supports effectiveness;
- whether residual risk remains;
- whether authority or conditions changed.

---

## 36. Reopening Findings

A closed finding may be reopened if:

- corrective action proves ineffective;
- the condition recurs;
- new evidence invalidates closure;
- a related incident occurs;
- configuration changes undermine remediation;
- the original closure was based on inadequate evidence.

Reopening should be recorded with rationale.

---

## 37. Governance Escalation

Corrective action should be escalated where:

- deadlines are repeatedly missed;
- owners fail to act;
- risk increases;
- interim controls become inadequate;
- systemic exposure emerges;
- authorisation conditions are affected;
- human control is compromised.

---

## 38. Corrective Action Records

Records should support reconstruction of:

**Finding → Decision → Action → Evidence → Verification → Effectiveness → Closure**

The record should remain linked to the original audit and relevant governance decisions.

---

## 39. Relationship to Continuous Assurance

Corrective action should feed Module 13.

Once implemented, relevant controls and indicators should be monitored to determine whether the improvement remains effective.

---

## 40. Relationship to Revalidation and Reauthorisation

Where corrective action changes the basis on which assurance or authority was established, the organisation should determine whether:

- revalidation is required;
- reauthorisation is required;
- additional TEVV is required;
- operational restrictions remain necessary.

Corrective action does not automatically restore previous assurance or authority.

---

## 41. Integration with D-AIGAAF

Corrective action should integrate with:

- Module 03 — Risk & Autonomy;
- Module 06 — AI Security;
- Module 08 — Human Authority;
- Module 09 — TEVV;
- Module 11 — Operational Authorisation;
- Module 13 — Continuous Assurance;
- Module 14 — Incident & Fail-Safe;
- Module 15 — Change & Reauthorisation.

---

## 42. Corrective Action Model

D-AIGAAF uses:

**Identify → Contain → Analyse Cause → Define Action → Assign Owner → Implement → Verify → Assess Effectiveness → Close → Monitor → Learn**

---

## 43. Core Rule

**A finding is not resolved merely because an action has been completed. D-AIGAAF requires material corrective action to address the underlying condition or cause, be supported by evidence, be verified, and demonstrate effective risk reduction before closure. Where remediation changes the basis of assurance or authority, revalidation or reauthorisation must be considered.**

---

## 44. Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Audit → Finding → Corrective Action → Verification → Continuous Assurance**
