# 08 — Audit Records, Traceability and Retention

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes requirements for creating, controlling, maintaining, tracing, protecting, retaining, and disposing of audit records within D-AIGAAF.

The objective is to ensure that an audit can be reconstructed sufficiently to demonstrate:

- what was examined;
- why it was examined;
- what criteria were applied;
- what evidence was considered;
- what testing was performed;
- what findings were identified;
- how conclusions were reached;
- who made material decisions;
- what corrective actions followed;
- how closure was verified.

---

## 2. Records Principle

Audit records should provide an auditable trail from the approved audit objective through final conclusion and follow-up.

The core chain is:

**Audit Objective → Scope → Criteria → Evidence → Testing → Findings → Conclusion → Corrective Action → Verification → Closure**

Records should be sufficient to support independent review without creating unnecessary administrative burden.

---

## 3. Audit Record Definition

Audit records are information retained as evidence of audit planning, execution, conclusions, decisions, findings, actions, and follow-up.

Records may include:

- audit plans;
- scope approvals;
- criteria;
- work programmes;
- evidence registers;
- test records;
- findings;
- reports;
- management responses;
- corrective actions;
- verification;
- closure decisions.

---

## 4. Audit Record Categories

Audit records may be grouped into:

### Planning Records
- audit objective;
- scope;
- risk assessment;
- criteria;
- methodology;
- team;
- independence assessment.

### Execution Records
- evidence requests;
- evidence register;
- interviews;
- observations;
- test procedures;
- test results;
- working papers.

### Finding Records
- findings;
- severity;
- supporting evidence;
- management response;
- disagreements.

### Closure Records
- corrective action;
- verification;
- effectiveness assessment;
- closure decision.

### Governance Records
- audit programme;
- oversight;
- escalations;
- governance review;
- systemic findings.

---

## 5. Record Creation

Material audit activities should generate appropriate records.

Records should be created sufficiently close to the relevant activity to preserve accuracy and context.

The level of record detail should be proportionate to:

- risk;
- consequence;
- audit significance;
- complexity;
- sensitivity.

---

## 6. Unique Identification

Material audit records should be uniquely identifiable where practical.

Identifiers may include:

- audit ID;
- finding ID;
- evidence ID;
- test ID;
- action ID;
- version;
- date.

Consistent identification supports traceability.

---

## 7. Traceability

Audit records should allow relevant information to be traced through:

**Criterion → Control → Evidence → Test → Finding → Conclusion**

Where applicable, traceability should extend to:

**Corrective Action → Verification → Closure**

---

## 8. Golden Thread Traceability

Where relevant, audit records should connect to the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Audit**

This allows audit conclusions to be understood within the broader governance lifecycle.

---

## 9. Evidence Traceability

Each material finding should be traceable to the evidence supporting it.

Evidence should be identifiable by:

- source;
- date;
- configuration;
- version;
- environment where relevant;
- evidence ID.

The relationship between evidence and finding should be explicit.

---

## 10. Test Traceability

Material tests should be traceable to:

- criterion;
- control;
- test objective;
- evidence;
- result;
- finding or conclusion.

This supports audit reproducibility and review.

---

## 11. Decision Traceability

Material audit decisions should identify, where appropriate:

- decision;
- rationale;
- evidence;
- authority;
- date;
- conditions;
- dissent or unresolved disagreement.

This is particularly important where decisions affect:

- operational authorisation;
- autonomy;
- human control;
- safety;
- security;
- major corrective actions.

---

## 12. Configuration Traceability

Where audit conclusions depend on an AI capability, records should identify the relevant configuration where practical.

This may include:

- model version;
- software version;
- hardware;
- data version;
- policy configuration;
- interface;
- autonomy configuration;
- dependencies.

This prevents evidence from being incorrectly attributed to a different system state.

---

## 13. Temporal Traceability

Records should preserve relevant dates and periods.

This is important for:

- audit scope;
- evidence;
- incidents;
- changes;
- operational employment;
- monitoring;
- authorisation;
- corrective actions.

Historical records should remain distinguishable from current status.

---

## 14. Environmental Traceability

Where relevant, records should identify the environment associated with evidence or testing.

This may include:

- nominal;
- degraded;
- disconnected;
- adversarial;
- unusual environmental conditions.

This prevents unsupported generalisation from one environment to another.

---

## 15. Record Integrity

Audit records should be protected from:

- unauthorised modification;
- deletion;
- substitution;
- corruption;
- loss.

Controls may include:

- access control;
- versioning;
- protected storage;
- audit trails;
- integrity mechanisms;
- controlled distribution.

---

## 16. Version Control

Material audit records should have appropriate version control.

Changes to final or approved records should be controlled and traceable.

Superseded versions may need to be retained where they are necessary to reconstruct decisions or findings.

---

## 17. Record Authenticity

Records should be attributable to their source.

Where appropriate, records should identify:

- author;
- owner;
- approving authority;
- creation date;
- modification date;
- version;
- source system.

---

## 18. Working Papers

Working papers may contain:

- analysis;
- notes;
- calculations;
- test results;
- interview notes;
- evidence references;
- draft findings.

Working papers should be managed so that final conclusions can be distinguished from preliminary analysis.

---

## 19. Final Records

Final audit records include approved:

- audit reports;
- findings;
- conclusions;
- corrective action decisions;
- closure decisions.

Final records should be clearly identified and protected from unauthorised alteration.

---

## 20. Sensitive Records

Audit records may contain:

- sensitive operational information;
- security information;
- personal information;
- supplier information;
- technical information;
- legally protected material.

Handling should follow applicable security, privacy, legal, and organisational requirements.

D-AIGAAF itself remains a generic and unclassified governance framework.

---

## 21. Access Control

Access should be based on:

- role;
- need;
- sensitivity;
- audit responsibility;
- governance authority.

Access should be controlled without preventing authorised review.

---

## 22. Evidence and Record Separation

Evidence and audit records should remain distinguishable.

An audit record may reference evidence rather than duplicate it unnecessarily.

This can reduce:

- duplication;
- exposure;
- inconsistent versions;
- unnecessary storage.

---

## 23. Record Retention

Records should be retained according to:

- applicable law;
- organisational policy;
- contractual obligations;
- operational requirements;
- audit requirements;
- investigation or legal holds.

Retention periods should reflect the significance and potential future use of records.

---

## 24. Retention Categories

Organisations may establish different retention periods for:

- routine audit records;
- material findings;
- critical findings;
- authorisation-impacting audits;
- incident-related audits;
- regulatory audits;
- systemic findings.

The organisation should define the applicable schedule.

---

## 25. Legal and Investigation Holds

Where records are subject to a legal, regulatory, or investigation hold, normal disposal should be suspended as required.

The hold should be appropriately documented and controlled.

---

## 26. Record Disposal

At the end of the authorised retention period, records may be disposed of according to applicable requirements.

Disposal should consider:

- sensitivity;
- legal holds;
- operational need;
- contractual obligations;
- security;
- privacy.

Where required, disposal should itself be recorded.

---

## 27. Record Retrieval

Records should be retrievable within a reasonable period by authorised personnel.

Retrievability should consider:

- searchability;
- identifiers;
- metadata;
- storage location;
- format;
- access permissions.

---

## 28. Record Format

Records should be maintained in formats that preserve:

- readability;
- integrity;
- relevant metadata;
- traceability;
- accessibility for the required retention period.

Migration or conversion should preserve material information.

---

## 29. Record Migration

Where systems or storage platforms change, audit records should be migrated or preserved without losing:

- content;
- provenance;
- timestamps;
- identifiers;
- version history;
- relationships.

Migration should be verified where records are material.

---

## 30. Record Loss

If material audit records are lost or corrupted:

1. identify the affected records;
2. assess impact;
3. attempt recovery;
4. identify alternative evidence;
5. assess effect on conclusions;
6. escalate where material;
7. document the event.

Record loss affecting critical evidence may require reassessment of the relevant audit conclusion.

---

## 31. Record Integrity Review

For material audits, record integrity should be periodically reviewed where appropriate.

The review may consider:

- completeness;
- accessibility;
- version control;
- evidence links;
- retention;
- protection.

---

## 32. Audit File Completeness

Before an audit is formally closed, the audit file should be reviewed for appropriate completeness.

It should contain, as applicable:

- approved plan;
- scope;
- criteria;
- evidence;
- testing;
- findings;
- report;
- management responses;
- corrective actions;
- closure or follow-up status.

---

## 33. Record Ownership

Responsibilities should be assigned for:

- record creation;
- evidence custody;
- audit file management;
- approval;
- access;
- retention;
- disposal.

Ownership should remain clear even when records are stored in shared systems.

---

## 34. Third-Party Records

Where audit relies on supplier or external records, the organisation should consider:

- provenance;
- authenticity;
- contractual rights;
- retention;
- accessibility;
- independence;
- continued availability.

Critical governance evidence should not depend on uncontrolled third-party retention where avoidable.

---

## 35. Records and Audit Independence

Audit records should preserve evidence of independent judgement where material.

This may include:

- independence declarations;
- reviewer comments;
- unresolved disagreements;
- escalation;
- changes to findings;
- rationale for conclusions.

---

## 36. Records of Disagreement

Material disagreements should be recorded where they affect:

- finding validity;
- severity;
- evidence interpretation;
- audit conclusion;
- corrective action;
- authorisation implications.

Resolution should be documented where achieved.

---

## 37. Records of Exceptions

Material exceptions should remain traceable even if they do not become formal findings.

This helps identify:

- recurring weaknesses;
- systemic issues;
- trends;
- future audit priorities.

---

## 38. Records and Corrective Action

Corrective-action records should link to:

- original finding;
- responsible owner;
- action;
- evidence;
- verification;
- effectiveness;
- closure.

This provides end-to-end accountability.

---

## 39. Records and Revalidation

Where an audit finding triggers revalidation, records should preserve the connection between:

**Finding → Risk → Revalidation → Evidence → Decision**

This supports later reconstruction of assurance changes.

---

## 40. Records and Reauthorisation

Where audit findings affect operational authority, records should link:

**Finding → Authorisation Condition → Risk → Decision → Restriction/Reauthorisation**

Audit should not itself create or modify operational authority.

---

## 41. Audit Records and Continuous Assurance

Audit records should provide inputs to continuous assurance where relevant.

Recurring findings, evidence gaps, and control weaknesses should inform assurance indicators and review priorities.

---

## 42. Records and Portfolio Governance

Where common findings appear across multiple AI capabilities, records should support aggregation and systemic analysis.

Portfolio governance should be able to identify patterns without losing traceability to individual audits.

---

## 43. Record Quality

Audit records should be:

- accurate;
- clear;
- complete enough for purpose;
- traceable;
- appropriately current;
- protected;
- retrievable.

Record volume should not be mistaken for record quality.

---

## 44. Record Review and Closure

Before formal audit closure, the responsible audit authority should confirm that:

- material records exist;
- findings are traceable;
- conclusions are supported;
- required follow-up is identified;
- retention requirements are established.

## 46. Records Governance Model

D-AIGAAF uses:

**Create → Identify → Link → Protect → Version → Review → Retain → Retrieve → Dispose**

This lifecycle ensures that audit evidence and decisions remain reconstructable throughout their required life.

---

## 47. Core Rule

**Audit accountability depends on records that allow material conclusions and decisions to be reconstructed. D-AIGAAF requires audit records to preserve traceability from criteria and evidence through testing, findings, corrective action, verification, and closure, while maintaining appropriate integrity, access control, retention, and protection.**

---

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Audit Planning → Criteria → Testing → Findings → Corrective Action → Verification → Closure → Records and Traceability**
