# 01 — Workforce Roles and Responsibilities

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document defines the workforce roles and responsibility architecture required to govern, assure, authorise, operate, secure, maintain, change, and retire AI capabilities under D-AIGAAF.

It establishes clear accountability without prescribing a single organisational structure.

The model is intended to be adaptable to different defence organisations, programmes, units, acquisition models, and AI capability types.

---

## 2. Role Architecture

Each consequential AI capability should have clearly identified responsibility for:

- mission need;
- capability ownership;
- risk;
- AI lifecycle management;
- data;
- security;
- TEVV;
- assurance;
- operational environment;
- operational authorisation;
- operational employment;
- incident response;
- change management;
- audit;
- retirement.

A role may perform multiple functions where appropriate, provided conflicts of interest, independence, competence, and authority are adequately managed.

The fundamental relationship is:

**Role → Responsibility → Authority → Competence → Evidence → Accountability**

---

## 3. Core Workforce Roles

Depending on organisational context, the following roles should be identified where applicable:

| Role | Primary Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Governance Authority | Establishes organisational governance and oversight |
| Mission Owner | Defines mission need and intended operational outcome |
| AI Capability Owner | Accountable for the capability across its lifecycle |
| Risk Owner | Owns identified risk and risk treatment |
| Authorising Authority | Determines whether operation is authorised |
| Operational Decision Authority | Exercises human decision rights during employment |
| AI Supervisor/Operator | Supervises and interacts with the AI capability |
| AI/Technical Lead | Responsible for technical design and implementation |
| Data Owner | Responsible for data governance and suitability |
| AI Security Lead | Responsible for AI security risk and controls |
| TEVV Lead | Plans and oversees testing, evaluation, verification and validation |
| Assurance Lead | Maintains continuing assurance and confidence |
| Auditor | Independently examines governance and control effectiveness |
| Maintainer | Maintains system health and approved configuration |
| Incident Lead | Coordinates response to AI-related incidents |
| Supplier Manager | Manages supplier performance and dependencies |
| Training/Competence Authority | Establishes workforce qualification and training requirements |

Not every capability requires every role as a separate person.

---

## 4. Responsibility Assignment

Responsibilities should be assigned explicitly for material decisions and activities.

At minimum, the organisation should identify who:

- defines the mission;
- defines intended AI use;
- establishes requirements;
- assesses risk;
- accepts residual risk;
- establishes controls;
- conducts TEVV;
- determines assurance;
- grants operational authorisation;
- supervises employment;
- exercises intervention or override;
- manages incidents;
- approves changes;
- conducts revalidation;
- conducts reauthorisation;
- performs independent audit;
- approves retirement.

Responsibility should not be inferred merely from job title.

---

## 5. Decision Rights and Authority

Responsibility should be matched with sufficient authority.

Personnel should know:

- what decisions they may make;
- what decisions require approval;
- what decisions may be delegated;
- when escalation is mandatory;
- when they must stop or restrict AI use.

Operational authority should remain distinct from technical authority.

A developer may determine how a capability works without being authorised to determine whether it may be operationally employed.

Similarly, an operator may be authorised to use a capability without being authorised to alter its configuration.

---

## 6. Human Authority and AI

AI systems do not hold organisational authority.

AI may:

- observe;
- analyse;
- recommend;
- support decisions;
- perform authorised actions within defined boundaries.

However, operational authority must remain assigned to authorised humans.

AI must not acquire authority through:

- automation;
- technical capability;
- system state;
- connectivity;
- interaction with another AI;
- loss of communications;
- portfolio inclusion;
- continuity mechanisms.

For consequential use, the responsible human authority should be identifiable.

---

## 7. Role Separation and Independence

Where practical, organisations should separate roles where combining them could create unacceptable conflicts.

Particular attention should be given to:

- development versus independent evaluation;
- supplier delivery versus acceptance;
- risk assessment versus risk acceptance;
- operational ownership versus independent audit;
- assurance versus authorisation;
- system operation versus configuration approval.

Where separation is not practical, compensating controls should be established.

Independent functions should retain sufficient authority to challenge assumptions, evidence, findings, and decisions.

---

## 8. Role Competence and Accountability

Personnel should possess competence appropriate to the decisions they make.

Competence may include:

- operational expertise;
- AI literacy;
- technical knowledge;
- risk management;
- security;
- assurance;
- human factors;
- legal and policy awareness.

Accountability should be supported by records of:

- role assignment;
- authority;
- delegation;
- qualification;
- material decisions;
- approvals;
- interventions;
- escalations.

A role should not carry accountability for decisions over which the individual had no meaningful authority or ability to influence.

---

## 9. Role Changes, Delegation and Continuity

Changes in personnel should not create ambiguity in authority.

Organisations should control:

- appointment;
- delegation;
- temporary substitution;
- relief;
- succession;
- transfer of responsibility.

Delegations should specify:

- scope;
- duration;
- limitations;
- conditions;
- escalation requirements.

Critical responsibilities should have continuity arrangements.

Transfer of responsibility should preserve relevant knowledge concerning:

- risks;
- assumptions;
- limitations;
- evidence;
- authorisation conditions;
- autonomy;
- configuration;
- incidents;
- unresolved issues.

### Core Rule

**Every material AI governance responsibility and decision should have an identifiable human role with appropriate authority, competence, independence where required, and accountability. No person should be held responsible for a decision without corresponding authority and access to the information necessary to exercise it.**

### Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Workforce Roles → Decision Rights → Accountability**
