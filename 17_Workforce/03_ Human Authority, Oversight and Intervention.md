# 03 — Human Authority, Oversight and Intervention

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document establishes workforce requirements for human authority, oversight, supervision, intervention, and control of AI capabilities within D-AIGAAF.

It applies particularly where AI outputs or actions may materially affect:

- human life or safety;
- property;
- mission outcomes;
- security;
- legal or policy obligations.

The objective is to ensure that human control is practical, competent, proportionate, and demonstrable.

---

## 2. Human Authority Model

Human authority should be explicitly assigned for consequential AI.

The organisation should identify who has authority to:

- approve use;
- supervise employment;
- accept or reject AI recommendations;
- authorise specified actions;
- intervene;
- override;
- pause;
- terminate;
- move the system to a safe or restricted state;
- escalate;
- suspend employment.

Authority should be bounded by:

- mission;
- environment;
- autonomy;
- conditions;
- risk;
- role;
- delegation.

AI itself does not hold organisational or operational authority.

---

## 3. Meaningful Human Control

Human presence does not automatically establish human control.

Meaningful human control requires, as appropriate:

- relevant understanding;
- adequate situational awareness;
- awareness of uncertainty;
- sufficient time to act;
- practical intervention capability;
- appropriate authority;
- competence;
- reliable system information.

The level of human control should be proportionate to the consequence and autonomy of the AI capability.

---

## 4. Oversight and Supervision

Personnel assigned supervisory responsibilities should understand:

- system purpose;
- authorised boundaries;
- current system state;
- autonomy state;
- relevant inputs and outputs;
- uncertainty;
- known limitations;
- environmental conditions;
- dependencies;
- intervention mechanisms.

Oversight should be designed around the actual decision tempo and workload of the operational context.

A nominal human approval step should not be considered meaningful control if the person cannot realistically understand or influence the decision.

---

## 5. Intervention and Override

Personnel should be capable of intervening within the required timeframe where intervention is part of the authorised control model.

Intervention mechanisms should be:

- identifiable;
- accessible;
- understandable;
- appropriately protected;
- tested;
- available under relevant operating conditions.

Where an override or intervention mechanism is required for safe operation, its availability should be demonstrated through appropriate TEVV and exercises.

If meaningful intervention cannot be maintained, the organisation should reassess continued operation.

---

## 6. Human Factors

Workforce governance should consider human factors that may degrade effective control, including:

- automation bias;
- cognitive overload;
- fatigue;
- alert saturation;
- excessive workload;
- time pressure;
- ambiguous interfaces;
- poor information presentation;
- reduced situational awareness;
- over-reliance on AI;
- under-reliance where AI is appropriately useful.

Human-AI interaction should support informed judgement rather than encourage passive acceptance.

---

## 7. Unexpected Behaviour and Loss of Control

Personnel should know how to respond when:

- AI outputs become unreliable;
- the system behaves unexpectedly;
- autonomy changes unexpectedly;
- boundaries are approached or exceeded;
- human intervention becomes difficult;
- communications are degraded;
- system state becomes uncertain.

The workforce response should follow applicable incident and fail-safe arrangements.

Where human control cannot be reliably maintained, appropriate actions may include:

**Restrict → Reduce Autonomy → Human Control → Override/Pause → Safe State → Suspend**

Personnel should not attempt to preserve normal operation merely to avoid interruption when continued operation cannot be justified.

---

## 8. Training, Exercises and Assurance

Human-control competence should be developed and tested through:

- role-specific training;
- practical exercises;
- simulations;
- controlled trials;
- degraded-environment scenarios;
- unexpected-behaviour scenarios;
- autonomy-transition scenarios;
- intervention and override exercises.

Evidence should support claims that personnel can exercise their assigned authority under relevant conditions.

Results should feed:

- TEVV;
- continuous assurance;
- incident learning;
- workforce development;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation where necessary.

---

## 9. Records, Accountability and Integration

Material human-control decisions and actions should be traceable to the responsible human authority where appropriate.

Records may include:

- authority assignment;
- delegation;
- supervision;
- approval;
- intervention;
- override;
- escalation;
- suspension;
- training;
- exercise results;
- competence assessment.

## 10. Core Rule and Golden Thread

### Core Rule

**Human oversight is meaningful only when authorised personnel possess the competence, information, situational awareness, time, authority, and practical means necessary to understand, supervise, challenge, intervene in, override, or safely terminate AI behaviour as required by the authorised use.**

### Human-Control Model

**Understand → Observe → Assess → Decide → Intervene → Verify → Escalate**

### Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Human Authority → Oversight → Intervention → Accountability**
