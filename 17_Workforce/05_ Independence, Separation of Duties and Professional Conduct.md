# 05 — Independence, Separation of Duties and Professional Conduct

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document establishes workforce requirements for independence, separation of duties, objectivity, professional conduct, and freedom to challenge within D-AIGAAF.

These requirements are particularly important where personnel make or influence decisions concerning:

- high-consequence AI;
- operational authorisation;
- risk acceptance;
- assurance;
- TEVV;
- audit;
- security;
- human control;
- autonomy.

The objective is to reduce conflicts of interest and ensure that material decisions can be challenged objectively.

---

## 2. Independence Principle

Personnel performing independent assurance, audit, evaluation, or challenge should have sufficient independence from the activity being assessed.

Independence should consider:

- organisational position;
- reporting relationships;
- decision authority;
- financial interests;
- operational responsibility;
- supplier relationships;
- prior involvement;
- personal conflicts.

Independence should be proportionate to consequence and risk.

---

## 3. Separation of Duties

Where practical, organisations should separate functions where combining them could create unacceptable conflicts.

Examples include:

- development and independent evaluation;
- supplier delivery and acceptance;
- risk assessment and risk acceptance;
- operational ownership and independent audit;
- assurance and authorisation;
- system operation and configuration approval;
- corrective-action implementation and independent closure verification.

Separation should not create unnecessary bureaucracy where the risk of combining functions is demonstrably low.

Where separation is impractical, compensating controls should be established.

---

## 4. Conflict of Interest

Personnel should identify and appropriately manage actual, potential, or perceived conflicts of interest.

Potential conflicts may arise from:

- financial interests;
- supplier relationships;
- personal relationships;
- previous responsibility for the system;
- performance incentives;
- organisational pressure;
- career interests.

Material conflicts should be declared and managed before relevant decisions are made.

Possible safeguards include:

- reassignment;
- independent review;
- additional approval;
- recusal;
- enhanced evidence requirements.

---

## 5. Objectivity and Evidence-Based Judgement

Material workforce decisions should be based on appropriate evidence rather than:

- organisational preference;
- schedule pressure;
- sunk cost;
- commercial pressure;
- personal confidence;
- assumptions presented as facts.

Personnel should distinguish between:

- evidence;
- interpretation;
- judgement;
- assumption;
- uncertainty.

Unsupported confidence should not substitute for evidence.

---

## 6. Authority to Challenge

Personnel responsible for assurance, TEVV, security, audit, or operational supervision should have an appropriate ability to challenge:

- technical claims;
- risk assessments;
- evidence;
- autonomy boundaries;
- human-control assumptions;
- security controls;
- environmental assumptions;
- authorisation conditions;
- corrective actions.

Challenge should be evidence-based and proportionate.

Legitimate challenge should not be suppressed merely because it may delay deployment or create additional work.

---

## 7. Professional Conduct and Speak-Up

Personnel should be expected to:

- report material concerns;
- preserve relevant evidence;
- disclose conflicts;
- follow approved procedures;
- respect decision rights;
- avoid concealment;
- challenge unsupported claims;
- escalate material risks.

Organisations should provide appropriate channels for raising concerns.

Personnel should be able to escalate concerns regarding:

- safety;
- security;
- unexpected AI behaviour;
- loss of human control;
- unauthorised autonomy;
- inadequate evidence;
- governance failures.

---

## 8. Organisational Pressure and Governance Integrity

Workforce governance should recognise pressure arising from:

- operational urgency;
- deployment schedules;
- procurement commitments;
- resource limitations;
- reputational concerns;
- commercial interests;
- organisational hierarchy.

Pressure should not result in:

- bypassing required assurance;
- suppressing negative findings;
- altering evidence;
- concealing uncertainty;
- exceeding authority;
- ignoring required escalation.

Where pressure materially affects governance integrity, the issue should be escalated.

---

## 9. Records, Review and Integration

Appropriate records should capture:

- independence declarations;
- conflicts of interest;
- recusals;
- compensating controls;
- material challenges;
- disagreements;
- escalations;
- review decisions.

Independence and separation arrangements should be periodically reviewed, particularly following:

- role changes;
- organisational restructuring;
- supplier changes;
- significant AI changes;
- incidents;
- major governance decisions.

## 10. Core Rule and Golden Thread

### Core Rule

**Personnel performing independent assurance, TEVV, audit, security, or other material challenge functions shall have sufficient independence, objectivity, competence, and authority to identify and escalate concerns without inappropriate interference. Where duties cannot be separated, compensating controls shall preserve governance integrity.**

### Independence Model

**Identify Conflict → Assess Independence → Apply Safeguards → Perform Independent Challenge → Record → Escalate → Review**

### Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Independence → Challenge → Objective Decision → Accountability**
