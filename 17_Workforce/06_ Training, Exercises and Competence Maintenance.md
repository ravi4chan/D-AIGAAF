# 06 — Training, Exercises and Competence Maintenance

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document establishes requirements for training, exercises, practical competence, and continuing workforce development for personnel involved in consequential AI.

Training should ensure that personnel can perform their assigned responsibilities under the conditions in which those responsibilities may actually be required.

The approach applies across:

- governance;
- technical development;
- AI security;
- TEVV;
- assurance;
- authorisation;
- operational employment;
- incident response;
- change management;
- audit.

---

## 2. Training Requirements

Training requirements should be derived from:

- role;
- responsibility;
- authority;
- AI capability;
- consequence;
- autonomy;
- operational environment;
- security requirements;
- known failure modes;
- applicable policy and law.

Training should be role-specific rather than relying solely on generic AI awareness.

Higher-consequence roles should have proportionately stronger training requirements.

---

## 3. Training Content

Depending on role, training may include:

- AI and machine-learning fundamentals;
- system purpose and limitations;
- uncertainty;
- data dependencies;
- failure modes;
- automation bias;
- human-AI interaction;
- autonomy;
- human authority;
- intervention and override;
- safe-state procedures;
- operational environment;
- AI security;
- incident response;
- change management;
- assurance and evidence;
- legal and policy requirements.

Personnel should understand both what the AI can do and what it should not be relied upon to do.

---

## 4. Practical Exercises

Where practical, competence should be developed through exercises rather than training alone.

Exercises may include:

- normal operation;
- degraded operation;
- disconnected operation;
- unexpected AI behaviour;
- loss of human control;
- autonomy transitions;
- security events;
- data-quality failures;
- conflicting information;
- time-pressure decisions;
- intervention and override.

Exercises should be designed around realistic governance and operational responsibilities without requiring sensitive operational detail.

---

## 5. Competence Assessment

Training should be followed by appropriate assessment where the role warrants it.

Assessment may include:

- knowledge tests;
- practical demonstrations;
- simulations;
- supervised employment;
- scenario-based assessments;
- intervention exercises;
- incident-response exercises;
- technical evaluation.

Assessment should examine judgement and behaviour where those are material to the role.

Successful attendance should not automatically equal demonstrated competence.

---

## 6. Qualification and Currency

Where appropriate, personnel should be formally qualified or designated before performing material functions.

Qualification should identify:

- role;
- authority;
- scope;
- limitations;
- validity;
- renewal requirements.

Competence should remain current through:

- refresher training;
- recurring assessments;
- exercises;
- supervised practice;
- continuing professional development.

Currency requirements should be proportionate to role and consequence.

---

## 7. Change, Incident and Requalification Triggers

Additional training or reassessment may be required following:

- significant system changes;
- model updates;
- changes in autonomy;
- new operational environments;
- major incidents;
- new threats;
- revised procedures;
- extended absence;
- repeated performance problems;
- changes in role.

Training and qualification should therefore connect to:

**Change → Impact Assessment → Competence Review → Training/Assessment → Requalification where required**

Where competence is temporarily inadequate, relevant authority or system access should be restricted as appropriate.

---

## 8. Exercise Evidence, Learning and Assurance

Exercises should generate appropriate evidence concerning:

- competence;
- human control;
- intervention;
- decision quality;
- incident response;
- understanding of uncertainty;
- autonomy management.

Findings should feed:

- continuous assurance;
- TEVV;
- incident learning;
- workforce risk;
- corrective action;
- future training.

Exercise results should not be used merely as attendance statistics.

---

## 9. Records, Governance and Continuous Improvement

Organisations should maintain appropriate records of:

- training;
- qualification;
- assessment;
- exercises;
- competence gaps;
- remediation;
- currency;
- restrictions.

Training programmes should be periodically reviewed using:

- audit findings;
- incidents;
- TEVV results;
- operational experience;
- changes in AI technology;
- changes in threats;
- changes in governance requirements.

## 10. Core Rule and Golden Thread

### Core Rule

**Training shall establish and maintain demonstrated workforce competence appropriate to role, consequence, autonomy, and operating conditions. For consequential AI, personnel should be capable of applying their knowledge under realistic conditions, including uncertainty, degradation, unexpected behaviour, and the need to exercise human authority.**

### Training and Competence Cycle

**Identify Need → Design Training → Train → Assess → Exercise → Qualify → Maintain Currency → Learn → Reassess → Improve**

### Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Workforce Training → Exercise → Demonstrated Competence → Effective Human Authority**
