# 07 — Workforce Risk, Monitoring and Corrective Action

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document establishes the framework for identifying, monitoring, assessing, and treating workforce risks that may affect the safe and effective governance of AI capabilities.

Workforce risk includes conditions that may reduce the organisation's ability to:

- exercise human authority;
- maintain meaningful human control;
- operate AI safely;
- maintain security;
- conduct assurance;
- respond to incidents;
- manage changes;
- sustain operational continuity.

---

## 2. Workforce Risk Categories

Workforce risks may arise from:

- inadequate competence;
- insufficient staffing;
- excessive workload;
- unclear responsibilities;
- inadequate authority;
- weak independence;
- key-person dependency;
- insufficient succession;
- inadequate training;
- loss of critical knowledge;
- poor human-AI interaction;
- insufficient intervention capability;
- inadequate incident-response capacity.

Risk assessment should consider both likelihood and consequence.

---

## 3. Risk Assessment

Workforce risk should be assessed in relation to the AI capability and its operating context.

Relevant factors may include:

- mission consequence;
- autonomy;
- human-control requirements;
- operational tempo;
- environmental conditions;
- system complexity;
- security requirements;
- dependency on specialist personnel;
- availability of alternative personnel.

Higher-consequence capabilities should receive proportionately stronger workforce controls.

---

## 4. Workforce Monitoring Indicators

Organisations may monitor indicators such as:

- staffing levels;
- qualification coverage;
- training currency;
- workload;
- fatigue indicators where appropriate;
- specialist availability;
- intervention performance;
- exercise performance;
- incident-response readiness;
- overdue competence remediation;
- key-person dependency.

Indicators should be interpreted in context.

No single workforce metric should be treated as proof of readiness.

---

## 5. Human-Control and Performance Monitoring

Where AI requires meaningful human control, workforce monitoring should consider whether personnel can realistically:

- understand AI outputs;
- recognise uncertainty;
- maintain situational awareness;
- supervise the system;
- make required decisions;
- intervene;
- override;
- move to an appropriate safe state;
- escalate.

A reduction in human-control capability should be treated as a potential assurance issue.

---

## 6. Corrective and Preventive Action

Workforce weaknesses may require:

- additional training;
- reassignment;
- increased supervision;
- staffing changes;
- revised workload;
- additional specialist support;
- improved procedures;
- reduced autonomy;
- restricted employment;
- additional assurance.

Corrective action should address underlying causes where practicable.

Preventive action should be considered where similar weaknesses could affect other capabilities.

---

## 7. Escalation and Operational Impact

Material workforce risks should be escalated through established governance channels.

Escalation may be required where workforce limitations affect:

- human authority;
- safety;
- security;
- autonomy;
- mission effectiveness;
- operational readiness;
- assurance confidence;
- incident response.

Possible governance outcomes include:

- continue;
- continue with conditions;
- enhanced monitoring;
- additional training;
- reduced autonomy;
- restricted use;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation;
- suspension.

Workforce limitations should not be concealed by assuming that AI automation eliminates the need for human capacity.

---

## 8. Assurance, Audit and Review

Workforce risk should form part of continuous assurance and may be examined through:

- TEVV;
- audit;
- incident investigation;
- exercises;
- operational reviews;
- governance reviews.

Findings should be linked to evidence.

Material weaknesses should be tracked until appropriately addressed and verified.

The effectiveness of corrective action should be assessed rather than assumed.

---

## 9. Records, Learning and Integration

Appropriate records should include:

- identified workforce risks;
- indicators;
- assessments;
- corrective actions;
- responsible owners;
- due dates;
- evidence;
- verification;
- residual risk;
- governance decisions.

Workforce learning should feed:

- competence requirements;
- training;
- exercises;
- risk management;
- assurance;
- change management;
- authorisation.

## 10. Core Rule and Golden Thread

### Core Rule

**Workforce weaknesses that could materially affect human control, operational safety, security, assurance, or mission effectiveness shall be identified, monitored, treated, and verified. Where competent human capacity cannot be sustained, continued AI employment shall be reassessed rather than relying on unverified assumptions of human availability.**

### Workforce Risk Cycle

**Identify → Assess → Monitor → Treat → Verify → Escalate → Learn → Improve**

### Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Workforce Risk → Corrective Action → Verified Human Capacity → Sustained Governance**
