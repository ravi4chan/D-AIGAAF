# 08 — Knowledge Management, Records and Continuous Learning

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document establishes requirements for managing workforce knowledge, records, institutional learning, and continuity within D-AIGAAF.

The objective is to ensure that critical knowledge required to govern and safely employ AI is:

- identified;
- captured;
- maintained;
- accessible to authorised personnel;
- transferred when roles change;
- protected;
- updated through learning.

Workforce knowledge should not depend unnecessarily on individual memory or informal relationships.

---

## 2. Critical Knowledge

Organisations should identify knowledge that is material to safe and effective AI governance.

This may include:

- mission purpose;
- system capabilities;
- limitations;
- assumptions;
- risks;
- autonomy;
- human-control arrangements;
- operating boundaries;
- configurations;
- dependencies;
- assurance evidence;
- incidents;
- lessons;
- authorisation conditions;
- unresolved issues.

The required level of knowledge management should be proportionate to consequence.

---

## 3. Knowledge Capture and Documentation

Material knowledge should be captured in appropriate organisational records.

Documentation should be:

- accurate;
- understandable;
- sufficiently complete;
- traceable;
- appropriately current;
- accessible to authorised personnel.

Knowledge should be captured at relevant points in the lifecycle rather than only when personnel depart.

Where practical, important decisions should include their rationale and supporting evidence.

---

## 4. Knowledge Transfer and Role Transition

When personnel change roles, knowledge transfer should address material information necessary for the successor to perform the role safely.

Transfer may include:

- current responsibilities;
- authority;
- risks;
- assumptions;
- limitations;
- evidence;
- open findings;
- incidents;
- changes;
- authorisation conditions;
- unresolved decisions.

The transfer should be verified where the role is critical.

---

## 5. Key-Person Dependency and Succession

Organisations should identify critical functions that depend heavily on a single individual.

Potential mitigations include:

- cross-training;
- deputies;
- succession plans;
- shared documentation;
- role redundancy;
- mentoring;
- independent review.

Critical governance knowledge should not be intentionally or inadvertently retained solely by one person.

---

## 6. Records and Information Protection

Workforce records may include:

- role assignments;
- delegations;
- qualifications;
- training;
- competence assessments;
- exercise results;
- independence declarations;
- performance evidence;
- corrective actions.

Records should be protected according to applicable:

- security requirements;
- privacy requirements;
- legal requirements;
- employment requirements;
- retention requirements.

Access should be based on role and legitimate need.

---

## 7. Continuous Learning

Workforce knowledge should be updated using lessons from:

- audits;
- incidents;
- TEVV;
- exercises;
- operational experience;
- security events;
- environmental changes;
- system changes;
- assurance reviews.

Learning should result in practical improvement where appropriate.

The learning cycle is:

**Observe → Capture → Assess → Learn → Apply → Verify → Institutionalise**

---

## 8. Knowledge Quality and Review

Critical knowledge should be periodically reviewed for:

- accuracy;
- currency;
- completeness;
- relevance;
- consistency;
- ownership;
- accessibility.

Outdated information should be identified and replaced or clearly marked as historical.

Conflicting information should be resolved or explicitly recorded as uncertainty.

Knowledge repositories should not become uncontrolled collections of obsolete material.

---

## 9. Integration and Governance

Knowledge management integrates with:

- **Module 04 — AI Lifecycle:** lifecycle records and system knowledge;
- **Module 08 — Human Authority:** decision rights and human-control knowledge;
- **Module 09 — TEVV:** evaluation evidence and findings;
- **Module 11 — Operational Authorisation:** authority, conditions and restrictions;
- **Module 12 — Operational Employment:** operational experience;
- **Module 13 — Continuous Assurance:** assurance evidence and lessons;
- **Module 14 — Incident & Fail-Safe:** incident knowledge;
- **Module 15 — Change & Reauthorisation:** configuration and change knowledge;
- **Module 16 — Audit & Evidence:** audit records and traceability.

Material workforce knowledge should remain linked to the relevant AI capability, mission, environment, authority, and lifecycle state.

---

## 10. Core Rule and Golden Thread

### Core Rule

**Critical AI governance knowledge shall be captured, protected, maintained, transferred, and continuously improved so that safe governance and human authority do not depend on individual memory or the continued availability of a single person.**

### Knowledge Continuity Model

**Identify → Capture → Validate → Protect → Share → Transfer → Review → Learn → Update**

### Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Workforce Knowledge → Records → Knowledge Transfer → Institutional Learning**
