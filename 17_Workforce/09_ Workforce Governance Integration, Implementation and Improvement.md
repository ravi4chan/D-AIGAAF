# 09 — Workforce Governance Integration, Implementation and Improvement

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document establishes how workforce governance is implemented, reviewed, and continuously improved within D-AIGAAF.

It brings together the workforce requirements established across Module 17 and connects them to the wider D-AIGAAF governance lifecycle.

The objective is to ensure that workforce arrangements remain:

- appropriate;
- competent;
- adequately staffed;
- independent where required;
- capable of maintaining human authority;
- resilient;
- auditable;
- responsive to change.

---

## 2. Workforce Governance Implementation

Organisations implementing D-AIGAAF should establish, as appropriate:

- defined workforce roles;
- decision rights;
- competence requirements;
- AI literacy requirements;
- qualification arrangements;
- training;
- exercise programmes;
- workforce capacity assessments;
- continuity arrangements;
- independence safeguards;
- workforce risk monitoring;
- knowledge-management arrangements.

Implementation should be proportionate to:

- consequence;
- autonomy;
- mission;
- environment;
- system complexity;
- organisational structure.

---

## 3. Workforce Readiness

Before a consequential AI capability is authorised or employed, the organisation should consider whether the workforce is ready to perform the required functions.

Readiness may include:

- qualified personnel available;
- required authority assigned;
- training current;
- supervision available;
- intervention capability demonstrated;
- workload sustainable;
- specialist support available;
- incident response available;
- continuity arrangements established;
- relevant knowledge accessible.

Workforce readiness should be supported by evidence.

A system should not be considered operationally ready solely because the technology itself is ready.

---

## 4. Workforce Governance Review

Workforce arrangements should be reviewed periodically and when material changes occur.

Review triggers may include:

- significant AI changes;
- changes in autonomy;
- new missions;
- new environments;
- major incidents;
- recurring audit findings;
- changes in threats;
- organisational restructuring;
- supplier changes;
- sustained workload increases.

Review should assess whether current workforce arrangements remain appropriate.

Possible outcomes include:

- continue;
- continue with conditions;
- additional training;
- additional staffing;
- enhanced supervision;
- reduced autonomy;
- restricted employment;
- revalidation;
- reauthorisation;
- suspension.

---

## 5. Workforce Metrics and Assurance

Organisations may maintain workforce governance indicators covering:

- role coverage;
- qualification status;
- training currency;
- competence;
- staffing;
- workload;
- intervention performance;
- exercise performance;
- incident-response readiness;
- key-person dependency;
- open workforce risks;
- overdue corrective actions.

Indicators should support decision-making rather than become artificial performance targets.

Workforce assurance should consider both quantitative indicators and qualitative evidence.

---

## 6. Integration Across the D-AIGAAF Lifecycle

Workforce governance should be integrated throughout:

**Mission → Risk → Requirements → Development/Acquisition → TEVV → Deployment → Authorisation → Employment → Monitoring → Change → Incident → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Retirement**

Workforce requirements should therefore be established early and revisited throughout the lifecycle.

Material changes in workforce competence, capacity, authority, or independence should be treated as potential governance changes.

## 8. Implementation and Continuous Improvement

Workforce governance should follow a continuous improvement cycle:

**Assess → Identify Gaps → Prioritise → Implement → Train → Exercise → Measure → Review → Improve**

Improvement should consider:

- audit findings;
- incidents;
- TEVV results;
- exercises;
- operational experience;
- workforce indicators;
- changes in AI technology;
- emerging threats;
- changes in governance requirements.

Lessons should be incorporated into policies, role definitions, competence requirements, training, exercises, and assurance where appropriate.

---

## 9. Workforce Governance Records and Accountability

Organisations should maintain appropriate evidence of workforce governance, including:

- role assignments;
- authority and delegations;
- competence requirements;
- qualifications;
- training;
- exercises;
- capacity assessments;
- continuity arrangements;
- independence assessments;
- workforce risks;
- corrective actions;
- governance reviews;
- material decisions.

Records should support reconstruction of who was responsible, what authority existed, what competence was demonstrated, and whether workforce conditions supported the relevant AI activity.

---

## 10. Core Rule and Golden Thread

### Core Rule

**Workforce governance shall be treated as an integral component of AI governance, assurance, operational readiness, and human authority. A consequential AI capability should not be authorised or employed where the organisation cannot demonstrate sufficient competent, authorised, available, and appropriately supported personnel to maintain required control and governance.**

### Workforce Governance Cycle

**Define → Assign → Qualify → Train → Exercise → Assess → Deploy → Monitor → Learn → Improve**

### Final Workforce Principle

**Assured ≠ Authorised ≠ Ready ≠ Deployed ≠ Employed**

Workforce readiness is one component of operational readiness and does not itself constitute operational authorisation.

### Golden Thread

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Workforce → Roles → Competence → Capacity → Human Authority → Accountability → Continuous Improvement**
