## 00 — Maturity Model Governance Framework

### 1. Purpose and Scope

The D-AIGAAF Maturity Model provides a structured method for assessing how consistently, effectively, and sustainably an organisation can govern, assure, authorise, employ, and continuously manage AI capabilities in defence contexts.

It applies across the AI capability lifecycle and the governance domains established by D-AIGAAF. It is intended to assess organisational and capability maturity rather than to certify that any individual AI system is safe, compliant, assured, or authorised for operational use.

The model may be applied at organisational, command, programme, capability, mission, system, or governance-process level, provided the assessment scope is explicitly defined.

### 2. Maturity Model Principles

The Maturity Model shall be:

1. **Evidence-based** — maturity claims shall be supported by credible evidence.
2. **Context-sensitive** — assessment shall consider mission, consequence, autonomy, environment, dependencies, and operational conditions.
3. **Consequence-aware** — higher-consequence AI capabilities require stronger governance maturity.
4. **Lifecycle-oriented** — maturity shall cover the complete lifecycle rather than only development or deployment.
5. **Outcome-focused** — the model shall assess whether governance processes work in practice, not merely whether documents exist.
6. **Repeatable** — assessments should produce reasonably consistent results when applied by competent assessors.
7. **Continuously improving** — maturity is expected to evolve as capabilities, threats, technologies, and missions change.

### 3. Maturity Model Governance Architecture

Maturity assessment shall operate as a governed process within D-AIGAAF.

The governance architecture should establish:

- assessment ownership and accountability;
- defined assessment scope and boundaries;
- approved maturity dimensions and criteria;
- assessment roles and independence requirements;
- evidence requirements;
- scoring and confidence rules;
- review and challenge mechanisms;
- approval and reporting arrangements;
- treatment of identified gaps and risks; and
- reassessment triggers and periodic review.

Maturity methodology itself shall be subject to governance review and controlled change.

### 4. Maturity Domains and Capability Areas

The Maturity Model shall assess the governance capability required across relevant D-AIGAAF domains, including as applicable:

- strategy and governance;
- mission and use-case definition;
- risk and autonomy;
- AI lifecycle;
- data and information;
- AI security;
- supply chain and sovereignty;
- human authority;
- TEVV;
- operational environment;
- operational authorisation;
- operational employment;
- continuous assurance;
- incident and fail-safe management;
- change and reauthorisation;
- audit and evidence;
- workforce;
- architecture and technical controls;
- legal, policy, acquisition, interoperability, documentation, and retirement arrangements.

The assessment shall distinguish between the existence of a process and its demonstrated effectiveness.

### 5. Maturity Levels

D-AIGAAF uses five indicative maturity levels:

| Level | Name | General Characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Initial | Activities are ad hoc, person-dependent, or inconsistently governed. |
| 2 | Developing | Basic processes and responsibilities are emerging but remain uneven. |
| 3 | Defined | Processes, responsibilities, controls, and expectations are formally established. |
| 4 | Managed | Processes are consistently implemented, measured, reviewed, and integrated. |
| 5 | Institutionalised | Governance is embedded, evidence-driven, continuously improved, resilient, and sustained across relevant contexts. |

These levels describe governance capability, not the safety or operational acceptability of an AI capability.

### 6. Assessment, Evidence and Confidence

A maturity assessment shall consider both **capability level** and **confidence in the assessment**.

Evidence may include:

- policies and standards;
- governance decisions;
- risk assessments;
- requirements and control records;
- TEVV results;
- assurance findings;
- operational records;
- training and competence records;
- audit evidence;
- incident and corrective-action records;
- change and reauthorisation records; and
- demonstrated performance of governance processes.

Assessors shall identify material evidence gaps. Unsupported assertions shall not be treated as equivalent to demonstrated capability.

### 7. Maturity Gaps, Risk and Improvement

Maturity gaps shall be evaluated in relation to consequence and operational need.

A low maturity level is not automatically an operational prohibition; however, insufficient maturity in a critical governance area may create unacceptable risk or prevent progression through D-AIGAAF lifecycle gates.

Improvement priorities should consider:

**Consequence → Risk → Dependency → Governance Gap → Evidence Gap → Required Capability → Improvement Action**

Critical gaps shall be escalated through the applicable governance and risk-management processes.

### 8. Governance Use of Maturity Results

Maturity results may support:

- governance prioritisation;
- investment and capability development;
- workforce planning;
- assurance planning;
- audit planning;
- supplier and dependency management;
- organisational benchmarking;
- improvement roadmaps;
- readiness discussions; and
- senior governance review.

Maturity results shall **not**, by themselves, constitute:

- compliance certification;
- TEVV acceptance;
- assurance approval;
- operational readiness;
- operational authorisation;
- permission to increase autonomy; or
- acceptance of residual operational risk.

### 9. Lifecycle Integration and Records

Maturity shall be considered throughout the D-AIGAAF lifecycle.

Material changes to missions, systems, models, data, environments, autonomy, dependencies, threats, workforce arrangements, or governance controls may trigger reassessment.

Assessment scope, methodology, evidence, findings, scores, confidence, decisions, improvement actions, approvals, and reassessment results shall be retained as governance records in accordance with applicable record-management requirements.

### 10. Core Rule and Golden Thread

**Core Rule:**  
> Maturity measures the strength and institutionalisation of the governance capability surrounding AI; it does not confer safety, assurance, authority, or operational permission.

The D-AIGAAF Maturity Model remains connected to the Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Workforce → Maturity → Continuous Improvement**

Maturity shall strengthen the organisation's ability to execute this Golden Thread reliably and repeatedly without becoming a substitute for any individual lifecycle, assurance, or authorisation decision.
