## 01 — Maturity Dimensions and Domains

### 1. Purpose

This document defines the dimensions and domains used to assess D-AIGAAF maturity.

The dimensions provide a common structure for evaluating whether an organisation or AI capability has the governance capability required to manage AI responsibly across its lifecycle, operational context, consequence level, and autonomy.

The dimensions shall be applied proportionately to the assessment scope and shall not be interpreted as a simple checklist.

### 2. Maturity Dimension Model

D-AIGAAF maturity shall be assessed across the following broad dimensions:

1. **Governance** — authority, accountability, decision rights, policy, oversight, and governance processes.
2. **Risk and Autonomy** — identification, assessment, treatment, acceptance, monitoring, and control of AI-related risk and autonomy.
3. **Lifecycle Management** — governance from requirements and development or acquisition through deployment, change, revalidation, retirement, and decommissioning.
4. **Data and Information** — governance, quality, provenance, representativeness, integrity, protection, and lifecycle management.
5. **Security and Resilience** — AI security, adversarial resilience, dependencies, continuity, and protection against compromise or misuse.
6. **Human Authority and Workforce** — human decision rights, meaningful control, competence, capacity, independence, accountability, and continuity.
7. **TEVV and Assurance** — testing, evaluation, verification, validation, evidence, assurance, independent challenge, and confidence.
8. **Operational Governance** — operational environment, authorisation, employment, monitoring, contingency arrangements, and safe-state management.
9. **Incident and Change Governance** — incident response, fail-safe arrangements, corrective action, configuration control, change assessment, revalidation, and reauthorisation.
10. **Institutionalisation and Improvement** — audit, knowledge management, learning, benchmarking, metrics, governance integration, and continuous improvement.

### 3. Governance Dimension

The Governance dimension evaluates whether AI governance is formally established and capable of functioning in practice.

It should consider:

- governance structures and committees;
- decision rights and delegated authority;
- accountability;
- policy and standards alignment;
- escalation pathways;
- governance independence;
- management review; and
- evidence that governance decisions are implemented.

A mature governance system demonstrates clear authority without creating ambiguity between technical capability and operational authority.

### 4. Risk, Autonomy and Lifecycle Dimensions

The **Risk and Autonomy** dimension evaluates whether risk and autonomy are systematically understood and controlled, including autonomy boundaries, transitions, loss-of-control scenarios, residual risk, and risk acceptance.

The **Lifecycle Management** dimension evaluates whether governance controls remain effective from mission need and requirements through development or acquisition, integration, deployment, monitoring, change, revalidation, reauthorisation, retirement, and decommissioning.

Maturity requires continuity across these stages rather than isolated excellence at one stage.

### 5. Data, Security and Supply Chain Dimensions

The **Data and Information** dimension evaluates whether information supporting AI is governed, traceable, sufficiently reliable, representative, protected, and appropriately managed throughout its lifecycle.

The **Security and Resilience** dimension evaluates protection against threats to AI systems, models, data, interfaces, dependencies, and supporting infrastructure, including adversarial conditions and degraded operations.

Supply-chain and sovereignty considerations shall be incorporated where external dependencies, suppliers, components, services, models, data, or infrastructure materially affect governance risk or continuity.

### 6. Human Authority and Workforce Dimension

This dimension evaluates whether people responsible for governing, assuring, authorising, supervising, and employing AI possess:

- appropriate authority;
- role-specific competence and AI literacy;
- adequate capacity;
- situational understanding;
- independence where required;
- intervention and override capability; and
- clear accountability.

A mature organisation does not rely on nominal human involvement. It demonstrates meaningful human control appropriate to the consequence and autonomy of the capability.

### 7. TEVV and Assurance Dimension

The TEVV and Assurance dimension evaluates whether claims about AI performance, safety, security, reliability, robustness, human control, autonomy, and mission effectiveness are supported by credible evidence.

It should consider:

- defined evaluation objectives;
- appropriate test methods;
- representative operational conditions;
- independent evaluation where required;
- evidence integrity and traceability;
- assurance confidence;
- unresolved findings; and
- continuing assurance after deployment.

High maturity requires the organisation to recognise uncertainty and evidence limitations rather than treating absence of evidence as evidence of acceptability.

### 8. Operational Governance Dimension

Operational governance evaluates whether AI can be governed under the conditions in which it may actually be used.

Relevant areas include:

- operational environment characterisation;
- degraded and disconnected conditions;
- adversarial conditions;
- operational authorisation;
- employment controls;
- autonomy boundaries;
- human intervention;
- monitoring;
- contingency arrangements; and
- safe-state management.

Operational maturity must reflect the difference between controlled test conditions and real operating conditions.

### 9. Incident, Change and Institutionalisation Dimensions

The **Incident and Change Governance** dimension evaluates whether the organisation can detect, respond to, investigate, correct, recover from, and learn from incidents and manage changes without losing justified assurance or authority.

The **Institutionalisation and Improvement** dimension evaluates whether governance is embedded beyond individual programmes or personnel through:

- audit;
- records and traceability;
- knowledge management;
- lessons learned;
- metrics;
- benchmarking;
- corrective and preventive action; and
- continuous improvement.

### 10. Cross-Domain Assessment Rule

Maturity shall be assessed across the relevant dimensions as an integrated system.

A strong score in one domain shall not compensate automatically for a critical weakness in another domain.

Particular attention shall be given to dependencies between:

**Mission → Risk → Autonomy → Human Authority → TEVV → Environment → Authorisation → Employment → Assurance → Change → Workforce → Improvement**

The assessment shall identify both domain maturity and material cross-domain weaknesses.

**Core Rule:**  
> D-AIGAAF maturity is a multi-dimensional measure of governance capability, effectiveness, resilience, and institutionalisation. It shall never be reduced to a single score that implies an AI capability is safe, assured, or authorised.
