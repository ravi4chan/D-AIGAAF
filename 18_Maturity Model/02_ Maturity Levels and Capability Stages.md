# 02-Maturity Levels and Capability Stages

### 1. Purpose

This document defines the maturity levels used by D-AIGAAF to describe the progression from ad hoc AI governance toward institutionalised, evidence-driven, resilient governance capability.

The levels provide a common language for assessment and improvement. They are not a certification scheme and do not independently determine whether an AI capability may be authorised or operationally employed.

### 2. Level 1 — Initial

At **Level 1 — Initial**, governance is primarily ad hoc, reactive, or dependent on individual expertise.

Typical characteristics include:

- unclear or overlapping responsibilities;
- inconsistent processes;
- limited documentation;
- person-dependent knowledge;
- reactive risk management;
- inconsistent evidence;
- limited lifecycle integration; and
- weak ability to demonstrate repeatable governance outcomes.

The organisation may still possess technically capable AI systems, but governance capability is not reliably institutionalised.

### 3. Level 2 — Developing

At **Level 2 — Developing**, foundational governance practices are emerging.

Typical characteristics include:

- basic policies and procedures;
- identified roles and responsibilities;
- initial risk-management processes;
- developing AI literacy;
- defined but incomplete lifecycle controls;
- increasing documentation;
- initial TEVV and assurance practices; and
- recognition of operational and security considerations.

Implementation may remain inconsistent across programmes, teams, or missions.

### 4. Level 3 — Defined

At **Level 3 — Defined**, governance processes and responsibilities are formally established and consistently specified.

Typical characteristics include:

- documented governance architecture;
- defined decision rights;
- formal risk and autonomy processes;
- lifecycle governance;
- established data and security controls;
- defined human-authority arrangements;
- planned TEVV;
- documented operational authorisation processes;
- controlled change management; and
- structured records and evidence.

The organisation can demonstrate that expected governance processes are defined and understood.

### 5. Level 4 — Managed

At **Level 4 — Managed**, governance is consistently implemented, measured, reviewed, and actively managed.

Typical characteristics include:

- reliable execution of governance processes;
- measurable control effectiveness;
- systematic assurance monitoring;
- meaningful management information;
- independent challenge where appropriate;
- proactive risk treatment;
- integrated operational and lifecycle governance;
- effective incident and change response;
- demonstrated workforce competence and capacity; and
- evidence-based corrective and preventive action.

Governance performance is monitored rather than assumed.

### 6. Level 5 — Institutionalised

At **Level 5 — Institutionalised**, governance is embedded across the organisation and sustained through changing people, technologies, missions, environments, and threats.

Typical characteristics include:

- governance embedded in organisational practice;
- strong cross-domain integration;
- resilient and repeatable processes;
- evidence-driven decision making;
- mature independent challenge;
- continuous assurance;
- systematic organisational learning;
- strong knowledge continuity;
- proactive adaptation to emerging risks; and
- demonstrated improvement over time.

Institutionalisation means governance does not depend on exceptional individuals or temporary programmes.

### 7. Capability Stage Progression

Progression through maturity levels should represent increasing capability in:

**Awareness → Repeatability → Definition → Management → Institutionalisation**

Progression should be demonstrated through evidence rather than declared solely on the basis of policy publication or organisational intent.

An organisation may have different maturity levels across different domains. Overall maturity shall therefore be presented with sufficient dimensional detail to avoid hiding critical weaknesses behind an aggregate result.

### 8. Context and Consequence Adjustment

Maturity expectations shall be proportionate to the consequences and conditions associated with the AI capability.

Capabilities involving higher levels of autonomy, greater potential for harm, critical missions, adversarial environments, degraded communications, significant external dependencies, or consequential decisions may require stronger maturity in specific domains before proceeding through relevant governance gates.

The same organisation may therefore require different maturity thresholds for different capabilities or missions.

### 9. Maturity Does Not Confer Authority

Maturity level shall remain distinct from:

- legal or policy compliance;
- technical performance;
- TEVV acceptance;
- assurance confidence;
- operational readiness;
- residual-risk acceptance;
- operational authorisation; and
- authority to increase autonomy.

A Level 5 organisation may still be unable to authorise a particular AI capability if the capability-specific evidence, controls, risk treatment, environmental readiness, human authority, or other conditions are inadequate.

### 10. Core Rule and Progression Logic

**Core Rule:**  
> Maturity levels describe the strength, consistency, effectiveness, resilience, and institutionalisation of governance capability. They do not declare an AI capability safe, acceptable, ready, or authorised.

The intended progression is:

**Initial → Developing → Defined → Managed → Institutionalised**

The objective is not to maximise a maturity score. The objective is to establish the governance capability necessary to manage AI proportionately to mission consequence, autonomy, operational environment, and risk.

Maturity improvement shall ultimately strengthen the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Workforce → Maturity → Continuous Improvement**
