# 04-Maturity Scoring and Evidence

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the principles for assigning maturity scores and determining the evidence required to support them within D-AIGAAF.

Scoring is intended to make assessment results comparable and actionable while preserving the limitations and context of the underlying evidence.

A score shall never be interpreted as a direct measure of AI safety, operational acceptability, or authorisation.

## 2. Scoring Model

Each applicable maturity domain shall be assigned a level from **1 to 5**:

| Score | Maturity Level |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Initial |
| 2 | Developing |
| 3 | Defined |
| 4 | Managed |
| 5 | Institutionalised |

The score shall represent the highest level for which the required characteristics are sufficiently demonstrated.

Where evidence is incomplete, conflicting, outdated, or materially dependent on assumptions, the assessor shall avoid assigning a higher level merely because some characteristics of that level are present.

## 3. Evidence Sufficiency

Evidence shall be evaluated for:

- relevance;
- completeness;
- reliability;
- currency;
- traceability;
- independence where appropriate;
- consistency across sources; and
- demonstrated effectiveness.

Evidence should establish both **what is supposed to happen** and **what actually happens**.

A documented process without evidence of implementation should not normally support a high maturity rating.

## 4. Evidence Categories

Evidence may be grouped into five broad categories:

1. **Documentary evidence** — policies, procedures, standards, plans, and governance records.
2. **Implementation evidence** — records demonstrating that processes and controls are actually used.
3. **Performance evidence** — metrics, evaluations, operational results, assurance findings, and observed outcomes.
4. **Independent evidence** — audits, independent assessments, reviews, or external assurance where appropriate.
5. **Learning evidence** — corrective actions, lessons learned, trend analysis, and demonstrated improvement.

Higher maturity should generally require increasing breadth and strength across these evidence categories.

## 5. Evidence-to-Level Logic

The following progression provides a general assessment rule:

| Maturity | Typical Evidence Expectation |
|---|---|
| 1 — Initial | Limited, inconsistent, or predominantly anecdotal evidence. |
| 2 — Developing | Basic documented processes with emerging implementation evidence. |
| 3 — Defined | Formal processes with repeatable implementation and traceable records. |
| 4 — Managed | Consistent implementation supported by performance measurement, review, and corrective action. |
| 5 — Institutionalised | Sustained, integrated, resilient, independently challenged, and continuously improving capability supported by longitudinal evidence. |

These expectations are indicative and shall be adapted to the assessment context.

## 6. Confidence in the Score

Each maturity result should include an assessment of confidence.

Confidence should consider:

- quantity and quality of evidence;
- consistency of evidence;
- evidence coverage;
- evidence currency;
- assessor independence;
- unresolved contradictions;
- material evidence gaps; and
- reliance on unverified assertions.

A high maturity score with low confidence shall not be treated as equivalent to a high maturity score supported by strong evidence.

Confidence and maturity shall therefore be reported separately.

## 7. Domain and Overall Results

D-AIGAAF should prioritise domain-level results over a single aggregate maturity score.

If an overall score is used for management purposes, the underlying domain scores, critical weaknesses, confidence levels, and assessment assumptions shall remain visible.

A critical weakness in a consequential governance domain shall not be hidden by averaging it with strong performance elsewhere.

## 8. Scoring Boundaries and Critical Weaknesses

Certain weaknesses may impose a scoring boundary.

Examples may include:

- absence of accountable governance authority;
- inability to demonstrate meaningful human control where required;
- inadequate evidence for consequential decisions;
- inability to manage material AI security risks;
- absence of required TEVV;
- inability to operate safely within the relevant environment;
- uncontrolled changes to a consequential capability; or
- inability to detect and respond to serious incidents.

Where such conditions materially undermine the assessed capability, the assessor shall document the limitation and may cap the relevant maturity level.

## 9. Evidence Records and Traceability

Every material score shall be traceable to:

**Criterion → Evidence → Assessment Finding → Maturity Level → Confidence → Gap/Action**

Assessment records should identify:

- evidence source;
- evidence date;
- responsible owner;
- applicable criterion;
- assessor judgement;
- limitations or assumptions; and
- disposition of contradictory evidence.

Records shall be retained according to applicable D-AIGAAF and organisational requirements.

## 10. Core Rule and Scoring Logic

**Core Rule:**
> A maturity score is a reasoned assessment of demonstrated governance capability, supported by evidence and accompanied by an explicit confidence judgement.

The scoring logic is:

**Defined Criteria → Credible Evidence → Evidence Evaluation → Assessor Judgement → Maturity Level + Confidence → Findings → Improvement**

The following must remain distinct:

**Maturity ≠ Compliance**  
**Maturity ≠ Assurance**  
**Maturity ≠ Readiness**  
**Maturity ≠ Authorisation**

Maturity scoring shall strengthen, not replace, the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread.
