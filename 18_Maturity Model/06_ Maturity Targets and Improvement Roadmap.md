# 06-Maturity Targets and Improvement Roadmap

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document defines how D-AIGAAF maturity targets shall be established and translated into practical improvement roadmaps.

Maturity targets should identify the governance capability required for a defined organisational, programme, mission, or AI capability context. They shall be proportionate to consequence, risk, autonomy, operational environment, and strategic importance.

The objective is not to achieve the highest possible maturity level in every domain, but to establish sufficient and sustainable capability for the intended context.

## 2. Establishing the Target State

A target maturity state should consider:

- mission requirements;
- consequence and risk;
- autonomy level;
- operational environment;
- applicable legal and policy obligations;
- security requirements;
- workforce capability;
- supply-chain dependencies;
- assurance expectations;
- organisational strategy; and
- expected future changes.

Target maturity should be defined separately for each relevant domain where different levels are justified by context.

## 3. Current-to-Target Analysis

The improvement process should begin by comparing demonstrated maturity with the required target state.

The analysis should identify:

| Element | Question |
|---|---|
| Current State | What capability is demonstrably available? |
| Target State | What capability is required? |
| Gap | What is missing or insufficient? |
| Risk | What consequence could result from the gap? |
| Dependency | What other domains or capabilities are affected? |
| Priority | How urgently should the gap be addressed? |

This analysis should use the evidence and findings produced through the maturity assessment.

## 4. Improvement Prioritisation

Improvement actions should be prioritised according to:

**Consequence → Risk → Governance Criticality → Dependency → Effort → Time → Strategic Value**

Priority should generally be given to weaknesses that could:

- undermine human authority;
- compromise security;
- invalidate assurance;
- prevent appropriate operational authorisation;
- create uncontrolled autonomy;
- impair incident response;
- affect consequential decision making; or
- create significant dependency or continuity risk.

## 5. Improvement Roadmap

A maturity improvement roadmap should normally define:

1. current maturity;
2. target maturity;
3. material gaps;
4. required actions;
5. responsible owners;
6. dependencies;
7. resources;
8. milestones;
9. evidence of completion; and
10. verification and reassessment arrangements.

Roadmaps may be organised into immediate, near-term, and longer-term actions where appropriate.

## 6. Capability-Building Actions

Improvement actions may include:

- establishing or revising governance structures;
- clarifying decision rights;
- developing risk and autonomy processes;
- strengthening lifecycle controls;
- improving data governance;
- implementing security controls;
- strengthening TEVV;
- improving operational controls;
- increasing workforce competence;
- improving audit and evidence management;
- reducing critical dependencies;
- strengthening incident and fail-safe capability; or
- improving continuous assurance.

Actions should address root causes rather than merely improve assessment scores.

## 7. Maturity Gates and Dependencies

Where maturity is required before progressing through a D-AIGAAF lifecycle or governance gate, the relevant target state shall be explicitly defined.

Examples may include requirements for:

- development or acquisition;
- deployment;
- operational readiness;
- operational authorisation;
- autonomy expansion;
- major change;
- reauthorisation; or
- continued employment.

A maturity target shall not override independent requirements for TEVV, assurance, risk acceptance, or operational authority.

## 8. Roadmap Governance and Tracking

Improvement roadmaps shall be governed as controlled plans.

Progress should be monitored using:

- milestone completion;
- evidence delivery;
- residual gaps;
- risk reduction;
- control effectiveness;
- dependency status;
- resource availability; and
- reassessment results.

Significant delays, failed actions, emerging risks, or changed assumptions should trigger governance review.

## 9. Verification and Reassessment

Completion of an improvement action shall not automatically mean that the maturity gap is closed.

Closure should require evidence that:

**Action Implemented → Capability Demonstrated → Effectiveness Verified → Gap Reassessed**

Where an improvement changes a consequential AI capability, its governance status may also require review under D-AIGAAF change, assurance, revalidation, or reauthorisation processes.

## 10. Core Rule and Improvement Logic

**Core Rule:**
> Maturity targets shall be driven by the governance capability required for the mission and risk context, not by an organisational desire to maximise maturity scores.

The improvement logic is:

**Current State → Required State → Gap → Risk → Priority → Action → Implementation → Evidence → Verification → Reassessment**

Maturity improvement should strengthen the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread and progressively increase the organisation's ability to govern AI with appropriate authority, assurance, resilience, and accountability.
