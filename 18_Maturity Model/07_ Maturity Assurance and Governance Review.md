# 07-Maturity Assurance and Governance Review

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document defines how D-AIGAAF maturity assessments and maturity improvement programmes shall be subject to assurance and governance review.

The purpose is to ensure that maturity results remain credible, evidence-based, appropriately challenged, and useful for governance decisions.

Maturity assurance does not replace technical assurance, TEVV, audit, operational authorisation, or risk acceptance.

## 2. Assurance Objectives

Maturity assurance should determine whether:

- the assessment scope was appropriate;
- the methodology was correctly applied;
- evidence was sufficient and credible;
- assessor judgements were reasonable;
- material weaknesses were identified;
- scoring boundaries were applied appropriately;
- confidence levels reflect evidence quality;
- improvement actions address significant gaps; and
- governance decisions based on maturity results remain justified.

## 3. Assessment Quality Review

Assessment results should undergo an appropriate quality review before formal governance use.

The review should consider:

1. scope and context;
2. assessment criteria;
3. evidence coverage;
4. assessor competence;
5. scoring consistency;
6. treatment of contradictory evidence;
7. confidence assessment;
8. critical gaps;
9. cross-domain dependencies; and
10. proposed conclusions and recommendations.

The depth of review should be proportionate to the consequence and intended use of the assessment.

## 4. Independent Challenge

Where maturity results may influence consequential governance decisions, an appropriate level of independent challenge should be provided.

Independent challenge may examine:

- unsupported claims;
- optimistic scoring;
- missing evidence;
- conflicts of interest;
- inappropriate aggregation;
- overlooked dependencies;
- unrecognised operational constraints; and
- assumptions that materially affect the result.

Challenge should be documented and disagreements should be resolved through defined governance processes.

## 5. Assurance of Evidence

Evidence supporting maturity claims should be assessed for:

- authenticity;
- integrity;
- relevance;
- completeness;
- currency;
- traceability;
- independence where applicable; and
- consistency.

Evidence that has become outdated or invalid due to material changes shall not continue to support a maturity claim without appropriate review.

## 6. Monitoring Maturity Over Time

Maturity shall not be treated as permanently established.

Ongoing monitoring should identify changes that may affect maturity, including:

- organisational restructuring;
- workforce turnover;
- technology changes;
- model or data changes;
- new threats;
- changes in operational environments;
- incidents;
- audit findings;
- supplier or dependency changes;
- degraded capability; and
- changes in applicable policy or legal requirements.

Significant deterioration should trigger appropriate governance review or reassessment.

## 7. Governance Review of Results

Governance bodies should review material maturity results in conjunction with:

- risk information;
- assurance confidence;
- TEVV findings;
- audit results;
- operational performance;
- incident trends;
- workforce readiness;
- supply-chain conditions; and
- relevant changes.

Maturity results should inform governance decisions without becoming a substitute for the specific evidence or authority required for those decisions.

## 8. Reassessment Triggers

A reassessment should be considered following:

- major organisational change;
- material AI system or model change;
- significant data or dependency change;
- serious incident;
- major audit finding;
- significant deterioration in performance or assurance;
- change in operational environment;
- change in autonomy or authority;
- material workforce capability loss; or
- expiry of an established assessment period.

Periodic reassessment should also be used where the capability is consequential or rapidly changing.

## 9. Records, Reporting and Corrective Action

Maturity assurance records should include:

- assessment scope;
- methodology and criteria;
- evidence reviewed;
- maturity results;
- confidence levels;
- challenge and review outcomes;
- material findings;
- corrective actions;
- governance decisions; and
- reassessment requirements.

Material weaknesses shall have accountable owners and tracked corrective actions.

## 10. Core Rule and Governance Review Logic

**Core Rule:**
> Maturity results shall remain subject to evidence, independent challenge, monitoring, and governance review; maturity shall never be treated as a permanent property of an AI capability or organisation.

The assurance logic is:

**Assess → Evidence → Score → Challenge → Assure → Govern → Improve → Monitor → Reassess**

This maintains the distinction between:

**Maturity → Capability**  
**Assurance → Confidence**  
**Authorisation → Authority**  
**Audit → Independent Examination**  
**TEVV → Technical and Operational Evidence**

Together, these mechanisms reinforce the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread.
