# 09-Maturity Implementation and Continuous Improvement

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This document defines how D-AIGAAF maturity assessment results shall be translated into sustained implementation, governance improvement, and organisational learning.

Maturity improvement shall focus on strengthening real governance capability rather than optimising assessment scores.

## 2. Implementation Governance

Maturity improvement should have:

- accountable leadership;
- defined ownership for each improvement action;
- approved priorities;
- allocated resources;
- measurable objectives;
- implementation milestones;
- evidence requirements; and
- governance oversight.

Responsibilities shall be aligned with existing D-AIGAAF decision rights and governance structures.

## 3. Improvement Action Planning

Each material maturity gap should be converted into an actionable improvement plan.

An action should identify:

1. the problem or gap;
2. required capability;
3. desired outcome;
4. responsible owner;
5. dependencies;
6. resources;
7. target date;
8. evidence of implementation;
9. verification method; and
10. reassessment requirement.

Actions should address root causes where practicable.

## 4. Implementation Priorities

Implementation priorities should reflect:

**Risk → Consequence → Governance Criticality → Dependency → Urgency → Feasibility → Strategic Value**

Priority should normally be given to weaknesses affecting:

- human authority;
- consequential decision making;
- autonomy control;
- security;
- TEVV and assurance;
- operational environment;
- incident and fail-safe capability;
- change control;
- workforce competence and capacity; or
- critical dependencies.

## 5. Integration Across D-AIGAAF

Maturity improvement shall not operate as an isolated governance activity.

Improvement actions should be integrated with:

- risk treatment;
- lifecycle management;
- TEVV;
- operational authorisation;
- continuous assurance;
- incident management;
- change and reauthorisation;
- audit;
- workforce development;
- supply-chain governance; and
- technical control implementation.

This ensures that maturity improvement produces practical governance outcomes.

## 6. Measuring Improvement

Progress should be measured using more than changes in maturity scores.

Useful indicators may include:

- closure of critical gaps;
- reduction in residual risk;
- improved control effectiveness;
- increased evidence quality;
- improved assurance confidence;
- successful completion of TEVV activities;
- improved workforce competence;
- reduced dependency risk;
- improved incident response;
- successful governance exercises; and
- demonstrated improvement in operational resilience.

Maturity scores may be used as one indicator within a broader performance picture.

## 7. Continuous Learning

Organisations shall use lessons from:

- assessments;
- audits;
- incidents;
- exercises;
- operational experience;
- TEVV;
- assurance activities;
- workforce feedback;
- supplier performance;
- technology changes; and
- emerging threats.

Learning should be converted into controlled changes to policies, processes, controls, training, architecture, assessment criteria, or governance arrangements where appropriate.

## 8. Reassessment and Adaptation

Maturity shall be reassessed periodically and when significant conditions change.

Triggers may include:

- major AI capability changes;
- changes in autonomy;
- new missions;
- significant operational changes;
- serious incidents;
- material security threats;
- organisational restructuring;
- workforce changes;
- major supplier changes;
- new legal or policy requirements; or
- evidence of governance deterioration.

Reassessment should determine whether previous maturity conclusions remain valid.

## 9. Governance Reporting and Review

Governance reporting should present:

- current maturity;
- target maturity;
- significant changes;
- critical gaps;
- improvement progress;
- residual risks;
- evidence confidence;
- overdue actions;
- emerging issues; and
- decisions required.

Senior governance bodies should review whether improvement activity is producing the intended capability and whether priorities remain appropriate.

## 10. Core Rule and Continuous Improvement Logic

**Core Rule:**
> D-AIGAAF maturity improvement is successful only when governance capability becomes demonstrably more effective, resilient, repeatable, and sustainable—not merely when the maturity score increases.

The continuous improvement cycle is:

**Assess → Identify Gaps → Prioritise → Plan → Implement → Evidence → Verify → Reassess → Learn → Improve**

This completes the Module 18 maturity cycle and reinforces the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change/Incident → Learning → Revalidation/Reauthorisation → Workforce → Maturity → Continuous Improvement**
