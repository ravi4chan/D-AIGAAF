# 09-Crosswalk to Indian AI Policy and Standards

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the relationship between D-AIGAAF — Defence AI Governance, Assurance & Operational Authorisation Framework and the broader **Indian AI policy, standards and governance ecosystem**.

The purpose is to prevent D-AIGAAF from being aligned only to international frameworks while overlooking relevant Indian policy, institutional, regulatory and standards developments.

This document is intentionally broader than:

- Serial 06 — IndiaAI Responsible AI Governance Framework;
- Serial 07 — IndiaAI Governance Guidelines; and
- Serial 08 — India DPDP Framework.

It provides a consolidated view of the wider Indian AI ecosystem and identifies where D-AIGAAF should maintain alignment.

## 2. Scope

The Indian AI ecosystem is not represented by a single comprehensive AI law or one universally applicable AI standard.

Relevant layers include:

- IndiaAI Mission;
- MeitY AI and Emerging Technologies policy work;
- IndiaAI reports and expert-group outputs;
- Responsible AI and Safe & Trusted AI initiatives;
- IndiaAI standards and global-standards work;
- Indian AI governance guidelines;
- Digital Personal Data Protection framework;
- Information Technology Rules and subsequent amendments;
- Indian cybersecurity and information-security requirements;
- relevant Bureau of Indian Standards activity;
- sector-specific regulation;
- Government procurement and technology policies;
- defence-specific AI initiatives;
- international standards adopted or referenced by Indian organisations.

MeitY's AI & Emerging Technologies Division is responsible for policy and strategy work in emerging technologies including AI and supports initiatives such as IndiaAI and related national programmes. citeturn0search3

## 3. Indian AI Policy Landscape

India's approach has developed as a **multi-layer governance ecosystem** rather than a single AI statute.

This has important implications for D-AIGAAF.

The framework should not assume that a single Indian instrument answers all AI-governance questions.

Instead, organisations should establish an applicable-stack assessment:

**AI Use Case**
→ **Indian Legal Requirements**
→ **Government AI Policy**
→ **Applicable Standards**
→ **Sector Requirements**
→ **Security Requirements**
→ **Contractual Requirements**
→ **Defence Requirements**
→ **D-AIGAAF Controls**

This approach allows D-AIGAAF to remain compatible with changes in Indian policy.

## 5. IndiaAI Mission

The IndiaAI Mission is a major pillar of India's national AI ecosystem.

Government material describes the Mission as supporting areas including AI innovation, computing access, data quality, indigenous AI capabilities, talent, industry collaboration and broader ecosystem development. citeturn0search15

D-AIGAAF is not a substitute for the IndiaAI Mission.

Instead, D-AIGAAF can serve as a governance architecture for organisations developing or deploying consequential AI capabilities within an Indian context.

Relevant D-AIGAAF areas include:

- AI strategy;
- AI portfolio governance;
- lifecycle governance;
- workforce competence;
- data governance;
- supply-chain governance;
- assurance;
- security;
- responsible deployment.

## 6. IndiaAI Reports and Expert-Group Work

IndiaAI has published reports and expert-group material covering AI policy, responsible AI, standards and ecosystem development.

These materials should be treated as policy references with differing levels of authority.

D-AIGAAF should classify each source according to its status:

| Source Type | D-AIGAAF Treatment |
|---|---|
| Statute | Mandatory legal requirement where applicable |
| Regulation / notified rule | Mandatory where applicable |
| Government directive | Mandatory where applicable to the organisation |
| Official guideline | Policy requirement or recommended control depending on status |
| Standard | Applicable when adopted, required or contractually incorporated |
| Expert-group report | Policy/reference input |
| Consultation document | Emerging policy signal |
| Industry guidance | Supporting reference |
| Academic work | Research/reference |

This prevents an important governance error:

> **Treating every Indian AI publication as if it had the same legal or normative authority.**

## 7. IndiaAI Standards and Global Standards Work

IndiaAI has published work examining global AI standards and the evolution of AI policy and standards.

Its standards-related material recognises that AI governance spans areas including:

- ethics;
- human rights;
- fairness;
- transparency;
- explainability;
- robustness;
- safety;
- security;
- accountability;
- interoperability;
- privacy.

The IndiaAI Global Standards on AI report also reviews policy and legal developments across jurisdictions and discusses areas including network security, interoperability, autonomous vehicles and autonomous weapons. citeturn0search24

D-AIGAAF should use such work primarily as an **alignment and standards-discovery layer**, then map applicable formal standards into the relevant D-AIGAAF modules.

## 8. Indian Standards and International Standards

D-AIGAAF should recognise that Indian organisations may use:

- Indian Standards;
- ISO/IEC standards adopted in India;
- IEC standards;
- international standards referenced through procurement;
- sector-specific standards;
- organisation-specific technical standards.

The existence of an Indian adoption or reference does not automatically mean that the entire standard applies to every AI system.

Applicability should be determined by:

- system type;
- use case;
- contractual requirements;
- legal requirements;
- sector;
- organisational policy;
- risk;
- mission;
- procurement conditions.

D-AIGAAF therefore uses standards as **control sources**, not as automatic authorisation mechanisms.

## 10. Indian Cybersecurity and AI Security

AI systems should be governed within India's broader cybersecurity environment.

D-AIGAAF therefore connects Indian cybersecurity expectations with:

- Module 06 — AI Security;
- Module 14 — Incident & Fail-Safe;
- Module 16 — Audit & Evidence;
- Module 21 — Legal & Policy;
- Module 24 — Architecture & Technical Controls.

AI-specific security should additionally consider:

- adversarial machine learning;
- data poisoning;
- model manipulation;
- model extraction;
- prompt/instruction attacks;
- insecure AI agents;
- tool misuse;
- model supply-chain compromise;
- AI-enabled cyber threats;
- privacy leakage;

Conventional cybersecurity controls remain necessary but are not sufficient for AI security.

## 11. Information Technology Rules

India's Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 have been amended over time.

MeitY's current published material records amendments through February 2026, including provisions concerning **synthetically generated information (SGI)**. citeturn0search2

These rules are not a general AI governance framework.

However, they can become relevant to AI systems involved in areas such as:

- online information;
- intermediary services;
- synthetic media;
- content provenance or labelling;
- platform governance;
- information integrity.

D-AIGAAF therefore places applicable IT Rules requirements within the legal/policy and AI-security layers rather than treating them as a universal AI compliance framework.

## 14. Legal and Policy Hierarchy

A D-AIGAAF implementation should classify applicable Indian instruments according to their authority.

A practical hierarchy is:

### Level 1 — Law

Acts and binding legal instruments.

### Level 2 — Regulation / Notified Rules

Binding subordinate legislation and applicable regulatory requirements.

### Level 3 — Government Directions

Applicable executive or institutional directions.

### Level 4 — Standards

Mandatory where incorporated by law, contract, policy or organisational requirement.

### Level 5 — Official Guidelines

Authoritative policy guidance where applicable.

### Level 6 — Frameworks and Principles

Responsible-AI and governance reference frameworks.

### Level 7 — Research and Industry Guidance

Supporting evidence and good-practice references.

This hierarchy should be recorded in the D-AIGAAF legal and policy register.

## 15. Policy-to-Control Mapping

For each applicable Indian instrument, D-AIGAAF recommends:

**Identify**
→ What is the instrument?

**Classify**
→ What is its legal or normative status?

**Determine Applicability**
→ Does it apply to this organisation, system and use case?

**Interpret**
→ What obligation or expectation is relevant?

**Translate**
→ What D-AIGAAF requirement addresses it?

**Control**
→ What process or technical control implements it?

**Evidence**
→ What proves implementation?

**Assure**
→ Has the control been independently evaluated?

**Review**
→ Does the requirement remain current?

This becomes the foundation for Serial 13's integrated crosswalk methodology.

## 19. Indian Policy Evolution

India's AI policy environment is continuing to evolve.

For example, MeitY's current orders and notices list the **AI Governance and Economic Group (AIGEG)** constituted in April 2026. citeturn0search0

This demonstrates why D-AIGAAF should not hard-code one snapshot of Indian AI policy.

Instead, it should maintain:

- a policy register;
- source status;
- effective date;
- applicability;
- affected controls;
- responsible owner;
- review date;
- change history.

Policy changes should enter D-AIGAAF through change governance rather than requiring uncontrolled modification of the core framework.

## 23. Limitations

This crosswalk does not claim:

- that India has a single comprehensive AI law;
- that every Indian AI policy document is legally binding;
- that every Indian standard applies to every AI system;
- that IndiaAI guidance automatically establishes compliance;
- that DPDP applies to every defence AI system;
- that IT Rules constitute general AI regulation;
- that policy alignment establishes operational authorisation;
- that standards compliance eliminates AI risk.

Formal legal applicability should be determined by competent legal and policy authorities.

## 25. Final Crosswalk Position

India's AI governance environment is best understood as a **stack of laws, regulations, government policy, responsible-AI frameworks, standards, sector requirements and institutional initiatives** rather than a single AI rulebook.

D-AIGAAF therefore adopts a layered alignment model:

**Indian Law**
→ **Regulation**
→ **Government Policy**
→ **AI Governance Guidance**
→ **Standards**
→ **Sector / Defence Requirements**
→ **D-AIGAAF Controls**
→ **Evidence**
→ **Assurance**
→ **Operational Authority**

The central principle is:

> **D-AIGAAF should remain aligned with India's evolving AI policy and standards ecosystem without confusing policy guidance, standards and legal obligations with one another.**

This creates a framework that can absorb future Indian AI laws, standards, guidelines and defence policies without requiring the core governance architecture to be rebuilt.
