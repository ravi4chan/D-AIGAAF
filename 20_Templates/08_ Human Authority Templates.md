# 08-Human Authority Templates

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the template set for **D-AIGAAF Module 08 — Human Authority**.

These templates establish practical mechanisms for ensuring that human authority over consequential AI remains:

- explicit;
- competent;
- informed;
- proportionate to consequence;
- operationally meaningful;
- traceable;
- reviewable;
- capable of intervention;
- supported by appropriate organisational authority.

The central principle is:

> **AI capability does not create human authority. Human authority must be explicitly assigned, bounded, competent, operationally exercisable and traceable.**

Human involvement alone is insufficient. A person who is nominally present but lacks information, time, competence, authority or ability to intervene does not necessarily provide meaningful human control.

# 3. Template 08-001 — Human Authority Governance Record

## Purpose

Defines the governance structure through which human authority over an AI capability is established and maintained.

## Required Fields

- Capability ID
- Mission ID
- Use Case ID
- Human authority owner
- Operational commander/authority
- AI decision authority
- Technical authority
- Risk owner
- Assurance authority
- Intervention authority
- Escalation authority
- Review authority
- Delegation arrangements
- Review date

## Governance Questions

- Who has authority over the AI?
- Who authorises its use?
- Who can restrict its use?
- Who can intervene?
- Who can override or terminate AI activity?
- Who accepts residual risk?
- Who reviews consequential AI decisions?
- What authority exists during degraded communications?

# 6. Template 08-004 — Human Control Assessment

## Purpose

Determines whether meaningful human control exists for a particular AI capability and mission.

## Assessment Areas

Assess whether the human:

- understands the AI's role;
- receives relevant information;
- receives meaningful uncertainty information;
- has sufficient time to act;
- has authority to intervene;
- can actually intervene;
- can reject AI recommendations;
- can terminate AI activity;
- understands consequences;
- remains accountable for authorised decisions.

## Required Fields

- Control objective
- Human role
- Required control
- Actual capability
- Evidence
- Gap
- Risk
- Remediation
- Assurance status

### Core Test

> **Can the designated human meaningfully understand, decide, intervene and remain accountable at the point where consequential action occurs?**

# 19. Meaningful Human Control Test

D-AIGAAF recommends evaluating meaningful human control through six questions:

### 1. Authority

Does the human have legitimate authority to make or prevent the decision?

### 2. Competence

Does the human understand the mission, AI capability and relevant risks?

### 3. Information

Does the human receive sufficient and timely information, including uncertainty?

### 4. Time

Does the human have enough time to assess and act?

### 5. Intervention

Can the human actually intervene or terminate the relevant action?

### 6. Accountability

Can the decision and responsible authority be reconstructed afterwards?

Failure of one or more elements may materially weaken meaningful human control.

---

# 20. Human Authority by Autonomy Level

Human authority should scale with the D-AIGAAF autonomy model.

| Level | AI Role | Human Authority Expectation |
|---|---|---|
| A0 | No meaningful AI decision | Normal system governance |
| A1 | Information / observation | Human interprets information |
| A2 | Analysis / recommendation | Human evaluates and decides |
| A3 | Human-authorised action | Explicit human authorisation |
| A4 | Supervised autonomous action | Defined supervision and intervention |
| A5 | Independent consequential autonomy | Exceptional governance, explicit authorisation and stringent assurance |

Higher autonomy does not remove human governance requirements.

---

# 21. Human Authority in Degraded Conditions

Human authority must remain meaningful under realistic operational degradation.

Assess:

- loss of communications;
- degraded sensors;
- incomplete information;
- cyber attack;
- electromagnetic disruption;
- high workload;
- personnel casualties;
- infrastructure failure;
- GPS/GNSS degradation;
- disconnected operation.

The framework should define what happens when the designated human cannot exercise normal control.

Possible responses include:

- reduced autonomy;
- restricted functionality;
- transfer of authority;
- safe state;
- mission termination;
- controlled continuation under pre-authorised conditions.

---

# 22. Human Authority and Uncertainty

AI systems should communicate uncertainty in a manner that allows the human decision-maker to act appropriately.

The record should distinguish:

- confidence;
- uncertainty;
- missing information;
- data freshness;
- known limitations;
- ambiguity;
- conflicting evidence.

The governing principle is:

> **A human cannot exercise meaningful control over an AI system if the AI obscures information necessary to understand the reliability or uncertainty of its output.**

---

# 23. Human Authority and Automation Bias

Human oversight can fail when operators systematically defer to AI recommendations.

Potential indicators include:

- excessive acceptance of recommendations;
- reduced independent assessment;
- failure to challenge anomalous outputs;
- confirmation behaviour;
- inappropriate trust;
- inability to explain decisions independently of AI output.

TEVV should assess these behaviours where consequential AI decisions are involved.

---

# 24. Human Authority and AI-to-AI Interaction

Where multiple AI systems interact, human authority must remain explicit.

AI systems should not acquire authority through:

- interaction with another AI;
- automated task delegation;
- API chaining;
- agent orchestration;
- tool invocation;
- model-generated instructions;
- emergent system behaviour.

The governing rule is:

> **No AI-to-AI interaction can create operational authority that has not been explicitly granted by an authorised human governance mechanism.**

---

# 25. Human Authority and Consequence

Human control requirements should increase with consequence.

A useful governance relationship is:

**Higher Consequence**
+ **Higher Autonomy**
+ **Higher Environmental Uncertainty**
+ **Higher Adversarial Exposure**
+ **Lower Reversibility**
→ **Stronger Human Authority Requirements**

This should influence:

- approval;
- supervision;
- intervention;
- competence;
- staffing;
- evidence;
- TEVV;
- authorisation.

# 27. Anti-Pattern — Human in the Loop = Human Control

D-AIGAAF rejects the simplistic equation:

**Human Present = Human Control**

Instead:

**Human Authority**
+ **Competence**
+ **Information**
+ **Time**
+ **Ability to Intervene**
+ **Accountability**
= **Potentially Meaningful Human Control**

This must be demonstrated for the relevant mission and operating environment.

---

# 28. Anti-Pattern — Rubber-Stamp Approval

A human approval mechanism is inadequate where:

- the decision is effectively predetermined;
- the human lacks relevant information;
- the human lacks time;
- the AI recommendation cannot realistically be challenged;
- intervention is technically ineffective;
- the operator fears overriding the system;
- workload prevents meaningful review.

A formal approval click is not equivalent to substantive human authority.

---

# 29. Anti-Pattern — Accountability Without Authority

D-AIGAAF rejects arrangements in which personnel are held responsible for AI-supported actions but lack:

- authority;
- information;
- competence;
- intervention capability;
- resources;
- time.

Accountability should be matched by the authority and capability required to exercise that accountability.

---

# 30. Final Human Authority Principle

D-AIGAAF treats human authority as an operational control, not merely an ethical principle.

The complete human-authority chain is:

**Authority**
→ **Role**
→ **Competence**
→ **Information**
→ **Situational Awareness**
→ **Decision**
→ **Intervention**
→ **Action**
→ **Accountability**
→ **Review**
→ **Learning**

The governing principle is:

> **Where AI can contribute to consequential decisions or actions, the responsible human authority must be explicit, competent, informed, operationally capable of exercising control, and accountable for the decisions within the authority assigned to them.**

Human authority must remain valid not only in normal conditions, but also under degraded, adversarial, disconnected and rapidly changing operational conditions.
