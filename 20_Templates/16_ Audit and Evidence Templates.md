# 16-Audit and Evidence Templates

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the template set for **D-AIGAAF Module 16 — Audit & Evidence**.

Audit and evidence provide the mechanism by which an organisation demonstrates that its AI governance controls are implemented, operating, effective and traceable.

D-AIGAAF treats evidence as a substantive governance asset rather than a documentation exercise.

The core audit lifecycle is:

**Plan → Define Criteria → Collect Evidence → Test → Find → Challenge → Correct → Verify → Report → Learn**

The central principle is:

> **A governance claim should be supported by evidence that is relevant, reliable, sufficiently current, traceable and proportionate to the consequence of the AI capability.**

# 7. Evidence Quality Assessment

Each significant evidence item should be assessed for:

### Relevance

Does it actually address the criterion?

### Reliability

Can the evidence be trusted?

### Integrity

Has it remained unaltered or appropriately controlled?

### Currency

Does it represent the current state?

### Traceability

Can it be linked to the relevant capability, configuration and decision?

### Independence

Where required, was it produced or reviewed independently?

### Sufficiency

Is the evidence adequate to support the conclusion?

# 14. Audit Independence

Audit independence should be proportionate to risk and consequence.

For higher-consequence capabilities, consider separation between:

- capability development;
- operational employment;
- control ownership;
- audit;
- assurance;
- authorisation.

A person should not be the sole judge of a control where they have a material conflict of interest.

Independence does not necessarily require organisational separation in every case; the required degree should reflect consequence, risk and the nature of the decision.

---

# 15. Evidence and Configuration

Evidence is meaningful only in relation to the configuration and context in which it was produced.

Where material, record:

- model;
- software;
- hardware;
- data;
- instructions;
- tools;
- security controls;
- environment;
- operator configuration.

A test result from a materially different configuration should not automatically be treated as evidence for the current configuration.

---

# 16. Evidence and Time

Evidence has a validity horizon.

Evidence may become stale because of:

- model changes;
- software changes;
- data drift;
- threat changes;
- environmental changes;
- mission changes;
- supplier changes;
- policy changes;
- changes in human roles.

Therefore:

> **Evidence should be evaluated for currency, not merely existence.**

---

# 17. Evidence and Uncertainty

Audit conclusions should explicitly communicate uncertainty.

Use distinctions such as:

- confirmed;
- strongly supported;
- reasonably supported;
- indicative;
- disputed;
- unknown.

Do not convert insufficient evidence into false certainty.

---

# 18. Audit Sampling

Sampling may be appropriate for large AI portfolios.

Sampling criteria can include:

- consequence;
- autonomy;
- mission criticality;
- deployment scale;
- change frequency;
- incident history;
- supplier dependency;
- prior findings;
- evidence quality.

High-consequence or high-autonomy systems may require more intensive or complete assessment.

---

# 19. Audit of AI-Specific Controls

Audits should be capable of assessing controls beyond conventional IT governance.

Examples include:

- uncertainty communication;
- model behaviour controls;
- autonomy boundaries;
- human intervention;
- AI-to-AI interactions;
- prompt/instruction controls;
- tool permissions;
- model integrity;
- data poisoning controls;
- adversarial resilience;
- fail-safe mechanisms;
- operational authorisation;
- environment restrictions.

---

# 20. Audit of Human Authority

Auditors should test whether human authority is real rather than nominal.

Questions include:

- Does the person have actual authority?
- Do they have sufficient information?
- Do they understand the AI's limitations?
- Do they have enough time to intervene?
- Can they override the system?
- Is intervention technically effective?
- Can they recognise uncertainty?
- Are workload and staffing realistic?
- Is accountability traceable?

Presence of a human does not by itself demonstrate meaningful human control.

---

# 21. Audit of Operational Authorisation

Audit should verify that operational authority is:

- explicit;
- current;
- bounded;
- traceable;
- associated with the correct capability;
- associated with the correct mission;
- associated with the correct environment;
- associated with the correct autonomy;
- associated with the correct human authority.

The core authorisation object remains:

**AI Capability × Mission × Environment × Autonomy × Human Authority**

---

# 22. Audit and Incidents

Significant incidents should trigger examination of:

- existing controls;
- evidence;
- assumptions;
- TEVV;
- configuration;
- authorisation;
- monitoring;
- human control.

An incident may reveal that:

**Control existed → but was ineffective**

or:

**Evidence existed → but was insufficient**

or:

**Authorisation existed → but conditions no longer held**

These distinctions matter for corrective action.

---

# 23. Audit and Change

Audit should examine whether the change-management process correctly identified:

- material changes;
- configuration changes;
- environment changes;
- autonomy changes;
- supplier changes;
- data changes.

A recurring audit finding that material changes are not being detected should be treated as a systemic governance weakness.

---

# 24. Audit and Evidence Hierarchy

Evidence should generally be evaluated according to its directness and reliability.

A useful hierarchy is:

**Direct operational evidence**
→ **Controlled test evidence**
→ **Independent evaluation**
→ **Validated records**
→ **Documented assertions**
→ **Unverified claims**

This hierarchy is contextual rather than absolute.

A lower-level source may be highly relevant to one question, while a formal report may be insufficient for another.

---

# 25. Anti-Pattern — Documentation Equals Evidence

A document stating that a control exists does not prove that the control:

- was implemented;
- operated;
- was effective;
- remained current.

Audit should distinguish:

**Policy**
→ **Implementation**
→ **Operation**
→ **Effectiveness**
→ **Evidence**

---

# 26. Anti-Pattern — More Evidence Equals More Assurance

Large volumes of poorly relevant evidence do not automatically increase confidence.

Evidence should be:

- relevant;
- reliable;
- current;
- traceable;
- sufficiently independent;
- proportionate.

The objective is **decision-useful evidence**, not maximum document volume.

---

# 27. Anti-Pattern — Audit as Compliance Theatre

Audit should not become a process of collecting documents merely to demonstrate activity.

A useful audit asks:

> **What does the evidence allow us to know about the actual governance and operational state of the AI capability?**

Audit findings should affect:

- risk;
- controls;
- assurance;
- authorisation;
- operational restrictions;
- improvement.

---

# 28. Evidence Failure

Where evidence is missing, contradictory or unreliable:

1. identify the uncertainty;
2. determine its consequence;
3. assess whether the assurance conclusion remains justified;
4. apply compensating controls where appropriate;
5. escalate where material;
6. obtain additional evidence;
7. reassess assurance.

Evidence weakness should not be silently converted into an assumption of compliance.

---

# 29. Audit Closure

An audit should not be considered closed merely because a report was issued.

Closure requires, where applicable:

**Finding → Corrective Action → Evidence → Verification → Acceptance → Learning**

Material unresolved findings should remain visible to the appropriate governance authority.

---

# 30. Golden Thread Integration

Audit & Evidence connects the D-AIGAAF Golden Thread to demonstrable evidence:

**Mission Need**
→ **Risk**
→ **Requirements**
→ **Controls**
→ **Testing**
→ **Evidence**
→ **Assurance**
→ **Authority**
→ **Conditions**
→ **Boundaries**
→ **Employment**
→ **Monitoring**
→ **Change / Incident**
→ **Learning**
→ **Revalidation / Reauthorisation**
→ **Audit**
→ **Corrective Action**
→ **Continuous Improvement**

Audit is therefore not a separate compliance layer. It is a mechanism for testing whether the Golden Thread remains intact.

---

# 31. Final Audit & Evidence Principle

D-AIGAAF treats audit and evidence as mechanisms for justified confidence and accountable decision-making.

The governing principle is:

> **No consequential AI governance, assurance or authorisation decision should rely solely on assertion where relevant evidence can reasonably be obtained; and where evidence is incomplete, uncertain or contradictory, that uncertainty should be explicitly recognised and reflected in the resulting risk, assurance and authority decisions.**
