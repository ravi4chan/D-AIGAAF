# 24-Retirement and Decommissioning Templates

## Purpose

This document provides reusable templates for governing the retirement, withdrawal, decommissioning, archival, disposal, and formal closure of AI capabilities throughout the D-AIGAAF lifecycle.

Retirement is not simply deletion. A capability may leave operational service while models, data, records, infrastructure, credentials, interfaces, supplier dependencies, backups, or derived artefacts continue to exist. D-AIGAAF therefore treats retirement and decommissioning as governed lifecycle activities subject to authority, evidence preservation, residual-risk management, verification, and closure.

## Core Principle

**Retirement is a governed lifecycle phase, not an administrative deletion event.**

A capability should not be considered fully decommissioned merely because it is no longer being actively used. Decommissioning should address the capability's operational authority, technical components, data, models, configurations, access, dependencies, infrastructure, records, and residual risks.

## Retirement and Decommissioning Distinction

The following states should be distinguished:

- **Suspension** — temporary restriction of operational use while the capability remains retained.
- **Withdrawal** — removal of the capability from a particular mission, environment, or authorised scope.
- **Retirement** — formal decision that the capability will no longer be used for its authorised operational purpose.
- **Decommissioning** — controlled removal or disabling of the capability and its operational dependencies.
- **Archival** — deliberate retention of defined components or records for evidence, legal, historical, research, security, or assurance purposes.
- **Disposal** — final destruction, sanitisation, transfer, or other authorised disposition of assets or information.

## Retirement Triggers

Retirement or decommissioning may be triggered by:

- Mission completion or mission cancellation
- Replacement by another capability
- Obsolescence
- Unacceptable residual risk
- Inability to maintain adequate assurance
- Repeated safety or security failures
- Compromise or loss of system integrity
- Supplier failure or loss of critical support
- Unsustainable strategic dependency
- Material environmental change
- Regulatory or policy change
- Loss of required data or infrastructure
- Inability to maintain human authority or competence
- Excessive operational burden
- End of contractual or support arrangements
- Strategic decision by the competent authority

## Retirement Governance Chain

**Trigger → Assessment → Decision → Withdrawal → Decommissioning Plan → Authority Revocation → Technical Decommissioning → Data / Model / Infrastructure Disposition → Evidence Preservation → Verification → Residual Risk Review → Closure → Learning**

## Golden Thread Extension

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change / Incident → Learning → Revalidation / Reauthorisation → Retirement → Decommissioning → Evidence Preservation → Residual Risk → Closure → Learning**

# Retirement Decision Rules

The following rules should apply across D-AIGAAF:

1. **Retirement requires explicit governance authority.**
2. **Suspension, withdrawal, retirement and decommissioning are distinct states.**
3. **Operational authority must be explicitly terminated, not assumed to disappear.**
4. **Emergency withdrawal should be possible when continued operation presents unacceptable risk.**
5. **Evidence should be preserved before destructive disposition.**
6. **Data disposal must account for backups, replicas, caches and derived data.**
7. **Model disposal must account for exported weights and retained copies.**
8. **Credentials, tokens, keys and service identities must be revoked.**
9. **Interfaces and dependencies must be identified and closed or formally transferred.**
10. **Supplier exit must be governed rather than treated as an administrative contract closure.**
11. **Infrastructure must be sanitised or disposed of according to applicable security requirements.**
12. **A capability is not fully decommissioned until reasonable verification demonstrates that it cannot be operationally reactivated without appropriate authority.**
13. **Archival retention must remain controlled and must not silently preserve operational authority.**
14. **Retirement may be phased or partial where operational continuity requires it.**
15. **Residual risks must be identified, assigned and accepted or treated.**
16. **Replacement by another AI does not automatically transfer the retired capability's authority, assurance, or assumptions.**
17. **Retirement findings and lessons must feed future lifecycle governance.**

## Retirement Assurance Test

Before closure, ask:

> **Can we demonstrate who authorised retirement, what operational authority was terminated, what components and dependencies existed, what evidence was preserved, what was destroyed or retained, what residual risks remain, and why the capability cannot be unintentionally or unauthorisedly returned to operational service?**

If the answer is no, retirement is not yet adequately governed.

## D-AIGAAF Integration

Retirement closes the operational lifecycle while preserving accountability:

**Retirement → Decommissioning → Evidence Preservation → Residual Risk → Closure → Learning**

The final state should be traceable back through the Golden Thread:

**Mission Need → Risk → Requirements → Controls → Testing → Evidence → Assurance → Authority → Conditions → Boundaries → Employment → Monitoring → Change / Incident → Learning → Revalidation / Reauthorisation → Retirement → Decommissioning → Evidence Preservation → Residual Risk → Closure → Learning**

## Final Principle

**An AI capability is not fully retired merely because it is no longer being used. It is retired when its operational authority has been appropriately terminated, its dependencies and access pathways have been controlled, its data and technical assets have been appropriately retained or disposed of, its evidence has been preserved where required, residual risks have been addressed, and closure has been independently or appropriately verified.**
